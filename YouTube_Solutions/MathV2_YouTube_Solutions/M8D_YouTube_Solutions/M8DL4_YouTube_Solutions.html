1 (i)Arithmetic mean of a and b is ______.Definition of the arithmetic meanArithmetic Means — Arithmetic Sequence🟢 Exact(a + b)/2
Concept A "mean" of two numbers is the number you must insert between them to complete a particular progression. Insert one number A between a and b so that a, A, b is an A.P. — the A you get is called the arithmetic mean. Because an A.P. has equal gaps, A must sit exactly halfway between a and b, which is why the answer is the ordinary average.
Formula a, A, b in A.P. ⇔ A − a = b − A ⇔ A = (a + b)/2 The middle term of any three-term A.P. is the average of its neighbours — this is the only fact you need.
Solution
1. Let A be the single arithmetic mean between a and b, so a, A, b form an A.P.
2. Equal common difference means the two gaps are equal: A − a = b − A.This is the defining property of an A.P. written for just three terms.
3. Bring the A's together: A + A = a + b, i.e. 2A = a + b.
4. Divide by 2: A = (a + b)/2.
5. Numeric check with a = 4, b = 10: A = 14/2 = 7, and 4, 7, 10 has equal gaps of 3 ✓
   a  ------  A  ------  b
      gap d      gap d          <-- equal gaps
   A - a = b - A  =>  2A = a + b  =>  A = (a+b)/2
⚡ JEE Trick Remember all three means as "middle term of the matching progression": A.M. for A.P., G.M. for G.P., H.M. for H.P. Trap: candidates write "arithmetic mean = √(ab)" under time pressure — the square root always belongs to the geometric mean.
Answer: (a + b)/2 — the arithmetic mean of a and b.
1 (ii)Geometric mean of between a and b is ______.Definition of the geometric meanHow to find the geometric mean between two numbers🟢 Exact√(ab)
Concept Repeat the mean construction, but with a G.P. instead of an A.P. Insert G between a and b so that a, G, b is a G.P. In a G.P. consecutive ratios are equal (not the differences), and that single condition pins G down to the square root of the product.
Formula a, G, b in G.P. ⇔ G/a = b/G ⇔ G² = ab ⇔ G = √(ab) Requires a, b > 0; if a and b have opposite signs the G.M. does not exist (the module says so explicitly).
Solution
1. Let G be the single geometric mean, so a, G, b is a G.P.
2. Equal common ratio: G/a = b/G.Ratio of 2nd to 1st equals ratio of 3rd to 2nd — the G.P. analogue of "equal gaps".
3. Cross-multiply: G · G = a · b, i.e. G² = ab.
4. Take the positive square root (a, b > 0): G = √(ab).
5. Numeric check with a = 4, b = 9: G = √36 = 6, and 4, 6, 9 has equal ratios 6/4 = 9/6 = 3/2 ✓
   a  ---x r---  G  ---x r---  b
   G/a = b/G   =>  G^2 = ab   =>  G = sqrt(ab)
   note:  G^2 = ab  is the form actually used in exams
⚡ JEE Trick Never carry the root around — carry G² = ab. Almost every G.M. question is solved faster in the squared form (e.g. "AM × HM = GM²" is instantly ab = ab). Trap: forgetting that the G.M. of two numbers of opposite sign does not exist.
Answer: √(ab) (equivalently G² = ab).
1 (iii)Harmonic mean of a and b is ______.Definition of the harmonic meanHow to insert the harmonic mean between two numbers🟢 Exact2ab/(a + b)
Concept Same construction a third time, now with an H.P. Insert H between a and b so that a, H, b is an H.P. An H.P. is defined by flipping to an A.P., so the correct move is: flip all three, apply the A.M. rule to the reciprocals, then flip back.
Formula a, H, b in H.P. ⇔ 1/a, 1/H, 1/b in A.P. ⇔ 2/H = 1/a + 1/b ⇔ H = 2ab/(a + b) "Twice the product over the sum" — the phrase to memorise.
Solution
1. a, H, b in H.P. means the reciprocals 1/a, 1/H, 1/b are in A.P.
2. Middle term of an A.P. is the average of its neighbours: 1/H = ½(1/a + 1/b).
3. Multiply by 2: 2/H = 1/a + 1/b = (b + a)/(ab).Put the right-hand side over a single denominator before inverting — this is where sign slips happen.
4. Invert both sides: H/2 = ab/(a + b), so H = 2ab/(a + b).
5. Numeric check with a = 4, b = 12: H = 2·48/16 = 6, and 1/4, 1/6, 1/12 has equal gaps of −1/12 ✓
   a , H , b  in H.P.
   flip:  1/a , 1/H , 1/b  in A.P.
          2/H = 1/a + 1/b = (a+b)/ab
   flip back:  H = 2ab/(a+b)
⚡ JEE Trick The three means in one line: A = (a+b)/2, G = √(ab), H = 2ab/(a+b). Multiply the outer two: A·H = (a+b)/2 · 2ab/(a+b) = ab = G². That identity G² = A·H solves half of this lecture in one step.
Answer: 2ab/(a + b) — the harmonic mean of a and b.
1 (iv)Arithmetic mean ______ Geometric mean.The A.M.–G.M. inequalityRelation Between AM, GM and HM | Class 11 Math | JEE🟢 Exact≥ (is greater than or equal to)
Concept The single most-used inequality in all of JEE algebra. For two positive numbers the arithmetic mean is never smaller than the geometric mean, and the two are equal only when the numbers coincide. The proof is one line — a perfect square is never negative.
Formula (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab) for a, b > 0, with equality ⇔ a = b. The module's Property (i) states A > G for two distinct positive numbers; with a = b allowed it becomes A ≥ G.
Solution
1. Start from a fact that is always true: (√a − √b)² ≥ 0 for a, b ≥ 0.
2. Expand: a − 2√(ab) + b ≥ 0.(√a)² = a and (√b)² = b because a, b are non-negative.
3. Rearrange: a + b ≥ 2√(ab).
4. Divide by 2: (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab), i.e. A.M. ≥ G.M.
5. Equality holds exactly when (√a − √b)² = 0, i.e. when a = b.
6. Numeric check with a = 2, b = 8: A = 5, G = 4, and indeed 5 > 4 ✓
   (sqrt a - sqrt b)^2 >= 0
    a - 2 sqrt(ab) + b >= 0
        a + b >= 2 sqrt(ab)
      (a+b)/2 >= sqrt(ab)        A.M. >= G.M.
   equality  <=>  a = b
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a question asks for a minimum of a sum whose product is constant (or a maximum of a product whose sum is constant), reach straight for A.M. ≥ G.M. Trap: applying it to numbers that are not all positive — the inequality then simply fails.
Answer: ≥ (Arithmetic mean ≥ Geometric mean, with equality only when the two numbers are equal).
1 (v)Arithmetic mean ______ Geometric mean ______ Harmonic mean.The full A.M. ≥ G.M. ≥ H.M. chainRelation Between AM, GM, and HM | Sequence and Series Part 4🟢 Exact≥ , ≥  (A.M. ≥ G.M. ≥ H.M.)
Concept The three means of two positive numbers always come in the fixed order A ≥ G ≥ H. The second half of the chain is not a new theorem: it is the first half applied to the reciprocals, because H is built out of 1/a and 1/b.
Formula A ≥ G ≥ H together with G² = A·H. The product identity plus the ordering means G always lies exactly "geometrically between" A and H.
Solution
1. Part 1 (proved in the previous part): A = (a+b)/2 ≥ √(ab) = G.
2. Part 2: apply A.M. ≥ G.M. to the positive numbers 1/a and 1/b: (1/a + 1/b)/2 ≥ √(1/(ab)).
3. The left side is 1/H by definition of the harmonic mean; the right side is 1/√(ab) = 1/G.
4. So 1/H ≥ 1/G. Both are positive, so inverting reverses the inequality: G ≥ H.This flip is the step students forget — inverting a true inequality between positives always turns ≥ into ≤.
5. Chain the two results: A ≥ G ≥ H.
6. Numeric check with a = 4, b = 16: A = 10, G = 8, H = 2·64/20 = 6.4 — and 10 ≥ 8 ≥ 6.4 ✓ Also G² = 64 = 10 × 6.4 ✓
   a = 4 , b = 16
   A = (4+16)/2 = 10
   G = sqrt(4*16) = 8
   H = 2*4*16/(4+16) = 128/20 = 6.4
   10 >= 8 >= 6.4          and   8^2 = 64 = 10 * 6.4
⚡ JEE Trick Store the two facts together: A ≥ G ≥ H and G² = AH. Given any two of A, G, H you can always find the third, and the ordering instantly kills impossible options (e.g. "A = 5, G = 7" is nonsense). Trap: assuming G is the average of A and H — it is their geometric mean, not arithmetic.
Answer: ≥ and ≥ — Arithmetic mean ≥ Geometric mean ≥ Harmonic mean.

Concept The two terms 3x and 31−x are always positive and their product is a constant: 3x·31−x = 31 = 3. Constant product plus "find the minimum of the sum" is the exact signature of A.M. ≥ G.M.
Formula (u + v)/2 ≥ √(uv) ⇒ u + v ≥ 2√(uv), here with u = 3x, v = 31−x. Also 31/2 = √3, which is how the answer shape q·rp/2 arises.
Solution
1. Both terms are positive for every real x, so A.M. ≥ G.M. applies.
2. Their product: 3x · 31−x = 3x + 1 − x = 31 = 3 — a constant, independent of x.This is the whole trick: the exponents were built to add to 1.
3. Therefore 3x + 31−x ≥ 2√3. So the minimum value is 2√3.
4. Match against q√r: q = 2, r = 3 (and 3 is prime, as the question demands).
5. Match against q·rp/2: 2·3p/2 = 2·31/2 ⇒ p/2 = 1/2 ⇒ p = 1.
6. Equality check: it occurs when 3x = 31−x, i.e. x = 1 − x, x = ½. Then 31/2 + 31/2 = 2√3 ≈ 3.464 ✓ and e.g. x = 0 gives 1 + 3 = 4 > 3.464 ✓
   u = 3^x  ,  v = 3^(1-x)      u*v = 3^1 = 3   (constant!)
   u + v >= 2 sqrt(uv) = 2 sqrt 3
   2 sqrt 3 = 2 * 3^(1/2) = q * r^(p/2)
   =>  q = 2 , r = 3 , p = 1
   equality at 3^x = 3^(1-x)  =>  x = 1/2
⚡ JEE Trick Spot the pattern ax + ak−x: the exponents sum to a constant, so the product is ak and the minimum is instantly 2·ak/2. Trap: reading the target shape carelessly — here you must extract p from an exponent, not from a coefficient.
Answer: p = 1, q = 2, r = 3 (minimum value = 2√3).

Concept This is the module's Property (ii) in disguise. Knowing the A.M. of two numbers fixes their sum; knowing the G.M. fixes their product; and sum + product is exactly what a monic quadratic stores. So the means and the quadratic are two encodings of the same information.
Formula x² − (sum)x + (product) = 0 with sum = 2A and product = G², giving x² − 2Ax + G² = 0. Sum = 2A because A = (α+β)/2; product = G² because G = √(αβ).
Solution
1. Let the roots be α and β. Given A = (α + β)/2 ⇒ α + β = 2A.
2. Given G = √(αβ) ⇒ αβ = G².
3. The monic quadratic with roots α, β is x² − (α + β)x + αβ = 0, i.e. x² − 2Ax + G² = 0.
4. Compare with the required form px² + qAx + rG² = 0, coefficient by coefficient:Match the x² term first, then the x term, then the constant.
5. x²-coefficient: p = 1. x-coefficient: qA = −2A ⇒ q = −2. Constant: rG² = G² ⇒ r = 1.
6. Numeric check: take α = 4, β = 16. Then A = 10, G = 8, and x² − 20x + 64 = 0 factors as (x − 4)(x − 16) = 0 ✓

   product = G^2
   x^2 - 2A x + G^2 = 0        <-- the standard equation
   p x^2 + qA x + rG^2 = 0
   =>  p = 1 , q = -2 , r = 1
   roots are then  A +- sqrt(A^2 - G^2)
⚡ JEE Trick Solving x² − 2Ax + G² = 0 by the quadratic formula gives the numbers themselves: A ± √(A² − G²) — that is the module's Property (iii), and it turns "A.M. and G.M. are given, find the numbers" into one substitution. Trap: writing +2A instead of −2A; sum of roots is minus the x-coefficient.
Answer: p = 1, q = −2, r = 1 (the equation is x² − 2Ax + G² = 0).
3 (iii)If a, b, c are in G.P. and x, y are A.M.'s between a, b and b, c respectively, then p/x + q/y = r/b. Find p, q and r.Mixing a G.P. with two arithmetic meansIf a, b, c are in G.P. and x, y are the arithmetic means of a,b and b,c respectively…🟢 Exactp = 1, q = 1, r = 2
Concept Two structures are combined: a G.P. (which lets you write b = ar, c = ar²) and two arithmetic means (which are plain averages). Substituting the G.P. parametrisation turns the messy fraction sum into a ratio of two polynomials in r that cancels completely.
Formula x = (a + b)/2, y = (b + c)/2 and b² = ac. Claim: 1/x + 1/y = 2/b. Writing b = ar, c = ar² automatically satisfies b² = ac and removes one variable.
Solution
1. x is the A.M. of a and b, so x = (a + b)/2 ⇒ 1/x = 2/(a + b).
2. y is the A.M. of b and c, so y = (b + c)/2 ⇒ 1/y = 2/(b + c).
3. Since a, b, c are in G.P., put b = ar and c = ar² (r = common ratio, a ≠ 0).
4. Then 1/x = 2/(a + ar) = 2/[a(1 + r)] and 1/y = 2/(ar + ar²) = 2/[ar(1 + r)].
5. Add: 1/x + 1/y = 2/[a(1+r)] + 2/[ar(1+r)] = (2/[a(1+r)])·(1 + 1/r) = (2/[a(1+r)])·((r+1)/r).Factor 2/[a(1+r)] out of both terms — the (1 + r) then cancels against (r + 1).
6. The (1 + r) factors cancel: 1/x + 1/y = 2/(ar) = 2/b (because b = ar).
7. Compare with p/x + q/y = r/b: p = 1, q = 1, r = 2.Note the letter r is reused by the question for the constant — it is not the common ratio here.
8. Numeric check: a = 2, b = 4, c = 8 (G.P.). x = 3, y = 6. 1/3 + 1/6 = 1/2 and 2/b = 2/4 = 1/2 ✓
   a , b=ar , c=ar^2
   x = a(1+r)/2      y = ar(1+r)/2
   1/x + 1/y = 2/[a(1+r)] + 2/[ar(1+r)]
             = 2(r + 1) / [a r (1+r)]
             = 2/(ar) = 2/b
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever "a, b, c are in G.P." appears, immediately substitute b = ar, c = ar²; the ratio r cancels out of every homogeneous identity. Trap: trying to clear denominators directly with b² = ac — it works, but takes four times as long.
Answer: p = 1, q = 1, r = 2 (i.e. 1/x + 1/y = 2/b).

Concept Sides in G.P. means a, ar, ar² with r > 1 (take them increasing), so the hypotenuse is the largest, ar². Pythagoras then gives a quartic in r that is really a quadratic in r² — and r² turns out to be the golden ratio. The answer is multi-correct because two of the four options are algebraically the same number.
Formula Pythagoras: a² + (ar)² = (ar²)² ⇒ r⁴ − r² − 1 = 0. Smallest angle θ is opposite the smallest side: tan θ = a/(ar) = 1/r. In a right triangle the two legs are a and ar, so the tangent of the angle opposite a is a/ar.
Solution
1. Let the sides be a, ar, ar² with r > 1, so ar² is the hypotenuse.
2. Pythagoras: a² + a²r² = a²r⁴. Divide by a²: 1 + r² = r⁴.
3. Write t = r²: t² − t − 1 = 0 ⇒ t = (1 ± √5)/2. Take the positive root: r² = (1 + √5)/2 (the golden ratio φ).The negative root (1 − √5)/2 ≈ −0.618 is rejected because r² > 0.
4. The smallest angle θ faces the smallest side a. Its opposite side is a and its adjacent side is the other leg, ar.
5. So tan θ = a/(ar) = 1/r = 1/√(r²) = 1/√((√5+1)/2) = √(2/(√5 + 1)) ⇒ option (c).
6. Rationalise: 2/(√5 + 1) = 2(√5 − 1)/[(√5)² − 1²] = 2(√5 − 1)/4 = (√5 − 1)/2.
7. Therefore tan θ = √((√5 − 1)/2) as well ⇒ option (b). Options (b) and (c) are the same number, which is why both are keyed.
8. Numeric check: (√5 − 1)/2 ≈ 1.236/2 = 0.618, so tan θ ≈ √0.618 ≈ 0.786, i.e. θ ≈ 38.2°. Sides: a = 1, r ≈ 1.272, so 1, 1.272, 1.618. Test Pythagoras: 1 + 1.618 = 2.618 = 1.618² ✓ And tan θ = 1/1.272 = 0.786 ✓
9. Reject (a) and (d): both exceed 1 (√1.618 ≈ 1.272 and √(2/1.236) ≈ 1.272), so they equal tan of the larger acute angle, not the smallest.
