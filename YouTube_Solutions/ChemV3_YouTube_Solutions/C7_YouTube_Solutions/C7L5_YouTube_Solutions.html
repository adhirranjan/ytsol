1 (i)Fill in the blank: According to Le Chatelier’s principle, if a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing the conditions, the position of equilibrium moves to ______ the change.Statement of the principleLe Chatelier's Principle | Effect of Concentration | Effect of Pressure | Effect of Temperature🟡 Similarcounteract (oppose / minimise)
Concept Le Chatelier’s principle in one clause: a disturbed equilibrium shifts in the direction that counteracts the disturbance. Book p.50 puts it as “the equilibrium will shift to the right or the left so as to minimise the change”.
Key relation disturbance → shift that partially opposes it → new equilibrium “Partially” matters — the system never fully undoes the change, or nothing would have happened at all.
Solution
1. The principle applies only to a system already at equilibrium, disturbed by an external agency.
2. Three things can do the disturbing (p.50): a change of concentration, a change of pressure or volume, and a change of temperature.
3. In each case the position of equilibrium moves so as to oppose — to counteract — the change imposed.
4. Add reactant and the system consumes some of it; raise the pressure and it moves to the side with fewer gas molecules; heat it and it absorbs heat by running the endothermic way.
5. The opposition is only partial. Adding reactant leaves more reactant present than before, just less than immediately after the addition.
6. So the blank is counteract (equivalently “oppose” or “minimise”). This is Column-II entry (s) of the match-the-column below, word for word.
 disturb : add reactant  -> shift consumes it
           raise P       -> shift to fewer moles
           raise T       -> shift the endothermic way

 the shift always OPPOSES, never reinforces
⚡ JEE Trick Every Le Chatelier question reduces to one sentence: name the change, then move the equilibrium the way that undoes it. No arithmetic is needed for the direction.
Answer: the position of equilibrium moves to counteract the change.

Concept The principle is not a general-purpose tool: it predicts the effect of exactly three kinds of change — concentration, pressure or volume, and temperature. Book p.50 lists them as (i), (ii) and (iii).
Key relation the three levers: [ ]  |  P or V  |  T A catalyst is deliberately absent from the list — it changes nothing about the equilibrium position.
Solution
1. Book p.50: “These are some factors by which a system in equilibrium can be disturbed — (i) changing the concentration of reactant and product, (ii) changing the pressure (or volume) of the system, (iii) changing the temperature.”
2. So the blank is filled by any of concentration, pressure (or volume), or temperature.
3. The three are not equivalent in effect. Concentration and pressure move the position but leave K unchanged; temperature changes K itself.
4. That distinction is what the rest of this lecture turns on, and it is why temperature questions need the sign of ΔH while pressure questions need only Δng.
5. Note what is not on the list: a catalyst, and an inert gas added at constant volume. Neither disturbs the equilibrium at all.
6. The principle also requires a system already at equilibrium — Suggested Problem 2 of this lecture makes that the whole question.

1 (iii)Fill in the blank: If a chemical system at equilibrium experiences a change in concentration, temperature, volume or partial pressure, then the equilibrium will shift to ______ the change.Direction of the shiftGCSE Chemistry - Le Chatelier's Principle - Position of Equilibrium in Reversible Reactions🟡 Similarcounteract (partially offset)
Concept The same statement as blank (i), now listing the disturbances explicitly. Whatever is changed, the equilibrium shifts so as to counteract that change.
Key relation shift direction = whichever way partially undoes the imposed change Writing it this way avoids memorising four separate rules — one principle generates all of them.
Solution
1. Concentration: increase a reactant and the shift consumes it; remove a product and the shift replaces some of it.
2. Temperature: heat the system and the shift absorbs heat, i.e. runs in the endothermic direction.
3. Volume: decrease the volume (raising the pressure) and the shift moves to the side with fewer gas molecules, which lowers the pressure again.
4. Partial pressure: raise the partial pressure of one gas and the shift consumes it, exactly as for concentration.
5. In all four cases the single word describing the shift is counteract.
6. Note the word “partially”. The system never restores the original state — it moves to a new equilibrium that lies between the old one and the disturbance.
7. Note the precise wording that the principle demands: the shift partially counteracts the change, it never cancels it. Doubling the pressure on a Δng < 0 equilibrium leaves the pressure higher than it was, only less high than it would have been without the shift. Blanks filled with “cancel” or “reverse” are therefore wrong even though they sound right.
 raise [reactant] -> consume some of it
 raise T          -> absorb heat (endothermic way)
 lower V          -> go to fewer gas moles
 raise p(i)       -> consume that gas

 all four : COUNTERACT, partially
⚡ JEE Trick If an option says the system “restores the original concentrations”, it is wrong. The opposition is partial, which is why a new equilibrium position exists at all.
Answer: the equilibrium shifts so as to counteract (partially offset) the change.
1 (iv)Fill in the blank: In an exothermic reaction, an increase in temperature will shift the equilibrium to the ______ side.Effect of temperatureLe Chȃtelier’s principle: Changing temperature | Equilibrium | AP Chemistry | Khan Academy🟡 Similarreactant (left) side
Concept Treat heat as a product of an exothermic reaction. Adding heat is then like adding a product, so the equilibrium shifts back toward the reactants.
Key relation exothermic: A + B ⇌ C + D + heat ⇒ raising T shifts left This is the one temperature rule; everything else about heating an equilibrium follows from it.
Solution
1. Write the exothermic reaction with heat on the product side: reactants ⇌ products + heat.
2. Increasing the temperature supplies heat, which is equivalent to adding a product.
3. By Le Chatelier, the system counteracts by consuming that heat — i.e. by running backwards.
4. So the equilibrium shifts to the reactant (left) side, and the yield of product falls.
5. In terms of the constant: for an exothermic reaction K decreases as T rises, which is the same statement in numbers.
6. The mirror case: for an endothermic reaction heat is on the reactant side, so heating shifts the equilibrium forward — item 2(iv) of this Quick Recap tests exactly that.
7. Note that the wording is deliberately the harder of the two forms. Most textbook statements are given for the endothermic case, so a candidate who has memorised “heat drives it forward” will fill this blank wrongly. Read the ΔH sign first, then apply the rule; the direction of the answer follows from the sign, never from the remembered sentence.
 exothermic : R <=> P + heat
   T up   -> shift LEFT  (K decreases)
   T down -> shift RIGHT (K increases)

 endothermic : R + heat <=> P   (opposite)
⚡ JEE Trick Write heat into the equation as a species — on the right for exothermic, on the left for endothermic. Then every temperature question becomes an ordinary “adding a species” question.
Answer: in an exothermic reaction, an increase in temperature shifts the equilibrium to the reactant (left) side.
1 (v)Fill in the blank: According to Le Chatelier’s principle, an increase in pressure will favour the side of the reaction that has the ______ number of moles of gas.Effect of pressureLeChatelier Principle: Change Pressure🟡 Similarsmaller (fewer)
Concept Raising the pressure squeezes the system, and the system counteracts by reducing the number of gas molecules. So the favoured side is the one with the smaller number of moles of gas.
Key relation P ↑ ⇒ shift toward fewer gas moles; P ↓ ⇒ shift toward more gas moles Only gaseous moles are counted — solids and liquids occupy negligible volume and take no part.
Solution
1. Increasing the pressure at constant temperature means compressing the gas mixture into a smaller volume.
2. The system opposes the compression by reducing the total number of gas molecules present.
3. That means shifting toward whichever side of the equation has fewer moles of gas. So the blank is smaller.
4. Example: N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 goes 4 moles → 2 moles, so high pressure favours ammonia — the basis of the Haber process.
5. Counter-example: N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2 goes 1 → 2, so high pressure pushes it back toward N2O4.
6. And when the two sides have equal gas moles (Δng = 0, as in H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI), pressure has no effect on the position at all.
7. Note also what the blank does not say. It refers to the number of moles of gas, not the number of species: CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g) has three formulae but only one gas, so pressure drives it entirely by the single CO2 term. Counting solids into the total is the commonest way this blank is filled incorrectly.
 dn(g) < 0 : high P favours PRODUCTS (Haber)
 dn(g) > 0 : high P favours REACTANTS
 dn(g) = 0 : pressure has NO effect

 count gases only
⚡ JEE Trick Count gas moles on each side before reading the options. Δng alone decides every pressure question in this lecture — the identity of the substances never matters.
Answer: an increase in pressure favours the side with the smaller number of moles of gas.
2 (i)True or false: The addition of a catalyst to a system at equilibrium changes the equilibrium position.CatalystLe-Chatelier's Principle: Adding a catalyst | Equilibrium | Chemistry | Khan Academy🟢 ExactFalse
Concept A catalyst speeds the forward and backward reactions by the same factor, so it changes neither K nor the equilibrium position. It only shortens the time taken to arrive.
Key relation kf → xkf, kb → xkb ⇒ K = kf/kb unchanged ⇒ position unchanged The position of equilibrium is the set of concentrations satisfying the K expression, so an unchanged K means an unchanged position.
Solution
1. Forward and reverse reactions pass through the same transition state, so a catalyst lowers the activation energy of both by the same amount.
2. Both rate constants are therefore multiplied by the same factor, and their ratio K is untouched.
3. Since the equilibrium position is fixed by K at that temperature, the position cannot change either.
4. So the statement is False.
5. What a catalyst does change is the time: the concentration–time curves flatten sooner, at the same heights.
6. Note that a catalyst is not among the three disturbances listed on book p.50 — it is deliberately excluded, because it disturbs nothing.
7. Note how the statement is built: it is true of the rate and false of the position, so a reader who half-remembers “a catalyst changes things” will mark it true. The safe test is K = kf/kb; since both rate constants are multiplied by the same factor, nothing about the equilibrium position can move.
 uncatalysed : slow approach, plateau at h
 catalysed   : fast approach, plateau at h

 same h -> same position -> same K
 only the time axis changes
⚡ JEE Trick In Le Chatelier questions the catalyst option is nearly always the distractor. It is the one intervention that changes neither the position nor the constant — only the speed.
Answer: False — a catalyst does not change the equilibrium position, only the time to reach it.
2 (ii)True or false: Decreasing the concentration of a product will shift the equilibrium to the right.Effect of concentrationLe Chatelier's Principle | Effect of Concentration | Effect of Pressure | Effect of Temperature🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Removing product makes Q fall below K, so the system runs forward to replace some of it. “Shifts to the right” is the same statement in arrow language.
Key relation remove product ⇒ Q < K ⇒ net forward reaction Q has the product concentrations in its numerator, so lowering them lowers Q.
Solution
1. Q = [products]ν/[reactants]ν. Decreasing a product concentration lowers the numerator.
2. So Q falls below K, and the system must run forward to bring Q back up.
3. Running forward means moving to the right of the written equation, which is exactly the claim. So the statement is True.
4. Book p.50, case (a): “If more reactant is added or some product is removed from an equilibrium mixture … the reaction moves in the forward direction (as Qc < Kc) … and more products are produced.”
5. This is the principle behind Level-4 Q3 of this lecture, where an absorbent removes SO3 as fast as it forms and so drives the reaction forward.
6. It is also why industrial ammonia plants condense NH3 out of the loop continuously — removing product keeps Q permanently below K.
7. Note that “right” here means toward the products, which is precisely how a chemist drives a reversible reaction to completion in the laboratory — distilling off a volatile product, or precipitating one, keeps Q permanently below K and the forward reaction never stops. The statement is the principle behind Le Chatelier-driven synthesis.
 remove product : numerator of Q falls
                  Q < K
                  -> net FORWARD (shift right)

 add reactant   : denominator falls too -> same
⚡ JEE Trick Adding reactant and removing product are the same move in disguise — both lower Q and both drive the reaction forward. Recognising that halves the rules to remember.
Answer: True — decreasing a product concentration shifts the equilibrium to the right.
2 (iii)True or false: Le Chatelier’s principle can be applied to all types of reactions.Scope of the principleEquilibrium || Chemical Equilibrium 05 || Le - Chatelier's Principle IIT JEE MAINS / NEET ||🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept The principle applies only to systems that are already at equilibrium. A reaction still on its way there, or an irreversible one, is outside its scope.
Key relation precondition: the system must be at equilibrium before the disturbance The principle predicts how an equilibrium moves; with no equilibrium there is nothing to move.
Solution
1. Book p.50 opens with the qualifier: “Le Chatelier’s principle is applicable for any system in equilibrium…”
2. So it covers homogeneous and heterogeneous equilibria alike — but only those actually at equilibrium.
3. An irreversible reaction never reaches equilibrium, so the principle says nothing about it.
4. Nor does it apply to a reversible mixture still approaching equilibrium; there the direction is set by comparing Q with K, not by Le Chatelier.
5. The claim “all types of reactions” is therefore too broad, and the statement is False.
6. Suggested Problem 2 of this lecture asks the same thing as a multiple choice, and the answer there is “systems not in equilibrium” — the one category excluded.
7. Note the boundary the statement crosses. Le Chatelier applies to any system that is at equilibrium, homogeneous or heterogeneous, gaseous or in solution; what it cannot describe is a system still on its way there, or one driven irreversibly. 2 (iv)True or false: A decrease in temperature will shift the equilibrium to the side of an endothermic reaction.Effect of temperatureLeChatelier: Change in Temperature🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept Cooling removes heat, so the system counteracts by producing heat — it runs the exothermic way, not the endothermic way. The statement has the direction inverted.
Key relation T ↓ ⇒ shift in the exothermic direction; T ↑ ⇒ shift in the endothermic direction Treat heat as a species: removing it pulls the equilibrium toward the side that makes it.
Solution
1. Write heat explicitly. For an exothermic forward reaction: R ⇌ P + heat.
2. Lowering the temperature removes heat from the system, which is like removing a product.
3. The system counteracts by making more heat, i.e. by running in the exothermic direction.
4. The statement claims cooling favours the endothermic side, which is the opposite. So it is False.
5. The correct pairing: heating → endothermic direction; cooling → exothermic direction.
6. Level-5 Q1 of this lecture asks the heating half of the same rule and the answer there is “favours the endothermic reaction” — consistent with this being false.
7. Note that cooling and the endothermic direction are opposites, which is why the statement fails. Cooling removes heat, and the system replaces it by running the direction that releases heat — the exothermic one. Pairing “decrease in temperature” with “endothermic” in one sentence is the standard construction for a false statement here.

⚡ JEE Trick The two temperature statements are mirror images and exam papers print one of them inverted. Fix the heating case in memory (“heat drives the endothermic way”) and derive the other.
Answer: False — a decrease in temperature shifts the equilibrium toward the exothermic side.

1. At constant temperature, expanding the container lowers the pressure of the gas mixture (Boyle’s law).
2. The system opposes that drop by increasing the number of gas molecules present.
3. That means shifting toward the side of the equation with more moles of gas, which is exactly what the statement says. True.
4. Example: PCl5 ⇌ PCl3 + Cl2 goes 1 → 2 moles, so expanding the vessel drives more dissociation — the reasoning behind Level-5 Q9(d) of this lecture.
