Free energy is the thermodynamic property which account for ______ and ______ of the system.		What G packages togetherClass 11 chapter 6 || Thermodynamics 12 || Gibb's Free Energy IIT JEE MAINS /NEET | Spontaneity |🟡 Similarenthalpy, entropy
Concept Two separate tendencies decide whether a change happens on its own: nature prefers to shed energy (an exothermic drop in enthalpy) and nature prefers to spread out (a rise in entropy). Neither alone is a reliable test — ice melts although it absorbs heat, and rusting is spontaneous although it orders the system. Gibbs packaged both into one quantity so that a single number settles the question.
Key relation G = H − TS  ⇒  ΔG = ΔH − TΔS ΔH is the energy term, TΔS is the entropy term; G is built out of exactly these two and nothing else.
Solution
1. Write the definition given on p.269: G = H − TS. The right-hand side contains H (enthalpy) and S (entropy) only, with T as the weighting factor.
2. Differencing between products and reactants at constant T gives ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, the Gibbs–Helmholtz equation.
3. The first term ΔH is the energy factor; a negative ΔH pushes the reaction forward.
4. The second term −TΔS is the entropy factor; a positive ΔS also pushes the reaction forward, and its influence grows with temperature.
5. Because G combines both drives with the correct sign, ΔG alone decides spontaneity, which is why it is called the overall criterion.
6. Hence the two blanks are enthalpy and entropy.
 G = H - T S
     |     |
     |     +--- entropy   factor (disorder)
     +--------- enthalpy  factor (energy)

 dG = dH - T dS
      (-ve helps)  (+ve dS helps)
⚡ JEE Trick Read G = H − TS as "energy minus disorder-times-temperature". Any blank asking what free energy "accounts for" is answered by naming the two symbols standing in that equation.
Answer: enthalpy and entropy
1 (ii)Gibbs free energy equation is ΔG = ______.Gibbs–Helmholtz equationTHERMODYNAMICS - 19 || GIBBS FREE ENERGY (∆G = ∆H - T∆S )🟢 ExactΔH − TΔS
Concept The Gibbs free energy equation converts the two-part spontaneity test into one arithmetic line. Starting from the definition G = H − TS and holding temperature constant, every term can be differenced between the final and initial state to give a working formula usable with tabulated ΔH and ΔS values.
Key relation ΔG = ΔH − TΔS Called the Gibbs–Helmholtz equation; it is eq. (iii) on p.269 of the chapter.
Solution
1. Begin with G = H − TS for both states, then subtract: ΔG = ΔH − Δ(TS).
2. At constant temperature T is a common factor, so Δ(TS) = TΔS and the equation collapses to ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
3. Units must match before use: ΔH is normally kJ mol−1 while ΔS is J K−1 mol−1, so divide ΔS by 1000 or multiply ΔH by 1000.
4. T is the absolute temperature in kelvin, never °C — this single slip is the most common source of a wrong sign.
5. An equivalent form follows from ΔG = −TΔStotal, which shows the equation is just the second law rewritten for the system alone.
6. Worked check: for ΔH = −120 kJ mol−1 and ΔS = 200 J K−1 mol−1 at 298 K, ΔG = −120 − (298)(0.200) = −179.6 kJ mol−1, comfortably spontaneous.
7. Hence the blank is ΔH − TΔS.
 G  = H - T S            (definition)
 dG = dH - d(TS)
 dG = dH - T dS          (T constant)

 units:  kJ/mol   K   J/K/mol -> /1000
⚡ JEE Trick Before substituting, force ΔS into kJ K−1 mol−1 by shifting the decimal three places. Doing the conversion first makes every Gibbs numerical a one-line subtraction.
Answer: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS
1 (iii)At equilibrium ΔG = ______.ΔG at equilibriumGibb's Energy and Spontaneity - Thermodynamics (Part 28)🟡 Similarzero
Concept Equilibrium is the state where the forward and backward changes are equally favoured, so the system has no remaining tendency to move either way. In Gibbs language the free energy has reached its minimum, and at a minimum the slope is flat — no further free energy can be released in either direction.
Key relation ΔG = 0  ⇔  ΔH = TΔS  ⇔  T = ΔH/ΔS This is Case III on p.270: the two tendencies are equal in magnitude and opposite in effect.
Solution
1. Spontaneous change requires ΔG < 0 and the reverse change requires ΔG > 0; the boundary between the two is ΔG = 0.
2. Setting ΔH − TΔS = 0 gives ΔH = TΔS, the balance point at which the energy drive exactly cancels the entropy drive.
3. Rearranged, T = ΔH/ΔS is the crossover temperature — for a phase change this is the melting or boiling point.
4. Water at 373 K and 1 bar is the standard illustration: liquid and vapour coexist because ΔGvap = 0 there.
5. Note the sharp distinction from ΔG°, which is not zero at equilibrium; instead ΔG° = −RT ln K.
6. Numerical illustration: for Ag2O decomposition ΔH = 30.56 kJ mol−1 and ΔS = 6.6 J K−1 mol−1, so ΔG vanishes only at T = 30560/6.6 = 4630 K (Example-3, p.271).
7. Hence the blank is zero.
 dG < 0   forward change is spontaneous
 dG = 0   EQUILIBRIUM, no net change
 dG > 0   reverse change is spontaneous

 dG = 0  ->  dH = T dS  ->  T = dH / dS
⚡ JEE Trick "At equilibrium" always converts to ΔG = 0 for the actual mixture and to ΔG° = −RT ln K for the standard state. Mixing up the two is the classic trap.
Answer: zero
1 (iv)For a spontaneous reaction ΔG must be ______ than zero.Sign of ΔG for spontaneityEasiest Trick to identify Spontaneous & non spontaneous reaction NEET/JEE🟡 Similarless
Concept A spontaneous change is one that proceeds on its own without outside help. The second law says such a change must raise the total entropy of the universe, and free energy is constructed precisely so that this requirement shows up as a negative sign on the system's own ΔG.
Key relation ΔGsystem = −TΔStotal Derived on p.269; since T is always positive, ΔStotal > 0 forces ΔG < 0.
Solution
1. Second law for a spontaneous change: ΔStotal = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurroundings > 0.
2. At constant T and P the heat lost to the surroundings is −ΔH, so ΔSsurr = −ΔH/T.
3. Substituting, TΔStotal = TΔSsystem − ΔH = −(ΔH − TΔS) = −ΔG.
4. Therefore ΔG = −TΔStotal. Because T > 0 always, ΔStotal positive is the same statement as ΔG negative.
5. So a spontaneous reaction must have ΔG less than zero; ΔG = 0 is equilibrium and ΔG > 0 is non-spontaneous in the forward direction.
6. Sanity check with melting ice at 298 K: ΔH = +6.97 kJ mol−1, TΔS = 298 × 0.0254 = 7.57 kJ mol−1, giving ΔG = −0.6 kJ mol−1 — negative, and indeed ice melts at room temperature.
7. Hence the blank is less.
 dS_total > 0        (second law, spontaneous)
 dG = -T dS_total
 T > 0 always
 -------------------------------------------
 => dG < 0   i.e. dG is LESS than zero
⚡ JEE Trick Remember the single bridge ΔG = −TΔStotal. It converts every entropy-of-the-universe statement into a free-energy statement and back, with only a sign flip.
Answer: less than zero
1 (v)If ΔG is negative the process is ______.ΔG as the spontaneity criterionGibbs Free Energy and Spontaneity - Thermodynamics | Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 5🟡 Similarspontaneous
Concept The sign of ΔG is the single verdict on whether a process runs forward by itself. Negative means the forward change releases free energy and is spontaneous (feasible); positive means only the reverse direction can occur; zero means the system sits at equilibrium.
Key relation ΔG < 0 → spontaneous  |  ΔG = 0 → equilibrium  |  ΔG > 0 → non-spontaneous The three-line summary printed on p.270 of the chapter.
Solution
1. ΔG measures the useful work obtainable: a ΔG of −300 kJ means up to 300 kJ of work can be drawn out of the reaction.
2. A system that can give out work has a natural push in that direction, so it moves without external help — that is exactly what spontaneous means.
3. If instead ΔG were +300 kJ, at least 300 kJ of work would have to be supplied to force the change, so it is non-spontaneous.
4. Spontaneous does not mean fast: diamond → graphite has ΔG < 0 yet takes geological time. Thermodynamics rules on direction, kinetics on speed.
5. A non-spontaneous step can still be driven by coupling it to a strongly negative-ΔG reaction, as with 2Fe2O3 + 6CO on p.270.
6. Watch the standard-state subscript: ΔG° < 0 only guarantees K > 1, whereas the verdict on the actual mixture always rests on ΔG itself.
7. Hence the blank is spontaneous (feasible).
 dG (kJ)   verdict            work
 -300      spontaneous        300 kJ obtainable
    0      equilibrium        none
 +300      non-spontaneous    300 kJ must be fed
⚡ JEE Trick Negative ΔG = the reaction "pays you" work; positive ΔG = you must "pay" the reaction. Spontaneity questions reduce to who is paying whom.
Answer: spontaneous (thermodynamically feasible)

1It is impossible to attain the lowest temperature known as zero degree absolute. This is simple statement of:
(a) first law of thermodynamics   (b) second law of thermodynamics   (c) third law of thermodynamics   (d) none of the above		Third law of thermodynamicsThird Law of Thermodynamics | Thermodynamics | Class 11🟢 Exactc
Concept Each law of thermodynamics answers a different question. The zeroth defines temperature, the first conserves energy, the second fixes the direction of change, and the third fixes the absolute zero of the entropy scale — and in doing so declares that absolute zero itself can never actually be reached in a finite number of steps.
Key relation S → 0 as T → 0 K for a perfect crystal (third law) The unattainability of 0 K is the standard alternative statement of the same law.
Solution
1. Read the claim: "it is impossible to attain the lowest temperature known as zero degree absolute". This is a statement about 0 K, not about energy bookkeeping.
2. Option (a), the first law, is ΔU = q + w — conservation of energy. It says nothing about attainable temperatures.
3. Option (b), the second law, gives ΔSuniverse > 0 for spontaneous change and defines the direction of natural processes, not a temperature limit.
4. Option (c), the third law, states that the entropy of a perfectly crystalline substance is zero at 0 K. Its Nernst unattainability corollary says no finite sequence of processes can cool a body to 0 K.
5. The cooling argument: every refrigeration step removes only a fraction of the remaining heat, so an infinite number of steps would be needed — hence 0 K is a limit approached but never reached.
6. Option (d) is therefore unnecessary; (c) matches exactly.
 law    content
 ----   ---------------------------------------
 0th    thermal equilibrium defines T
 1st    dU = q + w      (energy conserved)
 2nd    dS_univ > 0     (direction of change)
 3rd    S -> 0 at T = 0 K ; 0 K unattainable
⚡ JEE Trick Sort the laws by keyword: "energy" → first, "spontaneity/entropy increases" → second, "absolute zero / perfect crystal" → third. The phrase "zero degree absolute" is a giveaway for the third law.
Answer: (c) third law of thermodynamics

The single parent formula can be dressed in several equivalent costumes — natural log or base-10 log, volume ratio or inverse pressure ratio — and the question is whether each printed costume is actually correct.
Key relation ΔS = nR ln(V2/V1) = 2.303 nR log10(V2/V1) = nR ln(P1/P2) Boyle's law at fixed T gives P1V1 = P2V2, so V2/V1 = P1/P2.

Concept Three different-looking statements about a spontaneous reaction — the sign of ΔG°, the size of K, and the sign of the cell emf — are all the same statement wearing different hats. Two bridging equations tie them together, so fixing any one fixes the other two.

1Define gibbs free energy and change in free energy. What are the units of Gibbs free energy?

Derive the relation between ΔG and ΔStotal.		Definition of G and G ↔ StotalRELATIONSHIP BETWEEN CHANGE IN GIBBS FREE ENERGY AND USEFUL WORK / THERMODYNAMICS🟡 Similar—
Concept Gibbs free energy G is the property whose decrease measures the useful work a system can deliver at constant T and P. Its change ΔG is the criterion of spontaneity, and it is simply the total entropy change of the universe written from the system's point of view.
Key relation G = H − TS = E + PV − TS;   ΔG = ΔH − TΔS = −TΔStotal Both parts of the OR are answered from this one chain of identities.
Solution
1. Definition. Gibbs free energy is "the thermodynamic quantity of the system, the decrease in whose value during a process is equal to the useful work done by the system". Mathematically G = H − TS, and using H = E + PV, G = E + PV − TS.
2. Change in free energy. At constant T and P, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. A ΔG of −300 kJ means up to 300 kJ of useful work is obtainable; a ΔG of +300 kJ means that much work must be supplied.
3. Units. G is an energy, so it is measured in joules or kilojoules; per mole of reaction it is J mol−1 or kJ mol−1. Since H and S are extensive, G is extensive too, and G is a state function.
4. OR-part, step 1. For a system not isolated from its surroundings, ΔStotal = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurroundings.
5. OR-part, step 2. At constant T and P the heat given to the surroundings is (qP)surr = −ΔHsystem, so ΔSsurr = −ΔHsystem/T. Hence TΔStotal = TΔSsystem − ΔHsystem.
6. OR-part, step 3. Multiply by −1: −TΔStotal = ΔHsystem − TΔSsystem = ΔGsystem. Therefore ΔG = −TΔStotal, and because T > 0, ΔStotal > 0 is identical to ΔG < 0.

 -T dS_total = dH_sys - T dS_sys = dG_sys

2Explain Gibbs free energy and spontaneity of the process.Free energy criterion of spontaneityClass 11 Chemistry Chapter 6 | Gibbs Free Energy & Spontaneity - Thermodynamics |Class 11 CBSE/NCERT🟡 Similar—
Concept Neither a fall in energy nor a rise in disorder is by itself a dependable test of spontaneity. Gibbs free energy fuses the two into a single quantity whose sign decides the issue, and because temperature multiplies only the entropy term, the same reaction can change its verdict as the thermometer moves.
Key relation ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; ΔG < 0 spontaneous, = 0 equilibrium, > 0 non-spontaneous These three lines are the summary printed on p.270.
Solution
1. Why a combined criterion is needed. Melting ice is endothermic yet spontaneous above 0 °C; the rusting of iron lowers the system's entropy yet is spontaneous. So ΔH alone and ΔS alone both fail.
2. Case I — both favourable. ΔH = −ve and TΔS = +ve give ΔG = (−) − (+) = −ve at every temperature. The process is spontaneous at all T.
3. Case II — both unfavourable. ΔH = +ve and TΔS = −ve give ΔG = (+) − (−) = +ve at every temperature. The process is never spontaneous.
4. Case III — equal and opposite. When ΔH = TΔS the two drives cancel, ΔG = 0, and the system is at equilibrium.
5. Mixed cases. If both terms are negative, |ΔH| > |TΔS| is needed, which favours low temperature. If both are positive, |TΔS| > |ΔH| is needed, which favours high temperature. Hence an endothermic reaction can be forced by heating, an exothermic one by cooling.
6. Coupling. A reaction with ΔG = +ve can still be made to run by coupling: 2Fe2O3 → 4Fe + 3O2 (ΔG° = +1487 kJ) added to 6CO + 3O2 → 6CO2 (ΔG° = −1543.2 kJ) gives a net ΔG° = −52.2 kJ mol−1, which is spontaneous.

  -    -    -ve at low  T      spontaneous when cold
  +    +    -ve at high T      spontaneous when hot
  +    -    +ve at all T       never spontaneous
