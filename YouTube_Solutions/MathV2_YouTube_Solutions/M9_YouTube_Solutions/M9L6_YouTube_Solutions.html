Concept A complex number z = x + iy is nothing but the point P(x, y) of the Argand plane. Its modulus |z| is the straight-line distance of P from the origin, so plain Pythagoras delivers it. Its argument is the angle the ray OP makes with the positive real axis, measured anticlockwise. Modulus and argument are therefore just the polar coordinates (r, θ) of the same point, which is exactly why every z can be rewritten as r(cosθ + i sinθ).
Formula |z| = √(x² + y²) and arg(z) = tan⁻¹(y/x), corrected for the quadrant of (x, y). tanθ = y/x pins θ down only up to π, so the signs of x and y must choose between the two candidates.
Solution
1. Plot z = x + iy as P(x, y); put r = OP and θ = angle from OX to OP.
2. The foot of the perpendicular from P gives a right triangle with legs |x|, |y| and hypotenuse r, so r² = x² + y² ⇒ |z| = √(x² + y²) ≥ 0.
3. The same triangle gives x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ, hence z = r(cosθ + i sinθ). That identity is the polar form itself.
4. Dividing the two, y/x = tanθ ⇒ θ = tan⁻¹(y/x), which is the argument asked for.
5. Fix the branch: a calculator returns tan⁻¹ in (−π/2, π/2), so for negative x add or subtract π to land the principal value in (−π, π].
6. Test on z = 1 + i: |z| = √(1 + 1) = √2 and θ = tan⁻¹1 = π/4, so z = √2(cos π/4 + i sin π/4). Consistent, so both blanks are right.

Also note |z| can never be negative and |z|² = z·z̄, which is usually quicker than squaring x and y separately.

Formula r(cosθ + i sinθ) = r cosθ + i·r sinθ, with cos π/4 = sin π/4 = 1/√2. π/4 is the one angle whose sine and cosine are equal, which forces x = y and hence a 45° ray.

1 (iii)e^(iθ) = ____Euler formComplex Numbers - Exponential Form or Euler's Form | ExamSolutions Maths Revision Tutorials🟡 Similarcosθ + i sinθ
Concept Euler's formula is the bridge between the exponential and the circular functions, and it is what makes the polar form so powerful. Expanding e^x as a power series and feeding in the purely imaginary exponent iθ splits the series into two halves, because the powers of i cycle 1, i, −1, −i. The real half is precisely the cosine series and the imaginary half is precisely the sine series. So e^(iθ) is the unit-modulus complex number at angle θ.
Formula e^(iθ) = cosθ + i sinθ, hence z = r e^(iθ) is the Euler form of z. Multiplying exponents then adds angles automatically, which is why De Moivre's theorem is a one-line consequence.
Solution
1. Start from e^x = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + … and put x = iθ.
2. Use i² = −1, i³ = −i, i⁴ = 1, so powers of i repeat with period 4 (n mod 4).
3. Collect real terms: 1 − θ²/2! + θ⁴/4! − … = cosθ.
4. Collect imaginary terms: i(θ − θ³/3! + θ⁵/5! − …) = i sinθ. Both series converge for every real θ, so the split is legitimate.
5. Adding, e^(iθ) = cosθ + i sinθ; its modulus is √(cos²θ + sin²θ) = 1, so it always lies on the unit circle.
6. Spot-check: θ = π gives e^(iπ) = cosπ + i sinπ = −1, and θ = π/2 gives e^(iπ/2) = i. Both are the standard checkpoints of the formula.
 e^(i t) = sum over n of (i t)^n / n!
 powers of i : 1, i, -1, -i, then repeat
 real  part : 1 - t^2/2! + t^4/4! - ... = cos t
 imag  part : t - t^3/3! + t^5/5! - ... = sin t
 therefore  : e^(i t) = cos t + i sin t
 t = pi   ->  e^(i pi)   = -1
 t = pi/2 ->  e^(i pi/2) =  i
⚡ JEE Trick Treat e^(iθ) as a rotation operator, not as a number to be evaluated: multiplying by it turns a point through θ without changing its modulus. That converts most product and power problems into simple addition of angles. Watch the sign trap e^(−iθ) = cosθ − i sinθ, and never write e^(iθ) = cosθ + sinθ by dropping the i.
Answer: e^(iθ) = cosθ + i sinθ.

Concept De Moivre's theorem says that raising a unit-modulus complex number to a power multiplies its angle by that power. Since z = cos π/3 + i sin π/3 has modulus 1 and argument π/3, cubing it simply triples the angle to π. The point therefore rotates from 60° round to 180°, landing on the negative real axis. That location alone already tells you the answer must be the real number −1.
Formula (cosθ + i sinθ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ, equivalently (e^(iθ))ⁿ = e^(inθ). Modulus 1 stays 1 under any power, so only the argument moves.
Solution
1. Identify r = 1 and θ = π/3, so z = e^(iπ/3).
2. Apply De Moivre with n = 3: z³ = cos(3 · π/3) + i sin(3 · π/3) = cos π + i sin π.
3. Evaluate: cos π = −1 and sin π = 0, so z³ = −1 + i·0 = −1.
4. Cross-check in rectangular form: z = 1/2 + (√3/2)i, so z² = 1/4 − 3/4 + 2·(1/2)(√3/2)i = −1/2 + (√3/2)i.
5. Then z³ = z²·z = (−1/2 + (√3/2)i)(1/2 + (√3/2)i) = −1/4 − 3/4 + i(−√3/4 + √3/4) = −1. Imaginary parts cancel exactly, confirming the result is purely real.
6. Note z is a primitive 6th root of unity, so z⁶ = 1 and z³ = −1 is forced. z³ must square to 1 yet not equal 1, leaving only −1.

Concept Every factor here has modulus 1, so the whole product has modulus 1 and only the arguments matter. Writing each bracket as an exponential turns multiplication into addition of angles, and powers into multiplication of angles. The one thing to watch is the third bracket, which carries a minus sign before i and therefore represents e^(−iC), not e^(iC). Once all three are exponentials, comparing exponents reads off p, q and r directly.
Formula cosθ ± i sinθ = e^(±iθ) and (e^(iθ))ⁿ = e^(inθ). De Moivre in exponential clothing: the exponents simply add, so no trigonometric expansion is ever needed.
Solution
1. First bracket: (cos A + i sin A)² = (e^(iA))² = e^(2iA).
2. Second bracket: (cos B + i sin B)³ = (e^(iB))³ = e^(3iB).

Concept Two separate trigonometric conditions, one on cosines and one on sines, are really a single complex condition. 10Let z₁, z₂ be the roots of the equation z² + az + 12 = 0 and z₁, z₂ form an equilateral triangle with origin. Then, the value of |a| isRoots & equilateral triangleLet z1 , z2 be the roots of the equation z2 + az + 12 = 0 and z1 , z2 form an equilateral triangle🟢 Exact|a| = 6
Concept Three points z₁, z₂, z₃ in the Argand plane are the vertices of an equilateral triangle exactly when z₁² + z₂² + z₃² = z₁z₂ + z₂z₃ + z₃z₁. Here the third vertex is the origin, so putting z₃ = 0 collapses that identity to a single relation between the sum and the product of the two roots. Vieta already hands us that sum and product in terms of a and 12, so the whole problem is a substitution — the quadratic never has to be solved. Note that a may be complex, which is why the question asks for |a| and not a.
Formula z₁² + z₂² = z₁z₂ ⇔ (z₁ + z₂)² = 3z₁z₂. Adding 2z₁z₂ to both sides of the equilateral condition turns the left side into a perfect square.
Solution
1. Vieta on z² + az + 12 = 0 gives z₁ + z₂ = −a and z₁z₂ = 12.
2. Equilateral condition with z₃ = 0: z₁² + z₂² + 0 = z₁z₂ + 0 + 0, i.e. z₁² + z₂² = z₁z₂.
3. Add 2z₁z₂ to both sides: (z₁ + z₂)² = 3z₁z₂. This is the only form in which Vieta can be used directly.
4. Substitute: (−a)² = 3(12) = 36, so a² = 36 and |a| = 6.
5. Check a = 6: z = (−6 ± √(36 − 48))/2 = −3 ± i√3, so |z₁| = |z₂| = √(9 + 3) = 2√3.
6. Third side: |z₁ − z₂| = |2i√3| = 2√3, equal to both other sides, so the triangle really is equilateral. Two equal moduli alone would only force isosceles.
7. a = −6 gives z = 3 ± i√3, the reflection of the same triangle, so |a| = 6 either way.
  Vieta      : z1 + z2 = -a      z1*z2 = 12
  Condition  : z1^2 + z2^2 = z1*z2   (third vertex 0)
  Complete   : (z1 + z2)^2 = 3*z1*z2
  Substitute : a^2 = 3*12 = 36   ->  |a| = 6
  a = 6      : z = -3 + i*sqrt3 , -3 - i*sqrt3
  Moduli     : |z1| = |z2| = sqrt(12) = 2*sqrt3
  Side z1z2  : |z1 - z2| = 2*sqrt3  -> all sides equal
⚡ JEE Trick The trap is forgetting the factor 3: writing (z₁ + z₂)² = z₁z₂ gives a² = 12 and the wrong value |a| = 2√3. A second trap is chasing |z₁| = |z₂| from the roots, which only proves the triangle is isosceles — the third side |z₁ − z₂| must match too. Whenever one vertex sits at the origin, jump straight to (sum)² = 3(product) and read the answer off the coefficients in one line.
Answer: |a| = 6.
