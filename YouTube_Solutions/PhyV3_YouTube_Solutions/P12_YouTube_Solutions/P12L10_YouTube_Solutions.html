1A cylinder is rolling over a rough surface. Then it enters a smooth region. What will happen to its translational and rotational K.E.? (Figure: cylinder with centre velocity v0 and spin ω0 crossing from a “Rough” strip onto a “Smooth” strip.) — (Level-1)Pure rolling onto a frictionless floorRolling Motion Explained: Slipping vs. No-Slip Cases🟡 SimilarBoth remain unchanged
Concept On the rough strip the cylinder is already in pure rolling, so the contact point is instantaneously at rest and nothing tries to slide. A rolling body on level ground needs no friction to keep rolling. Removing friction therefore removes a force that was already zero, and the two kinetic energies — one governed by the net horizontal force, the other by the net torque about the centre — are each left untouched.
Formula K = ½Mv² + ½Icmω² with v = ωR. The rolling constraint links the variables, not the two energy accounts. Dynamics: Ma = ΣFx, Icmα = Στcm. Zero force does not by itself imply zero torque — check both.
Solution
1. Rough strip: v0 = ω0R, so contact-point velocity = v0 − ω0R = 0 → friction is static and its required value is zero.
2. Smooth strip: the only forces are Mg down at the centre and N = Mg up at the contact, so ΣFx = 0.
3. Ma = 0 → a = 0 → v stays v0, so Ktrans = ½Mv0² is unchanged.
4. Torque about the centre: Mg acts at the centre, N acts along the vertical through it → Στcm = 0 → α = 0 → ω stays ω0.
5. Krot = ½(½MR²)ω0² = ¼Mv0², also unchanged.Icm = MR²/2 for a solid cylinder.
6. Neither v nor ω changes, so v = ωR still holds: the cylinder keeps rolling on the frictionless floor and K = ¾Mv0² stays constant.Friction was dissipating nothing, so deleting it costs nothing.
 rough strip        |  smooth strip
 v = v0, w = w0     |  v = v0, w = w0
 f_static = 0       |  f = 0 (none available)
 SumFx = 0          |  SumFx = 0     -> a = 0
 Sum tau_cm = 0     |  Sum tau_cm = 0 -> alpha = 0
 K_tr  = M v0^2 / 2 |  unchanged
 K_rot = M v0^2 / 4 |  unchanged
⚡ JEE Trick The tempting wrong answer is “rotational KE converts to translational KE”. That conversion needs kinetic friction, i.e. slipping, and a body already in pure rolling has none to feed it. Check the entry condition first: if v = ωR on the rough part, the smooth part is a non-event.
Answer: both the translational and the rotational kinetic energy remain the same.

Concept In pure rolling the contact point is the instantaneous axis of rotation: the whole body, ring plus riders, turns about it with one angular velocity ω = v/R. So every particle's speed is just ω × (its distance from the contact point). Add the kinetic energies particle by particle; the ring itself is handled with the parallel-axis result about the contact point.

4. Left-hand particle m: same distance R√2 → K = mv².
5. Bottom particle 2m sits exactly on the contact point, d = 0 → K = ½(2m)(0)² = 0.The lowest point of a rolling body is momentarily at rest.

4A disc of mass m is rolling on the ground as shown in the figure. Find the angular momentum of the disc w.r.t. point O on the ground. (The disc has radius R, its centre moves with velocity v to the right, and it spins anticlockwise with ω = v/2R; O is the point of the ground directly below the disc.) — (Level-4)Angular momentum in combined motionAngular momentum of a body in combined rotation and translation🟡 Similar¾mvR (into the page)
Concept The lecture's master result splits the angular momentum about any fixed point O into a spin term about the centre of mass and an orbital term, as if all the mass sat at the centre. Here the two terms turn in opposite senses: the centre travelling rightwards above O gives a clockwise contribution, while the printed arrow shows the disc spinning anticlockwise. So they subtract.
Formula LO = Icmω + m(r⃗0 × v⃗0). Derived in the lecture; the cross terms vanish because Σmir⃗i,cm = 0. For a disc Icm = ½mR², and |r⃗0 × v⃗0| = Rv. The perpendicular distance from O to the line of v is just the height R.
Solution
1. Note v ≠ ωR here: ω = v/2R, so this is not pure rolling — the disc skids and the two terms must be tracked with their signs.
2. Orbital term: m|r⃗0 × v⃗0| = mvR, sense clockwise (into the page), because the centre passes a height R above O moving to the right.
3. Spin term: Icmω = ½mR² × v/(2R) = mvR/4, sense anticlockwise (out of the page), following the arrow drawn on the figure.
4. Combine with signs: LO = mvR − mvR/4 = ¾mvR, directed into the page.Opposite senses, so the smaller spin term is subtracted.
5. Sanity check on the sense: the orbital term is four times the spin term, so the resultant keeps the clockwise sense of the translation.
6. Contrast: for ordinary pure rolling (ω = v/R, clockwise) the terms add, LO = mvR + ½mvR = 3mvR/2 — twice the present answer.This is the value most students write out of habit.

             = m v R / 4          anticlockwise (+)
 -------------------------------------------------
 L_O = m v R - m v R / 4 = (3/4) m v R, into page
 (pure rolling instead would give (3/2) m v R)
⚡ JEE Trick The figure hands you two pieces of information most students skip: ω = v/2R (so the rolling condition fails) and the direction of the spin arrow. Miss either and you get 3mvR/2 or 5mvR/4 instead of 3mvR/4 — both of which are built into the usual distractor sets. Always fix the sense of each term before adding.
Answer: LO = ¾mvR, directed into the page.

Concept The string is inextensible and does not slip on the drum, so the point of the string leaving the rim moves exactly as fast as the rim itself. That rim speed is what the hanging block inherits. This is a pure kinematic constraint — no mass, no moment of inertia and no value of g enters, because the question asks for a speed at a given instant, not for an acceleration.
Formula v = ωr for a point on the rim. Every point of a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis has speed ω × (distance from the axis). Constraint: vblock = vrim. The string neither stretches nor slides, so the unwinding rate equals the descent rate.
Solution
1. Convert the radius to SI: r = 15 cm = 0.15 m.Leaving it in centimetres is the single commonest slip here.
2. Rim speed of the cylinder: vrim = ωr = (8)(0.15) = 1.2 m/s.
3. The string leaves the drum tangentially, so its free end moves with that same 1.2 m/s.
4. The block hangs from that end and the string is inextensible → vblock = 1.2 m/s downward.
5. Check by differentiating the length relation: if θ is the angle turned, the unwound length is s = rθ, so ds/dt = r dθ/dt = rω — the same 1.2 m/s.

1Starting from rest, an object rolls down along an incline that rises by 3 in every 5 (along it). The object gains a speed of √10 m/s as it travels a distance of 5/3 m along the incline. What can be the possible shape/s of the object? — (Level: Board)Identifying a body from k²/R²Rolling on Inclined Plane in 2 min | JEE Main & Advanced🟡 Similark²/R² = 1 → a ring or a hollow cylinder
Concept For a body released from rest and rolling without slipping, the height lost is shared between translation and spin in a ratio fixed entirely by the shape — by the single number k²/R², where k is the radius of gyration about the central axis. So measuring how fast the body arrives after a known drop is a way of weighing its shape: mass and radius cancel out completely, and only the mass distribution survives.
Formula v² = 2gh / (1 + k²/R²). From mgh = ½mv² + ½(mk²)(v/R)² with the rolling condition v = ωR. Geometry of the slope: h = L sinθ. “Rises 3 in every 5 along it” means the rise per unit slope length, i.e. sinθ = 3/5.
Solution
1. sinθ = 3/5 = 0.6 (rise measured along the incline, not along the base).
2. Height dropped: h = L sinθ = (5/3)(3/5) = 1 m.The awkward 5/3 was chosen precisely to give a clean 1 m.
3. Given data: v² = (√10)² = 10 m²/s², and with g = 10 m/s², 2gh = 2(10)(1) = 20.
4. Substitute: 10 = 20/(1 + k²/R²) → 1 + k²/R² = 2 → k²/R² = 1.
5. Read off the shape: k²/R² = 1 means I = mR², i.e. all the mass sits at distance R from the axis — a ring (thin hoop) or a thin-walled hollow cylinder.
6. Rule out the others: a disc/solid cylinder has k²/R² = ½ (v² = 40/3), a solid sphere 2/5 (v² = 100/7) and a hollow sphere 2/3 (v² = 12) — none gives 10.Larger k²/R² always means a slower arrival.

1A body is rolling without slipping on a horizontal surface and its rotational kinetic energy is equal to the translational kinetic energy. The body is (a) Disc (b) Sphere (c) Cylinder (d) Ring — (Level-1)Splitting KE between spin and translationTotal Kinetic Energy (Translational and Rotational)🟡 SimilardRing
Concept In pure rolling the spin and the translation are locked by v = ωR, so the ratio of the two kinetic energies is not a variable at all — it is a fixed property of the body's mass distribution. Setting the ratio equal to 1 therefore fixes the moment of inertia, and the moment of inertia names the shape. No speed, mass or radius can enter the answer.
Formula Krot/Ktrans = Icm/(mR²) = k²/R². Because Krot = ½I(v/R)² and Ktrans = ½mv²; the v² cancels. Standard values of Icm: ring mR², disc and solid cylinder ½mR², solid sphere (2/5)mR².
Solution
1. Write the condition: Krot = Ktrans → ½Iω² = ½mv².
2. Substitute the rolling constraint ω = v/R: ½I(v²/R²) = ½mv² → I = mR².Everything about the motion cancels; only the shape survives.
3. I = mR² means k²/R² = 1, i.e. every element of mass lies at distance R from the axis.
4. That is a ring (equivalently a thin hollow cylinder) → option (d).
5. Check the distractors: disc and cylinder both have I = ½mR², so Krot/Ktrans = ½ — spin carries half as much as translation, not the same.Options (a) and (c) are the same body in disguise, which is a hint that neither can be the unique answer.
6. Sphere: I = (2/5)mR² gives the ratio 2/5 — the smallest of the four, so (b) is furthest from equality.

2A solid sphere of mass M and radius R is placed on top of a rough inclined surface of height h. It rolls down the incline without slipping. Its kinetic energy on reaching the bottom of the incline will be: (a) Mgh (b) Less than Mgh as the surface is rough (c) More than Mgh because of additional rotational kinetic energy (d) Depending on the coefficient of static friction between the sphere and the surface. — (Level-1)Static friction does no workRigid Body Dynamics #31: Pure Rolling of a body on an Inclined Plane🟡 SimilaraMgh
Concept Roughness by itself does not dissipate energy — sliding does. In pure rolling the contact point is instantaneously at rest, so the friction force acts at a point of zero velocity and its power f·v is exactly zero. Friction is still essential (it supplies the torque that spins the sphere up), but it is a silent partner in the energy budget: every joule of lost gravitational PE reappears as kinetic energy.
Formula Work–energy: Kf − Ki = Wgravity + Wfriction + Wnormal. The normal force is perpendicular to the displacement, so its work is zero. Rolling split: K = ½Mv² + ½Iω² = (7/10)Mv². With I = (2/5)MR² and ω = v/R for a solid sphere.
Solution
1. Power delivered by static friction = f × (velocity of the contact point) = f × 0 = 0, so Wfriction = 0 over the whole descent.
2. Work by gravity as the centre falls h: Wgravity = Mgh.
3. Started from rest, so Kf = 0 + Mgh + 0 + 0 = Mgh → option (a).
4. That Mgh is then shared: (7/10)Mv² = Mgh gives v² = (10/7)gh, of which Ktrans = (5/7)Mgh and Krot = (2/7)Mgh.Rotation redistributes the energy; it does not create or destroy any.
5. Option (c) double-counts: rotational KE is part of the Mgh, not an extra on top of it — energy cannot exceed what gravity supplied.
6. Option (d) fails because μ only has to be large enough to prevent slipping (μ ≥ (2/7)tanθ); above that threshold its value never appears in the energy equation.Static friction adjusts itself to whatever the constraint needs.

The one-line test is whether the contact point moves; if it does not, static friction is workless no matter how rough the plane is.

5A solid cylinder of mass M and radius R rolls without slipping down an inclined plane of length L and height h. What is the speed of its centre of mass when the cylinder reaches its bottom? (a) √(2gh) (b) √(3gh/4) (c) √(4gh/3) (d) √(4gh) — (Level-1)Speed of a rolling cylinder at the foot of an inclineRotational Motion 17 | Pure Rolling on Inclined Plane | IIT JEE / NEET🟢 Exactc√(4gh/3)
Concept Rolling without slipping means the contact point is instantaneously at rest, so static friction acts through a point that does not move and therefore does no work. Mechanical energy is conserved, and the whole of Mgh reappears as kinetic energy — but split between translation and spin in a ratio fixed by the shape. A solid cylinder must give a share of the budget to rotation, so its centre necessarily arrives slower than a frictionlessly sliding block would.
Formula Mgh = ½Mv² + ½Icmω² with Icm = ½MR² and v = ωR. The rolling constraint is what lets one unknown, v, replace both v and ω. General result: v = √[2gh/(1 + k²/R²)]. Only the shape number k²/R² survives — M, R and L all cancel.
Solution

2. The spin term collapses: ½(½MR²)(v²/R²) = ¼Mv², so R disappears entirely.
3. Add the shares: Mgh = ½Mv² + ¼Mv² = ¾Mv².M cancels too — a heavy cylinder is no faster than a light one.
