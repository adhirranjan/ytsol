1 (i)Fill in the blank: According to ______ theory, an acid is a substance that produces hydrogen ions in solution, while a base produces hydroxide ions.Arrhenius conceptArrhenius Theory of Acids and Bases| Limitations of Arrhenius Concept | Theories of Acids and Bases🟢 ExactArrhenius
Concept The first and narrowest of the three theories. Arrhenius defines acids and bases by the ions they release in water — H+ for an acid, OH− for a base.
Key relation acid → H+(aq); base → OH−(aq) Both halves of the sentence name ions in aqueous solution, which is the signature of the Arrhenius picture.
Solution
1. The blank must be filled by whichever theory defines an acid by the production of hydrogen ions.
2. That is the Arrhenius definition: HCl → H+ + Cl− for a strong acid, CH3COOH ⇌ CH3COO− + H+ for a weak one.
3. The base half matches too: NaOH → Na+ + OH−, and NH4OH ⇌ NH4+ + OH−.
4. Brønsted–Lowry does not fit, because it speaks of proton transfer rather than of ions produced in water.
5. Lewis does not fit either, since it is framed entirely in terms of electron pairs.
6. The mention of solution is the giveaway: Arrhenius is the only one of the three that requires an aqueous medium at all.
7. That requirement is also its chief limitation — it cannot describe dry HCl acting as an acid, or NH3(g) + HCl(g) → NH4Cl with no solvent present.
 ARRHENIUS :
   acid  ->  H+  in aqueous solution
   base  ->  OH- in aqueous solution

 HCl   -> H+ + Cl-        (strong acid)
 NaOH  -> Na+ + OH-       (strong base)

 needs water : its main limitation
⚡ JEE Trick Match the theory to the currency it trades in: Arrhenius trades in ions in water, Brønsted in protons, Lewis in electron pairs. One word in the question usually names the currency.
Answer: Arrhenius theory.
1 (ii)Fill in the blank: ______ theory expands the definition of acids and bases to include substances that can accept or donate a proton.Brønsted–Lowry conceptBronsted Lowry Acids and Bases | Chemistry🟡 SimilarBrønsted–Lowry
Concept “Accept or donate a proton” is the Brønsted–Lowry currency exactly. It widens Arrhenius by dropping the requirement of an aqueous medium.
Key relation acid = proton donor (protogenic); base = proton acceptor (protophilic) Proposed independently by J. N. Brønsted and J. M. Lowry in 1923.
Solution
1. The sentence speaks of accepting or donating a proton, which is the Brønsted–Lowry vocabulary.
2. An acid is a proton donor: HCl → H+ + Cl−, CH3COOH → H+ + CH3COO−.
3. A base is a proton acceptor: NH3 + H+ → NH4+.
4. So the blank is Brønsted–Lowry.
5. The word “expands” is accurate: every Arrhenius acid is also a Brønsted acid, but the reverse does not hold, since Brønsted needs no water.
6. It also introduces conjugate pairs: donating a proton converts an acid into its conjugate base, which Arrhenius has no way of expressing.
7. Note that item 1(iii) of this same Quick Recap asks for the identical answer in different words — a duplication in the printed paper, not an error.
 BRONSTED-LOWRY (1923) :
   acid = proton DONOR    (protogenic)
   base = proton ACCEPTOR (protophilic)

 HCl  -> H+ + Cl-
 NH3 + H+  ->  NH4+

 no solvent required
 introduces conjugate acid-base pairs
⚡ JEE Trick The free proton never really exists — it is always solvated, as H3O+ in water. Writing HCl + H2O ⇌ Cl− + H3O+ rather than HCl → H+ + Cl− is what makes the conjugate pairs visible.
Answer: Brønsted–Lowry theory.
1 (iii)Fill in the blank: In ______ acid-base theory, an acid is a substance that can donate a proton, while a base is a substance that can accept a proton.Brønsted–Lowry conceptWhat Is The Bronsted Lowry Theory | Acids, Bases & Alkali's | Chemistry | FuseSchool🟢 ExactBrønsted–Lowry
Concept The definition spelled out in full: acid donates a proton, base accepts one. That is the Brønsted–Lowry concept, the same answer as item 1(ii).
Key relation HA + B ⇌ A− + BH+: acid HA → conjugate base A−; base B → conjugate acid BH+ Every Brønsted reaction is a proton handed from one species to another.
Solution
1. “Can donate a proton” for an acid and “can accept a proton” for a base is the Brønsted–Lowry definition word for word.
2. So the blank is Brønsted–Lowry — the same answer as item 1(ii), which states the idea more loosely.
3. The theory requires an acid and a base together: a proton donated must be accepted by something, so no Brønsted acid acts alone.
4. That is why the book stresses that “the proton donated by an acid is not capable to exist in free state and always gets solvated”.
5. In water the solvent itself is the base: CH3COOH + H2O ⇌ CH3COO− + H3O+.
6. Each side of such an equation carries one acid and one base, giving two conjugate pairs per reaction.
7. Here those pairs are CH3COOH/CH3COO− and H3O+/H2O — the pattern every Board question in this lecture asks you to label.

   pairs : CH3COOH/CH3COO-
           H3O+/H2O

Answer: Brønsted–Lowry acid-base theory.
1 (iv)Fill in the blank: The ______ theory is the most general acid-base theory, defining an acid as an electron pair acceptor and a base as an electron pair donor.Lewis conceptLewis Theory of Acids and Bases | Electron Pair Donor & Acceptor | Applications | Chemistry🟢 ExactLewis
Concept The broadest of the three. Lewis drops protons entirely and works with electron pairs, so it covers reactions with no hydrogen anywhere.
Key relation acid = electron-pair acceptor (electrophile); base = electron-pair donor (nucleophile) Because it needs neither water nor a proton, it subsumes the other two concepts.
Solution
1. “Electron pair acceptor” for an acid and “electron pair donor” for a base is the Lewis definition.
2. Standard examples: BF3 + F− → BF4−, where BF3 is the acid and F− the base.
3. And NH3 + H+ → NH4+, where NH3 is the base and H+ the acid.
4. It is called the “most general” theory because it requires neither an aqueous medium nor a transferable proton.
5. BF3 + NH3 → [H3N→BF3] is an acid-base reaction under Lewis and under neither of the other two — there is no proton and no water involved.
6. The product is called a co-ordinate complex, an adduct, made of an acid portion and a base portion joined by a dative bond.
7. Note the nesting: every Brønsted base is a Lewis base (accepting H+ means donating a pair to it), but the converse fails, which Level-6 Q8 of this lecture tests.
 LEWIS :
   acid = electron pair ACCEPTOR (electrophile)
   base = electron pair DONOR    (nucleophile)

 BF3 + F-   ->  BF4-
 NH3 + H+   ->  NH4+
 BF3 + NH3  ->  [H3N -> BF3]   adduct

 no proton, no water needed : most general
⚡ JEE Trick Lewis acid and electrophile mean the same thing, as do Lewis base and nucleophile. Recognising that link carries the whole concept straight into organic mechanism questions.
Answer: Lewis theory.

Concept A species that can go either way — donate a proton when faced with a base, accept one when faced with an acid — is called amphoteric, or more precisely amphiprotic.
Key relation H2O + HCl → H3O+ + Cl− (base); H2O + NH3 → OH− + NH4+ (acid) The same molecule plays opposite roles depending on its partner.
Solution
1. The sentence describes a species capable of either donating or accepting a proton, depending on what else is present.
2. That is the definition of an amphoteric (amphiprotic) substance, so that is the blank.
3. Water is the standard example. Against HCl it accepts a proton and becomes H3O+, so it acts as a base.
4. Against NH3 it donates a proton and becomes OH−, so it acts as an acid.
5. The book makes the same point under the Arrhenius section: “Water is amphoteric in nature as it furnishes both H+ and OH−”.
6. To be amphiprotic a species must have both an ionisable hydrogen to give away and a lone pair to accept one with — HCO3−, HSO4− and H2PO4− all qualify.

 amphoteric / amphiprotic :
   can donate AND accept a proton

 H2O + NH3 -> OH-  + NH4+    water = acid

2 (i)True or false: The Arrhenius theory is the most comprehensive of all the acid-base theories.Comparing the three theoriesAcid and Base Definitions | Arrhenius, Bronsted-Lowry, and Lewis🟡 SimilarFalse the Lewis theory is the most comprehensive, not Arrhenius
Concept The three theories nest one inside the other, and Arrhenius is the innermost — the narrowest, not the broadest. So the statement is false.
Key relation Arrhenius ⊂ Brønsted–Lowry ⊂ Lewis, in order of increasing generality Each theory relaxes a restriction the previous one imposed.
Solution
1. Arrhenius requires an aqueous medium and defines acidity by the release of H+.
2. Brønsted–Lowry drops the water requirement, needing only a proton transfer — so it covers strictly more reactions.
3. Lewis drops the proton requirement too, needing only an electron-pair transfer — so it covers more still.
4. Hence Lewis, not Arrhenius, is the most comprehensive. The statement is false.
5. The book lists four limitations of Arrhenius: it fails for non-aqueous solvents, for solvent-free neutralisations, for the acidity of salts like AlCl3, and it ignores that H+ exists as H3O+.
6. A concrete failure: NH3(g) + HCl(g) → NH4Cl is plainly a neutralisation, but with no water there are no H+ or OH− ions for Arrhenius to point at.
7. Item 1(iv) of this Quick Recap says outright that the Lewis theory is the most general — the two items contradict each other unless this one is read as false.
 generality :  Arrhenius  <  Bronsted  <  Lewis

 Arrhenius : needs water, needs H+/OH-
 Bronsted  : needs a proton, no water
 Lewis     : needs only an electron pair

 NH3(g) + HCl(g) -> NH4Cl
   neutralisation with NO water
   -> Arrhenius cannot describe it
⚡ JEE Trick Picture three nested circles with Lewis outermost. Any question about which theory is broader, or whether one class is contained in another, reads straight off that picture.
Answer: False — Lewis is the most comprehensive; Arrhenius is the narrowest.
2 (ii)True or false: All Arrhenius acids and bases are also Brønsted-Lowry acids and bases.Nesting of the theoriesConjugate Acid Base Pairs, Arrhenius, Bronsted Lowry and Lewis Definition - Chemistry🟡 SimilarTrue Arrhenius acids and bases both qualify under Brønsted–Lowry
Concept Brønsted–Lowry contains Arrhenius. An acid furnishing H+ is donating a proton, and a base furnishing OH− supplies a species that accepts one — so the statement is true.
Key relation Arrhenius acid ⇒ proton donor; Arrhenius base → OH−, a proton acceptor Containment runs one way only — not every Brønsted acid or base is an Arrhenius one.
Solution
1. An Arrhenius acid furnishes H+ in water. Furnishing H+ is donating a proton, so it is a Brønsted acid.
2. HCl illustrates both readings at once: HCl → H+ + Cl− (Arrhenius) and HCl + H2O ⇌ Cl− + H3O+ (Brønsted).
3. An Arrhenius base furnishes OH−, and OH− readily accepts a proton to become water — so it too acts as a Brønsted base.
4. So all Arrhenius acids and bases are also Brønsted–Lowry acids and bases. The statement is true.
5. The reverse containment fails: NH3 is a Brønsted base but furnishes no OH− itself, and dry HCl acts as an acid in benzene with no water present.
6. This is exactly why Level-6 Q8(a) of this lecture — “all Arrhenius bases are not Bronsted bases” — is listed there as a wrong statement.
7. The containment is therefore strict and one-directional: Arrhenius sits wholly inside Brønsted, with room to spare.

                              -> Bronsted acid
 Arrhenius base : gives OH-  -> OH- accepts H+
                              -> Bronsted base

 converse FAILS :
   NH3 is a Bronsted base, no OH- furnished
   dry HCl is an acid with no water
⚡ JEE Trick Containment questions are directional. “All A are B” and “all B are A” are different claims, and in this chapter exactly one of each such pair is true.
Answer: True — Arrhenius acids and bases are a subset of the Brønsted ones.

Solution
1. By the Lewis definition, a base is an electron pair donor — a nucleophile.
2. The statement says it donates a proton, which is the wrong particle entirely.
3. Worse, donating a proton is the defining act of a Brønsted acid, so the statement also gets the role backwards.
4. The statement is therefore false.

2 (iv)True or false: Water is an example of an amphoteric substance.Amphoteric nature of water8.1 Amphiprotic species (SL)🟡 SimilarTrue water acts as an acid with bases and as a base with acids
Concept Water is the textbook amphoteric substance: it accepts a proton from an acid and donates one to a base. So the statement is true.
Key relation H2O + HCl → H3O+ + Cl−; H2O + NH3 → OH− + NH4+ Water changes role according to its partner, which is what “amphoteric” means.
Solution
1. With an acid such as HCl, water accepts the proton to give H3O+, so it is behaving as a base.
2. With a base such as NH3, water donates a proton to give OH−, so it is behaving as an acid.
3. Since it can do both, water is amphoteric and the statement is true.
4. The Arrhenius section of the lecture puts it as H2O ⇌ H+ + OH− — water furnishing both ions itself.
5. Structurally it can do both because it has an ionisable hydrogen to give away and two lone pairs to accept a proton with.
6. This dual role is what makes water autoionise: H2O + H2O ⇌ H3O+ + OH−, one molecule acting as acid and the other as base.
7. Level-4 Q8 of this lecture uses the acid side of this behaviour, and Example-3 of the lecture lists H3O+ and OH− as water’s conjugate acid and base.

   H2O + HCl -> H3O+ + Cl-      water = BASE

   H2O + NH3 -> OH- + NH4+      water = ACID

 autoionisation :
   H2O + H2O <=> H3O+ + OH-

⚡ JEE Trick Water is the reference amphiprotic species and appears in almost every conjugate-pair question in this lecture. Fix H3O+ above it and OH− below it and the pairs come out instantly.
Answer: True — water is amphoteric.
