1Escape velocity on the earth (a) Is less than that on moon (b) Depends upon mass of body (c) Depends upon direction of projection (d) Depends upon the height from which it is projected. — (Level-1)Escape velocity dependence factorsEscape velocity on the earth [BHU 2001] (a) Is less than that on the moon (b) Depends upon the m...🟢 ExactdDepends upon the height from which it is projected
Concept Escape velocity is the launch speed at which the kinetic energy just cancels the (negative) gravitational potential energy at the launch point. It is set entirely by the planet and the launch distance r from its centre; the projectile mass cancels and, since energy is a scalar, the launch direction does not matter (ignoring air drag). The trap is to think a heavier body or a vertical launch needs a different speed.
Formula (1/2)mve² − GMm/r = 0 ⇒ ve = √(2GM/r) = √(2GM/(R + h)) Energy conservation from the launch point to infinity, where PE = 0 and KE is just zero.
Solution
1. Write energy at launch (distance r = R + h): E = (1/2)mv² − GMm/r. To reach infinity with zero speed, E = 0.
2. Solve: ve = √(2GM/r). The mass m cancels on both sides ⇒ option (b) is false.Every term in the energy equation is proportional to m.
3. Energy is a scalar, so ve needs only the speed, not the direction (as long as the path does not hit the planet) ⇒ option (c) is false.
4. Compare Earth and Moon: ve(Earth) = 11.2 km/s, ve(Moon) = √(2·gmRm) = √(2·1.62·1.74×10⁶) ≈ 2.4 km/s. Earth's value is larger ⇒ option (a) is false.
5. Height dependence: ve(h) = √(2GM/(R + h)) = 11.2·√(R/(R + h)) km/s, which falls as h grows ⇒ option (d) is true.
6. Only (d) survives.

Concept Escape velocity is fixed by M and R through ve = √(2GM/R). For a uniform sphere the mass is just density × volume, so replacing M by (4/3)πR³ρ turns the answer into a density form. The trap is cancelling R wrongly: one R survives outside the root. Mean density means the planet is treated as a uniform sphere, which is exactly the case where the shell theorem lets us put all of M at the centre.
Formula ve = √(2GM/R) with M = (4/3)πR³ρ Energy balance (1/2)mv² = GMm/R at the surface; M from mean density ρ of a sphere of radius R.
Solution
1. A body of mass m at the surface has PE = −GMm/R. To just reach infinity its total energy must be zero.
2. (1/2)mve² − GMm/R = 0 ⇒ ve = √(2GM/R).m cancels, so the result is independent of the body.
3. Mass of the planet from its mean density: M = ρ·V = ρ·(4/3)πR³.
4. Substitute: ve = √(2G·(4/3)πR³ρ/R) = √(8πGρR²/3).
5. Take R² out of the root: ve = R·√(8πρG/3). Hence proved.√(R²) = R for a positive radius.
6. Check units: ρG has units (kg/m³)(N m²/kg²) = s⁻², so √(ρG) is s⁻¹ and R·√(ρG) is m/s, a speed. ✓
7. Corollary: for equal density, ve ∝ R (a planet twice as big has twice the escape speed).

4Show that the escape velocity of a body from the surface of the earth is √2 Vc where Vc is the critical velocity of a body when it is orbiting very close to the earth's surface. — (Level-4)Escape vs critical orbital velocityIf v_e is escape velocity and v_o is orbital velocity of a satellite for orbit close to the earth...🟡 Similarve = √2 Vc = √(2gR)
Concept The critical (orbital) velocity Vc near the surface comes from a force balance: gravity supplies the centripetal force. The escape velocity comes from an energy balance: KE must cancel the negative PE. Comparing the two gives a fixed ratio √2 for the same radius. The trap is to confuse the two balances or to use h ≠ 0 for "very close to the surface".
Formula mVc²/R = GMm/R² ⇒ Vc = √(GM/R) = √(gR) and (1/2)mve² = GMm/R ⇒ ve = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR) Orbit radius r ≈ R for a satellite skimming the surface; GM = gR².
Solution
1. Satellite of mass m in a circular orbit of radius r ≈ R: centripetal force = gravitational force, mVc²/R = GMm/R².
2. Hence Vc = √(GM/R) = √(gR).Using GM = gR² at the surface.
3. Escape: total energy zero at infinity, (1/2)mve² − GMm/R = 0 ⇒ ve = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR).
4. Ratio: ve/Vc = √(2GM/R)/√(GM/R) = √2 ⇒ ve = √2·Vc. Hence proved.
5. Numbers for Earth: Vc = √(gR) = √(9.8 · 6.4 × 10⁶) = 7.92 × 10³ m/s ≈ 7.9 km/s.
6. Then ve = √2 · 7.92 = 11.2 km/s, the familiar value. ✓
7. Extra speed needed to escape from such an orbit: ve − Vc = (√2 − 1)√(gR) ≈ 3.28 km/s.

1An earth satellite can move in an orbit, the plane of which coincides with: (a) the plane of any great circle around the earth (b) the plane of any latitude circle around the earth (c) any plane not containing the centre of earth (d) the plane of tropic of cancer — (Level-1)Plane of satellite orbitIs it necessary for the plane of the orbit of a satellite to pass through the centre of the earth ?🟡 Similarathe plane of any great circle around the earth
Concept The only force on a satellite is Earth's gravity, which always points to the centre of the Earth. A central force keeps the motion in a fixed plane through the force centre, so every orbit plane must contain the Earth's centre. Planes that pass through the centre cut the Earth's surface in great circles. The trap is the latitude circles: apart from the equator, their planes miss the centre.
Formula τ = r × F = 0 ⇒ L = m(r × v) = constant F is along −r (central), so the torque about the centre vanishes; a constant L fixes the orbital plane, which contains the centre.
Solution
1. Gravity on the satellite acts along the line joining it to the Earth's centre O.
2. Torque about O: τ = r × F = 0 since F ∥ r ⇒ angular momentum L is constant in magnitude and direction.r and v are always ⊥ to a fixed L, so they stay in one plane through O.
3. Hence the orbital plane must pass through the Earth's centre. Any plane through the centre meets the surface in a great circle ⇒ (a) is possible.
4. (b) Latitude circles (other than the equator) have planes parallel to the equator but displaced from O; gravity would have a component pulling the satellite out of that plane ⇒ not possible in general.
5. (c) A plane not containing the centre is ruled out directly by step 3.
6. (d) The tropic of Cancer (23.5° N) is a latitude circle whose plane does not pass through O ⇒ not possible.
