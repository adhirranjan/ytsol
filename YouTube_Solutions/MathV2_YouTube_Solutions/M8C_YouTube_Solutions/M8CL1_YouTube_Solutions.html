1 (i)A sequence is said to be H.P. if the reciprocals of its terms are in ______.Definition of a harmonic progressionIntroduction to Harmonic Progression🟢 ExactA.P. (arithmetic progression)
Concept A harmonic progression is not defined on its own terms — it is defined by what happens when you flip every term over. Take any A.P. that never passes through 0, write down the reciprocal of each of its terms, and the list you get is called an H.P. This single sentence is the whole chapter: it is why an H.P. has an nth-term formula (inherited from the A.P.) but no sum formula (nothing to inherit).
Formula a₁, a₂, a₃, … in H.P. ⇔ 1/a₁, 1/a₂, 1/a₃, … in A.P. The A.P. must not contain 0, otherwise its reciprocal is undefined.
Solution
1. Read the sentence as a definition being completed: "reciprocals of its terms are in ______".
2. By the standard definition, the reciprocals of an H.P. form an arithmetic progression.Not a G.P. — flipping a G.P. gives another G.P., so that choice would define nothing new.
3. Check on the book's own example, the H.P. 6, 4, 3, …: the reciprocals are 1/6, 1/4, 1/3.
4. Their differences: 1/4 − 1/6 = 1/12 and 1/3 − 1/4 = 1/12 — constant, so the reciprocals are indeed in A.P. ✓
  H.P.   :   6      4      3     ...
  flip   :  1/6    1/4    1/3    ...
  gaps   :      +1/12  +1/12          <-- constant  =>  A.P.
⚡ JEE Trick Never work on an H.P. directly. The instruction hidden inside every H.P. question is "flip → solve as an A.P. → flip back". Trap: answering "G.P." — the reciprocal of a G.P. is again a G.P., so that option can never be the defining property of an H.P.
Answer: A.P. — an arithmetic progression.
1 (ii)The nth term of an H.P. is ______.General term of an H.P.How to find the nth term of a harmonic sequence🟢 Exact1 / [a + (n − 1)d]
Concept Because an H.P. is the reciprocal of an A.P., its general term is simply the reciprocal of the A.P.'s general term. The letters a and d therefore belong to the reciprocal A.P., never to the H.P. itself — mixing that up is the commonest source of wrong answers in this chapter.
Formula A.P. nth term = a + (n − 1)d, so H.P. nth term Tₙ = 1 / [a + (n − 1)d]. a = first term of the A.P. of reciprocals, d = its common difference.
Solution
1. Let the H.P. be a₁, a₂, a₃, …, so that 1/a₁, 1/a₂, 1/a₃, … is an A.P. with first term a and common difference d.
2. The nth term of that A.P. is a + (n − 1)d.Standard A.P. formula — the multiple of d is always one less than the position.
3. But that nth A.P. term is exactly 1/Tₙ, the reciprocal of the H.P.'s nth term.
4. Invert both sides: Tₙ = 1 / [a + (n − 1)d].
5. Quick check on 6, 4, 3, … : here a = 1/6, d = 1/12, so T₃ = 1/[1/6 + 2(1/12)] = 1/(1/3) = 3 ✓
  A.P. of reciprocals :  a , a+d , a+2d , ... , a+(n-1)d
                          |     |      |              |
  flip each           : 1/a  1/(a+d)  ...     1/[a+(n-1)d]  = T_n
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a question hands you two terms of an H.P., flip them, write two linear equations in a and d, and subtract to kill a. You will never need to guess. Trap: writing Tₙ = a + (n−1)d and forgetting the reciprocal — always check your formula against n = 1, which must return the first term.
Answer: Tₙ = 1 / [a + (n − 1)d], where a and d belong to the corresponding A.P.
1 (iii)There is no general formula for the sum of ______ series.Why an H.P. has no sum formulaHarmonic Progression — complete concepts🟡 SimilarHarmonic
Concept An A.P. sums by pairing the ends; a G.P. sums by multiplying by r and subtracting. A harmonic series admits neither trick — adding reciprocals of an A.P. produces no telescoping and no geometric collapse — so no closed form exists. In fact 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + … diverges, growing like ln n.
Formula A.P.: Sₙ = (n/2)[2a + (n−1)d] · G.P.: Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) · H.P.: no closed form. Only the first two progressions have a sum formula.
Solution
1. The blank names the one progression of the three whose sum cannot be written in closed form.
2. A.P. and G.P. both have standard sum formulas, so neither can be the answer.
3. Therefore the answer is the harmonic series.The module states this explicitly in L-1: "there is no general formula for the sum of a harmonic series".
4. Consequence for the exam: if a question appears to ask for the sum of H.P.-type terms, it must be a telescoping sum or a disguised A.P./G.P. — never a plug-in.
  A.P.  sum  ->  pair the ends            ->  formula exists
  G.P.  sum  ->  multiply by r, subtract  ->  formula exists
  H.P.  sum  ->  neither trick works      ->  NO formula
⚡ JEE Trick Seeing a "sum" in an H.P. question is a signal, not an obstacle: look for the telescope a₁a₂ + a₂a₃ + … + aₙ₋₁aₙ = (n − 1)a₁aₙ, which is the only H.P. "sum" the exam actually asks for. Trap: substituting H.P. terms into the A.P. sum formula.
Answer: harmonic (there is no general formula for the sum of a harmonic series).
1 (iv)If a, b, c are in H.P. then 2/b = ______.Three-term H.P. condition / harmonic meanIf a, b, c are in H.P. then b = 2ac/(a+c)🟢 Exact1/a + 1/c
Concept Three numbers are in H.P. exactly when their reciprocals are in A.P., and three numbers are in A.P. exactly when the middle one is the average of the outer two. Putting those together gives the single most-used identity of the chapter — the harmonic mean relation.
Formula 1/a, 1/b, 1/c in A.P. ⇒ 2·(1/b) = 1/a + 1/c, i.e. b = 2ac/(a + c). b is the harmonic mean of a and c.
Solution
1. a, b, c in H.P. ⇒ 1/a, 1/b, 1/c are in A.P.
2. In an A.P. the middle term is the arithmetic mean of its neighbours: 1/b = ½(1/a + 1/c).Equivalently, the two gaps are equal: 1/b − 1/a = 1/c − 1/b.
3. Multiply through by 2: 2/b = 1/a + 1/c.
4. Combining the right side over a common denominator, 2/b = (a + c)/(ac), which rearranges to b = 2ac/(a + c).
5. Check on 6, 4, 3: 2/4 = 1/2 and 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 ✓
  A.M. of a and c  =  (a + c)/2       <-- A.P. middle
  H.M. of a and c  =  2ac/(a + c)     <-- H.P. middle
  never mix the two up
⚡ JEE Trick Memorise the H.M. as "twice the product over the sum". Its most useful rearrangement is b(a + c) = 2ac, i.e. ab + bc = 2ac — that form turns questions like "ab + bc + ca = 21, find ac" into one line. Trap: writing b = (a + c)/2, which is the A.P. mean.
Answer: 2/b = 1/a + 1/c (equivalently b = 2ac/(a + c)).

Concept Two terms of an H.P. determine it completely. Flip both, get two linear equations in the reciprocal A.P.'s a and d, subtract to eliminate a, then read off the general term and match it against the required shape.
Formula 1/Tₙ = a + (n − 1)d; here 1/T₃ = 7 and 1/T₇ = 15. Reciprocals of the given terms are whole numbers — a strong hint that the setter wants you to flip immediately.
Solution
1. Flip: the reciprocal A.P. has a + 2d = 7 … (i) and a + 6d = 15 … (ii).
2. (ii) − (i): 4d = 8 ⇒ d = 2.Subtracting kills a in one step — never solve both equations simultaneously by substitution.
3. From (i): a = 7 − 2(2) = 3.
4. nth term of the A.P.: 3 + (n − 1)2 = 2n + 1.
5. Flip back: Tₙ = 1/(2n + 1).
6. Match with p/(qn − r): the numerator gives p = 1; the denominator 2n + 1 must equal qn − r, so q = 2 and −r = 1, i.e. r = −1.The required form has a minus sign built in, which is why r comes out negative.
7. Check: n = 3 gives 1/7 ✓ and n = 7 gives 1/15 ✓
   n :   1     2     3     4    ...    7
 A.P.:   3     5     7     9    ...   15      (2n+1)
 H.P.:  1/3   1/5   1/7   1/9   ...  1/15
⚡ JEE Trick When an H.P.'s given terms are reciprocals of integers, the reciprocal A.P. is always clean — flip first and the arithmetic becomes mental. Trap on the matching step: the target shape here is qn − r, so a "+1" in your answer forces r = −1, not r = 1.
Answer: p = 1, q = 2, r = −1 (so Tₙ = 1/(2n + 1)).

2. (a) a − d, a, a + d — this is the A.P. itself, not an H.P.Its reciprocals 1/(a−d), 1/a, 1/(a+d) are generally not in A.P.

Concept To prove three quantities are in H.P., prove their reciprocals are in A.P. — that is, show the two gaps between reciprocals are equal. Concept The elegant route is the "add 1 to every term" trick. Adding the same constant to each term of an A.P. leaves it an A.P., and here that single addition manufactures the common factor (a + b + c) which then cancels — collapsing the problem to the given hypothesis.

5If x > 1, y > 1, z > 1 are in G.P., then 1/(1 + ln x), 1/(1 + ln y), 1/(1 + ln z) are in   (a) A.P.   (b) G.P.   (c) H.P.   (d) A.G.P.Logs of a G.P., shifted and invertedIf x > 1, y > 1, z > 1 are in G.P, then 1/(1+ln x), 1/(1+ln y), 1/(1+ln z) are in🟢 ExactcH.P.
Concept Three moves, each preserving structure: logs turn a G.P. into an A.P.; adding 1 leaves an A.P. an A.P.; taking reciprocals turns an A.P. into an H.P. Reading the chain forwards gives the answer with no algebra.
Formula x, y, z in G.P. ⇒ y² = xz ⇒ 2 ln y = ln x + ln z, i.e. ln x, ln y, ln z in A.P. Adding a constant preserves an A.P.; flipping an A.P. gives an H.P.
Solution
1. x, y, z in G.P. ⇒ y² = xz. Take natural logs: 2 ln y = ln x + ln z.
2. That is exactly the condition for ln x, ln y, ln z to be in A.P.
3. Add 1 to every term: 1 + ln x, 1 + ln y, 1 + ln z — still an A.P., since adding a constant does not change the common difference.
4. Take reciprocals: 1/(1 + ln x), 1/(1 + ln y), 1/(1 + ln z) — the reciprocals of an A.P., hence an H.P. ⇒ option (c).
5. The condition x, y, z > 1 guarantees ln x, ln y, ln z > 0, so no denominator 1 + ln x can vanish.That is exactly why the question bothers to state x, y, z > 1 — it keeps the H.P. legal.
   x , y , z              G.P.
     | ln
   ln x , ln y , ln z     A.P.
     | + 1
   1+ln x, 1+ln y, 1+ln z A.P.
     | flip
   1/(1+ln x), ...        H.P.     <-- (c)
⚡ JEE Trick Track the chain G.P. →(log)→ A.P. →(+const)→ A.P. →(flip)→ H.P. Any question whose terms are reciprocals of (constant + log) is this chain in disguise, and the answer is always H.P. The condition "> 1" is there to keep every denominator positive — a hint that the setter has thought about the domain.

Concept Each sum is an infinite G.P. with first term 1, so x = 1/(1 − a) and similarly for y and z. The reciprocals are therefore 1 − a, 1 − b, 1 − c — an A.P., because negating and shifting an A.P. leaves it an A.P. Reciprocals in A.P. means the originals are in H.P.

Solution

2. Because |a| < 1 it converges to x = 1/(1 − a), and likewise y = 1/(1 − b), z = 1/(1 − c).
3. Take reciprocals: 1/x = 1 − a, 1/y = 1 − b, 1/z = 1 − c.
4. Since a, b, c are in A.P. with common difference d, the numbers 1 − a, 1 − b, 1 − c form an A.P. with common difference −d.Multiplying by −1 and adding 1 are both A.P.-preserving operations.

7If a, b, c are in A.P., p, q, r are in H.P. and ap, bq, cr are in G.P., then p/r + r/p =   (a) a/c + c/a   (b) a/c − c/a   (c) b/q + q/b   (d) b/q − a/pCombining all three progressions at onceIf a, b, c are in AP, p, q, r in HP and ap, bq, cr are in GP then p/r + r/p is equal to🟢 Exactaa/c + c/a
Concept Three hypotheses, one for each progression. Writing b as the A.M. of a and c, q as the H.M. of p and r, and imposing the G.P. condition on the products makes the middle letters disappear — leaving a symmetric relation between the two outer pairs.
Formula 2b = a + c · q = 2pr/(p + r) · (bq)² = (ap)(cr). The G.P. condition on ap, bq, cr is what ties the two triples together.
Solution
1. From the G.P.: (bq)² = (ap)(cr) ⇒ b²q² = ac·pr.
2. Substitute b = (a + c)/2 and q = 2pr/(p + r):
3. [(a + c)²/4] · [4p²r²/(p + r)²] = ac·pr. The 4s cancel.
4. So (a + c)²p²r²/(p + r)² = ac·pr ⇒ divide both sides by pr: (a + c)²·pr = ac·(p + r)².
5. Rearrange into two ratios: (a + c)²/(ac) = (p + r)²/(pr).
6. Expand each side: (a² + 2ac + c²)/(ac) = a/c + c/a + 2, and (p² + 2pr + r²)/(pr) = p/r + r/p + 2.
7. Cancel the 2 from both sides: p/r + r/p = a/c + c/a ⇒ option (a).
   (bq)^2 = (ap)(cr)
      b = (a+c)/2  ,  q = 2pr/(p+r)
   =>  (a+c)^2 * pr = ac * (p+r)^2
   =>  (a+c)^2/(ac) = (p+r)^2/(pr)
   =>  a/c + c/a + 2 = p/r + r/p + 2
   =>  p/r + r/p = a/c + c/a
⚡ JEE Trick Recognise the shape (u + v)²/(uv) = u/v + v/u + 2 — it converts any "sum squared over product" into the ratio form the options are written in. Since the same identity applies to both sides, the constant 2 cancels and the answer falls out. Trap: option (b) with a minus sign, which would need (a − c)²/(ac).
Answer: (a) a/c + c/a.
8If pa = qb = rc = sd and p, q, r, s are in G.P., then a, b, c, d are in   (a) A.P.   (b) G.P.   (c) H.P.   (d) NoneFour-letter version of the exponent–log bridgeIf p^a=q^b=r^c=s^d and p, q, r, s are in G.P then a, b, c, d are in🟢 ExactcH.P.
Concept Identical to Level-5 Q5 and Suggested Problem 9, extended to four letters. The exponents sit upstairs, so their reciprocals are proportional to the logarithms — and logs of a G.P. are an A.P.
Formula pa = k ⇒ 1/a = log p / log k; p, q, r, s in G.P. ⇒ log p, log q, log r, log s in A.P.
Solution
1. Let the common value be k, so pa = qb = rc = sd = k.
2. Take logs: a log p = b log q = c log r = d log s = log k.
3. Hence 1/a, 1/b, 1/c, 1/d are respectively log p, log q, log r, log s, each divided by log k.
4. With p, q, r, s in G.P. of ratio t, the logs are log p, log p + log t, log p + 2log t, log p + 3log t — an A.P. with common difference log t.
5. Dividing an A.P. by the non-zero constant log k leaves an A.P., so 1/a, 1/b, 1/c, 1/d are in A.P.
6. Therefore a, b, c, d are in H.P. ⇒ option (c).

   p,q,r,s = 2, 4, 8, 16   and  k = 16
   2^a = 16 -> a = 4 ;  4^b = 16 -> b = 2
   8^c = 16 -> c = 4/3 ; 16^d = 16 -> d = 1
   reciprocals : 1/4 , 1/2 , 3/4 , 1        A.P.  =>  a,b,c,d in H.P.

Solution
1. Reciprocate all three: log₂(5·2ˣ + 1), log₄(21−x + 1) and 1 must be in A.P.
2. A.P. condition: 2·log₄(21−x + 1) = log₂(5·2ˣ + 1) + 1.
3. Convert the base: 2·log₄B = 2·(½ log₂B) = log₂B, so the left side is simply log₂(21−x + 1).
4. The right side is log₂(5·2ˣ + 1) + log₂2 = log₂[2(5·2ˣ + 1)].
5. Equate the arguments: 21−x + 1 = 10·2ˣ + 2.
6. Put t = 2ˣ (so 21−x = 2/t), giving 2/t + 1 = 10t + 2 ⇒ 2/t = 10t + 1 ⇒ 10t² + t − 2 = 0.
