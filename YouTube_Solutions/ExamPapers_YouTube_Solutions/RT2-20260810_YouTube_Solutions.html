1Which of the sets given below may represent the magnitudes of three vectors adding to zero? (A) 2, 4, 8 (B) 4, 8, 16 (C) 1, 2, 1 (D) 0.5, 1, 2Triangle inequalityWhich set of three magnitudes cannot have zero resultant🟢 ExactC1, 2, 1similarP3 · L2 · Lvl5
Concept Three vectors add to zero only if, drawn head-to-tail, they close into a triangle. That is possible exactly when no single magnitude exceeds the sum of the other two — the triangle inequality. Equality is allowed: the “triangle” then collapses to a straight line (all three collinear), which still gives a zero resultant. Do not test only the two smallest by eye; check the largest against the sum of the other two.
Formula |a − b| ≤ c ≤ a + b for the three magnitudes. The resultant of the two smaller vectors can have any magnitude between their difference and their sum; the third must fall inside that window to cancel it.
Solution
1. (A) 2, 4, 8: largest 8, but 2 + 4 = 6 < 8 — cannot close.The two smaller vectors can never reach 8.
2. (B) 4, 8, 16: 4 + 8 = 12 < 16 — cannot close.
3. (C) 1, 2, 1: largest 2, and 1 + 1 = 2 ≥ 2 — closes (degenerate, collinear).Equality is the limiting case: the two 1s point the same way, opposite the 2.
4. (D) 0.5, 1, 2: 0.5 + 1 = 1.5 < 2 — cannot close.
5. Only set (C) satisfies the triangle inequality.
 set          largest   sum of other two   closes?
 2, 4, 8      8         6                  no
 4, 8, 16     16        12                 no
 1, 2, 1      2         2                  YES (equality)
 0.5, 1, 2    2         1.5                no
⚡ JEE Trick Scan for the largest magnitude first, then ask whether the other two can reach it. Sets like 2,4,8 and 4,8,16 are geometric-looking traps that always fail. And remember that equality counts — 1, 2, 1 works even though the three vectors end up collinear rather than forming a proper triangle.
Answer: (C) 1, 2, 1.
