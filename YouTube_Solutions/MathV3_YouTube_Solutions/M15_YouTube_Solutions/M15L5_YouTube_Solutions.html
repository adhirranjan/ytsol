Concept Every normal to a circle is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, and so lies along a radius.
Formula normal at P = line CP (C = centre). The tangent is perpendicular to the radius CP, so the line perpendicular to the tangent at P is the radius line itself.
Solution

2. The normal at P is the line through P perpendicular to the tangent. Unique perpendicular to the tangent at P.
3. Two lines through P perpendicular to the same tangent must coincide. So normal = line CP.

5. Check with x2+y2=r2: normal at (x1,y1) is y1x − x1y = 0. It is satisfied by (0,0), the centre.

Concept From an external point exactly two tangents can be drawn to a circle, and the two tangent segments to the points of contact have the same length.
Formula PA = PB = √(PC2 − r2) = √S1. Right triangles PAC and PBC share the hypotenuse PC and have CA = CB = r, so PA2 = PC2 − r2 = PB2.
Solution
1. Let P be outside the circle with centre C and radius r; tangents touch at A and B. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
2. So ∠PAC = ∠PBC = 90°, giving two right triangles PAC and PBC.
3. Both triangles have the common hypotenuse PC and equal legs CA = CB = r. Equal radii.
4. By Pythagoras PA2 = PC2 − r2 and PB2 = PC2 − r2.
5. Hence PA = PB = √S1, where S1 is the circle expression evaluated at P.
6. So the two tangent lengths are always equal: they are not different, not zero (P is outside) and not infinite (each is a finite number).
PC^2 = PA^2 + r^2   (right angle at A)
PC^2 = PB^2 + r^2   (right angle at B)
=> PA^2 = PB^2 => PA = PB
So both tangent lengths = sqrt(S1).
⚡ JEE Trick Equal tangent lengths also make PACB a kite, so PC is the perpendicular bisector of the chord of contact AB; this is used in many chord-of-contact and area problems.
Answer: (c) Equal

Concept The quadrilateral is a kite: origin O, the two points of contact A and B, and the centre C. Its area is twice the area of the right triangle OAC, i.e. r × L, where L = tangent length from O.
Formula Area = 2 × (1/2) r L = rL ; L2 = c ; r2 = g2+f2−c. L2 is the power of the origin, S1 = c.
Solution
1. Circle x2+y2+6x−10y+c = 0: centre (−3, 5), r2 = 9 + 25 − c = 34 − c.
2. Length of tangent from the origin: L2 = S1 = c, so L = √c.
3. Triangle OAC is right-angled at A (radius ⊥ tangent), with legs r and L; the kite is two such triangles.
4. Area = rL = √((34−c)c) = 15, so c(34−c) = 225.
5. c2 − 34c + 225 = 0 ⇒ (c − 9)(c − 25) = 0 ⇒ c = 9 or c = 25.
6. Check both: c = 9: r2 = 25, L = 3, area 15. c = 25: r2 = 9, L = 5, area 15. Both valid, so (a) and (d); (b) c=4 gives √120 and (c) c=5 gives √145.
r^2 = 34 - c , L^2 = c
Area = r L = sqrt(c(34 - c)) = 15
c^2 - 34c + 225 = 0
(c - 9)(c - 25) = 0
c = 9: r=5, L=3 -> 15 ; c = 25: r=3, L=5 -> 15
⚡ JEE Trick The two roots swap r and L (5,3 and 3,5), so both give the same area: a multiple-correct hint. Also confirm c < 34 so the circle is real.
Answer: (a) 9 and (d) 25
