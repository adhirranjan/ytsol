Concept A family of circles that share one common centre but have different radii. Only the size changes; the centre is fixed.
Formula x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0, centre (−g,−f). Two circles are concentric when (−g,−f) is identical and only c (hence r) differs.
Solution
1. A circle has a centre and a radius; concentricity is a statement about the centre only. Same centre is the defining word.
2. Keep g, f (so the centre −g,−f stays put) and change only the constant c. Changing c changes r2=g2+f2−c.
3. Example: x2+y2=4 and x2+y2=9 share centre (0,0), radii 2 and 3. Different radii, one centre.
4. Such a set of circles is called concentric. Latin con- (together) + centre.
5. They never intersect, so they nest one inside the other. No common point for distinct radii.
6. So the blank is filled by the word describing equal centre, unequal radius. Matches the given key.
 Centre fixed, radius varies:
  x^2 + y^2 = 4   -> O(0,0), r = 2
  x^2 + y^2 = 9   -> O(0,0), r = 3
  same O, different r  => CONCENTRIC
⚡ JEE Trick To build a circle concentric with a given one, copy its x and y terms verbatim and only replace the constant — never touch g or f.
Answer: Concentric

Concept The quantity g2+f2−c equals the square of the radius, so its sign decides whether the equation is a genuine circle.
Formula r=√(g2+f2−c). Completing the square on x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0 gives (x+g)2+(y+f)2=g2+f2−c.

4. If it equals 0, r=0 and the circle shrinks to a single point (point circle). Boundary case.

   = 0  -> point circle  (radius zero)
   < 0  -> imaginary     (no real locus)
⚡ JEE Trick Before drawing any general-form circle, test the sign of g2+f2−c first — a negative value means the circle has no real existence.

Concept A circle is the locus of all points kept at a fixed distance r (the radius) from a fixed point (h, k) (the centre). Converting that constant-distance condition into coordinates gives the standard (centre–radius) equation.
Formula (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2. it is simply PC2=r2 from the distance formula, with P(x, y) a general point and C(h, k) the centre.
Solution
1. Let P(x, y) be any point on the circle; its defining property is PC=r. every point of a circle is equidistant from the centre.
2. Distance formula: PC=√[(x−h)2+(y−k)2].
3. Put PC=r and square both sides ⇒ (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2, which is option (b).
4. Reject the others: (a) is the special case with centre at the origin; (c) has the signs of h, k reversed. a bracket (x−h) forces the centre coordinate to be +h, so +h inside means a sign error.
5. Reject (d): x2+y2+2hx+2ky=0 has centre (−h,−k) and passes through the origin — a different circle altogether. its form matches the general equation, not the perfect-square standard form.

   PC = sqrt((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2)
 PC = r  =>  (x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2   [option b]
⚡ JEE Trick Read the centre straight off the brackets by flipping signs: a term (x+3) means the centre's x is −3, not +3. This single sign rule disposes of options (a), (c) and (d) instantly.
