1A block of mass M rests on a rough horizontal surface of coefficient of static friction μs. The free-body diagram shows only the normal reaction N upward and the weight Mg downward — no horizontal force is applied. What is the value of the static friction force on the block? (Level-1)Static friction is self-adjustingFriction Lecture 1 — Static vs Kinetic Friction (JEE & NEET)🟡 Similarf = 0
Concept Static friction is a self-adjusting (reactive) force. It does not exist on its own — it appears only to oppose a tendency of relative sliding. If nothing is trying to make the surfaces slide, there is nothing for friction to oppose, so friction is exactly zero. The presence of a rough surface and a large μs only tells you the maximum friction available, not the friction actually acting.
Formula fs ≤ fmax = μsN — an inequality, not an equation. μsN is a ceiling. The actual value of fs is fixed by the equilibrium equation along the surface, never by μsN.
Solution
1. Resolve along the surface (horizontal). The only candidate horizontal forces are the applied force (zero here) and friction f.N and Mg are both vertical, so they contribute nothing horizontally.
2. Block is in equilibrium: ΣFx = 0 ⇒ 0 + f = 0 ⇒ f = 0 N.
3. Vertically, N = Mg — that fixes the available friction μsMg, which stays unused.
   N (up)
   ^
   |
 [ M ]  ---> no applied force, so f = 0
   |
   v Mg (down)
⚡ JEE Trick The classic trap is to write f = μsN reflexively. Never use f = μN unless the body is either sliding (kinetic) or on the verge of sliding (limiting). With no applied force, friction is zero even on sandpaper.
Answer: static friction f = 0 N.
2A body of weight 50 N is placed on a rough surface. If the force required to move the body on the surface is 30 N, find the coefficient of friction. (Level-2)Limiting friction, μ from FminStatic Friction: minimum force required to move a block (IIT JEE/NEET)🟡 Similarμ = 0.6
Concept "The force required to move the body" means the force that just starts the motion — i.e. it equals the limiting (maximum static) friction. Only at that instant may you write f = μN.
Formula fmax = μN and, for a horizontal surface with a horizontal pull, N = W. The pull is horizontal, so it does not change the normal reaction — this is what makes N = weight here.
Solution
1. N = W = 50 N.
2. At the verge of motion, applied force = limiting friction: 30 N = μ × 50 N.
3. μ = 30/50 = 0.6 (dimensionless).μ is a pure number — a ratio of two forces — so it carries no unit.
⚡ JEE Trick μ = (force to just move it) ÷ (weight) works only for a horizontal pull on a horizontal surface. If the pull is at an angle θ, N changes to W ∓ F sinθ and the shortcut collapses.
Answer: μ = 0.6
3An object of mass M is kept on a rough table. Two forces are applied on it in the plane of the table: 20 N along one direction and 15 N perpendicular to it. The object does not move. Find the magnitude and direction of the static friction. (Level-3)Friction opposes the resultantForce Applied at an Angle with Friction🟡 Similar25 N, opposite to the resultant Fr
Concept Friction is a vector. When several forces act in the plane of contact, friction does not oppose each of them separately — it opposes their vector resultant, and (while the body stays at rest) it is exactly equal and opposite to that resultant.
Formula Fr = √(F₁² + F₂²) for two perpendicular forces, and equilibrium gives f⃗ = −F⃗r. "Does not move" ⇒ ΣF = 0 in the plane, so friction must cancel the whole resultant.
Solution
1. The two applied forces are perpendicular: Fr = √(20² + 15²) = √(400 + 225) = √625 = 25 N.
2. Its direction makes angle tan⁻¹(15/20) = 36.87° with the 20 N force.
3. Since the body is static, friction = 25 N directed exactly opposite to Fr.This also tells you μsMg ≥ 25 N, otherwise the block would have slid.

⚡ JEE Trick The trap answer is "35 N" (20 + 15). Perpendicular forces never add arithmetically — always take the Pythagorean resultant first, then let friction mirror it.
Answer: friction = 25 N, directed opposite to the resultant force Fr.
4A 5 kg block rests on a rough horizontal floor with μ = 0.6. A 20 N force is applied at 37° to the horizontal, pushing the block towards the left (horizontal component 16 N leftwards, vertical component 12 N). Find the magnitude of the friction force acting on the block and its direction. Take g = 10 m/s². (Level-4)Angled push, friction below limitingA block of mass 5 kg is (i) pushed in case (A) and (ii) pulled in case (B). by a force F=20 N. ...🟢 ExactFr = 16 N, rightwards
Concept Two-step routine for every "friction on an angled force" problem: (1) find N from vertical equilibrium, (2) compare the horizontal component of the applied force with fmax = μN. If the horizontal component loses, the block stays put and friction equals that component exactly.
Formula N = mg ± F sinθ, fmax = μN, and if F cosθ < fmax then f = F cosθ. The ± sign is decided by whether the applied force presses the block into the floor (+) or lifts it (−).
Solution
1. Components: F cos37° = 20 × 0.8 = 16 N (horizontal, leftwards); F sin37° = 20 × 0.6 = 12 N (vertical, pressing down).
2. Vertical: N = mg + 12 = 5 × 10 + 12 = 62 N.A downward-slanting push increases N, which increases the friction available — that is why pushing a crate is harder than pulling it.
3. fmax = 0.6 × 62 = 37.2 N. Since 16 N < 37.2 N, the block does not move.
4. Therefore static friction = 16 N, directed opposite to the 16 N leftward push, i.e. rightwards.

⚡ JEE Trick Even if the answer had been "block moves", you would still need N = 62 N first. Compute N before you decide anything; the vertical component of the applied force is the number examiners hope you forget.
Answer: friction = 16 N, rightwards (static, well below the 37.2 N limit).

2Which of the following statements about static friction is TRUE? (a) It increases with surface area (b) It acts only after the object starts moving (c) It opposes the motion between two bodies in contact (d) It is always equal to the weight of the objectProperties of static frictionFriction Class 11 | L-1 | Static and Kinetic Friction, Limiting Friction🟡 SimilarcIt opposes the motion between two bodies in contact
Concept Static friction opposes the tendency of relative sliding between two surfaces in contact. It is independent of contact area (for a given N), it exists before motion begins (not after), and its magnitude is set by the equilibrium equation — not by the weight.
Key relation fs ≤ μsN, with fs adjusting itself to whatever the applied force is. "Area-independent" follows because a larger area means the same total load spread thinner — pressure drops in exact proportion.
Solution
1. (a) is false — for a fixed normal force, friction does not depend on the apparent contact area.
2. (b) is false — that describes kinetic friction; static friction acts precisely while the object is not moving.
3. (d) is false — friction is horizontal and its size has no fixed link to the weight; on a wall it can even be vertical.
4. (c) survives: friction always opposes relative motion (or its tendency) between the surfaces in contact. ✓
⚡ JEE Trick "Opposes relative motion" — not "opposes motion". Friction on the rear wheel of a car, or on a block riding on an accelerating cart, actually drives the body forward while still opposing relative slipping at the contact.
Answer: (c) It opposes the motion between two bodies in contact.
3The coefficient of static friction is generally: (a) Equal to coefficient of kinetic friction (b) Less than coefficient of kinetic friction (c) Greater than coefficient of kinetic friction (d) Unrelated to coefficient of kinetic frictionμs vs μkGrade 11 Physics — Static and Kinetic Friction🟡 SimilarcGreater than coefficient of kinetic friction
Concept At rest the microscopic asperities of the two surfaces have time to settle and "cold-weld" into each other, so a larger force is needed to break them. Once sliding starts these junctions have no time to form fully, so less force sustains the motion. Hence μs > μk — exactly the statement the L-2 text opens with.
Key relation μk < μs ⇒ fk < fmax for the same N. This inequality is the entire reason the f–F graph has a small drop (a "kink") at the moment sliding begins.
Solution
1. Push a heavy carton: a large shove is needed to start it, a smaller steady push keeps it going.
2. That everyday observation is the statement μs > μk.
3. So option (c).
⚡ JEE Trick In numerical problems the two coefficients are usually given separately (e.g. μs = 0.5, μk = 0.4). Use μs to decide whether it moves, then μk to compute how fast it moves. Mixing the two is the single most common error in this chapter.
Answer: (c) μs is greater than μk.
4What happens to the kinetic friction once motion starts? (a) It becomes zero (b) It increases gradually (c) It becomes constant (d) It equals the normal forceConstancy of kinetic frictionGrade 11 and 12 Forces — Static and kinetic friction🟡 SimilarcIt becomes constant
Concept Kinetic friction is fixed entirely by μk and N. Neither the applied force nor the sliding speed (for ordinary speeds) enters the formula, so once the body slides the friction locks at one value and stays there while N is unchanged.
Formula fk = μkN — no F, no v in the expression. This is why the f–F graph is a horizontal line (parallel to the F-axis) after the kink.
Solution
1. (a) is wrong — friction does not vanish, or nothing would ever slow down.
2. (b) is wrong — that is static friction's behaviour, growing with the applied force up to μsN.
3. (d) is wrong — fk = μkN and μk < 1 in nearly all cases, so fk < N.
4. (c) is right: fk stays constant at μkN.
⚡ JEE Trick "Constant" holds only while N is constant. Change the geometry — tilt the surface, add a vertical component to the push — and fk changes too, because N did.
Answer: (c) It becomes constant (fk = μkN).
5Frictional force is independent of: (a) Type of surfaces in contact (b) Contact surface area (c) Normal reaction (d) Coefficient of frictionArea-independence of frictionFRICTION Class 11 | Static, Limiting and Kinetic Friction force | Part 1🟡 SimilarbContact surface area
Concept The whole content of f = μN is that friction depends on exactly two things — the nature of the pair of surfaces (through μ) and how hard they are pressed together (through N). The apparent area of contact is absent from the formula. The L-2 text states it directly: sliding a book flat or on its edge gives the same friction.
Formula f = μN — contains μ (surface type) and N (normal reaction), but no area A. Microscopically the real contact area is proportional to N, not to the apparent area, which is why A cancels out.
Solution
1. (a) enters through μ — friction does depend on it.
2. (c) enters through N — friction is directly proportional to it.
3. (d) is μ itself — obviously relevant.
4. Only (b), the contact surface area, is absent ⇒ friction is independent of it.
⚡ JEE Trick Racing tyres are wide for heat dissipation and wear, not for extra μ. In JEE physics, "friction is independent of area" is exact — never let a real-world intuition tempt you into option (b) being wrong.
Answer: (b) Contact surface area.

1An object is slowing down on a rough horizontal plane with a deceleration of 2 m/s². What is the coefficient of kinetic friction? (Take g = 10 m/s².)μk from decelerationKinetic Friction — Skidding to a Stop🟡 Similarμk = 0.2
Concept On a horizontal plane with no applied force, friction is the only horizontal force. So the whole deceleration is produced by kinetic friction, and the mass cancels — a beautiful feature that makes the answer independent of how heavy the object is.
Formula μkmg = ma ⇒ μk = a/g. Both sides carry the factor m, so it cancels — the same reason all objects slide to rest with the same deceleration on the same floor.
Solution
1. Vertical: N = mg (nothing else vertical).
2. Horizontal: −μkN = −ma ⇒ μkmg = ma.
3. μk = a/g = 2/10 = 0.2 (dimensionless).
⚡ JEE Trick μ = a/g is worth memorising as a two-way tool: given μ you instantly get the retardation μg, and given the retardation you instantly get μ. It converts most "how far / how long until it stops" problems into one-line kinematics.
Answer: μk = 0.2

3In a train moving with a uniform acceleration, the brakes are applied. If the brakes produce the same effective retarding force on each bogey then: (a) the first bogey stops first (b) the last bogey stops first (c) all bogies stop at the same time (d) the middle bogey stops firstEqual retardation on coupled bodiesClass 11 chap 5 | Friction Force 01 | Static and Kinetic Friction (IIT JEE / NEET)🟡 Similarbthe last bogey stops first ⚠
Concept A train is a chain of coupled bodies. Coupling forces appear only when the parts try to accelerate differently. If every bogey experiences the same retarding force and every bogey has the same mass, each one's deceleration is identical, so no coupling force develops and the whole train behaves as one rigid body.
Key relation a = f/m for each bogey; all bogies start from the same speed v₀ and stop after t = v₀/a. Same v₀ and same a ⇒ same stopping time, for every bogey, regardless of its position in the train.
Solution
1. Each bogey: retarding force f, mass m ⇒ deceleration a = f/m, the same for all.
2. They were coupled, so they all share the initial speed v₀ at the instant of braking.
3. Stopping time t = v₀/a is therefore identical ⇒ they all stop together, and the couplings stay slack (zero force).
4. That is option (c). The printed key gives (b) — see the ⚠ note in the Answer column.
  [B1]--[B2]--[B3]--[B4]     each: retarding force f, mass m
   f     f     f     f       => a = f/m identical everywhere
  coupling force = m(a - a) = 0  => train stays rigid, stops as one
⚡ JEE Trick In any connected system, first test "would the parts accelerate identically if they were free?" If yes, the internal force is zero. Only when the free accelerations differ do tensions or coupling forces appear.
Answer: physics gives (c) all bogies stop at the same time; the ICAD key prints (b) — flagged as a key defect.
