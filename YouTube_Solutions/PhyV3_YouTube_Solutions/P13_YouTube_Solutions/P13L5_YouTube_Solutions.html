1A body of mass m Kg. starts falling from a point 2R above the earths surface. Its kinetic energy when it has fallen to a point ‘R’ above the earth’s surface. (a) GMm/2R (b) GMm/6R (c) 2GMm/3R (d) GMm/3R — (Level-1)KE gained falling from 2R to R heightA body of mass m kg starts falling from a point 2R above the earth\'s surface. Its kinetic energy🟢 ExactbGMm/6R
Concept Heights must be converted to distances from the Earth's centre before using U = −GMm/r. A height 2R above the surface is r = 3R; a height R above the surface is r = 2R. The KE gained equals the drop in potential energy between those two radii. The trap is to plug in r = 2R and r = R (heights instead of radii), which gives GMm/2R, option (a).
Formula KEgained = Ui − Uf = GMm(1/rf − 1/ri) Gravity is conservative and the body starts from rest, so all lost potential energy appears as kinetic energy; the field varies with r, so mgh cannot be used over a drop comparable to R.
Solution
1. Start: height 2R above surface ⇒ ri = R + 2R = 3R; body released from rest, so KEi = 0.
2. End: height R above surface ⇒ rf = R + R = 2R.
3. Initial PE: Ui = −GMm/ri = −GMm/3R.
4. Final PE: Uf = −GMm/rf = −GMm/2R.Closer to the Earth ⇒ more negative PE.
5. Energy conservation: KEf = Ui − Uf = −GMm/3R + GMm/2R.
6. Common denominator 6R: KE = GMm(1/2R − 1/3R) = GMm(3 − 2)/6R = GMm/6R.
7. Check sign: KE is positive, as it must be for a body that has fallen inward.
 r_i = R + 2R = 3R      U_i = -GMm/3R
 r_f = R +  R = 2R      U_f = -GMm/2R
 KE  = U_i - U_f = GMm(1/2R - 1/3R)
     = GMm(3 - 2)/6R
 -----------------------------------------

⚡ JEE Trick Always write r = R + h first. Shortcut for falling between radii a·R and b·R: KE = (GMm/R)(1/b − 1/a) with b < a; here (1/2 − 1/3) = 1/6. Trap: using heights 2R and R directly as radii gives GMm(1 − 1/2)/R = GMm/2R, which is option (a). Option (d) GMm/3R is just |Ui|, the PE at the start, not the KE gained.
Answer: (b) GMm/6R.

Concept To just reach height h the body must arrive there with zero speed, so the launch KE must equal the rise in gravitational PE between r = R and r = R + h. Because h = R/4 is not small compared with R, the uniform-field formula v = √(2gh) is wrong here; we must use U = −GMm/r and then convert GM = gR².

1. Initial radius r1 = R (surface); final radius r2 = R + R/4 = 5R/4.
2. Energy conservation: (1/2)mv² − GMm/R = 0 − GMm/(5R/4).KE at the highest point is zero for the minimum launch speed.

4. Use GM = gR² (surface gravity): (1/2)v² = gR²/5R = gR/5.
5. So v² = 2gR/5 ⇒ v = √(2gR/5) = √((2/5)gR).

4Find the potential energy of a system of four particles, each having mass ‘m’, placed at the vertices of a square of side l. Also obtain the potential at the centre of the square. — (Level-4)PE of four masses on squareFind the potential energy of a system of four particles placed at the vertices of a square of side L🟢 ExactU = −5.41 Gm²/l, V = −4√2 Gm/l
Concept Potential energy of a system is a sum over every distinct pair of particles, each counted once: U = −ΣGmimj/rij. Four particles give 4C2 = 6 pairs: four sides of length l and two diagonals of length √2 l. Potential at a point is different: a scalar sum of −Gm/r over the masses, with no pairing. The trap is counting 4 pairs (sides only) or 12 (double counting).
Formula U = −Σpairs Gmimj/rij , V = −Σ Gmi/ri PE is the work to assemble the system from infinity, pair by pair; potential is PE per unit test mass, so it is a plain sum of scalars.
Solution
1. Pairs: 4C2 = 6. Four are adjacent (separation l), two are diagonal (separation √2 l).
2. Side pairs: Us = 4·(−Gm²/l) = −4Gm²/l.
3. Diagonal pairs: Ud = 2·(−Gm²/(√2 l)) = −√2 Gm²/l.2/√2 = √2.
4. Total: U = −(4 + √2)Gm²/l = −(4 + 1.414)Gm²/l = −5.41 Gm²/l.
5. Centre: each vertex is half a diagonal away, r = √2 l/2 = l/√2.
6. Potential from one mass: −Gm/(l/√2) = −√2 Gm/l; four masses add as scalars: V = 4·(−√2 Gm/l) = −4√2 Gm/l ≈ −5.66 Gm/l.
7. Note the field at the centre is zero by symmetry, yet the potential is not zero: potential never cancels, it only adds negative terms.

Concept Every element of a ring is at the same distance √(a² + x²) from a point on its axis, so the potential there is simply V(x) = −GM/√(a² + x²). The work an external agent must do to move a mass slowly from x1 to x2 is the rise in potential energy, W = m[V(x2) − V(x1)]. Moving away from the ring makes V less negative, so W is positive. The numbers here are chosen to give 3-4-5 and 3-√27-6 triangles.
Formula V(x) = −GM/√(a² + x²) , W = m[V(x2) − V(x1)] Potential is a scalar, so the ring's contribution is just −G·dM/d summed with the same d for all elements; quasi-static moving means ΔKE = 0, so Wext = ΔU.

1. Data: ring M = 5 kg, radius a = 3 m; particle m = 1 kg; x1 = 4 m, x2 = 3√3 m.
2. Distance to ring at x1: d1 = √(3² + 4²) = √(9 + 16) = 5 m.
3. Distance at x2: d2 = √(3² + (3√3)²) = √(9 + 27) = 6 m.
4. V1 = −GM/d1 = −G·5/5 = −G J/kg.
5. V2 = −GM/d2 = −G·5/6 = −5G/6 J/kg.Farther from the ring ⇒ less negative.

Concept In a circular orbit the gravitational force always points toward the centre, while the velocity is always tangential. Force ⟂ displacement at every instant means zero power, hence zero work, at every moment, not just over a full revolution. Even independently of that, gravity is conservative, so the work around any closed path is zero. The other options are dimensionally inconsistent anyway.
Formula W = ∮F·dr = ∮F ds cos 90° = 0 Radial force, tangential displacement; and a conservative force does zero work over a closed loop.
Solution
1. Force on the satellite: F = GMm/R², directed toward the Earth's centre (radial).
2. Velocity in a circular orbit is tangent to the circle, so each small displacement dr is tangential.
3. Angle between F and dr is 90° at every point ⇒ dW = F·dr = F ds cos 90° = 0.
4. Summing over one revolution: W = ∮dW = 0.Even a fraction of a revolution gives zero.
5. Second argument: gravity is conservative, Wclosed loop = −ΔU = −(Ufinal − Uinitial) = 0 since the satellite returns to the same point.
6. Consistency: KE = GMm/2R is constant in a circular orbit, and work-energy theorem W = ΔKE = 0 agrees.

Concept Write the PE of the square as a function of the split m (total 2M fixed) and find its extremum. Equal masses sit on the diagonals, so the four sides each join an m with an (M − m), and the two diagonals join m with m and (M − m) with (M − m). Wording note: the only stationary point, m = M/2, is where the PE is most negative, i.e. its magnitude is maximum; the intended answer (JEE Main 2021) is this extremum.
Formula U = −(G/d)[4m(M − m) + (m² + (M − m)²)/√2] Four side pairs at distance d, two diagonal pairs at d√2.
Solution
1. Sides: top (m, M − m), bottom (M − m, m), left (m, M − m), right (M − m, m) ⇒ 4 pairs of product m(M − m) at distance d.
2. Diagonals: m–m and (M − m)–(M − m), each at distance d√2.
3. Let p = m(M − m). Then m² + (M − m)² = M² − 2p, so U = −(G/d)[4p + (M² − 2p)/√2] = −(G/d)[M²/√2 + p(4 − √2)].
4. U depends on m only through p, with coefficient (4 − √2) > 0. dU/dm = 0 ⇒ dp/dm = M − 2m = 0 ⇒ m = M/2.
5. At m = M/2, p is largest, so |U| is largest (U is most negative). This is the extremum the question means by "maximum".
6. Check: m = M/2 gives U = −(GM²/d)[1/√2 + (4 − √2)/4] ≈ −1.35GM²/d, versus −0.71GM²/d as m → 0.
7. M/m = 2 ⇒ ratio 2 : 1, so x = 2.

⚡ JEE Trick Any symmetric split of a fixed total, product m(M − m), peaks at the equal split m = M/2; no calculus needed. Strictly, with U as a signed quantity this is the minimum of U (|U| maximum); the true maximum would be the trivial limit m → 0. JEE accepted x = 2, so answer 2.
Answer: x = 2 (extremum at m = M/2, where the PE magnitude is greatest).
