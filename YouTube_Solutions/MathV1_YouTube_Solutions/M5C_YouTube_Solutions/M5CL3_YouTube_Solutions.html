Concept The list cos1°…cos180° sweeps a full half-turn, and the engine is the supplementary reflection cos(180°−k) = −cos k, which makes almost every term annihilate its mirror. The trap: students pair happily but forget the two self-standing terms — the mid value cos90° and the endpoint cos180° — which is where the whole answer actually lives.
Formula cos(180°−θ) = −cosθ reflection across 90°; cos90° = 0; cos180° = −1.
Solution
1. Pair each cos k° with cos(180°−k)°. Because cos(180°−k) = −cos k, every such pair sums to 0.e.g. cos1° + cos179° = cos1° − cos1° = 0.
2. The pairs (1°,179°), (2°,178°), …, (89°,91°) use up all angles except 90° and 180° — that is 89 pairs, each contributing 0.
3. k = 90° is its own reflection (180°−90° = 90°), and stands alone: cos90° = 0.
4. k = 180° is the endpoint with no partner inside the range: cos180° = −1.
5. Total = 89×0 + cos90° + cos180° = 0 + 0 + (−1) = −1.

Concept The numbers tan²(kπ/7) for k = 1,2,3 are the three roots of a cubic obtained from tan7θ = 0. Expanding tan7θ and setting the numerator to zero gives x³ − 21x² + 35x − 7 = 0 with x = tan².
