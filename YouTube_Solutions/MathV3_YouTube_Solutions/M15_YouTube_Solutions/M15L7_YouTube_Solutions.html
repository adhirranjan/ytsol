Concept A circle through the common points of a circle and a line is a member of a one-parameter family.
Formula S+λL=0. S and L both vanish at the intersection points, so S+λL does too, and it is a circle (same x2,y2 coefficients).
Solution
1. Let S=x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c and L=ax+by+d. Adding a line keeps the quadratic part unchanged, so the result is still a circle.
2. At any common point S=0 and L=0, so S+λL=0 holds for every λ. λ is then fixed by one extra condition (a point, minimum radius, ...).
3. Hence the family is S+λL=0.
⚡ JEE Trick For two circles use S1+λS2=0 (λ≠−1); for circle+line use S+λL=0.
Answer: S+λL=0

Concept At a point P of orthogonal intersection the radius of each circle is the tangent of the other.
Formula Tangent of C1 at P is the radius C2P. Orthogonal means the tangents at P are perpendicular, and a radius is perpendicular to its own circle's tangent.
Solution
1. Let the circles have centres C1,C2 meeting at P with tangents T1,T2. Orthogonal: T1⊥T2.
2. C1P⊥T1, and T1⊥T2, so C1P is parallel to T2. Both lines pass through P, so they coincide.
3. Thus T2 is the line C1P: the tangent of circle 2 passes through the centre of circle 1.
4. Likewise T1 passes through C2. So each tangent passes through the centre of the other circle.
⚡ JEE Trick Right angle at P gives d2=r12+r22, which is the orthogonality test.
Answer: centre(s)

Concept Circles are orthogonal (angle π/2) when 2g1g2+2f1f2=c1+c2.
Formula 2g1g2+2f1f2=c1+c2 ⇒ θ=90°. If the sum rule holds exactly, the angle between the circles is a right angle.
Solution
1. Circle 1: g1=1/2, f1=1/2, c1=0.
2. Circle 2: g2=1/2, f2=−1/2, c2=0.
3. LHS: 2(1/2)(1/2)+2(1/2)(−1/2)=1/2−1/2=0. Substitute into the left of the condition.
4. RHS: c1+c2=0. LHS=RHS, so the circles are orthogonal and θ=π/2. cosθ=0 means θ=90°.
5. Write θ=(p·q)/r=(π·1)/2: p=π, q=1, r=2.
⚡ JEE Trick Both circles pass through the origin and are mirror images in the x-axis, a pattern that often gives 90°.
Answer: p=π, q=1, r=2  (angle = π/2)

Solution

Answer: a + b = 12

Solution
1. Let the variable circle be x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0 with centre (−g, −f). General circle.
2. With S1 (g1=2, f1=−3, c1=9): 4g − 6f = c + 9. Orthogonality with the first circle.
3. With S2 (g2=−2, f2=3, c2=4): −4g + 6f = c + 4. Orthogonality with the second circle.
4. Subtract the second from the first: 8g − 12f = 5. c cancels out.
5. Centre (x, y) = (−g, −f), so g = −x, f = −y: −8x + 12y = 5. Replace g, f by the centre.
6. Hence 8x − 12y + 5 = 0, which is S1 − S2 = 0 (the radical axis); so k = 5. Compare with 8x − 12y + k = 0.

Concept The length of tangent from (x1,y1) to a circle S=0 is √S11. A ratio of lengths becomes a ratio of S values.

Range of k for a circle familyConsider a family of circles which are passing through M(1,1) and tangent to the x-axis 🟡 Similar(a) k ≥ 1/2
Concept A circle through a given point tangent to the x-axis has radius |k| where (h, k) is its centre; passing through the point gives a relation between h and k.
Formula (h+1)2+(1−k)2 = k2. Distance from centre to (−1,1) equals radius = |k| (tangent to x-axis).
Solution
1. Centre (h, k); tangent to the x-axis ⇒ radius = |k|. Distance from centre to the x-axis.
2. The circle passes through (−1, 1): (h+1)2 + (1−k)2 = k2. Distance to the point equals the radius.
3. Expand: (h+1)2 + 1 − 2k + k2 = k2. Square (1−k).
4. The k2 terms cancel: 2k = (h+1)2 + 1. Rearrange.
5. So k = [(h+1)2+1]/2. Express k.
6. Since (h+1)2 ≥ 0 for real h, k ≥ 1/2, with equality at h = −1. Perfect square is non-negative.
7. The point lies above the x-axis, so k > 0 consistently; the set of k is [1/2, ∞), option (a). Cross-check with the position of the point.
⚡ JEE Trick The k^2 terms cancel, leaving k as a perfect square plus a constant: read off the minimum (h = -1 gives k = 1/2).
Answer: (a) k ≥ 1/2
