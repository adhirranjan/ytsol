1Sum of exterior angles of any convex polygon (one per vertex)Polygon exterior anglesInterior & exterior angles of a polygon🟡 Similard360°
Concept An exterior angle is the turn you make at each vertex as you walk once around the boundary of the polygon. By the time you return to your start you have rotated through exactly one full turn — no more, no less — regardless of how many sides the polygon has. This "total turning = 360°" idea is why the sum is the same constant for a triangle, a square, or a 100-gon.
Formula Σ exterior angles = 360° (one exterior angle per vertex, convex polygon). Each exterior angle pairs with its interior angle as a straight line: interior + exterior = 180° at every vertex. For a regular n-gon each one is 360°/n.
Solution
1. At each vertex, interior + exterior = 180° (they form a straight angle).The extended side and the next side lie on a straight line.
2. Sum over all n vertices: Σinterior + Σexterior = n × 180°.
3. But Σinterior = (n − 2) × 180°.Standard interior-angle-sum result.
4. So Σexterior = n·180° − (n − 2)·180° = 180°(n − n + 2) = 2 × 180° = 360°.The n cancels — that is why the answer never depends on the number of sides.
 n     Σ interior        Σ exterior = n·180 − Σint
 3     180°              540 − 180 = 360°
 4     360°              720 − 360 = 360°
 6     720°              1080 − 720 = 360°
⚡ JEE Trick The sum of exterior angles of any convex polygon is always 360° — memorise it as "one full rotation." This instantly gives the number of sides of a regular polygon from one exterior angle: n = 360°/exterior. Trap: this holds for the sum only — a single exterior angle does depend on n.
Answer: 360°.
2Sum of interior angles of a hexagonPolygon interior anglesSum of interior angles of a hexagon🟢 Exactb720°
Concept Any polygon with n sides can be sliced into triangles by drawing all the diagonals from one single vertex. From one corner of an n-gon you can reach n − 3 other vertices, producing exactly (n − 2) triangles, and every one of those triangles contributes its full 180° to the interior angles of the polygon. So the interior-angle sum is just "count the triangles, multiply by 180°." A hexagon is the n = 6 case.
Formula Σ interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°. The (n − 2) triangles tile the polygon exactly, and their angles together make up precisely the polygon's interior angles. Here n = 6.
Solution
1. Identify the number of sides: a hexagon has n = 6.
2. Count the triangles from one vertex: n − 2 = 6 − 2 = 4 triangles.From one vertex you can draw diagonals to the 3 non-adjacent vertices, cutting the hexagon into 4 triangles.
3. Apply the formula: Σ = (n − 2) × 180° = 4 × 180° = 720°.
4. Cross-check via exterior angles: Σ interior = n·180° − 360° = 6 × 180° − 360° = 1080° − 360° = 720° ✓.Interior + exterior = 180° at each vertex, and all exterior angles sum to one full turn.
5. Sanity check for a regular hexagon: each angle would be 720°/6 = 120°, the familiar honeycomb angle ✓.
 n    triangles (n−2)   Σ interior = (n−2)·180°
 4    2                 360°
 5    3                 540°
 6    4                 720°   <-- answer
 8    6                 1080°
⚡ JEE Trick Read "(n − 2) × 180" as "every new side adds 180°" starting from the triangle's 180° — so hexagon = 180° + 3 × 180° jumps from n = 3. Trap: writing n × 180° (forgetting the −2) gives 1080°, and (n − 2) × 360° gives 1440° — both appear as wrong options; only the triangle count times 180° is right.
Answer: (b) 720°.
3Each interior angle of a regular octagonPolygon interior angleMeasure of each angle of a regular octagon🟢 Exactb135°
Concept A regular octagon has 8 equal sides and 8 equal interior angles. So one interior angle is simply the total interior-angle sum (n − 2) × 180° shared equally among the n vertices. There is also a slicker back-door: since interior + exterior = 180° at each vertex and each exterior angle of a regular n-gon is 360°/n, the interior angle is 180° minus that.
Formula each interior angle = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n, equivalently = 180° − 360°/n. Equal angles let you divide the total by n; the second form uses the straight-line pair with the exterior angle. Here n = 8.
Solution
1. Number of sides: octagon ⇒ n = 8.
2. Total interior sum = (n − 2) × 180° = 6 × 180° = 1080°.An octagon splits into 6 triangles from one vertex.
3. Each angle = 1080° ÷ 8 = 135°.All 8 angles are equal in a regular octagon.
4. Faster route: exterior angle = 360°/8 = 45°, so interior = 180° − 45° = 135° ✓.
5. Sanity: 135° is between 120° (hexagon) and 144° (decagon), as it must be — interior angles grow with n.More sides ⇒ the polygon is "rounder" ⇒ each corner is flatter.
6. Geometric picture: 135° = 90° + 45°, which is why a regular octagon looks like a square with its four corners chamfered off at 45°.
 n     exterior = 360/n   interior = 180 − 360/n
 6     60°                120°
 8     45°                135°   <-- answer
 10    36°                144°
 12    30°                150°
⚡ JEE Trick For "each angle of a regular n-gon" always jump via the exterior: 180° − 360°/n — one division, one subtraction, no big products. Trap: dividing the 1080° total by 6 (the triangle count) instead of by 8 (the vertex count) gives 180°, an impossible interior angle; divide by n, never by n − 2.
Answer: (b) 135°.

Concept Let the two regular polygons have 5x and 4x sides — the cleanest way to use a 5 : 4 ratio. Writing the 9° difference with these expressions makes the 180°'s cancel, leaving a simple equation in x alone.

Formula sector OAB = ½ r² θ (θ in radians), △OAB = ½ r² sin θ, segment = ½ r² (θ − sin θ). Triangle area = ½·OA·OB·sin(∠AOB) with OA = OB = r; the sector is the θ/2π share of πr². For a regular n-gon, θ = 2π/n.

     .-'  /|          triangle OAB = radii OA, OB + CHORD AB

   |    /  |            between chord and arc)
   O---+---B          θ = angle AOB
Solution
1. Let ∠AOB = θ (radians), OA = OB = r.All vertices lie on the circle, so both radii have the same length r.
2. Sector area = (θ/2π)·πr² = ½ r² θ.The sector is the fraction θ/2π of the whole disc.
3. Triangle area = ½·OA·OB·sin θ = ½ r² sin θ.Standard "two sides and the included angle" formula.
4. Difference = ½r²θ − ½r² sin θ = ½ r² (θ − sin θ) — the circular segment cut off by the chord AB.
5. Since sin θ < θ for every θ > 0, the difference is positive: sector OAB > △OAB, always.The arc is the longer, outward-bulging boundary, so it encloses more area than the straight chord.
6. Summing over all n sides of a regular n-gon (θ = 2π/n each): total sectors = n·½r²(2π/n) = πr² (the whole disc ✓), total triangles = ½ n r² sin(2π/n) (the polygon's area), so circle − polygon = ½nr²(2π/n − sin 2π/n).
7. As n → ∞, θ = 2π/n → 0 and sin θ → θ, so the polygon's area → πr² — the polygon fills the circle in the limit.A classic squeeze: the segment slivers vanish as the sides get short.
