1At angle of repose α, which of the following is true? (a) Limiting friction = 0 (b) Driving force = 0 (c) Limiting friction = Driving force (d) Frictional force > Driving forceAngle of repose Angle Of Friction & Angle Of Repose — Friction L2 (Concepts) 🟡 Similar cLimiting friction = Driving force
Concept The angle of repose α is the inclination at which a block placed on the plane is just on the verge of sliding down. On the verge means the block is still in equilibrium, but static friction has reached its maximum (limiting) value.
Formula On an incline θ: N = mg cosθ, driving force F = mg sinθ, limiting friction f_L = μ mg cosθ. Both f_L and F change with θ — f_L falls (cosθ ↓) while F rises (sinθ ↑), so they must cross at exactly one angle.
Solution
1. At θ = α the block is in equilibrium along the plane, so ΣFalong = 0 → f = F.Equilibrium is what "at rest" means; the friction that acts equals the driving force.
2. At θ = α it is also on the point of slipping, so the friction acting is the limiting value: f = f_L.Combining the two: f_L = F, i.e. option (c).
3. Substituting: μ mg cosα = mg sinα → tanα = μ → α = tan⁻¹μ.
   theta < alpha : F < f_L   block rests, f = F (static, not limiting)
   theta = alpha : F = f_L   verge of sliding      <-- angle of repose
   theta > alpha : F > f_L   block slides, a = g(sin@ - mu cos@)
⚡ JEE Trick Angle of repose = angle of friction, both equal tan⁻¹μ. The classic trap is (b): "driving force = 0" is wrong — mg sinα is at its largest sustainable value there, not zero. (a) is wrong for the same reason.
Answer: (c) Limiting friction = Driving force, i.e. tanα = μ

Concept Static friction can point either way. If m₂ is small, m₁ tends to slide down and friction acts up; if m₂ is large, m₁ tends to be dragged up and friction acts down. The two extreme cases give the two limits.
Formula T = m₂g, N = m₁g cos37° , f_L = μN, equilibrium: T ± f = m₁g sin37°. sin37° = 0.6, cos37° = 0.8.
Solution
1. Down-slope pull on m₁: m₁g sin37° = 10 × 10 × 0.6 = 60 N.
2. N = m₁g cos37° = 10 × 10 × 0.8 = 80 N → f_L = 0.5 × 80 = 40 N.
3. Minimum m₂: friction fully up the slope. T + 40 = 60 → T = 20 N → m₂(min) = 20/10 = 2 kg.Below 2 kg the string cannot hold m₁; it slides down.
4. Maximum m₂: friction fully down the slope. T = 60 + 40 = 100 N → m₂(max) = 100/10 = 10 kg.Above 10 kg the hanging mass drags m₁ up.
5. Zero friction: the string alone must balance the slope component, T = 60 N → m₂ = 6 kg.6 kg sits exactly midway between 2 and 10 — the centre of the "no-motion band".
   m2 (kg):  0 ....... 2 ================= 10 .......
             slides down |   both at rest   | m1 dragged up
                         2 kg            10 kg
             friction = 0 exactly at m2 = 6 kg
⚡ JEE Trick The band of equilibrium is always centred on the "friction = 0" value and is ±f_L/g wide in mass: 6 ± 40/10 = 6 ± 4 → 2 kg to 10 kg. Get the centre and half-width and both answers fall out at once.
Answer: (i) m₂(min) = 2 kg, m₂(max) = 10 kg (ii) m₂ = 6 kg

5In order to stop a car in the shortest distance on a horizontal road, one should (a) apply the brakes very hard so that the wheels stop rotating (b) apply the brakes hard enough to just prevent slipping (c) pump the brakes (press and release) (d) shut the engine off and not apply brakesStatic vs kinetic friction in braking HC Verma — In order to stop a car in the shortest distance on a horizontal road, one should 🟢 Exact bapply the brakes hard enough to just prevent slipping
