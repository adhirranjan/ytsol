1Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding significant figures? (Level-1) (a) All non-zero digits are significant (b) Significant figures are meaningful digits known with certainty plus one estimated/uncertain (c) Zero between two non-zero digits is significant (d) All of the aboveRules of significant figuresSignificant Figures - Rules (Class 11 JEE/NEET)🟡 SimilardAll of the above
Concept A significant figure is a digit that carries real information about a measurement. The count runs from the first non-zero digit to the last reliably reported digit: all of these are known with certainty except the final one, which is the estimated digit. Zeros are judged by position, not by looks — a zero trapped between non-zero digits is a measured value, whereas a leading zero only parks the decimal point.
Formula Three rules are on trial here: non-zero digits always count; sig figs = certain digits + 1 estimated digit; sandwiched zeros count. Each is a standard rule, so test them one at a time and only then decide whether "All of the above" is forced.
Solution
1. Test (a): every non-zero digit is a digit you actually read off the instrument — correct.
2. Test (b): a reading like 25.6 mL means 25 is certain and 6 is estimated — correct.The last digit is always the doubtful one; that is what "meaningful digits" means.
3. Test (c): in 102, the 0 sits between 1 and 2, so it was measured — correct.A zero you cannot delete without changing the reading is significant.
4. All three survive, so no single option (a), (b) or (c) can be the complete answer.
5. Hence the correct choice is (d) All of the above.
6. Check the trap: none of the three contradicts another — they are complementary, not rival, rules.
 statement                        verdict
 (a) non-zero digits count         TRUE
 (b) certain digits + 1 estimated  TRUE
 (c) zero between non-zeros counts TRUE
 --------------------------------  ----
 none is complete on its own
 -> (d) All of the above
⚡ JEE Trick In "which is/are correct" sig-fig questions, if two statements are clearly textbook rules, check the third rather than stopping — "All of the above" is the setter's usual target. Distractors (a), (b), (c) are manufactured by offering true-but-incomplete fragments so a hasty reader ticks the first one they recognise.
Answer: (d) All of the above.

1Calculate the volume of oxygen required for the complete combustion of 0.5 dm³ of H₂S at STP.Combustion of H₂S — volumeVolume of O2 at STP for complete combustion🟡 Similar—
Concept For gases measured at the same temperature and pressure, Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes lets you skip moles entirely: volumes react in the very same whole-number ratio as the coefficients of the balanced equation. So balance the combustion, read the H₂S : O₂ ratio, and scale the given volume by it. No molar mass and no 22.4 L are needed.
Formula 2H₂S + 3O₂ → 2SO₂ + 2H₂O, hence V(O₂) = V(H₂S) × 3/2. Equal volumes of gases at the same T and P hold equal numbers of molecules (Avogadro), so the mole ratio is the volume ratio.
Solution
1. Balance the combustion: 2H₂S + 3O₂ → 2SO₂ + 2H₂O.Complete combustion sends S to SO₂ and H to H₂O — no free S or CO here.
2. Ratio from the coefficients: H₂S : O₂ = 2 : 3.
3. Both are gases at the same STP, so that mole ratio applies directly to volumes.Avogadro's law makes volume proportional to moles at fixed T and P.
4. V(O₂) = V(H₂S) × 3/2 = 0.5 × 3/2 = 0.75 dm³.
5. Check the ratio: 0.75 ÷ 0.5 = 1.5 = 3/2 ✓.
6. Note 1 dm³ = 1 L, so this is 750 mL of O₂ at STP.
   2 H2S    +   3 O2   ->  2 SO2  +  2 H2O
 -----------  ---------
 coefficient      2           3
 volume ratio     2      :    3     (same T, P)
 given         0.5 dm3        ?
 scale by 3/2  0.5 x 3/2 = 0.75 dm3
 -----------------------------------------
 V(O2) = 0.75 dm3 = 750 mL at STP
⚡ JEE Trick Gas-volume-to-gas-volume at the same T and P: multiply by the coefficient ratio and stop — converting to moles via 22.4 L wastes a minute and invites arithmetic slips. Common trap: using the 2 : 2 H₂S : SO₂ ratio, or inverting to 2/3 and reporting 0.33 dm³. The bigger coefficient must give the bigger volume.
Answer: V(O₂) = 0.75 dm³ of oxygen at STP.

Concept The mole is the bridge between a weighed mass and a countable number of particles. Divide mass by molar mass to get moles, then multiply moles by Avogadro's number to get molecules. The only real hazard here is the unit: the data is in milligrams, and 1 mg = 10⁻³ g, so convert before dividing — mixing mg with g/mol inflates the answer a thousandfold.
Formula n = w ÷ M and N = n × NA, with NA = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. M is defined in grams per mole, so w must be in grams before the division is meaningful.
Solution
1. Molar mass of CH₄ = 12 + 4(1) = 16 g mol⁻¹.
2. Convert the mass: 0.032 mg = 0.032 × 10⁻³ g = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ g.1 mg = 10⁻³ g; skipping this step multiplies your answer by 1000.

4. N = n × NA = 2 × 10⁻⁶ × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10¹⁸ molecules.Multiply the mantissas and add the exponents: 2 × 6.022 = 12.044, and 10⁻⁶⁺²³ = 10¹⁷.
5. So 12.044 × 10¹⁷ is rewritten in standard form as 1.204 × 10¹⁸.
6. Sanity check: the sample is a millionth of a mole, so N must be about 10⁻⁶ of 10²³ ✓.

Concept Atomic mass in u and molar mass in g mol⁻¹ are numerically identical, and Avogadro's number is the exchange rate between them. One mole contains NA atoms, so multiplying the mass of a single atom by NA gives the mass of one mole in grams — and that number, read as u, is the atomic mass. Watch the units cancel and the answer is forced.
Formula Atomic mass (u) = mass of one atom (g) × NA (mol⁻¹), with NA = 6.022 × 10²³. g atom⁻¹ × atom mol⁻¹ = g mol⁻¹, and molar mass in g mol⁻¹ equals atomic mass in u by definition of the unified mass unit.
Solution
1. Given: mass of one O atom = 26.56896 × 10⁻²⁴ g.
2. Multiply by NA: atomic mass = 26.56896 × 10⁻²⁴ × 6.022 × 10²³.One mole is NA atoms, so this is simply the mass of a mole of O atoms.
3. Mantissas: 26.56896 × 6.022 = 159.98 (≈ 160.0).
4. Exponents: 10⁻²⁴ × 10²³ = 10⁻¹, so the product = 159.98 × 10⁻¹ = 16.0 g mol⁻¹.
5. Units cancel as shown below, leaving g mol⁻¹ ≡ u.Numerically molar mass in g mol⁻¹ and atomic mass in u are the same number.
6. Atomic mass of oxygen = 16.0 u, matching the periodic table ✓.

     g        atoms        g
   ----- x  --------  =  -----  == u (numerically)
    atom       mol         mol

4In multiplication, the result has significant figures equal to: (a) Highest number (b) Smallest number among operands (c) Average (d) Power of 10Multiplication ruleMultiplication & Division Sig Fig Rules🟡 SimilarbSmallest number among operands
Concept Arithmetic cannot manufacture precision. A product is only as trustworthy as its shakiest factor, so the answer must carry the smallest sig-fig count among the operands — the weakest link sets the strength of the chain. Do not confuse this with the neighbouring rule: × and ÷ are governed by significant figures, while + and − are governed by decimal places.
Formula s.f.(product) = min{ s.f.(factor₁), s.f.(factor₂), … }, applied once at the very end. Relative errors add in multiplication, so the factor with the largest relative error — the one with fewest sig figs — dominates the result's relative error.
Solution
1. Take 2.5 (2 s.f.) × 3.42 (3 s.f.). The raw product is 8.55.
2. 2.5 really means 2.5 ± 0.05 — about 2% uncertainty; 3.42 means ± 0.005, about 0.15%.The 2 s.f. factor is ~13× sloppier, so it, not the 3 s.f. factor, controls the answer.
3. Product spread: 8.379 to 8.721 — the second digit is already unsure.
4. So the answer can support only 2 sig figs = the smallest count → 8.6.Reporting 8.55 would claim a third digit that the ±0.05 on 2.5 has already destroyed.
5. Smallest, not highest → option (b).
6. Option (a) "highest" would let a precise factor rescue a crude one — impossible. (c) "average" and (d) "power of 10" are not rules at all.
 factor | s.f. | uncertainty
 ----------------------------
 2.5    | 2    | +/-0.05  (~2%)   <- weakest link, WINS
 3.42   | 3    | +/-0.005 (~0.15%)
 spread: 2.45 x 3.42 = 8.379 .. 2.55 x 3.42 = 8.721
 2nd digit unsure -> only 2 s.f. survive
 2.5 x 3.42 = 8.55 -> min(2,3) = 2 s.f. -> 8.6

Concept Powers of ten obey the index laws, and a unit is handled exactly like an algebraic symbol carrying its own exponent. So m ÷ s is written m s⁻¹, and mass conversions are just decimal shifts: kg → g multiplies by 10³. Since this is MULTIPLE CORRECT, adjudicate every option independently — "all four are right" is a legitimate outcome and is often the intended one.
Formula 10a × 10b = 10a+b, 10a ÷ 10b = 10a−b. 10a is just a copies of ten multiplied together, so writing two such blocks side by side pools the factors — hence the exponents add.
Solution
1. (a) Multiplying like bases adds exponents: 10² × 10³ = 10⁵ ✔ correct.
2. (b) Dividing like bases subtracts exponents: 10⁵ ÷ 10² = 10³ ✔ correct.Dividing by 10² is multiplying by 10⁻², and −2 added to 5 gives 3 — the same law.
3. (c) Units carry exponents, ± alike: speed = m s⁻¹, density = kg m⁻³ ✔ correct.A negative exponent just puts the unit in the denominator; SI prefers m s⁻¹ to m/s.
4. (d) Convert: 1 metric ton = 1000 kg = 10³ kg ✔ first half holds.
5. Chain on: 10³ kg × 10³ g/kg = 103+3 = 10⁶ g ✔ — (d) is fully correct.
