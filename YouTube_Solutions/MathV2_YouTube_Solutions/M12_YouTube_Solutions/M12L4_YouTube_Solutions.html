1 (i)Fill in the blanks: The line drawn from the eye of the observer to the point on the object viewed by the observer is ______.line of sight definitionAngle of Elevation, Angle of Depression and Line of Sight🟡 SimilarLine of sight
Concept Every heights-and-distances problem starts with three objects: the observer's eye O, the object point P, and the horizontal line through O. The straight segment OP joining the eye to the point actually being looked at is the line of sight. All angles of elevation and depression are measured between this line and the horizontal, never between the line and the vertical. The trap is to call OP the "hypotenuse" or the "slant height": it only becomes a hypotenuse after you drop a perpendicular.
Formula line of sight = segment OP (eye → object); with horizontal distance d and height difference h, OP = √(d² + h²), tanθ = h/d. The vertical through P and the horizontal through O meet at a right angle, so OP closes a right triangle.
Solution
1. Put the observer's eye at O and the object point (top of a tower, a boat, a bird) at P.
2. Draw the straight line OP: it is the path along which light from P reaches the eye.
3. Draw the horizontal line through O; drop the vertical from P to meet it at N, so ∠ONP = 90°.
4. The angle θ = ∠PON between OP and ON is the angle of elevation (P above O) or depression (P below O).That angle exists only because OP is defined first.
5. Example: a 20 m tower seen from 20 m away gives OP = √(20² + 20²) = √800 = 20√2 m and tanθ = 20/20 = 1 ⇒ θ = 45°.
6. Hence the blank is filled by the name of OP: the line of sight.
 eye O, object P, foot N (ON horizontal, NP vertical)
 OP = line of sight
 theta = angle(OP, ON) -> elevation / depression
 -----------------------------------------
 blank = Line of sight           <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick In any figure, first locate the line of sight, then mark the horizontal through the eye: the angle sits between those two lines only. Trap: measuring θ from the vertical (that is the complement, 90° − θ) or from the line joining the feet when the observer is on a tower.
Answer: (i) Line of sight.

Concept When the object is below the observer (a boat seen from a cliff, a car seen from a tower), the observer must look down. The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal through the eye is then the angle of depression. The trap is to measure it at the object's end or from the vertical; it is always measured at the observer, from the horizontal, downward.
Formula tan(depression) = h/d, h = height of the observer above the object, d = horizontal distance. The horizontal through the eye is parallel to the ground, so by alternate angles the depression at the eye equals the elevation of the eye seen from the object.

Concept The line of sight is the straight line from the observer's eye to the point of the object being viewed. It is the reference line for every angle in heights and distances: elevation and depression are measured between the line of sight and the horizontal through the eye.
