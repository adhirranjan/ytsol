1 (i)Fill in the blanks: Multiplication of two straight lines is ______.Multiplication of two lines = a pairEquation of Pair of Straight Lines | Maths | Class 11/12/IIT-JEE🟡 SimilarPair of (straight) lines
Concept A single first-degree equation such as a1x+b1y+c1=0 stands for exactly one straight line. When two such linear expressions are multiplied and the product is set equal to zero, the new equation is satisfied precisely when one OR the other factor vanishes — so the single second-degree equation now carries two lines at once. That object is called a pair of straight lines, and it is the whole subject of this lecture.
Formula (a1x+b1y+c1)(a2x+b2y+c2)=0. A product is zero if and only if at least one factor is zero, so the locus of the product equation is line-1 together with line-2 and nothing else.
Solution
1. Take two straight lines L1: a1x+b1y+c1=0 and L2: a2x+b2y+c2=0. None
2. Multiply the left sides only after each right side is made 0, giving L1·L2=0. If a right side were non-zero the product rule would no longer pick out the two lines.
3. A point (x, y) satisfies L1L2=0 only when L1=0 or L2=0. That is the complete union of the two lines.
4. Multiplying two degree-1 factors gives a degree-2 equation — the tell-tale sign of a line-pair.
5. Conversely, any factorable degree-2 equation can be split back into its two component lines.
6. So ‘multiplication of two straight lines’ produces a single equation of a pair of straight lines.
7. Example: y=x and y=−x multiply to y2−x2=0, one equation holding both lines.
L1 = a1x+b1y+c1 = 0   (one line)
L2 = a2x+b2y+c2 = 0   (one line)
L1 * L2 = 0  <=>  L1 = 0  OR  L2 = 0
=> both lines together = a PAIR of lines
eg (y-x)(y+x) = y^2 - x^2 = 0
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a degree-2 equation factors into two linear pieces, read it as two lines drawn on one graph. The product-equals-zero form is the very definition of a line-pair, so look to factor first.
Answer: A pair of straight lines.

Concept To combine two separate lines into one ‘joint’ (combined) equation, bring every term of each line to the left so that the right side is 0, and then multiply the two left sides together. The single product equation then holds both lines jointly — this is the standard way to fuse two lines into one second-degree equation.
Formula Joint equation = (a1x+b1y+c1)(a2x+b2y+c2)=0. Only with each right side equal to 0 does the product vanish exactly on the union of the two lines; a left-over constant would shift the locus.
Solution
1. Write both lines with zero on the right: a1x+b1y+c1=0 and a2x+b2y+c2=0. None
2. Multiply the two left-hand sides to form a single expression set to 0. Zero-out each RHS first, otherwise the combined equation is wrong.
3. The result (a1x+b1y+c1)(a2x+b2y+c2)=0 is the required joint equation.
4. It is second degree in x and y and factors straight back into the two lines. Degree-2, because 1+1=2 on multiplying the degrees.
5. Worked check: y−x=0 and y+x=0 multiply to (y−x)(y+x)=y2−x2=0.
6. Another: (x+y−1)(x−y+2)=0 collapses two general lines into one equation.
7. So the blank is the product of the two line-expressions set to zero.

Concept This is the reverse of combining. Given a joint equation already written as a product of two linear factors, each factor equated to zero is one of the two separate lines. Factoring the quadratic simply undoes the multiplication that built the pair.
Formula (a1x+b1y+c1)(a2x+b2y+c2)=0 ⇒ a1x+b1y+c1=0 and a2x+b2y+c2=0. A product is zero if and only if one factor is zero, and each factor here is itself a straight line.
Solution
1. The joint form is the product (a1x+b1y+c1)(a2x+b2y+c2)=0. None
2. Set the first factor to zero: a1x+b1y+c1=0 — that is line one. Every point on this line makes the whole product zero.
3. Set the second factor to zero: a2x+b2y+c2=0 — that is line two.
4. Together these two equations are the separate (component) lines of the pair. No point off both lines can satisfy the product.
5. For a homogeneous ax2+2hxy+by2=0 you may instead divide by x2 and solve the quadratic in y/x.
6. Either route recovers the two slopes, hence the two lines, uniquely.
7. So the separate equations are exactly the two factors each equated to 0.
