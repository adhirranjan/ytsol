1 (i)Fill in the blank: 8 sin(x/8) cos(x/2) cos(x/4) cos(x/8) is equal to ______Cosine product telescoping to sin x8 sin(x/8) cos(x/2) cos(x/4) cos(x/8) = sin x🟢 Exactsin x
Concept The angles x/2, x/4, x/8 form a halving chain, and a sine of the smallest angle is supplied in front. Every application of sin2A = 2 sinA cosA merges that sine with the equal-angle cosine, doubles the angle inside the sine, and consumes one factor of 2 from the leading 8. Three cosines mean three doublings, and 8 = 2³ is exactly the number of 2s needed — the product telescopes cleanly to a single sine.
Formula sinA cosA = ½ sin2A — the double-angle formula sin2A = 2 sinA cosA read right-to-left. General form: cosθ cos2θ … cos2ⁿ⁻¹θ = sin(2ⁿθ)/(2ⁿ sinθ).
Solution
1. Reorder so the smallest angles sit side by side: 8 sin(x/8) cos(x/8) · cos(x/4) · cos(x/2).sin can only merge with the cosine of the SAME angle, so start at x/8.
2. First doubling: 8 [sin(x/8) cos(x/8)] = 8 · ½ sin(x/4) = 4 sin(x/4), leaving 4 sin(x/4) cos(x/4) cos(x/2).
3. Second doubling: 4 [sin(x/4) cos(x/4)] = 4 · ½ sin(x/2) = 2 sin(x/2) cos(x/2).
4. Third doubling: 2 sin(x/2) cos(x/2) = sin x — the chain terminates with no factor left over.
5. Numeric check at x = π/2: 8 sin(π/16) cos(π/4) cos(π/8) cos(π/16) = 8(0.19509)(0.70711)(0.92388)(0.98079) = 1.0000 = sin(π/2). ✓A one-point check catches any lost factor of 2 instantly.

This "form the ratio to kill the unwanted half-angle" step is the entire problem.
Formula sinα + sinβ = 2 sin((α+β)/2) cos((α−β)/2) and cosα − cosβ = −2 sin((α+β)/2) sin((α−β)/2). Note the leading minus on the cosine difference — cos is decreasing, so the larger angle gives the smaller cosine. Missing this sign is the single most common error here.
