1 (i)State whether the following are true or false.
The heat of formation of compounds is independent of the manner of its formation.		Hess's law — ΔHf is path-independentClass 11 Chapter 6 | Thermodynamics 08 || Hess's Law || Hess's Law Enthalpy Change IIT JEE / NEET |🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Enthalpy is a state function, so the enthalpy change between a fixed set of reactants and a fixed set of products depends only on those two states — never on the route taken between them. That is exactly what Hess's law asserts, and the heat of formation of a compound is a particular case of it.
Key relation ΔH = Hfinal − Hinitial, a state-function difference. Because H depends only on the state, any two paths joining the same two states must give the same ΔH — otherwise energy could be created by going one way and back the other.
Solution
1. The heat of formation is defined for one specific change: elements in their standard states → one mole of the compound.
2. Both ends of that change are fixed, so Hinitial and Hfinal are fixed numbers.Fixing both endpoints is what makes the difference between them a fixed number too.
3. Whether the compound is made in one step or through a dozen intermediates, the difference Hfinal − Hinitial is unchanged.
4. This is the content of Hess's law, and it is the reason enthalpies of formation can be tabulated at all — a table would be meaningless if the value depended on the method.
5. The practical payoff: enthalpies that cannot be measured directly (CO from C, for instance) are obtained by adding measurable steps.
6. Hence the statement is True.
 elements  ---- one step ---->  compound
    |                              ^
    |  step 1        step 2        |
    +----> X -------> Y -----------+
 both routes: dH = H(compound) - H(elements)
 same endpoints -> same dH  (state function)
⚡ JEE Trick Any true/false statement containing “independent of the path / manner / number of steps” applied to a state function (H, U, S, G) is True; the same words applied to heat q or work w in general would be False, because those are path functions. Sorting the quantity into the right box answers most of these in one second.
Answer: True — the heat of formation is path-independent (Hess's law).
1 (ii)The heat of reaction is independent of the time consumed in the process.Thermodynamics says nothing about rateHess's Law and Heats of Formation🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Thermodynamics compares two states; it says nothing whatever about how quickly the system travels between them. The heat of reaction is fixed by the initial and final states, so a reaction that takes a microsecond and one that takes a century release exactly the same enthalpy per mole. Rate is the business of kinetics, not thermodynamics.
Key relation ΔrH = ΣHproducts − ΣHreactants — no time variable appears anywhere in it. Time enters only through rate laws, which are entirely separate from the energetics.
Solution
1. Write the definition of the heat of reaction: it is the enthalpy difference between products and reactants, both in specified states.
2. Nothing in that definition refers to duration, rate constant or mechanism.A catalyst changes the mechanism and speeds the reaction enormously, yet leaves ΔH untouched — the standard illustration of this point.
3. Two familiar examples: rusting of iron takes years, burning of iron wool takes seconds, and both give the same ΔH per mole of Fe2O3 formed.
4. Similarly the combustion of glucose in a bomb calorimeter (seconds) and in a living cell (minutes, many steps) release identical enthalpy per mole.
5. So the heat of reaction is indeed independent of the time consumed.
6. Hence the statement is True.
 thermodynamics : WHERE you start and finish
 kinetics       : HOW FAST you get there
 --------------------------------------------
 dH depends on : states of reactants/products
 dH does NOT   : time, rate, catalyst, path
 --------------------------------------------
 rusting (years) and burning (seconds): same dH
⚡ JEE Trick Keep a mental partition: ΔH, ΔU, ΔS, ΔG and spontaneity belong to thermodynamics; rate, order, activation energy and catalysts belong to kinetics. Any statement that mixes the two — “a faster reaction releases more heat”, “a catalyst lowers ΔH” — is False, while “ΔH is independent of time” is True.
Answer: True — ΔH depends on the states, not on how long the change takes.

1What does Hess's Law state?
(a) Enthalpy change depends on the path of the reaction   (b) Enthalpy change is independent of intermediate steps   (c) Heat of formation is always positive   (d) Heat of reaction is time-dependent		Statement of Hess's lawClass 11 Chapter 6 | Thermodynamics 08 || Hess's Law || Hess's Law Enthalpy Change IIT JEE / NEET |🟡 SimilarbEnthalpy change is independent of intermediate steps
Concept Hess's law is a single statement: the enthalpy change for a reaction is the same whether it proceeds directly or through any sequence of intermediates. It is a consequence of enthalpy being a state function, and the three wrong options each contradict some part of that.
Key relation ΔrH = ΣΔHsteps, independent of the route. The law is really a bookkeeping identity: what matters is only where you start and where you finish.
Solution
1. Statement of the law: for a given overall change, ΔH is fixed by the initial and final states alone.
2. That is precisely option (b) — independent of intermediate steps.
3. (a) is the direct negation: if ΔH depended on the path, enthalpy would not be a state function and a perpetual-motion cycle could be built.Going out by the low-ΔH route and back by the high one would create energy from nothing — which is why (a) must be false.
4. (c) “heat of formation is always positive” is plainly false: most stable compounds have negative formation enthalpies, water and carbon dioxide among them.
5. (d) “heat of reaction is time-dependent” confuses thermodynamics with kinetics; ΔH contains no time variable at all.
6. So the answer is (b).
 Hess's law
 --------------------------------------------------

 (b) independent of steps -> THE LAW        <-- (b)
 (c) dfH always positive  -> false (H2O, CO2)

2In Hess's Law, what is true when a reaction occurs through multiple steps?
(a) Enthalpy change increases with steps   (b) Each step adds heat   (c) Total enthalpy change remains the same   (d) Intermediate enthalpies must be zero		Total ΔH is route-independentHess's Law | Hess's Law Examples | Hess's Law Numerical Problems🟡 SimilarcTotal enthalpy change remains the same
Concept Splitting a reaction into steps changes how the enthalpy is accounted for, not how much of it there is. The individual step enthalpies may be positive, negative or zero in any combination, but their sum is fixed at the overall ΔH. This is the additive form of Hess's law.
Key relation ΔHoverall = ΔH1 + ΔH2 + … + ΔHn. Adding steps redistributes the total between them; it cannot change the total.
Solution
1. Consider any multi-step route from the same reactants to the same products.
2. Each step has its own ΔH, and the steps chain together so that every intermediate appears once as a product and once as a reactant.Those intermediates cancel in the sum, which is exactly why only the endpoints survive.

Concept Bringing oppositely charged ions together from infinite separation into a crystal lattice always releases energy — the electrostatic attraction does the work. Concept Three equations combine into the formation of calcium hydroxide from its elements. Equation (ii) is written as the decomposition of water, so it must be reversed to supply water; the other two run forwards. Reversing (ii) turns its large positive value into a large negative one, which dominates the answer.
Key relation Target = (iii) − (ii) + (i). Reversing (ii) gives H2 + ½O2 → H2O with ΔH = −68.37 kcal — the formation of water.

1. Target: Ca(s) + O2(g) + H2(g) → Ca(OH)2(s).

4. (i) CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2, ΔH = −15.26 kcal — used as written.

Concept Converting a constant-pressure heat into a constant-volume one needs the change in the number of gas moles. Here water is produced as vapour, so twelve moles of gas are made from six — a large positive Δng. The gas expands, work is done on the surroundings, and ΔU must therefore be more negative than ΔH.
Key relation ΔU = ΔH − ΔngRT. Glucose is a solid, so only the six O2 count on the left; on the right both the CO2 and the water vapour count.
Solution
1. Equation: C6H12O6(s) + 6O2(g) → 6CO2(g) + 6H2O(g), ΔH = −2721.18 kJ at 290 K.
2. Count gas moles: products 6 + 6 = 12, reactants 6 (the glucose is solid). So Δng = 12 − 6 = +6.Water as vapour is the crux — had it been liquid, Δng would be zero and ΔU would equal ΔH.

Concept Disproportionation means one species turning into two others, one more oxidised and one more reduced. Here two moles of MgCl(s) give one mole of magnesium metal and one mole of MgCl2. With formation enthalpies for all three species, the reaction enthalpy is an ordinary products-minus-reactants sum.
Key relation 2MgCl(s) → Mg(s) + MgCl2(s), ΔH = [ΔfH(Mg) + ΔfH(MgCl2)] − 2ΔfH(MgCl). Magnesium metal is an element in its standard state, so its formation enthalpy is zero.

3. Reactants: 2 × (−125) = −250 kJ.
4. Subtract: ΔH = −642 − (−250) = −642 + 250 = −392 kJ.
5. Match the printed form: −49x = −392, so x = 8.
