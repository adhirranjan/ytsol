M15L3VSuggQ1Position of a point
🟡 SimilarCIRCLE 1- | GENERAL…
(c) CP > r
Concept Position of a point P with respect to a circle of centre C and radius r is decided by comparing the distance CP with r.
Formula CP > r (outside), CP = r (on), CP < r (inside). S1 = x1² + y1² + 2gx1 + 2fy1 + c has the same sign as CP² − r²
Solution
1. Let the circle have centre C and radius r. Every point on it is at distance exactly r from C.

3. If CP < r then P is closer to the centre than the boundary, so P is inside (option b). the circle is the set of points at distance r
4. If CP > r then P is farther than the boundary, so P is outside (option c). S1 > 0 in coordinates
5. If CP = 0 then P is the centre itself, which is inside (option d).
6. Hence only CP > r describes an outside point.
⚡ JEE Trick In coordinates just substitute P in S = 0: positive means outside, zero on, negative inside.
Answer: (c) CP > r

Concept A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so the centre lies on the normal to the tangent through the contact point. The second centre-line then fixes the centre.
Formula normal at (2,5): slope = −1/(slope of tangent). the radius to a point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent
Solution

Find the equation of the circle

2. The radius through (2,5) has slope −(1/2): y − 5 = −(1/2)(x − 2), i.e. x + 2y = 12. perpendicular to the tangent
3. Centre also lies on x + y = 9; subtracting, y = 3, so x = 6. intersection of the two centre-lines

Concept The chord of contact from an external point fixes the point of tangency. A chord of a circle subtends a definite angle at the centre; when AB = r√2 the angle is 90°.
Formula chord of contact: xx1 + yy1 = r2; chord AB with OA · OB = 0. T = 0 from P(x1,y1) passes through both contact points
Solution
Given the point P(4, 0) and the circle x² + y² = 8 with tangent touching at A in the first quadrant, AB = 4
Find the coordinates of B
1. Circle radius r = 2√2, r2 = 8. Chord of contact from P(4,0): 4x = 8, so x = 2. T = 0 for P(4,0)
2. On x = 2: 4 + y2 = 8 gives y = ±2; first quadrant, so A = (2, 2). A lies on the circle and the chord of contact
3. Check tangency: PA = (−2, 2), OA = (2, 2); dot product = −4 + 4 = 0. OA ⊥ PA as required
4. Let B = (x, y) on the circle with |B − A|2 = 16. AB = 4
5. |B|2 + |A|2 − 2A·B = 16 gives 8 + 8 − 2A·B = 16, so A·B = 0. law of cosines in vectors; the chord subtends 90° at O
6. A·B = 0 means 2x + 2y = 0, so y = −x. B is perpendicular to OA
7. x2 + x2 = 8 gives x = ±2, so B = (2,−2) or (−2, 2).
⚡ JEE Trick AB = r√2 means a right angle at the centre, so B is just A rotated by ±90°: (2,2) → (−2,2) or (2,−2). Note B = (2,−2) is the other contact point of the tangents from P.

The range of values of r for which the point (−5 + r/√2, −3 + r/√2) is an interior point of the major segment of the circle x² + y² = 16 cut off by the line x + y = 2 is: (a) (−∞, 5√2)  (b) (4√2−√14, 5√2)  (c) (4√2−√14, 4√2+√14)  (d) none
M15L3V6Q3Interior of a major segment
🟢 ExactThe range of values…
(b) (4√2 − √14, 5√2)
Concept A point is interior to the major segment if it lies inside the circle and on the same side of the chord line as the centre. The given point moves along a 45° line with parameter r.
Formula point = (−5,−3) + (r/√2)(1,1); inside: x2+y2 < 16; side of centre: x + y < 2. distance r measured along direction (1/√2, 1/√2) from (−5,−3)
Solution
Given the point (−5 + r/√2, −3 + r/√2), the circle x² + y² = 16 and the line x + y = 2 cutting off a major segment
Find the range of r for an interior point of the major segment
1. Put t = r/√2, so the point is (t−5, t−3). one-parameter line
2. Centre O gives 0 + 0 = 0 < 2, so the major segment lies on x + y < 2. the larger part holds the centre
3. x + y = 2t − 8 < 2 gives t < 5, i.e. r < 5√2. side condition
4. Inside the circle: (t−5)2 + (t−3)2 < 16 gives 2t2 − 16t + 18 < 0, i.e. t2 − 8t + 9 < 0. expanded: 2t²−16t+34−16
5. Roots t = 4 ± √7, so 4 − √7 < t < 4 + √7. quadratic formula
6. Multiply by √2: 4√2 − √14 < r < 4√2 + √14 (about 2.51 < r < 9.40). r = √2 t
7. Intersect with r < 5√2 (about 7.07): the binding upper limit is 5√2, since 4√2 + √14 > 5√2. Range (4√2 − √14, 5√2).
⚡ JEE Trick Two inequalities, two bounds: take the tighter upper bound and the circle's lower bound. Choosing option (c) is the trap: it ignores the line and gives the whole interior of the circle.
Answer: (b) (4√2 − √14, 5√2)

A line through P(1, −2) cuts the circle x² + y² − x − y = 0 at points A and B. The maximum value of PA + PB is: (a) √26  (b) 8  (c) √8  (d) 2√8

🟡 SimilarA line is drawn thro…
(a) √26
Concept For a secant through P cutting the circle at A and B, PA and PB lie on the same side when P is outside, so PA + PB = |t1 + t2| for the parametric roots. The sum is a sinusoid in the direction angle, which gives the maximum.
Formula x = 1 + t cosθ, y = −2 + t sinθ: t2 + (cosθ − 5 sinθ)t + 6 = 0. substitute the line in parametric (distance) form into the circle
Solution
Given a line through P(1, −2) cutting the circle x² + y² − x − y = 0 at A and B
Find the maximum value of PA + PB
1. Power of P: S1 = 1 + 4 − 1 + 2 = 6 > 0, so P is outside and PA · PB = 6. both intersections on the same side of P
2. Take the line x = 1 + t cosθ, y = −2 + t sinθ. t is signed distance from P
3. x2 + y2 = 5 + 2t cosθ − 4t sinθ + t2 and −x − y = 1 − t cosθ − t sinθ. expanding each part
4. Add: t2 + (cosθ − 5 sinθ)t + 6 = 0. constant term 6 = power of P
5. t1t2 = 6 > 0, so t1, t2 have the same sign and PA + PB = |t1 + t2| = |5 sinθ − cosθ|. sum of roots is −(coefficient of t)
6. Max of |5 sinθ − cosθ| is √(25 + 1) = √26. amplitude of a sinθ + b cosθ
7. Reality needs discriminant (cosθ − 5 sinθ)2 ≥ 24, and √26 gives 26 ≥ 24, so this maximum is attainable; PA + PB max = √26.
⚡ JEE Trick PA + PB is the sum of roots in the distance parameter, an amplitude problem. Always check that the maximising direction still cuts the circle (discriminant ≥ 0).
Answer: (a) √26

(c) 4 + 2√2
Concept The centre of a circle touching a line at a given point lies on the normal to the line there; with the centre on the x-axis this fixes it. The largest x-coordinate on a circle is centre-x plus radius.
Formula max α = h + r. the rightmost point of a circle is (h + r, k)
Solution
Given a circle with centre on the x-axis touching the line x + y = 0 at (2, −2); (α, β) on the circle
Find the greatest value of α
1. Line x + y = 0 has slope −1, so the radius to (2,−2) has slope 1: y + 2 = x − 2, i.e. y = x − 4. normal is perpendicular to the tangent
2. Centre on the x-axis (y = 0): x = 4, so centre (4, 0). intersect normal with y = 0
3. Radius r = distance to (2,−2) = √(4 + 4) = 2√2. contact point lies on the circle
4. Cross-check by distance to the line: |4 + 0|/√2 = 2√2. tangent distance = radius
5. Circle: (x−4)2 + y2 = 8, so any point has (α−4)2 ≤ 8. y² ≥ 0
6. Hence α ≤ 4 + 2√2, attained at (4 + 2√2, 0). rightmost point
7. Numerically 4 + 2.83 = 6.83, which exceeds option (b) 6, so (c).
⚡ JEE Trick Greatest x on any circle = h + r; never solve with calculus. Verify r by both the contact point and the tangent-distance formula.
Answer: (c) 4 + 2√2

The number of integral values of y for which the chord of the circle x² + y² = 125 passing through P(8, y) is bisected at P(8, y) and has an integral slope is: (a) 8  (b) 6  (c) 4  (d) 2
M15L3V6Q6Bisected chord with an integral slope
🟡 SimilarChord Bisected at a…
(b) 6
Concept A chord of x2 + y2 = a2 bisected at P is perpendicular to OP, and such a chord exists only when P lies strictly inside the circle. Integral slope then restricts y.
Formula slope of chord = −1/(slope of OP) = −x1/y1; need x12 + y12 < a2. the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord
Solution
Given the circle x² + y² = 125 and the point P(8, y)
Find the number of integral y for which the chord through P is bisected at P with integral slope
1. P(8, y) must be interior: 64 + y2 < 125, so y2 < 61, |y| ≤ 7 for integers. a chord bisected at P needs P inside
2. OP has slope y/8, so the chord has slope m = −8/y. chord ⊥ OP
3. y = 0 gives a vertical chord, slope undefined, so y ≠ 0. slope must be an integer
4. m = −8/y is an integer exactly when y divides 8: y ∈ {±1, ±2, ±4, ±8}. divisors of 8
5. Remove ±8 since |y| ≤ 7 (64 + 64 = 128 > 125). interior condition
6. Left: y = 1, −1, 2, −2, 4, −4 giving slopes −8, 8, −4, 4, −2, 2. each integral
7. That is 6 integral values of y.
⚡ JEE Trick Two filters decide the count: the interior inequality (this kills ±8) and y ≠ 0 (vertical chord). The trap answer 8 forgets the first filter.
Answer: (b) 6

If a circle of radius r touches the lines x² − 4xy + y² = 0 in the first quadrant at points A and B, then the area of triangle OAB (O being the origin) is: (a) 3√3·r²/4  (b) √3·r²/4  (c) 3r²/4  (d) r²
M15L3V6Q7Area from a pair of tangent lines
🟡 SimilarCircles 09 | Pair of…
(a) 3√3·r²/4
Concept A homogeneous pair x2 − 4xy + y2 = 0 is two lines through O. A circle touching both is inscribed in the angle between them; O is the external point and A, B are the contact points of the two tangents.
Formula tangent length OA = OB = r cot(φ/2), area = (1/2)·OA·OB sinφ. two tangents from one point are equal; φ is the angle between them
Solution
Given a circle of radius r touching the lines x² − 4xy + y² = 0 in the first quadrant at A and B
Find the area of triangle OAB, O the origin
1. Slopes: m2 − 4m + 1 = 0 gives m = 2 ± √3 = tan 75° and tan 15°. divide the pair by x²
2. Angle between lines φ = 75° − 15° = 60°. first quadrant: both slopes positive
3. Check by formula: tan φ = 2√(h2−ab)/|a+b| = 2√(4−1)/2 = √3, so φ = 60°. a=1, b=1, h=−2
4. The centre sits on the bisector, with OC sin 30° = r, so OC = 2r. right triangle OAC, angle at O is φ/2 = 30°
5. OA = OB = r/tan 30° = r√3. tangent length = r cot(φ/2)
6. Area = (1/2)(OA)(OB) sin 60° = (1/2)(3r2)(√3/2). two sides and the included angle
7. Area = 3√3 r2/4.
⚡ JEE Trick Get the angle from tanφ = 2√(h²−ab)/|a+b|, then tangent length = r cot(φ/2). Do not fall for option (b), which is the triangle area for the wrong angle.
Answer: (a) 3√3·r²/4

(b) x² + y² = (4/3)r²
Concept Tangents at parameters α and β meet at a point on the line x cos((α+β)/2) + y sin((α+β)/2) = r cos((α−β)/2). Its distance from the centre is fixed by the angle difference, so the locus is a concentric circle.
Formula OP = r / cos(Δ/2), Δ = difference of parameters. pole of the chord joining the two points lies along the bisector direction
Solution
Given tangents to x = r cos α, y = r sin α at points whose parametric angles differ by π/3
Find the locus of their intersection point
1. Take tangents at α and α + π/3: x cosα + y sinα = r and x cos(α+π/3) + y sin(α+π/3) = r. tangent at the parametric point
2. Their intersection P lies on the bisector direction α + π/6 (by symmetry). equal tangent lengths
3. Let A be the point of contact at angle α; then angle AOP = π/6 and OA = r. OP bisects the angle between the two radii
4. Triangle OAP has a right angle at A, so OA = OP cos(π/6). radius ⊥ tangent
5. So OP = r / cos(π/6) = 2r/√3. distance of the external point
6. OP2 = 4r2/3, independent of α. fixed distance gives a circle
7. Locus of P: x2 + y2 = (4/3)r2.
⚡ JEE Trick For tangents at parametric angles differing by θ, the locus is x² + y² = r² sec²(θ/2). For θ = π/3: sec²(π/6) = 4/3. Memorise this form.
Answer: (b) x² + y² = (4/3)r²

(Multiple Correct) A circle passes through the point (3, √(7/2)) and touches the line pair x² − y² − 2x + 1 = 0. The coordinates of the centre of the circle are: (a) (4, 0)  (b) (5, 0)  (c) (6, 0)  (d) (0, 4)
M15L3V6Q10Circle touching a line pair
🟢 ExactA circle passes thro…
(a) (4, 0) and (c) (6, 0)
Concept A circle touching two intersecting lines has its centre on one of their angle bisectors, with radius equal to the distance from the centre to either line. Impose that it passes through the given point.
Formula line pair (x−1)2 = y2: x − y − 1 = 0 and x + y − 1 = 0. factor the pair; bisectors are y = 0 and x = 1
Solution
Given a circle through (3, √(7/2)) touching the line pair x² − y² − 2x + 1 = 0
Find the possible centres of the circle
1. x2 − y2 − 2x + 1 = (x−1)2 − y2 = 0, i.e. lines y = x − 1 and y = 1 − x, meeting at (1, 0). difference of squares
2. Angle bisectors of these perpendicular lines: y = 0 and x = 1. symmetric about both
3. Centre (h, 0): r = |h − 1|/√2, so r2 = (h−1)2/2. distance to x − y − 1 = 0
4. Through (3, √(7/2)): (3−h)2 + 7/2 = (h−1)2/2. distance from centre = radius
5. Multiply by 2: 2(9 − 6h + h2) + 7 = h2 − 2h + 1, so h2 − 10h + 24 = 0, giving h = 4 or 6. collect terms
6. Centre on x = 1, (1, k): 4 + (√(7/2) − k)2 = k2/2 gives k2 − 4√(7/2)k + 15 = 0, discriminant 56 − 60 < 0, no real k. no circle on that bisector
7. So centres are (4, 0) and (6, 0); (5, 0) fails and (0, 4) is on neither bisector.
⚡ JEE Trick Always split into the two bisector families and test both; one family can be empty. Checking options (b) and (d) by substitution is a fast sanity test.
Answer: (a) (4,0) and (c) (6,0)
