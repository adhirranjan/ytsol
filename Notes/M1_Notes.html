L-1

Angles, Quadrants & Systems of Measurement

  • Angle = figure obtained by rotating a ray about its end point. Parts: two arms and a vertex (common end point).
  • Initial side = starting position; terminal side = final position; the revolving ray is the generating line.
  • Sense: anticlockwise rotation → positive angle; clockwise → negative angle.
  • Standard position: vertex at origin O, initial side along positive x-axis (OX).

Quadrants of the terminal side

QuadrantRange of θ
I0° < θ < 90°
II90° < θ < 180°
III180° < θ < 270°
IV270° < θ < 360°
  • Quadrantal angle = terminal side lies on an axis: θ = 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 360° (0, right, straight, reflex-boundary, complete).
  • Co-terminal angles = same initial & terminal sides, different measures (e.g. 45° and −315° differ by 360°). They always differ by an integral multiple of 360° (2π).
  • Triangle of reference: drop a perpendicular PM from a point P on the terminal side to the x-axis; right triangle OMP is the reference triangle of ∠XOP.

Three systems of measurement

sexagesimal (English)1 right angle = 90° ; 1° = 60′ ; 1′ = 60″
centesimal (French)1 right angle = 100g ; 1g = 100′ ; 1′ = 100″
circular (radian)1 radian (1c) = angle subtended at the centre by an arc of length = radius
Why a radian is a constant angle
For a circle radius r, cut arc AP = r so ∠AOP = 1c. Produce AO to B (∠AOB = straight angle). Since central angles ∝ their arcs: ∠AOP/∠AOB = arc AP / arc APB = r / (πr) = 1/π. Hence 1c = (straight angle)/π = 180°/π — a fixed quantity, independent of r.
degree ↔ radianπ radian = 180° ⇒ 1c = 180°/π ≈ 57° 16′ 22″ ; 1° = π/180 rad
arc–angleθ = s/r radian (s = arc length, r = radius) ⇒ s = r θ
relation between all threeD/90° = 2R/π = G/100g (D degrees, R radians, G grades)
Deriving D/90 = 2R/π = G/100
Express one angle θ in each system as a fraction of a right angle: θ = D/90 right angles (since 1° = 1/90 R.A.); θ = 2R/π right angles (since π rad = 2 R.A. ⇒ 1 rad = 2/π R.A.); θ = G/100 right angles (since 1g = 1/100 R.A.). Equate the three ⇒ D/90 = 2R/π = G/100.
★ Note the θ ladderθ = 0° zero angle · 90° right · 180° straight · 270° reflex (between 180° & 360°) · 360° complete angle.
💡 TipTo convert a raw decimal degree to D° M′ S″, take the fractional degree × 60 for minutes, then the fractional minute × 60 for seconds. Use π = 22/7 in these conversions.
⚠ TrapGrades and minutes/seconds are not the same between English & French systems — French 1′ = (1/100)g, English 1′ = (1/60)°. Keep the systems separate.

Book Example · L-1
Find the length of an arc of a circle of radius 5 cm subtending a central angle measuring 15°.
Convert: θ = 15° = 15 × π/180 = (π/12)c.
θ = s/r ⇒ π/12 = s/5 ⇒ s = 5π/12 cm.
arc s = 5π/12 cm
Book Example · L-1
The angles of a triangle are in A.P. The number of degrees in the least angle is to the number of radians in the greatest angle as 60 : π. Find the angles in degrees.
Let the angles be (a − d)°, a°, (a + d)°. Sum = 180° ⇒ 3a = 180 ⇒ a = 60. Least = (60 − d)°, greatest = (60 + d)°.
Greatest in radians = (60 + d)(π/180). Given (60 − d) / [(60 + d)(π/180)] = 60/π.
⇒ 180(60 − d)/[60(60 + d)] = 60/60 ⇒ 3(60 − d) = (60 + d) ⇒ 180 − 3d = 60 + d ⇒ 4d = 120 ⇒ d = 30.
Angles are 30°, 60°, 90°
Book Example · L-1
Arcs of the same length in two circles subtend angles of 60° and 75° at their centres. Find the ratio of their radii.
60° = (π/3)c, 75° = (5π/12)c. Using θ = s/r with equal arc s: (π/3)r₁ = s and (5π/12)r₂ = s.
⇒ (π/3)r₁ = (5π/12)r₂ ⇒ 4r₁ = 5r₂ ⇒ r₁/r₂ = 5/4.
r₁ : r₂ = 5 : 4

Polygons, Sectors of a Circle & Clock Angles

Angles of a polygon

sum of interior anglesS = (n − 2) × 180° (n = number of sides)
each interior angle (regular)= [180(n − 2)]/n = [180n − 360]/n = (2n − 4)/n right angles
interior ↔ exteriorinterior + exterior = 180° ; Σ exterior angles = 360° (any convex polygon)
sides from exterior angle (regular)n = 360° / (each exterior angle)
  • Convex polygon: every interior angle < 180°. Concave: at least one interior angle > 180°.
  • Triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°, pentagon 540°, hexagon 720°, heptagon 900°, octagon 1080°, nonagon 1260°, decagon 1440°.
Why Σ exterior = 360°
At each vertex, interior + exterior = 180° (linear pair). For n vertices the total of all linear pairs = 180n. Subtract the interior sum: Σ exterior = 180n − (n − 2)180 = 180n − 180n + 360 = 360° — independent of n.

Sector of a circle

arc lengthℓ = (θ/360°) × 2πr = (θπr)/180° = r θ (θ in radian)
perimeter of sectorP = 2r + ℓ = 2r + r θ (θ in radian)
area of sectorA = (θ/360°) × πr² = ½ r² θ (θ in radian) = ½ ℓ r
💡 HandyWhen you know arc length ℓ and radius r, the fastest area formula is A = ℓr/2 — no θ needed.

Clock angles

hour hand12 h = 360° ⇒ 1 h = 30° ; 1 minute → hour hand moves (½)°
minute hand60 min = 360° ⇒ 1 minute = 6°
★ Between two digitsThe angle between two consecutive hour-marks on a clock is 30° (= π/6 radian).
⚠ TrapAt H : M the hour hand has already moved past the hour mark by (½ × M)°. Angle = |30H − 5.5M|° — don't forget the hour hand drifts as the minutes pass.

A circular arc whose radius is 12 cm makes an angle of 30° at the centre. Find the perimeter of the sector formed. (Use π = 3.14.)
θ = 30° = 30 × π/180 = π/6. Perimeter P = 2r + rθ = 2(12) + 12(π/6) = 24 + 2π.

If the angle of the sector with radius 4 units is 45°, find the area and length of the sector. (Use π = 22/7.)
Area = (θ/360°)πr² = (45/360)(22/7)(4)(4) = (1/8)(22/7)(16) = 44/7 square units.
Length ℓ = (θ/360°) × 2πr = (45/360)(2)(22/7)(4) = 22/7.

Book Example · L-2
Express the angle of a regular decagon in degrees, grades and radians.
Each interior angle = (2n − 4)/n right angles = (2×10 − 4)/10 = 8/5 right angles.
Degrees: (8/5)(90°) = 144°. Grades: (8/5)(100g) = 160g. Radians: (8/5)(π/2) = 4π/5.
144° = 160g = (4π/5)c
Book Example · L-2
The moon's distance from the earth is 360000 km and its diameter subtends an angle of 31′ at the eye of the observer. Find the diameter of the moon. (Use π = 22/7.)
Take diameter d as arc AB, r = 360000 km. θ = 31′ = (31/60)° = (31/60)(π/180) rad.
θ = arc/radius ⇒ d = θ·r = (31/60)(π/180)(360000) = (31π/108)(3600) = (31 × 22 × 3600)/(7 × 108).
= 2455200/756 = 3247.619…
Diameter of the moon ≈ 3247.62 km
Book Example · L-2
The minute hand of a watch is 1.5 cm long. How far does its tip move in 40 minutes? (Use π = 3.14.)
Minute hand turns 6° per minute ⇒ in 40 min it turns (40 × 6)° = 240° = 240 × π/180 = (4π/3)c.
arc = rθ = 1.5 × (4π/3) = 2π = 2 × 3.14.
Tip travels 6.28 cm
Book Example · L-2
Find the angle between the minute hand and the hour hand of a clock when the time is 7 : 20 a.m.
Hour hand in 12 h = 360°; at 7 h 20 min = 22/3 h ⇒ angle = (360/12)(22/3) = 220°.
Minute hand in 20 min = (360/60)(20) = 120°.
Required angle = 220° − 120°.
