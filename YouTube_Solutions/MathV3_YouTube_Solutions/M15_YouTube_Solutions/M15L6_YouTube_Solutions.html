Fill in the blanks: When the distance between the centres of two circles is equal to the sum of their radii then the two circles touch each other ______.
M15L6VRecapQ1iCondition for external touch
🟡 SimilarNumber of common tan…
externally
Concept Two circles with centres C1, C2 and radii r1, r2 are classified by comparing the distance between centres with r1+r2 and |r1−r2|.
Formula C1C2 = r1+r2 ⇒ external touch. The circles meet at exactly one point lying between the centres, on the segment C1C2.
Solution
1. Let the distance between centres be d = C1C2 and the radii r1, r2. Standard set-up.
2. Given d = r1+r2. This is the stated condition.
3. Walk from C1 a distance r1 along C1C2; you land exactly on the boundary of circle 2 after a further r2. Both boundaries pass through that one point P.
4. Any other point of circle 1 is farther than r2 from C2 (triangle inequality), so P is the only common point. Exactly one common point means touching.
5. The circles lie on opposite sides of the common tangent at P, i.e. each is outside the other. Opposite sides = external contact.
6. Contrast: d = |r1−r2| would give internal touch, with one circle inside the other. That is a different case.
⚡ JEE Trick Sum of radii = d ⇒ external; difference of radii = d ⇒ internal. The blank wants one word: “externally”.
Answer: externally
1 (ii)
Fill in the blanks: When two circles neither touch nor intersect each other then the total number of common tangents is equal to ______.
M15L6VRecapQ1iiTangents to two separate circles
🟡 SimilarNumber of common tan…
4
Concept The number of common tangents depends on the relative position: separate 4, external touch 3, intersecting 2, internal touch 1, one inside the other 0.
Formula d > r1+r2 ⇒ 4 common tangents. The standard textbook phrase “neither touch nor intersect” describes two circles lying completely outside each other (d > r1+r2).
Solution
1. Two circles that neither touch nor intersect are either separate or one inside the other. These are the only non-meeting cases.
2. For the nested case no line can be tangent to both, so that case would give 0. A tangent to the inner circle cuts the outer one.
3. The question uses the standard phrasing for two circles lying outside each other, so take d > r1+r2. Separate circles.
4. For separate circles there are 2 direct (external) common tangents. They do not cross between the circles.
5. There are also 2 transverse (internal) common tangents, crossing between the circles. Possible because the circles are apart.
6. Total = 2 + 2 = 4. Add direct and transverse tangents.
⚡ JEE Trick Memory ladder by decreasing d: 4, 3, 2, 1, 0. Separate circles give the maximum, 4.
Answer: 4

Fill in the blanks: When one circle lies completely inside the other circle then the number of common tangents is equal to ______.
M15L6VRecapQ1iiiTangents, one circle inside other
🟡 SimilarNumber of common tan…
0 (zero)
Concept If one circle lies completely inside another, the inner circle's tangent always cuts the outer circle, so no line can touch both.
Formula d < |r1−r2| ⇒ 0 common tangents. A tangent to the inner circle passes through interior points of the outer one, so it cannot be tangent there.
Solution
1. Let the larger circle have radius R and the inner one radius r, with d < R−r. Complete nesting condition.
2. Every point of the inner circle lies strictly inside the outer circle. Since d + r < R.
3. A tangent to the inner circle touches it at a point strictly inside the outer circle. That point is an interior point of the outer disc.
4. A line through an interior point of a circle meets that circle in two points. So it is a secant, not a tangent, of the outer circle.
5. Hence no line is simultaneously tangent to both circles. Contradiction for every candidate line.
6. Number of common tangents = 0. Concentric circles are the special case d = 0.
⚡ JEE Trick Count by position: 4 (apart), 3 (ext. touch), 2 (cross), 1 (int. touch), 0 (nested).
Answer: 0 (zero)

Concept The relative position of two circles is decided by d = C1C2 against r1+r2 and |r1−r2|.

Concept The lines joining the origin to the intersection points of a line and a curve are found by homogenising the curve with the line (chord) equation.
Formula homogenise: x2+y2 = 16((x+y)/4)2. Writing the constant 16 as 16·(line/4)2 makes every term degree 2: a pair of lines through the origin.
Solution
Given the circles x² + y² − 4x − 4y = 0 and x² + y² = 16
Find the angle the common chord subtends at the origin
1. Common chord: subtract the circles: (x2+y2−4x−4y)−(x2+y2−16) = 0 ⇒ x+y=4. Radical axis.
2. Rewrite as (x+y)/4 = 1, a homogenising factor equal to 1 on the chord. 1 on the chord.

Concept The radical centre is the point having equal power with respect to all three circles. For circles on the sides of a triangle as diameters, the altitudes are the radical axes, so the radical centre is the orthocentre.
Formula Radical axis of two circles = locus of points of equal power; three radical axes concur at the radical centre. A foot of an altitude sees the side at 90 degrees, so it lies on the circle with that side as diameter (angle in a semicircle).
Solution
1. Triangle ABC, altitudes AD, BE, CF meeting at H. The circles have diameters BC, CA, AB.
2. Angle ADB = 90° and angle ADC = 90°, so D lies on the circles with diameters AB and AC. A right angle at D puts D on the circle whose diameter is the hypotenuse.

7. Options (b),(c),(d) fail: circumcentre, incentre and centroid do not lie on all three altitudes in general.

M15L6V6Q6Concentric circles & chord of contact
🟡 Similarvideo pending
(a) x²+y²=ab
Concept Chord PQ of circle C is the chord of contact of the external point T where the tangents at P and Q meet. Since PQ touches the circle x2+y2=a2, the distance of PQ from O is a; and the pole T of PQ lies at distance ρ2/a from O.
Formula pole of a line at distance p from O w.r.t. x2+y2=ρ2 lies at distance ρ2/p. T(x1,y1) has polar x x1+y y1=ρ2, at distance ρ2/|OT| from O; so |OT| = ρ2/p.
Solution
Given a tangent to x² + y² = a² meeting a concentric circle C at P and Q, whose tangents at P and Q meet on x² + y² = b²
Find the equation of circle C
1. Let C: x2+y2=ρ2 (concentric with the circle of radius a). Concentric means the same centre O, only a different radius.
2. Tangents to C at P and Q meet at T, so PQ is the polar (chord of contact) of T: x x1+y y1=ρ2. The chord joining the contact points is the polar of the external point.
3. Distance of this chord from O is ρ2/√(x12+y12) = ρ2/OT. Distance formula for the line x x1+y y1−ρ2=0 from the origin.
4. PQ is a tangent to x2+y2=a2, so its distance from O equals a: ρ2/OT = a. A tangent line lies at distance equal to the radius.
5. T lies on x2+y2=b2, so OT = b. Given in the problem.
6. Hence ρ2/b = a ⇒ ρ2 = ab. Eliminate OT between the two relations.
7. Therefore C is x2+y2=ab, option (a). (Consistent: a<ρ<b needs a<b, as for any pole outside C.) Chord PQ exists only if a<ρ, and T is outside C when b>ρ.
⚡ JEE Trick This is the geometric-mean pattern: distance(chord) × distance(its pole) = (radius)2, so the middle radius squared is the product a·b. Quick check: with a=b the circle collapses to x2+y2=a2.
Answer: (a) x2+y2=ab

M15L6V6Q7Distance from a centre to a common chord

(c) 2
Concept The common chord of two intersecting circles lies on their radical axis S1−S2=0. The required length is just the perpendicular distance from the centre (1,0) of the circle x2+y2=2x to that line.
Formula radical axis S1−S2=0; distance = |Ax0+By0+C|/√(A2+B2). Subtracting the equations cancels x2+y2, leaving the line through the two common points.
Solution
Given the circle x² + y² = 2x and the circles x² + y² + 5x − 8y + 1 = 0 and x² + y² − 3x + 7y − 25 = 0
Find the distance from the centre of the first circle to their common chord
1. S1: x2+y2+5x−8y+1=0 and S2: x2+y2−3x+7y−25=0. Both have unit coefficient on the square terms.
2. S1−S2: (5+3)x + (−8−7)y + (1+25) = 0 ⇒ 8x−15y+26=0. Subtract term by term: x: 5−(−3)=8; y: −8−7=−15; constant 1−(−25)=26.
3. Rewrite x2+y2=2x as (x−1)2+y2=1, so the centre is (1,0). Complete the square on x.
4. Distance from (1,0) to 8x−15y+26=0: |8(1)−15(0)+26| = 34. Substitute the point into the line's left side.
5. Denominator: √(82+152) = √(64+225) = √289 = 17. 8-15-17 is a Pythagorean triple.
6. So the distance = 34/17 = 2. Option (c).
7. Options (a), (b) and (d) do not equal 34/17; only (c) matches. The distance must be taken from the centre (1,0), not from the origin.
⚡ JEE Trick Never compute the intersection points: radical axis plus a point-to-line distance does it in two lines. Watch that the circle in the question (x2+y2=2x) is a third circle, not one of the pair.
Answer: (c) 2

(b) 3+2√2
Concept Circle C2 of radius r>1 touches both axes (so its centre is (r,r)) and also touches C1, centre (1,1), radius 1. Touching C1 externally means the distance between centres equals r+1.
Formula centre distance = r1+r2 for external contact. Two circles touch externally when the distance between centres equals the sum of their radii.
Solution
Given C₁: radius 1 touching both axes; C₂: radius > 1 touching both axes and C₁
Find the radius of C₂
1. C1 has radius 1 and touches both axes, so its centre is (1,1). A circle touching both axes has centre (r,r) in the first quadrant, with distance r from each axis.
2. C2 has radius r>1 and touches both axes, so its centre is (r,r). Same reasoning with radius r.
3. Distance between centres = √((r−1)2+(r−1)2) = √2 (r−1). Both coordinates differ by r−1, and r>1 so r−1>0.
4. C2 is bigger and touches C1 from outside along the diagonal, so √2(r−1) = 1+r. External touching: distance equals sum of radii (internal would give r−1, impossible since √2(r−1) > r−1).
5. √2 r − √2 = r + 1 ⇒ r(√2−1) = √2+1. Collect r on the left.
6. r = (√2+1)/(√2−1) = (√2+1)2/((√2)2−1) = (√2+1)2 = 3+2√2. Rationalise by multiplying numerator and denominator by (√2+1).
7. Numerically 3+2√2 ≈ 5.83 > 1, valid; the other option 3−2√2 ≈ 0.17 is the radius less than 1 (the small circle), rejected. The problem insists the radius is greater than 1.
⚡ JEE Trick The two solutions of |√2(r−1)| = r+1 are 3+2√2 and 3−2√2, reciprocal of each other; the condition r>1 picks the larger. The ratio (√2+1)/(√2−1) is the standard trap to simplify.
Answer: (b) 3+2√2

(a), (b), (c), (d) all correct
Concept For two circles with centres C1, C2, radii r1, r2 and d=C1C2, the number of common tangents, orthogonality, common tangent length and common chord length all follow from d, r1, r2. Test each option separately.
Formula |r1−r2|<d<r1+r2 (2 tangents); orthogonal: 2(g1g2+f1f2)=c1+c2. Intersecting circles have exactly 2 common tangents; orthogonality means d2=r12+r22, which is the same as the g,f,c relation.
Solution
Given the circles x² + y² + 2x + 4y − 20 = 0 and x² + y² + 6x − 8y + 10 = 0
Find which statements about their common tangents and orthogonality are correct
1. C1: centre (−1,−2), r1=√(1+4+20)=5. C2: centre (−3,4), r2=√(9+16−10)=√15. d2=4+36=40, so d=2√10 ≈ 6.32. Complete the squares for both.
2. (a) r1−r2 ≈ 1.13 < 6.32 < r1+r2 ≈ 8.87, so the circles intersect and have exactly 2 common tangents. TRUE. Intersect at two points gives two direct common tangents.
3. (b) 2(g1g2+f1f2) = 2(1·3+2·(−4)) = −10 and c1+c2 = −20+10 = −10. Equal, so orthogonal. TRUE. Check also d2=40 vs r12+r22=25+15=40.
4. (c) Direct common tangent length2 = d2−(r1−r2)2 = 40−(25+15−10√15) = 10√15. Given [5(12/5)1/4]2 = 25√(12/5) = 10√15. TRUE. Right triangle: d is hypotenuse, r1−r2 one leg, tangent length the other.
5. (d) Radical axis: S1−S2: −4x+12y−30=0, i.e. 2x−6y+15=0. Distance from (−1,−2): |−2+12+15|/√40 = 25/(2√10). Common chord lies on the radical axis.
6. Half-chord2 = 25 − 625/40 = 75/8, so chord = 2√(75/8) = √(75/2) = 5√(3/2). TRUE. Half-chord2 = r12−(distance)2.
7. All of (a), (b), (c), (d) hold. Multiple-correct, so list every true option.
⚡ JEE Trick Orthogonal circles automatically intersect, so (a) follows from (b). For orthogonal circles the common chord is 2r1r2/d = 2·5√15/(2√10) = 5√(3/2), a 10-second check of (d).
Answer: (a), (b), (c), (d) all four are correct

M15L6V6Q10Centres of circles (multi-correct)
🟢 ExactThe centre of a circ…
(c) (1/2, √2) and (d) (1/2, −√2)
Concept A circle through the two points (0,0) and (1,0) has its centre on their perpendicular bisector x=1/2. If it passes through the origin and touches x2+y2=9 (centre O, radius 3), the centre is at distance equal to its own radius from O, which forces internal tangency.
Formula internal tangency: OC = 3 − ρ; here OC = ρ (circle passes through O). Two circles touch internally when the distance of centres equals the difference of radii, and OC=ρ because the origin is a point on the moving circle.
Solution
Given circles through (0, 0) and (1, 0) touching x² + y² = 9
Find which of the options (a)–(d) are possible centres
1. Centre C=(1/2,k) lies on the perpendicular bisector of (0,0),(1,0). Any point on a circle's chord bisector is the centre candidate.
2. Radius ρ = CO = √(1/4+k2). The circle passes through the origin.
3. External tangency needs OC = 3+ρ, but OC = ρ, so this is impossible. 3 + ρ > ρ always.
4. Internal tangency needs OC = |3−ρ|. Since the circle must lie inside radius 3 (its centre is inside), OC = 3−ρ. The point O is inside the circle of radius 3, so this circle sits inside it.
5. Substitute OC=ρ: ρ = 3−ρ ⇒ ρ = 3/2. Single linear equation for ρ.
6. Then 1/4+k2 = 9/4 ⇒ k2 = 2 ⇒ k = ±√2. Square the radius expression.
7. Centres (1/2, √2) and (1/2, −√2): options (c) and (d). Options (a) (3/2,1/2) and (b) (1/2,3/2) are not on x=1/2 with k=±√2, so they are wrong. Multiple-correct, both signs of k satisfy the symmetric condition.
⚡ JEE Trick Because the moving circle passes through O, distance-to-centre equals its radius, so tangency with a radius-R circle at O always gives ρ=R/2. Symmetry about the x-axis gives both centres.
Answer: (c) (1/2, √2) and (d) (1/2, −√2)
