1When a planet is closer to the Sun, its speed is: (a) Less (b) Zero (c) Greater (d) Constant — (Level-1)Kepler second law speed variationWhy planets move faster when closer to sun and slower when farther away?🟡 SimilarcGreater
Concept The Sun's pull on a planet is a central force: it always points along the line joining planet and Sun, so it exerts zero torque about the Sun. Angular momentum about the Sun is therefore conserved, which is exactly Kepler's second law (equal areas in equal times). A smaller radius must be compensated by a larger speed. The trap is to think the orbit is uniform circular motion and pick "constant".
Formula L = m v r sinφ = constant, and at perihelion/aphelion φ = 90° so vp rp = va ra. Torque τ = r × F = 0 because F is parallel to r for gravity.
Solution
1. Gravity on the planet acts along the Sun-planet line ⇒ τ = r × F = 0 about the Sun.
2. Zero torque ⇒ dL/dt = 0 ⇒ L = m v r sinφ stays fixed all along the ellipse.
3. At the nearest point (perihelion) and farthest point (aphelion) the velocity is perpendicular to r, so sinφ = 1 and L = m v r.These are the two ends of the major axis.
4. Equate: m vp rp = m va ra ⇒ vp/va = ra/rp > 1.
5. Example check: if ra = 2rp, then vp = va·ra/rp = va·2 = 2va, so the planet moves twice as fast when nearer.
6. Energy view agrees: E = (1/2)mv² − GMm/r is constant; smaller r makes −GMm/r more negative, so KE must rise ⇒ speed greater.
 tau = r x F = 0       (central force)
 L = m v r = const     (at perihelion / aphelion)
 v_p r_p = v_a r_a  =>  v_p / v_a = r_a / r_p > 1
 -----------------------------------------
 nearer the Sun  =>  speed GREATER   <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick Remember "perihelion = fastest, aphelion = slowest", like a skater pulling the arms in to spin faster. "Constant" (d) is only true for a perfectly circular orbit, where r never changes, so it cannot describe "closer". "Zero" (b) would mean the planet falls into the Sun, and "Less" (a) contradicts both angular momentum and energy conservation.
Answer: (c) Greater.

Concept Kepler's third law says the square of a planet's period is proportional to the cube of its orbital radius, with the same constant for every body orbiting the same Sun. So the new year length follows from a pure ratio: no G, no M, no numbers for the Earth-Sun distance are needed. The trap is to scale T linearly (3 years) or as r² (9 years).
Formula T² = (4π²/GM)·r³ ⇒ T ∝ r3/2, so T2/T1 = (r2/r1)3/2. Gravity supplies the centripetal force: GMm/r² = m(2π/T)²r, and the Sun's mass M is unchanged.
Solution
1. For a circular orbit: GMm/r² = mω²r = m(4π²/T²)r ⇒ T² = 4π²r³/(GM).
2. Same Sun ⇒ 4π²/(GM) is a common constant ⇒ T² ∝ r³.The Earth's own mass m cancels, so it never enters.
3. Take ratios: (T2/T1)² = (r2/r1)³ = 3³ = 27.
4. T2 = T1(r2/r1)3/2 = 1 yr × 33/2 = 1 × √27 = 3√3 years.
5. Numerically: √3 = 1.732 ⇒ 3√3 = 3 × 1.732 = 5.196 years.
6. In days: 5.196 × 365 ≈ 1897 days, i.e. a year about 5.2 times longer than now.
7. Cross-check with orbital speed: v = √(GM/r) ∝ r−1/2, so the planet moves 1/√3 times as fast along a path 3 times as long ⇒ time = 3 × √3 = 3√3 times longer. Same result, different route.

Trap: forgetting the 3/2 power and answering 3 years, or squaring and answering 9 years. The key's 5.196 years matches exactly.

1According to Kepler’s second law, the areal velocity of a planet moving around the Sun remains constant. This implies that: (a) The linear speed of the planet is constant (b) The angular momentum of the planet about the Sun is constant (c) The gravitational force on the planet is constant (d) The radial velocity of the planet is constant — (Level-1)Areal velocity and angular momentumWhy Kepler's Second Law Is Just Conservation of Angular Momentum🟡 SimilarbThe angular momentum of the planet about the Sun is constant
Concept Areal velocity is the rate at which the Sun-planet line sweeps area. A short geometric argument shows it equals L/(2m), so "constant areal velocity" is the same statement as constant angular momentum about the Sun. Speed, force and radial velocity all change along an ellipse. The trap is to read "velocity" in the law as linear speed.
Formula dA/dt = (1/2)|r × v| = L/(2m). In time dt the radius vector sweeps a thin triangle of area (1/2)|r × v dt|.
Solution
1. In time dt the planet moves dr = v dt; the swept triangle has area dA = (1/2)|r × dr| = (1/2)|r × v| dt.
2. So dA/dt = (1/2)|r × v| = |r × mv|/(2m) = L/(2m).L = r × p about the Sun.
3. Kepler II: dA/dt = constant ⇒ L/(2m) = constant ⇒ L = constant, since m is fixed. This makes (b) true.
4. (a) Linear speed: L = m v r sinφ fixed but r varies ⇒ v varies (fast at perihelion). False.
5. (c) Force: F = GMm/r² changes as r changes along the ellipse. False.
6. (d) Radial velocity dr/dt is zero at perihelion and aphelion but non-zero in between. False.
7. Why L is conserved at all: gravity is central, so torque τ = r × F = 0 about the Sun ⇒ dL/dt = 0. Kepler found the equal-area rule from data; Newton showed it is this conservation law.
 dA = (1/2)|r x v dt|
 dA/dt = (1/2)|r x v| = L/(2m)
 Kepler II: dA/dt = const  =>  L = const
 v, F = GMm/r^2, dr/dt all vary with r on ellipse
 -----------------------------------------

⚡ JEE Trick Memorise dA/dt = L/2m; it turns every Kepler-II question into angular momentum conservation (vprp = vara). Trap: option (a) looks natural because the law contains "velocity", but areal velocity is area per time, not speed. Only for a circular orbit would (a), (c) and (d) also hold, and the law is stated for ellipses.
Answer: (b) The angular momentum of the planet about the Sun is constant.

3Kepler’s II law is a consequence of: (a) conservation of energy (b) conservation of linear momentum (c) conservation of angular momentum (d) none of these — (Level-1)Kepler second law conservation basisKepler's second law is a consequence of (a) conservation of energy (b) conservation of linear mom...🟢 Exactcconservation of angular momentum
Concept Kepler's second law (equal areas in equal times) is the observational form of a dynamical fact: the Sun's gravity on a planet is a central force, so it has zero torque about the Sun and the planet's angular momentum about the Sun cannot change. Energy is also conserved, but energy conservation alone cannot fix the rate of sweeping area. The trap is to pick energy because "everything in orbits is energy".
Formula τ = r × F = 0 ⇒ L = const ⇒ dA/dt = L/(2m) = const. F is antiparallel to r, so their cross product vanishes.
Solution
1. Gravity on the planet: F = −(GMm/r²) r̂, directed along −r.
2. Torque about the Sun: τ = r × F = −(GMm/r²)(r × r̂) = 0.Cross product of parallel vectors is zero.
3. τ = dL/dt ⇒ dL/dt = 0 ⇒ L = constant in magnitude and direction (orbit stays in a fixed plane too).
4. Area swept in dt: dA = (1/2)|r × v dt| ⇒ dA/dt = |r × mv|/(2m) = L/(2m) = constant, which is Kepler II.
5. Why not (a): energy conservation gives v at each r, but says nothing about the sweep rate. Why not (b): linear momentum is NOT conserved, since a net force acts and the velocity keeps turning.
6. Hence (d) "none" is wrong too; the law is a consequence of angular momentum conservation.
7. Note the generality: step 2 never used the 1/r² form, so equal areas hold for any central force; Kepler II alone cannot reveal the inverse-square law.
 F = -(GMm/r^2) r_hat          (central force)
 tau = r x F = 0
 dL/dt = tau = 0   =>  L = const
 dA/dt = L/(2m)    =>  equal areas, equal times
 -----------------------------------------
 Kepler II  <=  conservation of L     <-- (c)
⚡ JEE Trick Map the laws: Kepler I (ellipse) ⇐ inverse-square force; Kepler II (areas) ⇐ angular momentum conservation (true for ANY central force); Kepler III (T² ∝ r³) ⇐ inverse-square force. Trap: (b) linear momentum, since the planet clearly accelerates; (a) energy is conserved but is the wrong "consequence".
Answer: (c) conservation of angular momentum.
