Concept Shifting the origin is a translation: every point moves by the same vector (−h, −k) in the new frame, so the shape and size of any figure are untouched. Area, length and angle are therefore invariants of a change of origin — only the co-ordinate labels change, never the geometry. So the triangle on (2, 3), (5, 7), (−3, −1) keeps exactly the area it had.
Formula Area = (1/2)|x1(y2−y3) + x2(y3−y1) + x3(y1−y2)|. Each new co-ordinate is old−shift, and the shift cancels in every difference (y2−y3), leaving the area unchanged.
Solution
1. Under a shift to (h, k) a point (x, y) becomes (x−h, y−k); all three vertices move by the same (−h, −k).
2. In the area formula only differences of co-ordinates appear, e.g. (y2−y3) = (y2−k)−(y3−k). The k cancels, so the bracket is identical in both frames.
3. Hence the computed area is the same before and after the shift — it is invariant.
4. Value check: Area = (1/2)|2(7−(−1)) + 5((−1)−3) + (−3)(3−7)|.
5. = (1/2)|2(8) + 5(−4) + (−3)(−4)| = (1/2)|16 − 20 + 12| = (1/2)(8) = 4.
6. So the area is 4 sq units with respect to the old origin, and still 4 after the origin is moved to (−1, 3). The fill-in word is “invariant” (unchanged).
  new vertex = (x-h, y-k)  ->  differences lose h,k
  A = 1/2 |16 - 20 + 12| = 1/2 (8) = 4
  before shift = after shift = 4  (invariant)
⚡ JEE Trick Translation never changes area, length or angle — only rotation/reflection can touch an angle, and nothing changes area except scaling. If a problem shifts the origin and asks for an area, answer with the original area immediately; no re-computation in the new frame is needed.
Answer: Invariant — the area stays 4 sq units.
1 (ii)Fill in the blanks: The area of the rhombus formed by |x| + |y| = 1 is ______.Area of the rhombus |x|+|y|=1Area of Rhombus Formula, How to prove it?🟡 Similar2
Concept The graph of |x| + |y| = 1 is a square (a special rhombus) whose four vertices sit on the axes at (±1, 0) and (0, ±1). In each quadrant the modulus equation becomes a straight line, e.g. in the first quadrant x + y = 1, so the boundary is four line segments joining those axis points. Its two diagonals lie along the axes, so their lengths are easy to read off, and the area of any rhombus is half the product of its diagonals.
Formula Area of a rhombus = (1/2) × d1 × d2. The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors of each other, so they split it into four right triangles whose total area is (1/2)d1d2.
Solution
1. Set y = 0: |x| = 1 ⇒ x = ±1, giving vertices (1, 0) and (−1, 0).
2. Set x = 0: |y| = 1 ⇒ y = ±1, giving vertices (0, 1) and (0, −1).
3. The figure is the rhombus with these four vertices — a square tilted 45°. All four sides equal √2, confirming it is a rhombus.
4. Diagonal along the x-axis: from (−1, 0) to (1, 0), length d1 = 2.
5. Diagonal along the y-axis: from (0, −1) to (0, 1), length d2 = 2.
6. Area = (1/2)(2)(2) = 2 square units. Equivalently, the diagonals cut the square into four right triangles of legs 1 and 1, total 4 × (1/2)(1)(1) = 2 — the same answer.
7. So the rhombus (unit “taxicab circle”) encloses an area of exactly 2. Its perimeter is 4√2, but the question asks only for the area.
  vertices: (1,0)(0,1)(-1,0)(0,-1)
  d1 = 2 (x-axis) , d2 = 2 (y-axis)
  Area = 1/2 * 2 * 2 = 2
⚡ JEE Trick For |x|/a + |y|/b = 1 the enclosed figure is a rhombus of area 2ab (diagonals 2a and 2b). Here a = b = 1, so area = 2 instantly — no need to split into triangles or integrate.
Answer: 2 square units.
1 (iii)Fill in the blanks: If the axes are transformed from the origin to the point (−2, 1), then the new co-ordinates of (4, −5) are ______.New co-ordinates after a shift of originTransformation of axes L1: Change the Origin without altering direction of the axes with examples🟡 Similar(6, −6)
Concept “Transforming the axes from the origin to (h, k)” means moving the origin to that point while keeping the axis directions parallel to the old ones (a pure translation). The point in space does not move; only the ruler we measure it against does. A point’s new co-ordinates are therefore its old co-ordinates minus the shift, because we now measure the same point from a nearer reference. Here the new origin is (h, k) = (−2, 1) and the point is (4, −5).
Formula New (X, Y) = (x − h, y − k). The new origin sits at (h, k), so the displacement of the point from it is (x−h, y−k).
Solution
1. The origin is shifted to (h, k) = (−2, 1); the point is (x, y) = (4, −5).
2. New abscissa: X = x − h = 4 − (−2) = 4 + 2 = 6.
3. New ordinate: Y = y − k = −5 − 1 = −6.
4. So the new co-ordinates are (6, −6). Subtracting a negative h adds, pushing X to the right of the new origin.
5. Sanity check: old point = new + shift = (6, −6) + (−2, 1) = (4, −5) — recovers the original.
6. Hence (4, −5) is labelled (6, −6) in the shifted frame. Both new co-ordinates are positive-then-negative, placing the point to the right of and below the new origin.
7. Note the axis directions are unchanged, so this is a translation only — no sin/cos rotation terms enter.
  h,k = -2, 1   point = 4, -5
  X = 4 - (-2) = 6
  Y = -5 - 1   = -6
  check: (6,-6)+(-2,1) = (4,-5)  OK
⚡ JEE Trick Read it as “subtract the new origin.” Watch the double sign when h or k is negative: 4 − (−2) = 6, not 2. A quick back-check (new + shift = old) catches any sign slip in one line.
Answer: (6, −6).

Concept Shifting the origin to (1, −2) without rotation removes the first-degree terms of this central conic and reduces it to the form px2 + qy2 = r. Substitute x = X + 1, y = Y − 2 and collect; the coefficients give p and q, and the constant moved to the right is r.

Solution
1. Put x = X + 1, y = Y − 2 in 2x2 + y2 − 4x + 4y = 0.
2. 2(X + 1)2 + (Y − 2)2 − 4(X + 1) + 4(Y − 2) = 0.

1Shifting origin + rotation produces: (a) Only shift (b) Only rotation (c) Combined transformation (d) No effectCombined transformation of axesLecture : 4 | Shifting of Origin | Rotation of Axis | Change of origin and Rotation of Axis.🟡 SimilarcCombined transformation
Concept Shifting the origin is a translation; rotating the axes is a turn about the origin. Doing both at once produces a combined transformation — the new co-ordinates depend on the shift (h, k) and on the rotation angle θ. It is neither “only a shift” nor “only a rotation”, and it certainly has an effect, so only option (c) fits.
Formula x = α + x′cosθ − y′sinθ,   y = β + x′sinθ + y′cosθ. The constants α, β carry the shift; the sin/cos terms carry the rotation — both appear, hence “combined”.
Solution
1. A translation alone changes the origin but keeps axis directions.
2. A rotation alone keeps the origin but turns the axes through θ.
3. Applying both together moves the origin to (α, β) and turns the axes through θ. The lecture’s master formula x = α + x′cosθ − y′sinθ mixes the two effects.
4. The result is therefore a genuine combined transformation, not a pure shift or pure rotation.
5. Option (a) “only shift” and (b) “only rotation” each describe just one half; (d) “no effect” is false since co-ordinates change.
6. Hence the correct choice is (c), the combined transformation. Setting θ = 0 recovers a pure shift; setting α = β = 0 recovers a pure rotation — both are special cases of (c).
  translation : origin moves, axes parallel
  rotation    : origin fixed, axes turn by theta
  both        : x = a + x'cos t - y'sin t  (combined)
  answer = (c)
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a problem applies a shift and a turn, expect the general formula with both (h, k) and θ. Pure shift and pure rotation are just the θ = 0 and (h, k) = (0, 0) corners of this one combined rule.
Answer: (c) Combined transformation.
