1A particle starts from rest and moves with uniform acceleration. Then the ratio of distance covered in nth second to n seconds is (A) n²/(2n − 1) (B) 2/n − 1/n² (C) n²/(n + 1) (D) (2n + 1)/n²Distance in nth secondA car starts from rest with constant acceleration: ratio of distance in nth second to distance in n seconds🟢 ExactB2/n − 1/n²exactP4 · L2 · Sugg · Q4
Concept Two different distances are being compared. Sn (the nth-second distance) is the distance covered only between t = n − 1 and t = n, while s(n) is the total distance from t = 0 to t = n. Starting from rest (u = 0), both depend only on a and n, so the acceleration cancels in the ratio.
Formula Sn = u + (a/2)(2n − 1); s = ut + (1/2)at². Sn = s(n) − s(n − 1); with u = 0 the nth-second distance grows like the odd numbers 1, 3, 5, … (Galileo's law).
Solution
1. With u = 0: Sn = (a/2)(2n − 1).Check: (a/2)n² − (a/2)(n − 1)² = (a/2)(2n − 1).
2. Distance in n seconds: s(n) = (1/2)a n².
3. Ratio = Sn/s(n) = [(a/2)(2n − 1)] / [(a/2)n²] = (2n − 1)/n².
4. Split the fraction: (2n − 1)/n² = 2n/n² − 1/n² = 2/n − 1/n².That is exactly option (B); (D) has the wrong sign, (A) is inverted.
5. Sanity check n = 1: ratio = 2 − 1 = 1 — correct, since in the first second both distances are the same.
⚡ JEE Trick Plug n = 1: the ratio must be 1. (A) gives 1, (B) gives 1, (C) gives 1/2, (D) gives 3. Then try n = 2: distances go 1 : 3, so S2 = 3 units and s(2) = 4 units, ratio 3/4. (B) gives 1 − 1/4 = 3/4, (A) gives 4/3. Two quick plug-ins settle it without algebra.
Answer: (B) 2/n − 1/n².
2A particle “P” moves with constant velocity u along the x-axis. Q always has velocity towards P. Concept Q's path is a curve, so we cannot track it directly. Instead use two exact statements that hold at every instant: (i) the rate at which the separation PQ shrinks, and (ii) the fact that Q's x-velocity must, on the whole, catch up with P's x-displacement. Let θ be the angle between Q's velocity (along QP) and the direction of P's motion (+x). Initially θ = 90°.
Formula dr/dt = −(V − u cosθ); ∫V cosθ dt = u t. Along the line QP, Q closes at V while P runs away at u cosθ; along x, both must cover the same total distance by the time they meet.
Solution
1. Integrate the closing-rate equation from r = l to r = 0 over time T: l = VT − u∫cosθ dt.
2. x-coordinates: Q starts at the same x as P and meets it, so ∫V cosθ dt = uT ⇒ ∫cosθ dt = uT/V.P moves uT along x; Q's x-velocity is V cosθ.
3. Substitute: l = VT − u(uT/V) = T(V² − u²)/V ⇒ T = Vl/(V² − u²).
4. Q moves at constant speed V, so distance = VT = V²l/(V² − u²).
5. Checks: u = 0 gives distance l (straight line); u → V gives infinity (Q never catches P). Both sensible. Dimensions: V²l/V² = length, so (C) and (A) fail dimensionally or physically.
⚡ JEE Trick For every pursuit problem write just two lines — “rate of approach along the line joining them” and “component along the chased particle's velocity” — and add them. The unknown ∫cosθ dt cancels out. Then test limits: u = 0 must return l, and u = V must blow up; only (B) does both.
Answer: (B) V²l/(V² − u²).
