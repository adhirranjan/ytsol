★ Core ideaCircular motion needs a net inward (centripetal) force mv²/r. It is not a new force — it is supplied by whatever is available: tension, friction, normal reaction, gravity, or a component of these. A tangential force only changes the speed; the centripetal part only changes the direction.
L-1

Angular Displacement, Velocity & Acceleration

  • Angular position θ — angle swept from a reference; angular displacement Δθ is the change in θ (radians). A full revolution = 2π rad.
  • Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular position; angular acceleration is the rate of change of angular velocity.
angular velocity & accelerationω = dθ/dt  ·  α = dω/dt = d²θ/dt²

Relation between ω and α

  • If ω and α are parallel (same sense) the body speeds up; if antiparallel, it slows down.

Linear ↔ angular velocity

for a rigid body rotating with ω⃗v⃗ = ω⃗ × r⃗  (r⃗ = position of the point from a point on the axis)
  • Speed of a point = ωr, directed tangent to the circle; points farther from the axis move faster.
💡 Reading the calculusGiven θ(t): differentiate once for ω, twice for α. Given α(t) or ω(t): integrate (with limits/initial values) to go back the other way.

L-2

Kinematic Equations · Centripetal & Net Acceleration

Uniform angular acceleration

circular analogues of v = u + at, etc.ω = ω₀ + αt  ·  θ = ω₀t + ½αt²  ·  ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

Centripetal acceleration

directed toward the centrear = v²/r = rω²

Non-uniform (variable α)

use calculus, not the uniform-α formulas∫dθ = ∫ω dt  ·  ∫dω = ∫α dt  ·  ∫ω dω = ∫α dθ

Net acceleration

vector sum of radial and tangential partsuniform: a = ar = rω²  ·  non-uniform: a = √(ac² + at²)
⚠ TrapUse the uniform-α equations only when α is constant. If α depends on t (or on θ), you must integrate — Examples 6 & 7 below.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-2
A fan accelerates uniformly from rest to 400 rpm in 5 s. How long to reach half its maximum speed?
From rest with constant α, ω ∝ t, so half the speed is reached in half the time.
t = 5/2.
t = 2.5 s
Book Example · L-2
A wheel starts from rest with α = 2 rad/s². How many revolutions in the first 10 s?
θ = ½αt² = ½(2)(10²) = 100 rad.
n = θ/2π = 100/2π ≈ 15.9.
θ = 100 rad ≈ 16 revolutions
Book Example · L-2
A body accelerates uniformly from rest to 100 rev/s in 4 s. Find α and the angle turned.
ω = 100×2π = 200π rad/s; α = ω/t = 200π/4 = 50π rad/s².
ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ ⇒ (200π)² = 2(50π)θ ⇒ θ = 400π rad.
α = 50π rad s⁻², θ = 400π rad
Book Example · L-2
A particle moves in a circle of radius 1.0 cm with speed v = 2t (cm/s). Find its radial acceleration at t = 1 s.
v = 2(1) = 2 cm/s ⇒ ar = v²/r = (2)²/1.
ar = 4 cm s⁻²
Book Example · L-2
A particle moves in a circle of radius 0.1 m with angular speed 2 rad/s. Find its radial acceleration.
ar = rω² = (0.1)(2)² = 0.4.
ar = 0.4 m s⁻²
Book Example · L-2
A shaft turns at ω₀ = 65 rad/s at t = 0, then α = (−10 − 5t) rad/s². Find (a) ω at t = 3 s, (b) the angle turned in these 3 s.
(a) ω − 65 = ∫₀³(−10 − 5t)dt = [−10t − 2.5t²]₀³ = −30 − 22.5 = −52.5 ⇒ ω = 12.5 rad/s.
(b) θ = ∫₀³ ω dt = ∫₀³(−10t − 2.5t² + 65)dt = [−5t² − (5t³/6) + 65t]₀³ = −45 − 22.5 + 195.
ω = 12.5 rad s⁻¹ ; θ = 127.5 rad
Book Example · L-2
A gear has ω = 12 − 3t² (rad/s). Find the net angular displacement from t = 0 to 3 s and the number of revolutions.
Δθ = ∫₀³(12 − 3t²)dt = [12t − t³]₀³ = 36 − 27 = 9 rad.
n = 9/2π ≈ 1.43.
Δθ = 9 rad ≈ 1.43 revolutions
Book Example · L-2
A particle moves in a circle of radius r = 2 m with speed v = t². Find the radial, tangential and net acceleration at t = 2 s.
v = (2)² = 4 m/s ⇒ ar = v²/r = 16/2 = 8 m/s².
at = dv/dt = 2t = 4 m/s².
a = √(at² + ar²) = √(16 + 64) = √80 = 4√5.
ar = 8, at = 4, a = 4√5 ≈ 8.94 m s⁻²
Book Example · L-2
A particle moves in a circle of radius 1.0 cm with v = 2t (cm/s). At t = 1 s find (a) ar, (b) at, (c) net acceleration.
v = 2 cm/s ⇒ ar = v²/r = 4/1 = 4 cm/s².
at = dv/dt = 2 cm/s².
a = √(at² + ar²) = √(4 + 16) = 2√5.
ar = 4, at = 2, a = 2√5 ≈ 4.47 cm s⁻²
L-3

at/ar Problems · Relative Angular Speed · Centripetal & Tangential Force

  • ac = ar = v²/r changes only direction (present whenever the body moves in a circle). at = dv/dt changes only speed (zero if speed is constant).

Relative angular speed

rate at which the line joining two bodies rotatesω₂₁ = (v₂ sinθ₂ − v₁ sinθ₁)/r  (take velocity components ⟂ to the joining line)

Centripetal force

the inward force that sustains the circleFc = m ar = mv²/r = mrω²

Tangential force & radius of curvature

resolving the net accel a along/⟂ to vat = a cosθ  ·  an = a sinθ = v²/R  ⇒  R = v²/an
💡 Radius of curvatureAny curved path can be treated locally as a circular arc of radius R = v²/an, where an is the acceleration component ⟂ to the velocity. At a projectile's peak the whole of g is ⟂ to v, so R = u²cos²θ/g.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-3
An object moves in a circle of radius 2 m at constant speed 5 m/s. Find its tangential acceleration.
Speed is constant ⇒ at = dv/dt = 0.
at = 0 (only radial acceleration acts)
Book Example · L-3
An object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at constant speed 6 m/s. Find its radial acceleration.
ar = v²/r = 6²/4 = 36/4.
ar = 9 m s⁻²
Book Example · L-3
Two particles A and B move as shown; the line AB has length r. Find the angular speed of A relative to B.
Only the velocity components ⟂ to AB rotate the line: ωAB = (vA sinθA − vB sinθB)/r.
ωAB = (vA sinθA − vB sinθB)/r
Book Example · L-3
Particles P₁, P₂ have speeds 1 m/s and 2 m/s making θ₁ = 30° and θ₂ = 60° with the joining line (length 2 m). Find the relative angular speed of P₂ w.r.t. P₁.
ω₂₁ = (v₂ sinθ₂ − v₁ sinθ₁)/r = (2·sin60° − 1·sin30°)/2 = (√3 − 0.5)/2.
ω₂₁ = (2√3 − 1)/4 ≈ 0.62 rad s⁻¹ (anticlockwise, since v₂sinθ₂ > v₁sinθ₁)
Book Example · L-3
A rod (length 20 cm) is pivoted near one end and rotated in a horizontal plane. A ball hangs from a string (length 20 cm) at the other end; the string makes 30° with the vertical. Find the angular speed. (g = 10)
Radius of the ball's circle r = L(1 + sinθ); horizontal & vertical eqns give T sinθ = m r ω², T cosθ = mg.
Dividing: tanθ = L(1 + sinθ)ω²/g ⇒ ω = √( g tanθ / [L(1 + sinθ)] ) = √( 10·tan30° / [0.2(1 + sin30°)] ) = √(5.774/0.3).
ω = √19.25 ≈ 4.39 rad s⁻¹ (the module rounds this to 4.34)
Book Example · L-3
A ball of mass 0.5 kg on a string (L = 0.5 m) is whirled in a horizontal circle about a vertical axis. If the string can bear at most 324 N, find the maximum angular velocity.
T sinθ = m r ω² with r = L sinθ ⇒ T = mLω² (the sinθ cancels).
ω = √(T/mL) = √(324/(0.5·0.5)) = √(324/0.25) = √1296.
ωmax = 36 rad s⁻¹
Book Example · L-3
A particle is projected with speed u at angle θ. Treating the top of its path as a circular arc, find the radius of curvature there.
At the peak v = u cosθ and the whole of g is ⟂ to v, so an = g.
an = v²/R ⇒ g = (u cosθ)²/R ⇒ R = u²cos²θ/g.
R = u²cos²θ / g
L-4

Motion in a Vertical Circle

  • Speed is maximum at the bottom, minimum at the top (energy is traded between KE and PE). Total mechanical energy is conserved (smooth track/string).
speed at height h above the lowest pointv² = u² − 2gh
tension at angle θ from the vertical (bottom)T − mg cosθ = mv²/r

Critical speeds (string / loop)

from bottom of the circleloop: u ≥ √(5gr)  ·  leaves circle: √(2gr) < u < √(5gr)  ·  oscillates: u ≤ √(2gr)
  • At the top: minimum vtop = √(gr) (there T = 0 and gravity alone provides mv²/r). At the bottom: minimum vbot = √(5gr).
  • Between √(2gr) and √(5gr) the string goes slack in the upper half; below √(2gr) the bob just swings like a pendulum.
⚠ Trap“Reaches the top of the loop” needs vtop = √(gr), not v = 0. A string/track can only push/pull inward; if the required inward force exceeds what gravity + tension supply, the body leaves the circle.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-4
A stone on a string of length L is whirled in a vertical circle. At the lowest point its speed is u. Find the magnitude of the change in velocity when it reaches the position where the string is horizontal.
At the horizontal position h = L: v = √(u² − 2gL); the two velocities are ⟂ (θ = 90°).
|Δv⃗| = √(v² + u² − 2vu cos90°) = √(u² − 2gL + u²) = √(2(u² − gL)).
|Δv⃗| = √(2(u² − gL))
Book Example · L-4
With what minimum speed must a small ball be pushed inside a smooth vertical tube from a height h so that it just reaches the top? Tube radius R.
Inside a tube the wall can push both ways, so “reach the top” means v = 0 there: v² = u² − 2g(2R − h) = 0.
u = √(2g(2R − h))
Book Example · L-4
A particle on a string of length 5 m is projected from the equilibrium (lowest) position; the string slackens after it rises 8 m above the lowest point. Find the speed just before slackening and how much higher it rises. (g = 9.8)
Height above centre = 8 − 5 = 3 m ⇒ cosα = 3/5. String slack ⇒ T = 0 ⇒ mg cosα = mv²/r ⇒ v² = g r cosα = 9.8·5·(3/5) = 29.4 ⇒ v = 5.42 m/s.
Then projectile rise h = v²sin²α/2g = (5.42)²(4/5)²/(2·9.8) ≈ 0.96 m.
v ≈ 5.42 m s⁻¹ ; rises a further ≈ 0.96 m
Book Example · L-4
A heavy particle hangs from a string of length ℓ and is projected horizontally with speed √(gℓ). Find its speed and the string's inclination to the vertical at the instant the tension equals the particle's weight.
Energy: v² = gℓ − 2gℓ(1 − cosθ) = gℓ(2cosθ − 1). Newton (T = mg): mg − mg cosθ = mv²/ℓ ⇒ v² = gℓ(1 − cosθ).
Equate: 2cosθ − 1 = 1 − cosθ ⇒ cosθ = 2/3 ⇒ v = √(gℓ(1 − 2/3)) = √(gℓ/3).
θ = cos⁻¹(2/3) ; v = √(gℓ/3)
Book Example · L-4
A bob on a light string (fixed at the centre, radius R) is given u = √(4gR) at the bottom. (a) At what height does the string slacken? (b) Its speed just then?
Since √(2gR) < √(4gR) < √(5gR), the string slacks in the upper half. At angle θ above horizontal: v² = 4gR − 2gR(1 + sinθ). Slack (T = 0): mg sinθ = mv²/R ⇒ v² = gR sinθ.
Equate: 2gR(1 − sinθ) = gR sinθ ⇒ sinθ = 2/3 ⇒ h = R(1 + sinθ) = 5R/3 ; v = √(2gR/3).
(a) h = 5R/3   (b) v = √(2gR/3)
Book Example · L-4
Same setup, but u = √(gR). (a) Through what angle θ (from vertical) does v become zero? (b) The tension there?
u < √(2gR) ⇒ oscillation. v = 0: u = √(2gR(1 − cosθ)) ⇒ √(gR) = √(2gR(1 − cosθ)) ⇒ 1 − cosθ = 0.5 ⇒ θ = 60°.
At v = 0: T − mg cosθ = 0 ⇒ T = mg cos60° = mg/2.
θ = 60° ; T = mg/2
Book Example · L-4
A box slides from a platform a height h above the top of a smooth loop of radius r and goes around without leaving the track. Find the minimum h.
Speed at the bottom: u = √(2g(h + 2r)). For looping umin = √(5gr).
2g(h + 2r) = 5gr ⇒ h + 2r = 5r/2 ⇒ h = r/2.
hmin = r/2
L-5

Applications: Well of Death · Hemisphere · Bucket of Water

Motorcyclist in the “well / globe of death”

rider on the inner vertical wallN = mv²/r = mrω²  ·  friction holds weight: f = μN = mg
  • Combining: minimum ω = √(g/μr), minimum v = √(gr/μ) — the wall's normal reaction supplies the centripetal force; friction (∝ N) holds the rider up.

Particle sliding on a smooth hemisphere

leaves the surface when N = 0mg cosθ = mv²/r  ⇒  cosθ = (u² + 2gr)/(3gr)
  • Starting from rest at the top (u = 0): it leaves at cosθ = 2/3 with speed √(2gr/3), then becomes a projectile.

Bucket of water in a vertical circle

water stays in at the top if N ≥ 0top: mg + N = mv²/r ⇒ v ≥ √(gr)  ·  then bottom reaction N′ = 2mg
★ One idea, three facesWell of death, hemisphere and bucket are all the same physics: write the radial (Newton) equation, then impose the contact condition — friction = mg (well), N = 0 (leaving the hemisphere), or N ≥ 0 (water in bucket).
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-5
A 70 kg man stands against the inner wall of a hollow drum of radius 3 m rotating about its vertical axis; μ = 0.15. Find the minimum rotational speed so he stays stuck when the floor is removed. (g = 10)
N = mrω² and friction μN = mg ⇒ μ·mrω² = mg.
ω = √(g/μr) = √(10/(0.15·3)) = √(10/0.45).
ω = √22.2 ≈ 4.71 rad s⁻¹
Book Example · L-5
In a rotor of radius 2 m, μ = 0.2 between wall and person. Find the minimum speed at which the floor may be removed. (g = 10)
μmv²/r = mg ⇒ v = √(gr/μ) = √(10·2/0.2) = √100.
v = 10 m s⁻¹
Book Example · L-5
A small ball slides from rest at the top of a fixed smooth hemisphere of radius r. Find the distance s from the centre of the base to where it lands.
Leaves at cosθ = 2/3 (u = 0), with v = √(2gr/3); it then undergoes projectile motion from height h = r cosθ.
Horizontal range R = (v cosθ/g)(v sinθ + √(v²sin²θ + 2gh)) = 0.38r; s = r sinθ + R = r(√5/3) + 0.38r.
s ≈ 1.125 r
Book Example · L-5
Water in a bucket does not fall even when the bucket is inverted at the top of its vertical circle. We conclude that at this position (a) mg = mv²/r (b) mg > mv²/r (c) mg is not greater than mv²/r (d) mg is not less than mv²/r.
At the top mg + N = mv²/r with N ≥ 0 ⇒ mv²/r ≥ mg, i.e. mg is not greater than mv²/r.
Option (c)
Book Example · L-5
1 kg of water in a bucket is whirled in a vertical circle of radius r = 1 m. Find the minimum speed at the top so the water keeps contact with the bucket. (g = 10)
v ≥ √(gr) = √(10·1).
vmin = √10 ≈ 3.16 m s⁻¹
L-6 & L-7

Conical Pendulum · Centrifugal Force · Banking of Roads

Conical pendulum

bob sweeps a horizontal circle; string a coneT cosθ = mg  ·  T sinθ = mrω²  ⇒  tanθ = rω²/g
with r = L sinθω = √( g / L cosθ )  ·  time period T = 2π√( L cosθ / g )

Centrifugal force (pseudo force)

  • In a frame rotating at ω₀, a body of mass m at radius r feels an outward pseudo force mω₀²r. It is real only in the rotating frame — an accounting device for Newton's laws there.

Car on a level circular road

friction supplies the whole centripetal forceμmg ≥ mv²/r  ⇒  v ≤ √(μrg)

Car on a banked road

no friction — design (optimum) speedtanθ = v₀²/rg  ⇒  v₀ = √(rg·tanθ)
with friction — v < v₀ (friction up) / v > v₀ (friction down)f = | mg tanθ − mv²/r | / (sinθ tanθ + cosθ)  ·  vmax = √( rg(μ+tanθ)/(1−μtanθ) )
💡 Which way does friction point?On a banked road, if the car goes slower than v₀ it tends to slide down, so friction acts up the slope; if faster, it tends to fly outward/up, so friction acts down the slope. Flip the sign of the friction term accordingly.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
Find the time period of a conical pendulum if the string length is 0.5 m and the angle with the vertical is 30°. (g = 10)
T = 2π√(L cosθ/g) = 2π√((0.5)cos30°/10) = 2π√(0.0433) = 2π(0.208).
T ≈ 1.31 s
⚠ Book misprintThe module prints T = 0.21 s — it evaluated √(L cosθ/g) = 0.208 but dropped the 2π factor. The correct period is 2π × 0.208 ≈ 1.31 s.
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
A conical pendulum has angular speed 10 rad/s and string length 0.3 m. Find the angle with the vertical. (g = 10)
cosθ = g/(Lω²) = 10/((0.3)(10)²) = 10/30 = 0.333.
θ = cos⁻¹(0.33) ≈ 70.5°
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
Blocks A (1 kg) and B (3 kg) are joined by strings on a smooth table and rotated at ω = 2 rad/s; A is 1 m and B is 2 m from the axis. Find the centrifugal force on (a) A as seen by B, (b) B as seen by A.
(a) In B's frame (rotating with the system) the pseudo force on A = mA·rAω²... the module uses aB = rBω² = 2·4 = 8, FA = mAaB = 1·8 = 8 N outward.
(b) aA = rAω² = 1·4 = 4, FB = mBaA = 3·4 = 12 N outward.
(a) 8 N outward   (b) 12 N outward
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
A particle of mass m rotates with uniform angular speed ω, viewed from a frame rotating about the z-axis at ω₀. What is the centrifugal force on the particle?
The pseudo (centrifugal) force depends on the frame's angular speed ω₀, not on ω: F = mω₀²r, directed away from the centre.
F = mω₀²r (radially outward)
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
A car travels along a level circular road at 4 m/s; the radius of the turn is 10 m. Find the minimum coefficient of friction for a safe turn. (g = 10)
μ ≥ v²/rg = 4²/(10·10) = 16/100.
μmin = 0.16
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
A turn has radius 10 m. If a vehicle rounds it at 18 km/h, what is the proper angle of banking (no friction)? (g = 10)
v₀ = 18 km/h = 5 m/s; tanθ = v₀²/rg = 5²/(10·10) = 0.25.
θ = tan⁻¹(0.25) ≈ 14°
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
A circular road of radius 50 m is banked at 30°. At what speed should a vehicle move so that friction is not used? (g = 10)
v₀ = √(rg tanθ) = √(10·50·tan30°) = √(500·0.577) = √288.7.
v₀ ≈ 17 m s⁻¹
Book Example · L-6 & L-7
A turn of radius 20 m is banked for a 200 kg vehicle going at 10 m/s. Find the magnitude and direction of friction if it moves at (a) 5 m/s, (b) 15 m/s. (g = 10)
Design: v₀ = √(gr tanθ) ⇒ 10 = √(10·20·tanθ) ⇒ tanθ = ½ (sinθ = 1/√5, cosθ = 2/√5).
(a) v < v₀ ⇒ friction up: f = (mg tanθ − mv²/r)/(sinθ tanθ + cosθ) = (1000 − 250)/(√5/2) = 750/1.118 = 670.8 N up.
(b) v > v₀ ⇒ friction down: f = (mv²/r − mg tanθ)/(sinθ tanθ + cosθ) = (2250 − 1000)/1.118 = 1250/1.118 = 1118 N down.
(a) ≈ 670.8 N up the incline   (b) ≈ 1118 N down the incline

In vertical circular motion by a thread of length r, the tension is zero at 30° (above the horizontal through the centre). Find the speed at the bottom point A. Options: √5gr, √(7gr/2), √4gr, √(5gr/2).
At the T = 0 point the inward radial component of gravity supplies mv²/r: mg sin30° = mv²P/r ⇒ v²P = gr sin30° = gr/2.
Height of P above A = r + r sin30° = 3r/2 ⇒ v²A = v²P + 2g(3r/2) = gr/2 + 3gr = 7gr/2.
vA = √(7gr/2) — option (b)
Strong Box · PYQ 2 · JEE Main 2026
A drum of radius R spins at ω about its axis; a body of mass M stays stuck to the inner wall (coefficient μ). Find the minimum ω. Options: √(μg/R), √(2g/μR), √(g/2μR), √(g/μR).
Wall's normal N = Mω²R gives centripetal force; friction holds the weight: μN ≥ Mg ⇒ μMω²R ≥ Mg.
ω ≥ √(g/μR).
ωmin = √(g/μR) — option (d)
Strong Box · PYQ 3 · JEE Main 2025
A bob (string length ℓ) is given horizontal velocity v₀ at the lowest point A; the string goes slack at the top point D. Points C and B lie 60° and 120° from D. Find the ratio of kinetic energies at B and C. Options: 1, 2, 4, 3.
Slack at the top D ⇒ v²D = gℓ ⇒ KED = mgℓ/2. Heights (from centre): hD = +ℓ, hC = +ℓ/2, hB = −ℓ/2.
KEB = KED + mg(ℓ + ℓ/2) = mgℓ/2 + 3mgℓ/2 = 2mgℓ; KEC = KED + mg(ℓ − ℓ/2) = mgℓ.
KEB : KEC = 2 : 1 — option (b)
Strong Box · PYQ 4 · JEE Main 2025
A car on a banked road (radius r, angle θ) has maximum permissible speed v₀. Find the coefficient of friction μ.
v₀² = rg(tanθ + μ)/(1 − μtanθ). Cross-multiply: v₀² − v₀²μtanθ = rg tanθ + rgμ.
v₀² − rg tanθ = μ(rg + v₀²tanθ) ⇒ μ = (v₀² − rg tanθ)/(rg + v₀²tanθ).
μ = (v₀² − rg tanθ)/(rg + v₀²tanθ) — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 5 · JEE Main 2025
A body on a string (other end fixed at the centre) goes in a vertical circle of radius R. If the top speed is n√(gR) (n ≥ 1), find the ratio of KE at the bottom to KE at the top. Options: n²/(n²+4), (n²+4)/n², (n+4)/n, n/(n+4).
v²top = n²gR; v²bot = v²top + 2g(2R) = n²gR + 4gR = (n²+4)gR.
Ratio = v²bot/v²top = (n²+4)/n².
(n²+4)/n² — option (b)
Strong Box · PYQ 6 · JEE Main 2024
A table rotates at ω about its axis; a smooth radial groove holds a ball placed at r = 1 m. Table radius 3 m. The ball's radial velocity (relative to table) as it leaves is x√2·ω. Find x.
In the rotating frame r̈ = ω²r. Multiply by ṙ and integrate: ṙ² = ω²(r² − 1) (since ṙ = 0 at r = 1).
At r = 3: ṙ² = ω²(9 − 1) = 8ω² ⇒ ṙ = 2√2·ω = x√2·ω ⇒ x = 2.
x = 2
Strong Box · PYQ 7 · JEE Main 2024
A particle in a circle of radius 50 cm keeps its normal and tangential accelerations equal; speed at t = 0 is 4 m/s. The time for the first revolution is (1/α)[1 − e−2π] s. Find α.
at = ac: v dv/ds = v²/r ⇒ v = v₀es/r. Then t = ∫ds/v = (r/v₀)(1 − e−s/r).
One revolution s = 2πr ⇒ t = (r/v₀)(1 − e−2π) = (0.5/4)(1 − e−2π) = (1/8)(1 − e−2π).
α = 8
Strong Box · PYQ 8 · JEE Main 2024
An 800 kg car takes a banked turn of radius 300 m, banking 30°, μ = 0.2. Find the maximum safe speed. (g = 10, √3 = 1.73). Options: 264, 51.4, 70.4, 102.8 m/s.
v²max = rg(tanθ + μ)/(1 − μtanθ) = 3000(0.577 + 0.2)/(1 − 0.2·0.577) = 3000(0.777)/(0.8845).
v²max ≈ 2637 ⇒ vmax ≈ 51.35 m/s.
vmax ≈ 51.4 m s⁻¹ — option (b)
Strong Box · PYQ 9 · JEE Main 2024
A bob (string length L) gets a minimum horizontal velocity at the lowest point A so that it just completes the vertical circle to the topmost point B. Find KEA : KEB. Options: 3:2, 5:1, 2:5, 1:5.
"Just completes" ⇒ v²B = gL (top minimum) ⇒ v²A = v²B + 2g(2L) = gL + 4gL = 5gL.
KEA/KEB = v²A/v²B = 5gL/gL.
KEA : KEB = 5 : 1 — option (b)
Strong Box · PYQ 10 · JEE Main 2023
A 100 g block is tied to a spring (k = 7.5 N/m, natural length 20 cm) and moves in a circle on a smooth horizontal surface at ω = 5 rad/s about the fixed end. Find the spring tension. Options: 0.75, 0.25, 0.50, 1.5 N.
kx = mω²(L₀ + x): 7.5x = 0.1·25·(0.2 + x) = 2.5(0.2 + x) ⇒ 7.5x − 2.5x = 0.5 ⇒ x = 0.1 m.
T = kx = 7.5·0.1.
T = 0.75 N — option (a)
Strong Box · PYQ 11 · JEE Main 2023
A closed circular tube (average radius 15 cm), rough inside, is vertical. A 1 kg block enters at the top at 22 m/s and, after 5 oscillations, stops at the bottom. Find the work done by the tube on the block. (g = 10)
Work–energy: Wtube + Wgravity = ΔKE = 0 − ½(1)(22²) = −242 J.
Top → bottom drop = 2r = 0.3 m ⇒ Wgravity = mg(2r) = 1·10·0.3 = 3 J ⇒ Wtube = −242 − 3.
Wtube = −245 J
Strong Box · PYQ 12 · JEE Main 2023
A car on a circular path of radius 600 m keeps at = ac; initial speed 54 km/h. The time to complete the first quarter revolution is t(1 − e−π/2) s. Find t.
54 km/h = 15 m/s. As in PYQ 7, t = (r/v₀)(1 − e−s/r); quarter revolution ⇒ s = πr/2 ⇒ s/r = π/2.
t = (600/15)(1 − e−π/2) = 40(1 − e−π/2).
t = 40 s
Strong Box · PYQ 13 · JEE Main 2023
A 200 g body on a spring (k = 12.5 N/m) moves in a circle on a smooth horizontal surface at ω = 5 rad/s about O. Find the ratio (extension : natural length). Options: 1:2, 1:1, 2:3, 2:5.
kx = mω²(L₀ + x): 12.5x = 0.2·25·(L₀ + x) = 5(L₀ + x) ⇒ 7.5x = 5L₀.
x/L₀ = 5/7.5 = 2/3.
x : L₀ = 2 : 3 — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 14 · JEE Main 2023
A car moves at constant 20 m/s on a circular horizontal track of radius 40 m. A bob hangs from the roof by a string. Find the angle of the string with the vertical. (g = 10). Options: π/6, π/2, π/4, π/3.
tanθ = ac/g = v²/rg = 20²/(40·10) = 400/400 = 1.
θ = π/4 — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 15 · JEE Main 2023
A particle P revolves about O at radius r, angular velocity ω; at t = 0 it is at 30° above the x-axis, and T = 6 s. Find the projection of OP on the x-axis, x(t).
The x-projection is r·cos(angle). With initial phase π/6: x(t) = r cos(ωt + π/6).

Strong Box · PYQ 17 · JEE Main 2022
A ball is released from rest at P of a smooth hemispherical vessel. Let A = (centripetal force)/(normal reaction) at Q, where Q is at angular position α from P. Which graph shows A vs α from Q to R?
At angle θ (from the bottom): v² = 2gR cosθ ⇒ Fc = mv²/R = 2mg cosθ; N = mg cosθ + mv²/R = 3mg cosθ.
A = Fc/N = 2mg cosθ / 3mg cosθ = 2/3 — constant, independent of position.
A = 2/3 (a horizontal straight line) — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 18 · JEE Main 2022
A boy whirls a 100 g stone on a 2 m string in a horizontal plane; the string bears at most 80 N. The maximum speed is (K/π) rev/min. Find K. Options: 400, 300, 600, 800.
vmax = √(Tr/m) = √(80·2/0.1) = √1600 = 40 m/s ⇒ ω = v/r = 20 rad/s.
rev/min = 60·ω/2π = 60·20/2π = 600/π ⇒ K = 600.
K = 600 — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 19 · JEE Main 2022
A stone tied to a string is whirled in a vertical circle at uniform speed. The tension in the string is: (a) same throughout, (b) minimum when horizontal, (c) minimum at the highest position, (d) minimum at the lowest position.
T − mg cosθ = mv²/r; with v uniform, T is largest where cosθ is largest (bottom) and smallest at the top (θ = 180°): Ttop = mv²/r − mg.
Minimum at the highest position — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 20 · JEE Main 2022
A disc rotates at ω; a beaker sits at distance R from the centre (coefficient μ). The beaker revolves with the disc if: options R ≤ μg/2ω², R ≤ μg/ω², R ≥ μg/2ω², R ≥ μg/ω².
Friction supplies the centripetal force: μmg ≥ mω²R ⇒ R ≤ μg/ω².

Strong Box · PYQ 21 · JEE Advanced 2016
On a disc rotating at ω about a vertical axis, a smooth diametral slot holds a block placed at r = R/2 at t = 0. Find the distance r at time t.
In the rotating frame r̈ = ω²r (constrained to the slot), with r(0) = R/2, ṙ(0) = 0.
r = (R/2)cosh(ωt) = (R/4)(eωt + e−ωt).
r = (R/4)(eωt + e−ωt) — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 22 · JEE Advanced 2016
For the same block, find the net reaction of the disc on the block.
The slot wall supplies the sideways (ĵ) force balancing Coriolis: Ny = 2mω ṙ = 2mω·(R/2)ω sinh(ωt) = ½mω²R(eωt − e−ωt); the disc surface supplies vertical mg k̂.
N⃗ = ½mω²R(eωt − e−ωt)ĵ + mg k̂ — option (b)
Strong Box · PYQ 23 · JEE Advanced 2014
A bob (string ℓ₁) has just enough speed to complete a vertical circle. At the top it collides elastically with an equal-mass bob (string ℓ₂) at rest, which then just completes its own vertical circle. Find ℓ₁/ℓ₂. Options: 1, 3, 5, 1/5.
First bob's top speed = √(gℓ₁). Equal-mass elastic collision ⇒ velocities exchange, so bob 2 gets √(gℓ₁).
Bob 2 must complete its circle: √(gℓ₁) = √(5gℓ₂) ⇒ ℓ₁ = 5ℓ₂.
ℓ₁/ℓ₂ = 5 — option (c)
Strong Box · PYQ 24 · JEE Advanced 2014
A wire is bent into a quarter circle (radius R), fixed vertically, with A at the top and B at the base. A bead released near A slides down (frictionless) to B. What is the direction of the force the bead exerts on the wire?
At angle θ from the vertical: v² = 2gR(1 − cosθ). Radial law (toward centre O): mg cosθ + N = mv²/R ⇒ N = mg(2 − 3cosθ) (N = wire's force on bead, +ve inward).
θ small (cosθ > 2/3): N < 0, wire pushes bead outward ⇒ bead pushes wire inward. θ large (cosθ < 2/3): N > 0 ⇒ bead pushes wire outward.
Radially inwards initially, radially outwards later — option (d)
Strong Box · PYQ 25 · JEE Advanced 2020 (multiple correct)
A student skates up a 30° ramp with speed v₀ and turns around over a semicircular path xyz (radius R) in the ramp plane, reaching maximum height h at y. At the highest point y the turning force is provided by weight only. Which statements are correct?
At y, N = 0 and the centripetal direction is down-slope: the in-plane gravity component g sin30° = g/2 supplies it ⇒ v²y/R = g/2 ⇒ v²y = gR/2.
Energy y: v²y = v₀² − 2gh ⇒ v₀² − 2gh = gR/2 (option a). Speed is greatest at the low ends x, z ⇒ centripetal force mv²/R is maximum there (option d).
Correct: (a) v₀² − 2gh = ½gR  and  (d) centripetal force is maximum at x and z
