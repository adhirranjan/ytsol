Concept The magnitude of a vector is the square root of the sum of the squares of its components. A scalar sitting in front merely scales that length, so pull it out and handle the bracket on its own: |cV| = |c|·|V|. Do not confuse this with normalising to a unit vector, where you would instead divide the vector by its magnitude.
Formula |cV| = |c|·√(Vx²+Vy²+Vz²). The three Cartesian components are mutually perpendicular, so their squares add under the 3-D Pythagoras rule.
Solution
1. Factor the scalar out: A = (2/√3)(î + ĵ + k̂), and the bracket has all three components equal to 1.
2. |î + ĵ + k̂| = √(1²+1²+1²) = √3.Sum of the squares of the three unit components.
3. |A| = (2/√3)·√3 = 2.The √3 cancels, leaving the clean integer 2.
 bracket     |bracket|   |A| = (2/sqrt3)*|bracket|
 i + j + k   sqrt(3)     (2/sqrt3)*sqrt3 = 2
⚡ JEE Trick When a vector is a scalar times a bracket, the scalar stays outside the square root — never square it alongside the components. Here (2/√3) is engineered to cancel the √3 from the three unit components, so the answer collapses to a tidy 2. Distractors 3, 4 and 7 come from dropping the scalar or squaring it wrongly.
Answer: (B) 2.

Concept P splits into two disjoint pieces — the part shared with Q and the part outside it — so n(P − Q) = n(P) − n(P ∩ Q). With K in hand, factorise it and apply the divisor-count formula. The trap is reaching for n(P ∪ Q) or the symmetric difference, neither of which is asked and both of which need n(Q).
Formula n(P − Q) = n(P) − n(P ∩ Q); if K = pa·qb then d(K) = (a+1)(b+1). P = (P ∩ Q) ∪ (P − Q) is a disjoint split, so their counts add.
Solution
1. K = n(P − Q) = n(P) − n(P ∩ Q) = 75 − 55 = 20.Remove from P exactly the elements it shares with Q.
2. Prime-factorise: 20 = 22 · 51.
3. Divisor count d(20) = (2 + 1)(1 + 1) = 3 × 2 = 6.Each prime's exponent runs 0…a, giving (a + 1) choices.
4. List to confirm: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20 — exactly 6 divisors.
5. Guard: n(Q) is never needed; the difference count uses only n(P) and n(P ∩ Q).
6. So the number of divisors of K is 6.
