1In a non-inertial frame, what additional force must be considered? (a) Tension (b) Friction (c) Pseudo force (d) Magnetic force — (Level-1)Non-inertial framesNon-Inertial Frames & Pseudo Forces — ALLEN JEE🟡 SimilarcPseudo force
Concept Newton's second law ΣF = ma is only valid as written in an inertial frame (one that is unaccelerated). If you insist on using it inside a frame that is itself accelerating — a braking bus, a lift, a rotating disc — the bookkeeping only closes if you add one extra term. That term is the pseudo (fictitious/inertial) force. Tension, friction and magnetic force are all real interaction forces that exist in every frame; only the pseudo force appears purely because of the observer's acceleration.
Formula For a frame with acceleration a₀ (m/s²), the equation of motion of a body of mass m (kg) as seen in that frame is ΣFreal + Fpseudo = m arel, with Fpseudo = − m a₀ (N). Mass (kg) × acceleration (m/s²) = newton, so the pseudo term is dimensionally a genuine force and can be added straight into the free-body diagram.
Solution
1. Write Newton's law in the ground (inertial) frame: ΣFreal = m aground.
2. Relate the accelerations: aground = arel + a₀, where a₀ (m/s²) is the frame's own acceleration.Simple vector addition of accelerations — this is the only physics input needed.
3. Substitute: ΣFreal = m(arel + a₀) ⇒ ΣFreal − m a₀ = m arel.
4. The term − m a₀ (N) has been moved onto the force side. Treated as a force it lets you use ΣF = ma unchanged inside the accelerating frame. This is precisely the pseudo force.It always points opposite to the frame's acceleration — which is why you are thrown forward when a bus brakes.
5. Check the distractors: tension is the real pull of a string; friction is the real contact force between surfaces; magnetic force is a real electromagnetic force. None of them is created by the choice of frame. ⇒ option (c).

  │ m │──▸ a = a₀                  │ m │  at rest (a_rel = 0)
  └───┘                            └───┘

  ΣF_real = m a₀        ⇔        ΣF_real + (− m a₀) = 0

In lift problems this simply replaces g by an "effective g": geff = g + a₀ going up, g − a₀ going down. Trap: (b) friction is tempting because friction is what you feel keeping you in place inside an accelerating vehicle — but friction is real and exists in the ground frame too; the question asks what is additional.
Answer: (c) Pseudo force — magnitude m·a₀ (N), directed opposite to the frame's acceleration.

"Moves with constant speed" is the licence to use v = distance/time directly instead of any kinematic equation with acceleration. Everything else is one substitution into ½mv².

  KE = ½ × 0.020 kg × (7.5 m/s)²

3A rain drop of radius 2 mm falls from a height of 250 m above the ground. What is the work done by the gravitational force on the drop? (density ρ = 1000 kg/m³) — (Level-3)W = mgh, mass from densityRain drop of radius 2 mm — work done by gravity (NCERT WEP)🟡 Similar0.0837 J
Concept Work done by gravity depends only on the mass and the vertical drop — never on the path, never on the speed, and never on whatever air resistance is doing. So the entire problem reduces to finding the drop's mass from its radius and density. The tempting mistake is to think the drop's decreasing acceleration (viscous drag) changes Wgravity; it does not — drag affects the kinetic energy, not the work done by weight.
Formula Volume of a sphere V = (4/3)πr³ — r in m, V in m³. Mass m = ρV — ρ in kg/m³, m in kg. Work by gravity W = mgh — g in m/s², h in m, W in J. kg/m³ × m³ = kg ✓, then kg × m/s² × m = J ✓. The book's numbers are built on g = 10 m/s² (with 9.8 the answer would be 0.082 J).
Solution
1. Convert the radius to SI: r = 2 mm = 2 × 10⁻³ m.Cubing a millimetre error costs a factor of 10⁹ — always convert before cubing.
2. Volume of the (spherical) drop:
  V = (4/3)π(2×10⁻³)³ = (4/3)π(8×10⁻⁹) = 3.351×10⁻⁸ m³.
3. Mass from the density:
  m = ρV = 1000 × 3.351×10⁻⁸ = 3.351×10⁻⁵ kg (about 33.5 milligrams).
4. Work done by gravity over the full 250 m fall:
  W = mgh = (3.351×10⁻⁵)(10)(250)
5. Evaluate: W = 3.351×10⁻⁵ × 2500 = 0.0838 J ≈ 0.0837 J.Rounding π·8/3 as 8.378 gives 0.08378 J; the key's 0.0837 J is the same number to three significant figures.
6. Sign: the weight points down and the displacement is down, so the work is positive.

4A block of mass 'm' is placed on a rough inclined plane. The plane is moving with a constant velocity 'v'. The block is not sliding on the inclined plane. Calculate the work done in the time interval of 't' by (i) gravity, (ii) normal reaction, (iii) friction force. — (Level-4)Work by N and f on a translating wedgeJEE Adv — work done by friction in the wedge–block system🟡 Similar0,  −mg sinθ cosθ·vt,  +mg sinθ cosθ·vt
Concept The wedge slides horizontally at constant velocity and the block rides on it without slipping, so the block's displacement is purely horizontal, s = v t, and its acceleration is zero. Zero acceleration means the three forces (weight, normal, friction) balance exactly — and because the total work must equal ΔKE = 0, the three works must sum to zero. The individual works are not zero: N and f each have a horizontal component and therefore each do work, and they must cancel.
Formula W = F⃗ · s⃗ = F s cos α — F in N, s in m, W in J, α the angle between them. Equilibrium on the incline: N = mg cosθ (N) perpendicular, f = mg sinθ (N) up the slope. Displacement s = v t (m), horizontal. Every product below is (newton)×(metre) = joule ✓. No acceleration ⇒ no pseudo force is needed even though the wedge moves.
Solution
1. Set axes: x horizontal (direction of v), y vertical up. Displacement of the block in time t is s⃗ = (vt, 0) m.The block does not slip, so it shares the wedge's motion exactly — constant velocity, so a = 0.
2. (i) Gravity. The weight is (0, −mg) N, perpendicular to a purely horizontal displacement:
  Wg = (0)(vt) + (−mg)(0) = 0.cos 90° = 0 — the block never changes height, so gravity can do no work.
3. (ii) Normal reaction. With the slope rising in the direction of motion, the outward normal is the unit vector (−sinθ, cosθ), and N = mg cosθ:
  N⃗ = mg cosθ(−sinθ, cosθ)
4. Dot with the displacement:
  WN = mg cosθ(−sinθ)(vt) = − mg sinθ cosθ · v t (J).The normal has a backward horizontal component here, so it does negative work — the classic surprise of this problem: N is not always "the force that does no work".
5. (iii) Friction. Static friction holds the block against sliding down, so it acts up the slope, along the unit vector (cosθ, sinθ), with magnitude f = mg sinθ:
  Wf = mg sinθ (cosθ)(vt) = + mg sinθ cosθ · v t (J).
6. Check with the work–energy theorem: Wg + WN + Wf = 0 − mgvt sinθcosθ + mgvt sinθcosθ = 0 = ΔKE ✓, since the block moves at constant velocity.

⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a body rides on a translating support at constant velocity, write ΔKE = 0 first — then you only need to compute one of the two non-zero works and the other is minus it. Trap: the reflex "the normal force never does work" is only true when the surface is stationary (or when the displacement is along the surface). Here the surface itself moves, so N does −mgvt sinθcosθ.
Answer: (i) Wgravity = 0  (ii) Wnormal = − mg sinθ cosθ · v t  (iii) Wfriction = + mg sinθ cosθ · v t  (all in joule; they sum to zero).

It does not slide on the wedge, so static friction acts along the incline face with exactly the magnitude needed to make the along-incline equation work out. Because the displacement is horizontal but the friction is along the slope, only the friction's horizontal component f cosθ does work.
Formula Resolve along and perpendicular to the incline, remembering that the block's acceleration a (m/s²) is horizontal so it has components a cosθ along the slope and − a sinθ perpendicular to it. Solution
1. Kinematics of the block: it starts with the truck and accelerates at a (m/s²), so in time t it moves horizontally
  s = ½ a t² (m).
2. Free-body of the block: weight mg down, normal N ⊥ to the face, static friction f along the face (up the slope).
3. Perpendicular to the incline: N − mg cosθ = m(− a sinθ) ⇒ N = mg cosθ − ma sinθ (N).The horizontal acceleration has a component into the slope, which reduces N.
4. Along the incline (up-slope positive): f − mg sinθ = m(a cosθ) ⇒ f = mg sinθ + ma cosθ (N).Friction must both hold the block against gravity and supply its along-slope acceleration, so the two terms add.

1If the momentum of a particle is plotted on X-axis and its kinetic energy on the Y-axis, the graph is a (a) straight line (b) parabola (c) rectangular hyperbola (d) circle — (Level-1)KE–p graph shapeIf the momentum of a particle is plotted on `X-`axis and its kinetic energy on the `Y-`axis🟢 Exactbparabola
Concept The shape of a graph is decided entirely by the power the plotted variable is raised to. Kinetic energy is quadratic in momentum, K = p²/2m, so with p on the x-axis and K on the y-axis you are plotting y = (constant)·x² — the definition of a parabola through the origin. The mass is held fixed, which is what makes 1/2m a constant.
Formula p = m v — p in kg·m/s, m in kg, v in m/s. K = ½ m v² — K in J. Eliminating v: K = p² / (2m). (kg·m/s)²/kg = kg·m²/s² = J ✓ — the relation is dimensionally sound.
Solution
1. Start from the two definitions and eliminate the velocity: v = p/m.
2. Substitute into the kinetic energy: K = ½ m (p/m)² = ½ p²/m ⇒ K = p²/(2m).This is the single most-used identity of the whole chapter — worth memorising in both directions, p = √(2mK).
3. Compare with the standard curve forms: y = kx is a straight line, y = kx² is a parabola, xy = k is a rectangular hyperbola, x²+y² = k is a circle.
4. Here K = (1/2m) p², i.e. y = k x² with k = 1/2m ⇒ a parabola with its vertex at the origin, opening upwards.Both K and p are taken as positive magnitudes, so only the right-hand half of the parabola is physically drawn.
5. Slope check: dK/dp = p/m = v — the slope at any point equals the speed, which grows with p, confirming a curve that steepens (not a straight line).

2When two identical balls are moving with equal speeds in opposite direction, which of the following is true? For the system of two bodies (a) momentum is zero, kinetic energy is zero (b) momentum is not zero, kinetic energy is zero (c) momentum is zero, kinetic energy not zero (d) momentum is not zero, kinetic energy is not zero — (Level-1)Vector vs scalar additionWhen two identical balls are moving with equal speed in opposite direction, which of the🟢 Exactcmomentum is zero, kinetic energy not zero
Concept This question is a one-line test of a single idea: momentum is a vector, kinetic energy is a scalar. Opposite directions make the two momenta cancel, but kinetic energy contains v², and squaring destroys the sign, so the two kinetic energies add. This is exactly why a head-on collision of two identical cars is so destructive even though the total momentum is zero.
Formula p⃗total = m v⃗₁ + m v⃗₂ (kg·m/s) — a vector sum, so directions matter. Ktotal = ½ m v₁² + ½ m v₂² (J) — a scalar sum of two non-negative numbers. Both sides of the momentum equation are kg·m/s and both sides of the energy equation are J; the difference is purely that one carries a direction and the other does not.
Solution
1. Set up coordinates: ball 1 has v⃗₁ = +v x̂, ball 2 has v⃗₂ = −v x̂, both of mass m (identical balls, equal speeds, opposite directions).
2. Total momentum: p⃗ = m(+v) + m(−v) = mv − mv = 0.Equal magnitudes, opposite signs — exact cancellation, which is why the system's centre of mass is at rest.
3. Total kinetic energy: K = ½mv² + ½m(−v)² = ½mv² + ½mv² = mv², which is strictly positive for v ≠ 0.(−v)² = +v²: squaring erases the minus sign, so nothing can cancel.
4. Therefore momentum = 0 but kinetic energy ≠ 0 ⇒ option (c).
5. Rule out the rest: (a) needs K = 0, which would require both balls at rest; (b) is impossible because K = 0 forces both speeds to zero, which forces p = 0 too; (d) contradicts step 2.
      ●──▸ v                    v ◂──●
     m  (ball 1)            (ball 2)  m

   momentum (vector) :  +mv  +  (−mv)   = 0
   kinetic energy (scalar):
                       ½mv²  +   ½mv²   = mv²  ≠ 0

   ⇒ p = 0  but  K > 0            (option c)
⚡ JEE Trick Carry this one-liner: "K = 0 ⇒ p = 0, but p = 0 does NOT imply K = 0." That single implication kills options (a), (b) and (d) at once and is the whole content of the question. It also explains a classic follow-up: in a perfectly inelastic head-on collision of these two balls, momentum stays zero and all the kinetic energy mv² is converted to heat/deformation.
Answer: (c) momentum is zero, kinetic energy is not zero (K = mv²).

4Two bodies having kinetic energies K₁ and K₂ have equal masses. Their momenta are p₁ and p₂ respectively, then p₁/p₂ is (a) K₁:K₂ (b) K₂:K₁ (c) √K₂ : √K₁ (d) √K₁ : √K₂ — (Level-1)p = √(2mK), equal massesMomentum & Kinetic Energy relation — KE = p²/2m🟡 Similard√K₁ : √K₂
Concept Invert the master relation: from K = p²/2m you get p = √(2mK). With the masses equal, the factor √(2m) is common to both bodies and cancels in the ratio, leaving momentum proportional to the square root of kinetic energy. Recognising which quantity is held fixed (mass here, energy in the next question) is the entire skill being tested.
Formula p = √(2 m K) — p in kg·m/s, m in kg, K in J. √(kg × kg·m²/s²) = √(kg²m²/s²) = kg·m/s ✓. With m₁ = m₂ = m: p₁/p₂ = √(K₁/K₂).
Solution
1. Rearrange K = p²/(2m) for p: multiply both sides by 2m and take the positive square root ⇒ p = √(2mK).
2. Apply it to each body: p₁ = √(2m K₁), p₂ = √(2m K₂) — the same m in both, since the masses are equal.
3. Take the ratio:

Formula Kinetic friction f = μ m g (N); crate's acceleration a = μ g (m/s²); time to match speeds t₀ = v/a (s); crate displacement sc = v²/(2a) (m); belt displacement sb = v t₀ (m). Works: Won crate = +f sc, Won belt = −f sb, motor supplies Wmotor = +f sb (J). N·m = J ✓. g = 10 m/s².
Solution
1. Option (a): acceleration. The only horizontal force on the crate is kinetic friction:
  a = μ g = 0.4 × 10 = 4 m/s², not 2 m/s² ⇒ (a) is wrong.The mass cancels — the 300 kg is irrelevant to the acceleration, though it matters for the forces and energies.
2. Time and distances. The crate speeds up from 0 to 4 m/s:
  t₀ = 4/4 = 1 s; crate travels sc = ½(4)(1²) = 2 m; belt travels sb = 4 × 1 = 4 m (it never slows down).
3. Friction force: f = μ m g = 0.4 × 300 × 10 = 1200 N.
4. Option (b): work on the crate. Friction acts forward on the crate over its 2 m:
  W = +1200 × 2 = +2400 J = 2.4 kJ ⇒ (b) is correct. (Check: ΔKE = ½(300)(4²) = 2400 J ✓.)
5. Option (c): work on the belt. By Newton's third law the crate drags the belt backward with 1200 N, while the belt moves forward 4 m:
  W = −1200 × 4 = −4800 J = −4.8 kJ ⇒ (c) is correct.
6. Option (d): work by the motor. To keep the belt at constant speed the motor must exactly cancel that −4.8 kJ:
  Wmotor = +4800 J = 4.8 kJ, not 7.2 kJ ⇒ (d) is wrong.
7. Energy audit (a good habit): motor supplies 4.8 kJ; the crate gains 2.4 kJ of kinetic energy; the remaining 4.8 − 2.4 = 2.4 kJ becomes heat, equal to f × (relative sliding) = 1200 × (4 − 2) = 2400 J ✓. Exactly half the motor's energy is wasted — a famous result for a crate dropped from rest onto a belt.

Concept Two distinct special positions must be kept apart. The maximum extension is where the system is momentarily at rest (KE = 0), found from a pure rest-to-rest energy balance. The maximum kinetic energy occurs at the equilibrium position, where the spring force just balances the hanging weight — that is where the net force, and hence dK/dx, is zero. Everything else in the option list follows from evaluating quantities at those two positions.
Formula Let x (m) be the extension = distance the hanging block 2m has descended. Energy: K(x) = 2mg x − ½ k x² (J). Maximum extension: K = 0. Maximum K: dK/dx = 2mg − kx = 0 ⇒ xeq = 2mg/k. Spring energy: U = ½ k x² (J). kg·m/s²·m = J and (N/m)m² = J ✓; note m²g²/k has units kg²(m/s²)²/(N/m) = J ✓.
Solution
1. Set up the energy function. The string is inextensible, so both blocks move the same distance x; the block m slides on a frictionless table (no energy change from gravity), while 2m descends x:
  K(x) = 2 m g x − ½ k x² (J).

  2 m g x = ½ k x² ⇒ xmax = 4mg/k ⇒ (a) is correct.Once again the maximum deformation is exactly twice the equilibrium value 2mg/k.
3. Option (b): maximum kinetic energy. K(x) is a downward parabola, maximum where dK/dx = 2mg − kx = 0 ⇒ xeq = 2mg/k. Substitute:
  Kmax = 2mg(2mg/k) − ½k(2mg/k)² = 4m²g²/k − 2m²g²/k = 2m²g²/k ⇒ (b) is correct.
