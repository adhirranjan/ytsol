1 (i)Fill in the blank: 1 + ω + ω² = ____Cube roots of unityComplex Numbers 07 | Cube Roots of Unity | Class 11 | JEE | Pace Series🟡 Similar0
Concept The cube roots of unity are the three solutions of z³ = 1. Factorising z³ − 1 = (z − 1)(z² + z + 1) splits them into the real root z = 1 and the two non-real roots of z² + z + 1 = 0, which are called ω and ω². So the relation 1 + ω + ω² = 0 is not a separate fact to memorise — it is literally the quadratic that produced ω, evaluated at z = ω. Geometrically the three roots sit at the vertices of an equilateral triangle inscribed in the unit circle, so their vector sum has to land on the centre, 0.
Formula ω = (−1 + i√3)/2, ω² = (−1 − i√3)/2, ω³ = 1. Both non-real roots come from the quadratic formula applied to z² + z + 1 = 0.
Solution
1. Write the defining equation as z³ − 1 = (z − 1)(z² + z + 1) = 0, so either z = 1 or z² + z + 1 = 0.
2. Solve the quadratic: z = (−1 ± √(1 − 4))/2 = (−1 ± i√3)/2, so ω = (−1 + i√3)/2 and ω² is its conjugate.
3. Vieta on z² + z + 1 = 0 gives ω + ω² = −1 and ω·ω² = 1. Sum of roots = −b/a = −1, product = c/a = 1.
4. Therefore 1 + ω + ω² = 1 + (ω + ω²) = 1 + (−1) = 0.
5. Direct check by addition: 1 + (−1 + i√3)/2 + (−1 − i√3)/2 = 1 + (−2/2) = 1 − 1 = 0. The imaginary parts are equal and opposite, so they cancel before the real parts do.
6. Geometric-series check: 1 + ω + ω² = (ω³ − 1)/(ω − 1) = 0/(ω − 1) = 0, legitimate because ω ≠ 1. This is the same argument that later gives the sum of all n nth roots of unity as 0.

Concept ω is by definition a root of z³ = 1, so ω³ = 1 exactly. Raising an expression that already equals 1 to any integer power still gives 1, and that single observation is the whole question. The subtlety worth noticing is that n is stated to lie in I, the integers, so n may be negative or zero as well as positive; the result survives all three cases because ω ≠ 0 makes negative powers perfectly well defined. This is why powers of ω only ever need the exponent's remainder modulo 3 — every multiple of 3 in the exponent is invisible.
Formula ω³ = 1 ⟹ ω³ⁿ = (ω³)ⁿ = 1ⁿ = 1, and ω^m = ω^(m mod 3). The index law (a^p)^q = a^pq holds for all integers p, q whenever a ≠ 0.
Solution
1. Start from the definition: ω satisfies z³ = 1, hence ω³ = 1.
2. Regroup the exponent: ω³ⁿ = ω^(3·n) = (ω³)ⁿ = 1ⁿ = 1.
3. Case n > 0: ω³ⁿ is a product of n copies of ω³, i.e. 1·1·…·1 = 1.
4. Case n = 0: ω⁰ = 1 since ω ≠ 0, so the value is again 1. ω has modulus 1, so it can never be 0 and no power is undefined.
5. Case n < 0, say n = −m with m > 0: ω³ⁿ = 1/(ω³)^m = 1/1 = 1, so negative integers give the same answer. This is exactly why ω⁻¹ = ω² and ω⁻² = ω are usable identities.
6. Sample values confirm it: ω⁶ = (ω³)² = 1, ω⁹ = 1, ω⁻³ = 1. Any exponent splits as 3n + r, and only the remainder r ∈ {0, 1, 2} controls the value.

Concept The sum 1 + ω^k + ω^2k is the standard "filter" built from cube roots of unity, and it has only two possible values. Because ω³ = 1, the powers ω^k and ω^2k depend solely on k mod 3, so the whole sum is decided by that remainder. When k is a multiple of 3 both powers collapse to 1 and the three terms add to 3; for any other k the three terms are 1, ω, ω² in some order and add to 0. Here k ∈ 3n with n ∈ N, i.e. k is a positive multiple of 3, so we are in the collapsing case.
Formula 1 + ω^k + ω^2k = 3 if 3 | k, and = 0 otherwise. Because ω^k = ω^(k mod 3), the sum only ever reads the remainder of k on division by 3.
Solution
1. Put k = 3n with n ∈ N, so the two exponents are k = 3n and 2k = 6n, both multiples of 3.
2. Reduce the middle term: ω^k = ω³ⁿ = (ω³)ⁿ = 1ⁿ = 1.
3. Reduce the last term: ω^2k = ω⁶ⁿ = (ω³)²ⁿ = 1²ⁿ = 1. Doubling a multiple of 3 keeps it a multiple of 3, so both terms collapse together.
4. Add: 1 + ω^k + ω^2k = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3.
5. Spot-check k = 3: 1 + ω³ + ω⁶ = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3; and k = 6: 1 + ω⁶ + ω¹² = 3, matching the general result.
6. Contrast with k = 4 (not a multiple of 3): ω⁴ = ω and ω⁸ = ω², so the sum is 1 + ω + ω² = 0. This is what makes the expression a selector: it returns 3 exactly on the multiples of 3 and 0 elsewhere, which is how coefficients of x³ⁿ are extracted from a binomial expansion.

The classic error is quoting 1 + ω + ω² = 0 reflexively and answering 0 here, without noticing that ω^3n is 1, not ω. Remember also that 2k is a multiple of 3 whenever k is, so the two non-constant terms always switch together and no mixed case such as 1 + 1 + ω can arise.

Concept This is the companion case to the previous blank. Since ω³ = 1, every power of ω is one of 1, ω, ω², chosen by the exponent's remainder modulo 3. When k leaves remainder 1, the exponent k gives ω and the exponent 2k leaves remainder 2 and gives ω², so the three terms of 1 + ω^k + ω^2k are precisely 1, ω, ω² — the full set of cube roots of unity — and their sum is 0. The point of the pair of blanks is that this expression is a 3-way selector: it equals 3 when 3 divides k and 0 for both other remainders.
Formula ω^k = ω^(k mod 3) and 1 + ω + ω² = 0. The first identity reduces the exponents, the second finishes the addition in one line.
Solution

2. Reduce the middle term: ω^k = ω^(3n+1) = (ω³)ⁿ·ω = 1·ω = ω.
3. Reduce the last term: ω^2k = ω^(6n+2) = (ω³)²ⁿ·ω² = 1·ω² = ω². 6n + 2 leaves remainder 2 on division by 3, so this term lands on ω², not ω.
4. Add the reduced terms: 1 + ω^k + ω^2k = 1 + ω + ω² = 0, by the defining relation of ω.
5. Spot-check k = 4: ω⁴ = ω and ω⁸ = ω², so the sum is 1 + ω + ω² = 0; k = 7 gives ω⁷ = ω and ω¹⁴ = ω², again 0.
6. The remaining case k = 3n + 2 also gives 0, because then ω^k = ω² and ω^2k = ω⁴ = ω, the same three terms reordered. Addition is commutative, so a reordering of {1, ω, ω²} cannot change the sum.

Concept This is the ω-factorisation of the symmetric expression a³ + b³ + c³ − 3abc. The real-algebra identity a³ + b³ + c³ − 3abc = (a + b + c)(a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca) is standard, and cube roots of unity split the awkward quadratic factor into two conjugate linear factors. The engine is 1 + ω + ω² = 0, which turns every cross term ω + ω² into −1, together with ω³ = 1 clearing the higher powers. Once the product of the last two brackets is shown to be a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca, the identity is complete and each exponent on the right is 3.
Formula ω³ = 1, ω⁴ = ω, ω + ω² = −1. These three reductions are the only facts used in the expansion.
Solution
1. Multiply the last two brackets term by term: (a + bω + cω²)(a + bω² + cω) = a² + abω² + acω + abω + b²ω³ + bcω² + acω² + bcω⁴ + c²ω³.
2. Clear the high powers with ω³ = 1 and ω⁴ = ω: b²ω³ = b², c²ω³ = c², bcω⁴ = bcω.
3. Group the cross terms: ab(ω + ω²) + bc(ω² + ω) + ac(ω + ω²) = −ab − bc − ca, since ω + ω² = −1. Every pair of unlike letters picks up exactly one ω and one ω², which is why the identity is symmetric.
4. So (a + bω + cω²)(a + bω² + cω) = a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca.
5. Multiply by the first bracket: (a + b + c)(a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca) = a³ + b³ + c³ − 3abc, the standard identity.
6. Compare with a^p + b^q + c^r − 3abc term by term: p = 3, q = 3, r = 3. A quick sanity test with a = 1, b = 1, c = 0 gives left side 2·(1 + ω)(1 + ω²) = 2·(−ω²)(−ω) = 2, and 1³ + 1³ + 0 − 0 = 2.

Concept "Complex cube roots of unity" means the two non-real roots ω and ω², excluding the real root 1. A monic quadratic is completely determined by the sum and product of its roots, so all that is needed is ω + ω² and ω·ω². Both come free from the two standing relations 1 + ω + ω² = 0 and ω³ = 1. The word "simplest" fixes the overall scaling: any multiple k(x² + x + 1) = 0 has the same roots, so the intended answer is the primitive one with p = 1.
Formula x² − (sum)x + (product) = 0. This is Vieta run backwards, and it is monic by construction, which is exactly what "simplest form" asks for.
Solution
1. Name the roots: the complex cube roots of unity are ω and ω², the two solutions of z³ = 1 other than z = 1.
2. Sum of roots: ω + ω² = −1, read straight off 1 + ω + ω² = 0.
3. Product of roots: ω·ω² = ω³ = 1. ω and ω² are conjugates of modulus 1, so their product is |ω|² = 1, which agrees.
4. Build the quadratic: x² − (−1)x + (1) = 0, i.e. x² + x + 1 = 0.

Concept Any equation of the form (something)³ = k has three roots, and cube roots of unity generate the other two from the obvious one. The mechanism is simple: if u³ = v³ then (u/v)³ = 1, so u/v ∈ {1, ω, ω²} and u = v, vω or vω². Here the substitution w = z − 3 turns the equation into w³ = −27, whose obvious cube root is w = −3, and the remaining two are −3ω and −3ω². Undoing the shift with z = w + 3 then produces all three values of z at once, no cubic expansion required.
Formula w³ = c³ ⟹ w = c, cω, cω². Dividing by c reduces the equation to (w/c)³ = 1, the definition of the cube roots of unity.
Solution
