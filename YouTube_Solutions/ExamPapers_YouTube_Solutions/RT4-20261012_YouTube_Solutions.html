5The potential energy of a particle under a conservative force is given by U(x) = 2x2 − 5x. Determine the equilibrium position(s) of the particle. (A) x = 5/4 (B) x = 7/4 (C) x = 3/4 (D) x = 9/4Equilibrium from U(x)The potential energy of a conservative force field is given by U = ax² − bx: find the equilibrium position and its nature🟡 SimilarAx = 5/4similarP8 · L5 · SolvedEx · Ex-4
Concept For a conservative force, F = −dU/dx. A particle is in equilibrium where the net force is zero, i.e. where the slope of the U–x graph is zero. So differentiate U, set the derivative to zero and solve. The second derivative then tells us the type: positive means a minimum of U (stable), negative means a maximum (unstable).
Formula F = −dU/dx = 0 at equilibrium; d2U/dx2 > 0 ⇒ stable. Zero slope of the potential-energy curve means zero force on the particle.
Solution
1. dU/dx = d(2x2 − 5x)/dx = 4x − 5.
2. F = −(4x − 5) = 5 − 4x.Force is the negative gradient of potential energy.
3. Equilibrium: 5 − 4x = 0 ⇒ x = 5/4 = 1.25.
4. Nature: d2U/dx2 = 4 > 0, so U is a minimum at x = 5/4 — stable equilibrium (and the only one, since U is a quadratic).An upward parabola has exactly one turning point, its vertex.
 U  = 2x^2 - 5x
 U' = 4x - 5 = 0   ->  x = 5/4
 U''= 4 > 0        ->  stable (minimum of U)
⚡ JEE Trick For U = ax2 − bx, equilibrium is at the vertex x = b/2a = 5/(2 × 2) = 5/4 — no calculus needed. Do not set U = 0 (that gives x = 0 or 5/2, the roots of U, not the equilibrium); equilibrium is about the slope of U, not its value.
Answer: (A) x = 5/4.
