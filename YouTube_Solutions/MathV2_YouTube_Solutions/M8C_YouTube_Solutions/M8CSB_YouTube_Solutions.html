1If a₁, a₂, …, aₙ are in H.P., then the expression a₁a₂ + a₂a₃ + … + aₙ₋₁aₙ is equal to   (a) n(a₁ − aₙ)   (b) (n − 1)(a₁ − aₙ)   (c) na₁aₙ   (d) (n − 1)a₁aₙ   [JEE Main 2006] (Level-3)The telescoping product sum of an H.P.If a₁, a₂, …, aₙ are in HP, then a₁a₂ + a₂a₃ + … + aₙ₋₁aₙ [AIEEE 2006]🟢 Exactd(n − 1)a₁aₙ
Concept An H.P. has no sum formula — but this is not a sum of the terms, it is a sum of consecutive products, and that telescopes. Each product aₖaₖ₊₁ can be rewritten as a difference divided by the common difference of the reciprocal A.P., after which the interior terms annihilate in pairs. This is Solved Example-3 of the module's L-1, repeated verbatim by AIEEE.
Formula 1/aₖ₊₁ − 1/aₖ = d ⇒ aₖaₖ₊₁ = (aₖ − aₖ₊₁)/d, and end to end 1/aₙ − 1/a₁ = (n − 1)d. n terms are separated by exactly n − 1 gaps — that is where the (n − 1) comes from.
Solution
1. Since a₁, …, aₙ are in H.P., their reciprocals form an A.P.; call its common difference d, so 1/aₖ₊₁ − 1/aₖ = d for every k.
2. Combine that over a common denominator: (aₖ − aₖ₊₁)/(aₖaₖ₊₁) = d, hence aₖaₖ₊₁ = (aₖ − aₖ₊₁)/d.This is the key rewrite — it converts a product into a difference.
3. Sum for k = 1 to n − 1: Σ aₖaₖ₊₁ = [(a₁ − a₂) + (a₂ − a₃) + … + (aₙ₋₁ − aₙ)]/d.
4. Every interior term appears once with a plus and once with a minus, so the bracket collapses to just (a₁ − aₙ). The sum is therefore (a₁ − aₙ)/d.
5. Now relate d to the two end terms: stepping from 1/a₁ to 1/aₙ takes n − 1 gaps, so 1/aₙ − 1/a₁ = (n − 1)d, i.e. (a₁ − aₙ)/(a₁aₙ) = (n − 1)d.
6. Rearranging that gives (a₁ − aₙ)/d = (n − 1)a₁aₙ, which is exactly the sum computed in step 4.
7. So the expression equals (n − 1)a₁aₙ ⇒ option (d).
8. Verify on the H.P. 6, 4, 3 (n = 3): 6·4 + 4·3 = 24 + 12 = 36, and (n − 1)a₁aₙ = 2 × 6 × 3 = 36 ✓ — options (a), (b), (c) give 9, 6 and 54.
  a1a2 + a2a3 + ... + a(n-1)an
    = [ (a1-a2) + (a2-a3) + ... + (a(n-1)-an) ] / d      <-- telescope
    = (a1 - an) / d
  and  1/an - 1/a1 = (n-1)d   =>   (a1-an)/(a1 an) = (n-1)d
                              =>   (a1-an)/d = (n-1) a1 an
⚡ JEE Trick Do not derive this under exam pressure — test it. The smallest H.P. everyone knows is 6, 4, 3 with n = 3; substituting kills three options in five seconds. Note the dimensional tell too: the expression is a sum of products, so the answer must contain a₁aₙ, which already eliminates (a) and (b).
Answer: (d) (n − 1)a₁aₙ.
2If in a triangle ABC the altitudes from the vertices A, B, C on the opposite sides are in H.P., then sin A, sin B, sin C are in   (a) G.P.   (b) Arithmetic–Geometric Progression   (c) H.P.   (d) A.P.   [JEE Main 2005] (Level-5)Altitudes, sides and the sine rule[IIT 1998] In triangle ABC, if sin A, sin B, sin C are in A.P. then the altitudes are in H.P.🟢 ExactdA.P.
Concept One number — the area — ties all three altitudes together. Because Δ = ½ × base × height is the same whichever side you call the base, each altitude is inversely proportional to its own side. So "altitudes in H.P." flips straight into "sides in A.P.", and the sine rule carries that over to the sines.
Formula Δ = ½·a·p_a = ½·b·p_b = ½·c·p_c ⇒ p_a = 2Δ/a · sine rule a = 2R sin A. 2Δ and 2R are constants for the triangle, so proportionality is preserved.
Solution
1. Let p_a, p_b, p_c be the altitudes from A, B, C onto sides a, b, c, and let Δ be the area.
2. Computing the area three ways: Δ = ½·a·p_a = ½·b·p_b = ½·c·p_c, so p_a = 2Δ/a, p_b = 2Δ/b, p_c = 2Δ/c.
3. Hence the altitudes are proportional to 1/a, 1/b, 1/c — the constant 2Δ scales all three equally and does not affect which progression they form.
4. Given: p_a, p_b, p_c are in H.P. ⇒ their reciprocals are in A.P. ⇒ a/(2Δ), b/(2Δ), c/(2Δ) are in A.P. ⇒ a, b, c are in A.P.Dividing an A.P. by the non-zero constant 2Δ leaves an A.P.
5. By the sine rule a = 2R sin A, b = 2R sin B, c = 2R sin C — again a common constant factor 2R.
6. So sin A, sin B, sin C are a fixed multiple of a, b, c, and therefore also in A.P. ⇒ option (d).
7. Sanity check with a 3-4-5 right triangle (sides in A.P.): area = 6, so the altitudes are 12/3 = 4, 12/4 = 3, 12/5 = 2.4. Their reciprocals 1/4, 1/3, 5/12 = 3/12, 4/12, 5/12 are in A.P. ✓ — the altitudes really are in H.P.
   altitudes  p_a : p_b : p_c  =  1/a : 1/b : 1/c
   p's in H.P.  ->  1/p's in A.P.  ->  a, b, c in A.P.
   sine rule    ->  sinA, sinB, sinC in A.P.        <-- (d)

   check 3-4-5 :  area 6, altitudes 4, 3, 2.4
                  reciprocals 3/12, 4/12, 5/12   A.P.  ok
⚡ JEE Trick Memorise the equivalence chain altitudes in H.P. ⇔ sides in A.P. ⇔ sines in A.P. — it is reversible and both directions have been asked (IIT 1998 posed it the other way round, which is what the linked video solves). The engine is always p_a = 2Δ/a. Trap: answering H.P. because the question said H.P.; the reciprocal relationship swaps it to A.P.
Answer: (d) A.P.
3Let a₁, a₂, a₃, … be in harmonic progression with a₁ = 5 and a₂₀ = 25. The least positive integer n for which aₙ < 0 is   (a) 25   (b) 22   (c) 23   (d) 24   [JEE Advanced 2012 (Paper 2)] (Level-4)Where an H.P. changes signLet a₁, a₂, a₃ … be in harmonic progression with a₁ = 5 and a₂₀ = 25 — the least positive integer n🟢 Exacta25
Concept An H.P. whose terms grow has a reciprocal A.P. that shrinks — and a shrinking A.P. must eventually cross zero and go negative. A number and its reciprocal always share a sign, so aₙ < 0 happens exactly when the reciprocal A.P. term goes negative. That turns the whole question into one linear inequality.
Formula bₙ = 1/aₙ = b₁ + (n − 1)d with b₁ = 1/5 and b₂₀ = 1/25, so 19d = 1/25 − 1/5. Then solve bₙ < 0. Nineteen gaps between positions 1 and 20.
Solution
1. Work in the reciprocal A.P.: b₁ = 1/5 and b₂₀ = 1/25.
2. 19d = 1/25 − 1/5 = (1 − 5)/25 = −4/25 ⇒ d = −4/475.d is negative, which is what forces the sign change.
3. Since aₙ and bₙ = 1/aₙ always share a sign, the condition aₙ < 0 is the same as bₙ < 0.
4. So require 1/5 + (n − 1)(−4/475) < 0, i.e. (n − 1)·(4/475) > 1/5.
5. Multiply both sides by 475/4: n − 1 > 475/(5 × 4) = 23.75, so n > 24.75.
6. The least integer strictly greater than 24.75 is n = 25 ⇒ option (a).
7. Confirm the boundary: b₂₄ = 1/5 − 23(4/475) = 95/475 − 92/475 = +3/475 > 0, while b₂₅ = 95/475 − 96/475 = −1/475 < 0 ✓ — so a₂₄ is still positive (and huge, ≈ 158) and a₂₅ is the first negative term.
   b_n = 1/5 - (n-1)(4/475)   =  (95 - 4(n-1)) / 475
   b_24 = (95 - 92)/475 = +3/475   ->  a_24 = +158.3   still positive
   b_25 = (95 - 96)/475 = -1/475   ->  a_25 = -475     first negative
   least n = 25
⚡ JEE Trick Never test H.P. terms one by one — solve the linear inequality on the reciprocal A.P. and take the first integer past the boundary. Note the term just before the flip is enormous (a₂₄ ≈ 158, up from a₁ = 5): that blow-up is the signature of an H.P. approaching its sign change. The same reasoning answers "largest positive term" questions (Suggested Problem 5 of L-1).
Answer: (a) 25.
