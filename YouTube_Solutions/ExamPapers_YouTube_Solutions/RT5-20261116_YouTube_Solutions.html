✓		A chain of length l and mass m lies on the surface of a smooth hemisphere of radius R > l with one end tied to the top of the hemisphere. Find the gravitational potential energy of the chain. (A) (mgR2/l) sin(l/R) (B) (2mgR2/l) sin(l/R) (C) (3mgR2/l) sin(l/R) (D) (4mgR2/l) sin(l/R)PE of chain on hemisphereA chain of length l and mass m lies on the surface of a smooth hemisphere of radius R > l with one end tied to the top 🟢 ExactA(mgR2/l) sin(l/R)exactP8 · SB · Lvl10 · Q1
Concept The chain is not a point mass, so its potential energy is the sum of mgh over every small piece. Each piece sits at a different height on the curved surface, so we slice the chain into arc elements, measure each one’s height above the centre (the flat base of the hemisphere, our zero level) and integrate along the angle the chain covers, l/R radian.
Formula U = ∫0l/R (m/l)(R dθ) g (R cosθ) = (mgR2/l) sin(l/R). Linear density m/l, element length R dθ, height R cosθ above the centre, with θ measured from the vertical through the top.
Solution
1. Linear mass density λ = m/l; an element at angle θ from the top has length R dθ and mass dm = (m/l)R dθ.
2. Its height above the base plane is h = R cosθ.The surface is a circle of radius R about the centre.
3. dU = dm·g·h = (m/l)R dθ · g · R cosθ = (mgR2/l) cosθ dθ.
4. Limits: the chain starts at θ = 0 (top) and covers arc l, i.e. up to θ = l/R (< 1 rad since R > l, so it never passes the rim).
5. U = (mgR2/l)[sinθ]0l/R = (mgR2/l) sin(l/R).
6. Check: for a very short chain sin(l/R) ≈ l/R, so U → mgR — the whole chain at the top, height R. ✓
⚡ JEE Trick All four options share sin(l/R) and differ only by a number, so a limit check decides it instantly: as l → 0 the chain is a point at height R and U must be mgR. Option (A) gives mgR; (B), (C), (D) give 2mgR, 3mgR, 4mgR — impossible for a body never higher than R.
Answer: (A) (mgR2/l) sin(l/R).
