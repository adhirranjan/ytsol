1Expression for the time taken to reach the highest point from the point of projection: (a) u cosθ/g (b) 2u sinθ/g (c) u sinθ/g (d) g/u sinθTime to reach maximum heightA body is projected up with speed u; time taken T to reach maximum height H🟡 Similarcu sinθ / g
Concept A projectile's motion splits into two independent one-dimensional motions: horizontal (constant velocity, ax = 0) and vertical (uniform acceleration ay = −g). The highest point is defined purely by the vertical motion — it is the instant when the vertical velocity component becomes zero. The horizontal component u cosθ never changes and never vanishes, so it plays no part in locating the peak.
Formula Resolve the launch velocity: ux = u cosθ (m s⁻¹), uy = u sinθ (m s⁻¹). Vertical motion obeys vy = u sinθ − g·t, with vy in m s⁻¹, g in m s⁻² and t in s. Taking "up" as positive makes gravity −g, so vy falls linearly with time. At the peak vy = 0.
Solution
1. Resolve u into components: uy = u sinθ (m s⁻¹) upward, ux = u cosθ (m s⁻¹) horizontal.Only uy is acted on by gravity; ux stays constant throughout.
2. Apply v = u + at to the vertical direction only: vy(t) = u sinθ − g·t (m s⁻¹).
3. Impose the peak condition vy = 0 at t = tp: 0 = u sinθ − g·tp.At the highest point the body has stopped rising and has not yet started falling, so its vertical velocity is momentarily zero.
4. Solve for tp: g·tp = u sinθ ⇒ tp = u sinθ / g (s) — option (c).
5. Dimensional check: (m s⁻¹) ÷ (m s⁻²) = s ✓ — a time, as required.Option (d) g/(u sinθ) inverts this and gives s⁻¹, so it cannot be a time.

5Water flowing through a horizontal pipe fixed at a height of 2 m above the ground strikes the ground at a distance of 3 m from the pipe. Concept Water leaving a horizontal pipe is a horizontal projectile: at the moment it leaves it has speed v pointing horizontally and zero vertical velocity. From then on gravity alone acts, so the vertical drop is a free fall from rest through the pipe height, while the horizontal travel is at the unchanging speed v. The two motions share only one thing — the same time of flight — and that shared t is the bridge that links the known height (2 m) and the known ground distance (3 m) to the unknown v.
Formula Vertical (free fall from rest, uy = 0): h = ½·g·t² ⇒ t = √(2h/g), h in m, g in m s⁻², t in s. Horizontal (constant velocity): x = v·t ⇒ v = x / t, x in m, v in m s⁻¹. Assumption: the jet is treated as a projectile — air drag and the spreading/streaming of the water are neglected, so every element follows the same parabola. Given h = 2 m, x = 3 m, g = 9.8 m s⁻².
Solution
1. Set the initial conditions at the pipe mouth: ux = v (m s⁻¹, unknown), uy = 0 m s⁻¹, drop h = 2 m, horizontal distance x = 3 m.The pipe is horizontal, so no part of the exit velocity points downward at t = 0.
2. Vertical: 2 m = ½ × 9.8 m s⁻² × t² ⇒ t² = (2 × 2 m)/(9.8 m s⁻²) = 4/9.8 = 0.4082 s².
3. Take the square root: t = √0.4082 s² = 0.6389 s.This fall time depends only on h and g — a faster jet would land further away, but not sooner.
4. Horizontal: in that same 0.6389 s the water covers x = 3 m at constant speed v, so v = x ÷ t = 3 m ÷ 0.6389 s.
5. v = 4.70 m s⁻¹ (≈ 4.7 m/s).Units check: m ÷ s = m s⁻¹ ✓ a speed.
6. Verify by going forward: in 0.6389 s at 4.70 m s⁻¹ the jet travels 4.70 × 0.6389 = 3.00 m ✓, while dropping ½ × 9.8 × 0.6389² = 2.00 m ✓.
