1If adhesive force > cohesive force, the meniscus is: (a) Convex (b) Flat (c) Concave (d) Irregular (Level-1)Meniscus shape: adhesion vs cohesionStatement-1 : If adhesion is greater than cohesion, then the meniscus is concave. And Statement-2 : 🟡 SimilarcConcave
Concept When adhesion between liquid and wall exceeds cohesion inside the liquid, the liquid is pulled up the wall and wets it, so the contact angle is acute.
Formula Acute contact angle (θ < 90°) ⇒ concave meniscus, liquid rises; cohesion > adhesion gives θ > 90°, convex (mercury).
Solution
1. Adhesive force (liquid–glass) > cohesive force (liquid–liquid).
2. Liquid molecules at the wall are pulled more strongly by glass than by neighbours, so the liquid climbs the wall.
3. The liquid edge curves upward at the wall: surface is concave (viewed from above), contact angle θ < 90°.
4. Pressure just below a concave surface is lower than above it by 2T/R, so the liquid rises in a capillary.
5. A flat meniscus needs θ = 90° (adhesion balances cohesion component); convex needs cohesion > adhesion.
  adhesion > cohesion => theta < 90 deg => concave
  cohesion > adhesion => theta > 90 deg => convex (Hg-glass)
  balanced            => theta = 90 deg => flat
⚡ JEE Trick Wetting = concave = rise; non-wetting = convex = depression. Quick check: water in glass is concave.
Answer: (c) Concave

The atmospheric pressure is P_0 and ... 🟡 SimilarL = P0hr / (2T − ρgrh) + h
Concept Trapped air above the water is compressed isothermally (Boyle) as water enters; the final air pressure plus capillary and hydrostatic terms must balance P0 at the outside water level.
Formula P0L = P(L − h) and P = P0 + 2T/r − ρgh; concave meniscus (θ = 0): liquid just below it is at P − 2T/r, and going down h adds ρgh to reach P0.
Solution
1. Initially the tube (length L, area A) holds air at P0. Water rises h, so air length = L − h at pressure P.
2. Boyle: P0LA = P(L − h)A ⇒ P = P0L/(L − h).
3. Meniscus balance at outside water level: P − 2T/r + ρgh = P0 ⇒ P = P0 + 2T/r − ρgh.
4. Equate: P0L/(L − h) = X, where X = P0 + 2T/r − ρgh. So P0L = X(L − h).
5. L(X − P0) = Xh ⇒ L = Xh/(2T/r − ρgh).
6. Write X = P0 + (2T/r − ρgh): L = P0h/(2T/r − ρgh) + h = P0hr/(2T − ρgrh) + h.
  P0*L = P*(L-h),  P = P0 + 2T/r - rho*g*h
  L = X*h/(X-P0),  X-P0 = 2T/r - rho*g*h
  L = P0*h*r/(2T - rho*g*r*h) + h
⚡ JEE Trick Treat the sealed air as a gas that must be compressed: without it you would get the open-tube h = 2T/(rρg). Check: P0 → 0 gives L = h.
Answer: L = P0hr / (2T − ρgrh) + h

  (column fills the tube; no weight to balance)

Concept Closing the lower end and lifting keeps the whole water column (length ℓ + h) in the tube. After reopening, the hanging column has two surfaces: a concave meniscus on top and a convex one at the bottom, so surface tension can support up to twice the single-ended rise.
Formula single meniscus supports h = 2T/(rρg) a hanging column rests on two menisci, each giving a pressure 2T/r = ρgh, so max length = 2h
Solution
1. Originally the tube's lower end is at depth ℓ, and water stands h above the outside level, so water in the tube has length ℓ + h.
2. Closing the lower end and lifting out keeps this ℓ + h column inside the tube.
3. On opening, the column can hang only if its length is within what two menisci can support: 2h.
4. If ℓ + h > 2h, i.e. ℓ > h, the excess drains until the length is 2h (bottom meniscus becomes a convex cap of the required curvature).
5. If ℓ + h ≤ 2h, i.e. ℓ ≤ h, nothing drains: length stays ℓ + h. This is option (a).
