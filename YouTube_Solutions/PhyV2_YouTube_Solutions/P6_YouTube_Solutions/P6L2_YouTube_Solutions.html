1According to Newton's Second Law, force is directly proportional to: Mass only / Acceleration only / Rate of change of momentum / DisplacementNewton's second lawForce is the rate of change of momentum🟢 ExactcRate of change of momentum
Concept Newton's second law, in its most general and correct form, states that the net external force equals the rate of change of linear momentum: the faster momentum p = mv changes, the larger the force. The familiar F = ma is only the special case obtained when the mass is constant. So the quantity force is directly proportional to is dp/dt — not mass alone, not acceleration alone, and definitely not displacement.
Formula F = dp/dt = d(mv)/dt; for constant m this reduces to F = m(dv/dt) = ma (N = kg × m s⁻²). Momentum p = mv has units kg·m s⁻¹, so its time-derivative is kg·m s⁻² = newton — the definition is the force unit itself.
Solution
1. "Mass only": a heavy body sitting at rest or moving uniformly needs no force, so force cannot be proportional to mass alone. ✗
2. "Acceleration only": for a given a, a loaded truck needs far more force than a bicycle, so mass matters too — a alone is incomplete. ✗
3. "Rate of change of momentum": F = dp/dt is exactly Newton's own statement, and it even covers variable-mass systems such as rockets. ✓
4. "Displacement": displacement enters work and energy, not the force law; a body can have large displacement with zero force. ✗

 acceleration only   incomplete             x
 rate of change p    F = dp/dt              <-- answer
 displacement        no                     x
⚡ JEE Trick Read the law as "force = how fast momentum changes." That one line handles constant-mass (F = ma) AND variable-mass (rocket, falling chain, conveyor) problems in a single stroke. Trap: picking F = ma and then answering "acceleration only" — a is only half the story; the momentum form is the true definition.
Answer: (c) Rate of change of momentum.

Concept A frictionless pulley cannot change the magnitude of the tension in an ideal (massless, inextensible) string — it only changes the string's direction. So both segments of the string carry the same tension T, fixed by the hanging block. The pulley is then pulled by each segment along that segment's own direction, and the force the string exerts on the pulley is the vector sum of those two pulls — not their arithmetic sum.
Formula Hanging block at rest: T = mg (N = kg × m s⁻²). Force on pulley: |F| = √(T₁² + T₂² + 2T₁T₂cosα) where α is the angle between the two segments; for α = 90° with T₁ = T₂ = T this collapses to |F| = T√2 (N). Solution

Concept The phrase "all surfaces are smooth" is the whole question. If the interface between the two blocks is frictionless, there is no horizontal force available to drag the upper block along — the only thing the lower block can push on it with is a normal force, and a normal force is perpendicular to the surface, i.e. purely vertical. So the top block simply does not accelerate; the bottom block slides out from under it. This overturns the reflex "they move together".
Formula F = ma (N = kg × m s⁻²) applied separately to each block, plus N = mBg for the vertical balance of the upper block (N in N, m in kg, g in m s⁻²). Newton's second law is applied per body; the only interaction here is the vertical normal pair between A and B.
Solution
Given (from the book figure): B = 50 kg rests on top of A = 1 kg; the 10 N horizontal force is applied to the lower 1 kg block A; all surfaces smooth; g = 10 m s⁻².
1. FBD of B (upper, 50 kg): weight 50 × 10 = 500 N down, normal N from A up. Horizontal forces on B: none (the contact is smooth, so A can push B only vertically).Friction is the only horizontal contact force available, and it is zero on a smooth surface.
2. So for B: ΣFx = 0 = mBaB ⇒ aB = 0 m s⁻² — block B stays put.

4. FBD of A (lower, 1 kg), horizontal: only the applied 10 N acts (the ground is smooth, and B exerts no horizontal force on A).

Concept Three forces act on block A: the horizontal string to the wall (T₁), the inclined string that runs over the pulley (T₂), and its own weight m₁g. In equilibrium a body under three forces has zero net force in every direction, so resolving along x and y gives two equations. The pulley contributes the key extra fact: the same string continues down to m₂, so T₂ = m₂g. Combining the three relations pins both requested ratios.
Formula Equilibrium: ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0 (all terms in N). Ideal pulley: T₂ = m₂g (N). Use sin37° = 3/5 = 0.6 and cos37° = 4/5 = 0.8. Both equations are sums of newtons set to zero, so the resulting ratios are pure numbers.
Solution
Geometry read from the book figure: block A (m₁) is suspended — held by a horizontal string T₁ to the wall on its left and by string T₂ leaving its top-right corner at 37° above the horizontal, running over a pulley fixed to the ceiling and down to the hanging block B (m₂). Ideal strings, frictionless massless pulley, system at rest.
1. (i) FBD of A: T₁ horizontal (← toward the wall); T₂ at 37° above horizontal (up-and-right); weight m₁g straight down.

2. B at rest: T₂ − m₂g = 0 ⇒ T₂ = m₂g (N).
3. A, vertical: only T₂ has an upward component, and it must carry A's whole weight: T₂ sin37° − m₁g = 0 ⇒ T₂ (3/5) = m₁g.

5. (ii) Therefore m₁ : m₂ = 3 : 5 (a pure ratio — as it must be, both sides being masses).

7. (iii) T₁/T₂ = cos37° = 4/5 = 0.8 (dimensionless — a ratio of two tensions in N).

1Inside a closed capsule you feel pushed to your right. Explain the cause(s); feeling or reality? Give one more such situationPseudo force / non-inertial frameReal vs Pseudo Force🟡 Similar—
Concept Newton's laws only hold as written in an inertial frame (one that is not accelerating). Inside a sealed capsule you cannot see outside, so you cannot tell whether your frame is inertial. If the capsule accelerates, everything inside it appears to be pushed the opposite way — that apparent push is a pseudo (fictitious) force. It has no source object and no third-law partner, yet the sensation it produces is genuine.
Key relation In a frame accelerating with a⃗frame, every body of mass m feels an extra term F⃗pseudo = −m a⃗frame (N = kg × m s⁻²), opposite to the frame's acceleration. It is exactly the term you must add to make ΣF⃗ = ma⃗ work in a non-inertial frame; it is proportional to your own mass, which is why it feels like gravity.
Solution
1. Identify the sensation: you are pushed toward your right. Since F⃗pseudo = −m a⃗frame, the capsule must be accelerating toward your left.The pseudo force always points opposite the frame's acceleration.
2. Possible causes, all giving a leftward a⃗frame:
  • the capsule is turning left (centripetal acceleration points left, magnitude v²/r in m s⁻²);
  • the capsule is speeding up toward the left in a straight line;
  • the capsule is moving right and decelerating (acceleration still points left).
3. Is it a feeling or a reality? The push is a feeling, not a real force. No object touches your right side and no field acts there; you have no third-law partner to point to.Real forces come in action–reaction pairs and can be traced to an agent — pseudo forces cannot.
4. What is physically real: your body's inertia (it tends to keep going straight, Newton's first law) while the capsule wall accelerates leftward into you. In the ground (inertial) frame, the wall pushes you left with a real normal force — no rightward force exists at all.
5. One more such situation: standing in a bus that turns sharply — you lurch outward; or a lift that accelerates upward, where you feel heavier (pseudo force mg downward extra); or a car that brakes hard, throwing you forward.

Whenever the frame is a vehicle/lift/turntable, either work in the ground frame (no pseudo term) or in the vehicle frame with −ma added to every body — never half-and-half. Trap: calling this "centrifugal force" and treating it as a real outward push; it exists only in the rotating frame.
Key point: the capsule is accelerating toward your left (a turn or a change of speed), so in the capsule's non-inertial frame a pseudo force −ma⃗ appears to push you right. 2Among the four fundamental forces, only one governs daily life almost entirely — justify by stating that forceFour fundamental forcesThe four fundamental forces of nature🟡 Similar—
Concept Nature is built from exactly four fundamental interactions — gravitational, electromagnetic, strong nuclear and weak nuclear. Two of them (strong, weak) have a range of about 10⁻¹⁵ m, i.e. the size of a nucleus, so they never reach the scale of everyday life. Gravity is long-ranged but astonishingly weak, and it only matters when one of the bodies is planet-sized. That leaves one force to run essentially everything you touch, see and do: the electromagnetic force.
Key relation Relative strengths (taking strong = 1): strong 1 : electromagnetic 10⁻² : weak 10⁻¹³ : gravitational 10⁻³⁸ — dimensionless ratios. Ranges: strong ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m, weak ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ m, electromagnetic ∞, gravitational ∞ (m). A force needs both adequate strength and adequate range to shape daily life; only electromagnetism has both at the human scale.
Solution
1. Eliminate the strong force: range ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m. It binds protons and neutrons inside a nucleus and never acts between two objects on your desk.Its range is a hundred-thousandth of an atom's size.
2. Eliminate the weak force: range ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ m and ~10⁻¹³ of the strong force. It only governs β-decay and similar nuclear transformations.
3. Gravity is long-ranged but ~10⁻³⁸ of the strong force: between two 60 kg people 1 m apart, F = Gm₁m₂/r² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(60)(60)/1² ≈ 2.4×10⁻⁷ N — utterly negligible. It only shows up because the Earth (6×10²⁴ kg) is enormous.
4. That leaves the electromagnetic force — long-ranged and only ~10² times weaker than the strong force.
5. Justify it: every contact force you meet is electromagnetic at bottom — the normal force from a chair, friction, tension in a rope, the elastic force in a spring, viscous drag, all arise from electron-cloud repulsion between atoms. So are chemical bonds, muscle contraction, light, and every electrical appliance.

3Distinguish between (1) real force and pseudo force (2) contact force and non-contact forceReal/pseudo & contact forcesReal and Pseudo force (Class 11)🟡 Similar—
Concept Both distinctions ask the same underlying question: where does the force come from? A real force always has a physical agent and obeys Newton's third law; a pseudo force is a bookkeeping term invented to save F = ma inside an accelerating frame. Similarly, contact forces need the agent to be touching the body, while non-contact (field) forces act across empty space. Getting these labels right is what makes a free-body diagram trustworthy.
Key relation Pseudo force: F⃗pseudo = −m a⃗frame (N = kg × m s⁻²), present only in a non-inertial frame and always opposite the frame's acceleration. Real forces: F⃗AB = −F⃗BA (Newton's third law, N). Only real forces come in equal-and-opposite pairs; that pairing is the practical test.
Solution
(1) Real force vs pseudo force
1. Agent: a real force is exerted by a specific object (a rope, the Earth, a magnet). A pseudo force has no agent — nothing exerts it.
2. Third law: a real force has an equal and opposite reaction on the agent. A pseudo force has no reaction pair.There is no body for the reaction to act on, since there is no agent.
3. Frame: a real force exists in every frame. A pseudo force exists only in a non-inertial (accelerating) frame and vanishes the moment you switch to the ground frame.
4. Magnitude/direction: real forces are set by the physical interaction; a pseudo force is always exactly −ma⃗frame, i.e. proportional to the body's own mass and opposite to the frame's acceleration.
5. Examples: real — weight (60 kg × 10 m s⁻² = 600 N), tension, normal, friction. Pseudo — the outward "centrifugal" push in a turning bus, the extra heaviness in an accelerating lift.
(2) Contact force vs non-contact force
6. Contact: the two bodies must physically touch; the force acts only while contact lasts. Examples: normal reaction, friction, tension, spring force, air drag, buoyancy. All are electromagnetic at the atomic level.
7. Non-contact (field/action-at-a-distance): acts across empty space through a field, with no touching required. Examples: gravitational (weight), electrostatic, magnetic.
8. Note both kinds are real — the contact/non-contact split is about how the agent reaches the body, whereas real/pseudo is about whether there is an agent at all.

⚡ JEE Trick Two-second test — "Name the agent, name the reaction." Can't do both ⇒ pseudo. Then, "Is the agent touching the body?" Yes ⇒ contact, No ⇒ field. Trap: calling weight a contact force because you feel it through your feet — what you feel through your feet is the normal force (contact); weight itself acts at a distance on every atom of you.
Key point: a real force has a physical agent, a third-law partner and exists in every frame; a pseudo force (= −ma⃗frame) has neither agent nor partner and appears only in accelerating frames. A contact force requires touching (normal, friction, tension); a non-contact force acts through a field across space (weight, electrostatic, magnetic) — both of these are real.
4Find the odd one out: (i) force for a string to become taut (ii) weight of an object (iii) force by which we hold an objectContact vs non-contact forceReal and Pseudo force / contact forces🟡 Similar—
Concept The three items are all forces, so the classification must be finer than "force". Sort them by whether the agent has to touch the body: tension in a taut string and the grip force of your hand both require physical contact, whereas the weight of an object is exerted by the Earth's gravitational field across empty space, with no contact at all. So the odd one out is (ii) — it is the only non-contact force in the list.
Key relation Weight (non-contact): W = mg — for m in kg and g = 10 m s⁻², W is in N, and it acts even when the body is in free fall touching nothing. Contact forces (tension T, normal N, friction f) all measured in N too but exist only while surfaces touch. Same unit, different mechanism — the unit cannot classify them, only the mechanism can.
Solution
1. Item (i) — the force that makes a string taut on stretching = tension. Agent: the string's own stretched material, touching the body at the knot. ⇒ contact force.
2. Item (ii) — the weight of an object = m × g (N). Agent: the Earth, ~6.4×10⁶ m away at its surface, acting through the gravitational field. Nothing touches the object. ⇒ non-contact force.
3. Item (iii) — the force by which you hold an object in hand = normal reaction + friction from your palm. Agent: your hand, in physical contact. ⇒ contact force.
4. Compare: (i) and (iii) are both contact forces (and both electromagnetic at the atomic level); (ii) is a gravitational field force.Two of a kind and one different — the definition of "odd one out".
5. Cross-check by removing contact: cut the string (tension → 0 N) and open your hand (grip → 0 N), but the weight stays exactly mg. Only (ii) survives with nothing touching.

5Sitting next to a friend — is there gravitational attraction? Why don't you come together? Any other force involved?Gravitation between everyday objectsThe gravitational attraction of two people🟡 Similar—
Concept Newton's law of gravitation is universal: every pair of masses attracts, with no exceptions for being small or being people. So yes, you and your friend do attract each other. The reason nothing happens is purely one of magnitude — G is about 6.67×10⁻¹¹ in SI units, so the attraction between two human-sized masses is ~10⁻⁷ N, while the static friction available from the ground is of order hundreds of newtons. The gravitational pull loses by nine orders of magnitude.
Formula F = G m₁ m₂ ÷ r² with G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², masses in kg, r in m, F in N. Friction limit: fmax = μ N = μ mg (N). Check the units: (N m² kg⁻²)(kg)(kg)/(m²) = N ✓ — the constant is built so the answer comes out in newtons.
Solution
1. Is there attraction? Yes — gravitation acts between any two masses. Assume each person m₁ = m₂ = 60 kg, sitting r = 0.5 m apart (centre to centre).
2. Compute it: F = (6.67×10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²)(60 kg)(60 kg) ÷ (0.5 m)² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 3600) ÷ 0.25 N.
