1 (iii)Fill in the blank: If Qc > Kc, the reaction will proceed in the ______ direction to reach equilibrium.Direction from Q vs KPredicting the Direction of a Reaction & Reaction Quotient - Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Ch 6🟡 Similarreverse (backward)
Concept Q > K means the mixture already holds more product, relative to reactant, than equilibrium allows. The excess must be undone, so the reaction runs backwards.
Key relation Qc > Kc ⇒ net reverse reaction until Q falls to K Q is a products-over-reactants ratio, so lowering it means converting product back into reactant.
Solution
1. Both Q and K are the same ratio [products]/[reactants], so they can be compared directly.
2. If Q > K, the numerator is too big relative to the denominator for this temperature.
3. To reduce Q the system must consume product and regenerate reactant — that is the reverse direction.
4. Book p.41 states it: “if QC > Kc, the reaction will proceed in the direction of reactants (reverse reaction)”.
5. The motion continues until Q has fallen to K, at which point the net reaction stops and the system is at equilibrium.
6. So the blank is reverse (backward). The mirror statement, Q < K → forward, is tested at item 2(iii) of this same Quick Recap.
7. Note that the rule needs no knowledge of which species is which. Q and K are single numbers on the same scale, so the comparison alone fixes the direction — which is why Level-2 Q2 of this lecture can be answered from “K = 10, Q = 2” and nothing else.

1 (v)Fill in the blank: If the reaction is exergonic (ΔG < 0), the reaction proceeds in the ______ direction.Sign of ΔGGibbs Free Energy🟡 Similarforward
Concept “Exergonic” means free energy is released, ΔG < 0. A negative ΔG is the criterion for spontaneity, so the reaction runs in the forward direction as written.
Key relation ΔG < 0 ⇒ spontaneous forward; ΔG > 0 ⇒ spontaneous in reverse; ΔG = 0 ⇒ equilibrium ΔG is evaluated for the reaction as written, so its sign points along or against the printed arrow.
Solution
1. Decode the word: exergonic = “energy out” in the free-energy sense, i.e. ΔG < 0, exactly as the stem states.
2. A process with ΔG < 0 proceeds spontaneously in the direction for which ΔG was calculated.
3. Since ΔG is quoted for the reaction as written, that direction is the forward one.
4. If instead ΔG were positive, the forward reaction would be non-spontaneous and the reverse would run instead.
5. And ΔG = 0 is the equilibrium condition — no net reaction in either direction, which is what Level-3 Q1 of this lecture exploits.
6. Careful: this is about ΔG, not ΔG°. A reaction with ΔG° > 0 can still run forward if Q is small enough, because ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q.
7. A caution on the two symbols: ΔG° < 0 tells you K > 1, a property of the reaction; ΔG < 0 tells you this particular mixture will move forward. Level-3 Q3 of this lecture has ΔG° negative and ΔG positive at the same time, which is only possible because they are different quantities.

 dG = dG0 + RT ln Q
⚡ JEE Trick Do not confuse exergonic (ΔG < 0, about free energy) with exothermic (ΔH < 0, about heat). A reaction can be endothermic and still exergonic if the entropy term is large enough.

Concept α is the fraction of the substance that has dissociated. A fraction of a whole cannot exceed the whole, so α is confined to the interval 0 to 1.

Solution
1. Take 1 mol of PCl5. At equilibrium the undissociated amount is 1 − α.
2. A concentration cannot be negative, so 1 − α ≥ 0, giving α ≤ 1.
3. And α cannot be negative either, since it counts material that has dissociated. So 0 ≤ α ≤ 1.
4. α = 1 corresponds to complete dissociation, which a reversible reaction approaches but never reaches — it is the limiting case.

2 (ii)True or false: If Qc = Kc, the system is already at equilibrium.Q = KK (Equilibrium Constant) vs Q (Reaction Quotient)🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Q and K are the same ratio. When the current value of that ratio already equals its equilibrium value, there is nothing for the system to adjust — it is at equilibrium.
Key relation Qc = Kc ⇔ the mixture is at equilibrium This is an if-and-only-if: equality of the ratio is exactly what the equilibrium condition rf = rb produces.
Solution
1. Q is evaluated from the concentrations present at that instant; K is the value that ratio takes at equilibrium.
2. If the two coincide, the concentrations are already the equilibrium ones and no net change follows.
3. Book p.41: “If QC = Kc, the reaction mixture is already at equilibrium.”
4. Thermodynamically the same statement reads ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q = −RT ln K + RT ln Q = RT ln(Q/K), which is zero exactly when Q = K.
5. So the statement is True, and it is the basis of Level-3 Q1 of this lecture, where ΔG = 0 forces Q = K = 10.
6. Note that a mixture can satisfy Q = K at many different absolute concentrations — what matters is the ratio, not the individual amounts.
7. Note the practical consequence: to test whether a given mixture is at equilibrium you do not need to watch it change. Compute Q, compare with K, and the answer is immediate — which is exactly what Level-2 Q5 of this lecture asks.

Solution
1. The statement claims Q < K sends the reaction in reverse. Test it against the definition.
2. Q < K means the products-over-reactants ratio is below its equilibrium value.
3. Raising that ratio requires making more product — the forward direction.

2 (iv)True or false: A negative value for ΔG indicates that the reaction is spontaneous.SpontaneityGibbs Free Energy🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept A negative ΔG is the thermodynamic criterion for spontaneity at constant temperature and pressure. It is the definition of a spontaneous (exergonic) process.
Key relation ΔG < 0 spontaneous; ΔG = 0 equilibrium; ΔG > 0 non-spontaneous ΔG = ΔH − TΔS packages the enthalpy and entropy criteria into one number for the system alone.
Solution
1. At constant T and P, a process occurs of its own accord when it lowers the Gibbs free energy of the system.
2. So ΔG < 0 marks a spontaneous change — the statement is True.
3. This is the same content as blank 1(v) of this Quick Recap, where an exergonic reaction was said to run forward.
4. Careful with the standard state: ΔG° < 0 means K > 1, i.e. products favoured under standard conditions. Spontaneity in a particular mixture is governed by ΔG, not ΔG°.
5. Since ΔG = RT ln(Q/K), a reaction with ΔG° > 0 (so K < 1) still runs forward whenever Q < K.
6. And spontaneity says nothing about speed: diamond → graphite has ΔG < 0 but is immeasurably slow.
7. Two cautions worth carrying. Spontaneous does not mean fast — the diamond-to-graphite conversion has ΔG < 0 and takes geological time. And the criterion holds at constant temperature and pressure; at constant temperature and volume the Helmholtz free energy plays the same role.

⚡ JEE Trick “Spontaneous” in thermodynamics means “thermodynamically allowed”, not “fast”. Any option linking the sign of ΔG to a rate is wrong.
Answer: True — a negative ΔG indicates a spontaneous reaction.
2 (v)True or false: The reaction quotient Q and the equilibrium constant K are calculated using the same equation.Q and K share one expressionIntroduction to reaction quotient Qc | Chemical equilibrium | Chemistry | Khan Academy🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Q and K share one algebraic expression. What separates them is when it is evaluated: Q at any instant, K only at equilibrium.
Key relation Qc = [C]c[D]d/([A]a[B]b) = Kc when the mixture is at equilibrium Same formula, different substitution — which is precisely why the two can be compared as numbers.
Solution
1. Book p.41: “The reaction quotient is defined in the same way as the equilibrium constant … at any stage of reaction.”
2. So the products go in the numerator, the reactants in the denominator, and each is raised to its stoichiometric coefficient — identical rules for both.
3. The only difference lies in the values substituted: arbitrary current concentrations for Q, equilibrium concentrations for K.
4. Hence the statement is True.
5. This shared form is what makes the comparison meaningful: Q and K are numbers on the same scale, so Q < K, Q = K and Q > K each carry a direct physical reading.
6. Note also that pure solids and pure liquids are omitted from Q exactly as they are from K — the omission rule is part of the shared expression.
7. One consequence worth stating: because the expression is shared, Q carries the same units as K. So a K quoted in atm−1 implies a Q in atm−1 too, and the comparison between them is dimensionally sound.

2 (vi)True or false: If Q > K at a given temperature, the reaction will shift toward the reactants.Direction from Q vs KK vs Q Explained: How to Predict Reaction Shifts in Equilibrium Chemistry🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept The same rule as blank 1(iii), stated in words. Q > K means an excess of product, so the system shifts back toward the reactants.
Key relation Q > K ⇒ net reverse reaction ⇒ shift toward reactants “Shift toward the reactants” and “proceed in the reverse direction” are two descriptions of the same motion.
Solution
1. Q > K means the products-over-reactants ratio currently exceeds its equilibrium value.
2. To bring Q down, product must be consumed and reactant regenerated.
3. That is a shift toward the reactant side, i.e. the reverse reaction — so the statement is True.
4. Book p.41 puts it as “a reaction has a tendency to form reactants if QC > KC”.
5. Thermodynamically: ΔG = RT ln(Q/K), so Q > K makes ΔG positive, the forward reaction non-spontaneous, and the reverse spontaneous.
6. The shift continues only until Q has fallen to K — the system does not overshoot, because at Q = K the driving force vanishes.
7. Note that the shift is finite, not total. The system moves toward the reactants only until Q has fallen to K — it does not run backwards to completion, because the driving force vanishes the moment the two are equal.
