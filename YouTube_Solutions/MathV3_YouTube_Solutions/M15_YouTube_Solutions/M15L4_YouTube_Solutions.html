Concept Two tangents to a circle that meet at right angles meet on a fixed circle concentric with the given one. That locus has a special name.
Formula Locus of P with perpendicular tangents: x2+y2 = 2r2. the tangent length is r and the angle at P is 90°, so P lies at distance r√2 from the centre
Solution
1. Let the circle be x2+y2=r2 with centre O and let the tangents from P meet at 90°.
2. Join OP and the two contact points A, B. OAPB has right angles at A, B and P, and OA = OB = r. so it is a square
3. A square of side r has diagonal OP = r√2. diagonal = side × √2
4. So every such P is at the fixed distance r√2 from O, and the locus is x2+y2=2r2.
5. This circle is concentric with the given one and has radius r√2. It is called the director circle.
⚡ JEE Trick Perpendicular tangents means a square, so the diagonal is r√2. Do not confuse it with the radical circle or the auxiliary circle.
Answer: Director circle

Solution
1. Write the circle as (x+a)2+(y−b)2 = 2b2. Complete the square: constant term is a2+b2−(a2−b2) = 2b2.
2. Centre C = (−a, b) and radius r = √2·|b|.
3. Director circle has the same centre and radius R = r√2 = 2|b|. Locus of points from which tangents are perpendicular.
4. For P on it, tangent length L = √(R2−r2) = √(4b2−2b2) = √2|b| = r. Equal tangent length and radius confirms the right angle at P (square C-A-P-B).
5. Chord of contact AB = 2rL/√(r2+L2) = 2r2/(r√2) = r√2.
6. Substitute r = √2|b|: AB = √2·√2|b| = 2|b|.
7. Taking b > 0 as in the key, AB = 2b (it equals the diagonal of the square CAPB, i.e. R).
⚡ JEE Trick Perpendicular tangents make C-A-P-B a square of side r, so the chord of contact is its other diagonal AB = CP = r√2 = R. Hence the chord length always equals the director-circle radius.
Answer: 2b

Concept Director circle of a circle of radius r has radius r√2 (same centre). So going S₂ → S₁ → given circle, radii grow by √2 each step; reversing, radii shrink by 1/√2 each step, forming a geometric series.
Formula sum = r0/(1 − 1/√2), with r02 = g2+f2−c. The given circle has radius r0; S₁ has r0/√2, S₂ has r0/2, and so on (common ratio 1/√2 < 1, so the sum converges).
Solution
1. Given circle: 2g = 4, 2f = −2√2 ⇒ g = 2, f = −√2, so r02 = 4+2−c = 6−c. Radius formula √(g2+f2−c).
2. It is the director circle of S₁, so r(S₁) = r0/√2. R = r√2 reversed.
3. Similarly r(S₂) = r0/2, r(S₃) = r0/(2√2), …
4. Sum of all radii (including the given circle) = r0[1 + 1/√2 + 1/2 + …] = r0√2/(√2−1).
5. Set equal to 2: r0 = 2(1 − 1/√2) = 2 − √2.
6. Square: r02 = 4 − 4√2 + 2 = 6 − 4√2.
7. Equate with 6 − c: c = 4√2 = k√2 ⇒ k = 4.
⚡ JEE Trick Do not drop the given circle from the series: the question says the radii of ALL these circles (starting with the given one). Dropping it would give a different c.
Answer: k = 4
3(Passage) Let an α-chord of a circle be a chord subtending an angle α at the centre. Concept An α-chord subtends α at the centre. Its perpendicular distance from the centre is d = r cos(α/2), since the perpendicular from the centre bisects both the chord and the angle.
Formula d = r·cos(α/2), chord = 2r·sin(α/2). In the right triangle formed by the centre, the chord midpoint and one chord end, the angle at the centre is α/2 and the adjacent side is d.
Solution
1. Circle x2+y2=1 has centre O(0,0), radius r = 1.
2. Distance of x+y−1 = 0 from O: d = |−1|/√(1+1) = 1/√2. Standard point-to-line distance.
3. Since d = 1/√2 < r = 1, the line does cut the circle, so it is a chord (option (d) is out).
4. Use d = r cos(α/2): cos(α/2) = 1/√2. Equate the two expressions for d with r = 1.
5. Hence α/2 = π/4, so α = π/2.
6. Check: chord length = 2√(1−1/2) = √2 = 2 sin(π/4), consistent with a right angle at O (chord = r√2).
⚡ JEE Trick A chord of length r√2 always subtends 90° at the centre; here the line x+y=1 meets the circle at (1,0) and (0,1), which are visibly 90° apart.
Answer: (b) π/2

Concept A π/3-chord of x2+y2=4 lies at distance d = r cos(α/2) from the centre. A line of slope 1 at that distance from the origin is the required chord.
Formula d = r cos(α/2); line x−y+c=0 has d = |c|/√2. Slope 1 fixes the form x−y+c=0; the distance condition fixes c.
Solution
1. Radius r = 2 and α = π/3, so α/2 = π/6.
2. d = 2 cos(π/6) = 2·(√3/2) = √3. Perpendicular distance of the chord from the centre.
3. Slope 1 ⇒ line y = x + k, i.e. x − y + c = 0 (c = −k).
4. Distance from O(0,0): |c|/√(1+1) = |c|/√2. Point-to-line distance.
5. Equate: |c|/√2 = √3 ⇒ |c| = √6.
6. So the lines are x − y ± √6 = 0.
7. Of the options, only (a) x − y + √6 = 0 matches (the other sign x − y − √6 = 0 is not listed).
⚡ JEE Trick Options (b), (c), (d) give |c| = 2√3, √3, √3 — none equals √6. Compute d first, then |c| = d√(1+m2) = √3·√2.
Answer: (a) x − y + √6 = 0

Formula Chord of contact from (x1,y1): (x1−4)(x−4)+(y1−8)(y−8) = 20. The tangency points of the two tangents from an external point are exactly the intersections of its chord of contact with the circle.

Concept Tangents from P are perpendicular exactly when P lies on the director circle, centre O and radius r√2. So the required points are the intersections of the line x = 2 with the director circle.
Formula director circle: x2+y2 = 2r2. The angle between tangents is 2 sin-1(r/OP); it equals 90° iff OP = r√2.
Solution

2. Director circle: x2+y2 = 2·16 = 32. Radius becomes 4√2.

Concept A line cutting a chord of length 2l from a circle of radius r lies at distance d = √(r2−l2) from the centre. Find all lines through (2,3) at that distance from the origin.
Formula d = √(r2−l2); line through (2,3): y−3 = m(x−2). Half-chord, d and r form a right triangle with hypotenuse r (the centre-to-chord perpendicular bisects the chord).
