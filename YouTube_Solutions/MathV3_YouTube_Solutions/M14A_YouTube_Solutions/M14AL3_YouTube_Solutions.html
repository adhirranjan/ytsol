1 (i)Fill in the blanks: Point of intersection of the lines 2x + y = 4 and 6x − y = 4 is ______.Point of intersection of two linesx+y=6 x–y=4 | solve graphically | practice set 1.2 | maths 1 | linear equations in two variables🟡 Similar(1, 2)
Concept The point of intersection of two lines is the single ordered pair (x, y) that satisfies both equations at once — geometrically the one place the two straight lines cross. Here the two y-terms are +y and −y, so simple elimination by addition clears y in one stroke and hands us x immediately, with no messy substitution.
Formula 2x + y = 4,   6x − y = 4 ⇒ add ⇒ 8x = 8. Because y appears as +y in one line and −y in the other, adding the equations cancels y and isolates x in a single step.
Solution
1. Label the lines: 2x + y = 4 …(1) and 6x − y = 4 …(2). Two independent linear equations fix one common point when the lines are not parallel.
2. Add (1) and (2): (2x + 6x) + (y − y) = 4 + 4, i.e. 8x = 8.
3. Divide by 8: x = 1.
4. Substitute x = 1 into (1): 2(1) + y = 4 ⇒ y = 4 − 2 = 2. Either line may be used for the second coordinate; both must agree.
5. So the lines meet at the point (1, 2).
6. Verify in (2): 6(1) − 2 = 6 − 2 = 4 ✓. A true intersection satisfies both equations exactly.
  2x + y = 4            (1)
  6x - y = 4            (2)
  (1)+(2):  8x = 8  ->  x = 1
  from (1):  y = 4 - 2(1) = 2
  intersection = (1, 2)
⚡ JEE Trick When one variable is +y in one equation and −y in the other, ADD the equations to kill it instantly; if the signs are the same, subtract. Reserve substitution for the very last coordinate — it saves a line of algebra every time.
Answer: (1, 2).
1 (ii)Fill in the blanks: Equation of the line x + y = √2 in normal form is ______.Normal form of a lineReduce the equation `x+y-sqrt2=0` to the normal form `x cos alpha+y sin alpha=p`, and hence find🟢 Exactx cos45° + y sin45° = 1
Concept The normal (perpendicular) form of a line is x cosα + y sinα = p, where p ≥ 0 is the length of the perpendicular dropped from the origin onto the line and α is the angle that perpendicular makes with the positive x-axis. To put a line in this form we divide the whole equation by √(A2 + B2) so that the coefficients of x and y become a genuine cosine–sine pair (their squares add to 1).
Formula Ax + By = C ⇒ divide by √(A2+B2); here √(12+12) = √2. Dividing by √2 forces the x- and y-coefficients to satisfy cos2α + sin2α = 1, which is exactly what the normal form demands.
Solution
1. Start with x + y = √2; here A = 1, B = 1, C = √2.
2. Compute √(A2 + B2) = √(1 + 1) = √2.
3. Divide every term by √2: (1/√2)x + (1/√2)y = √2/√2 = 1. Now the coefficients (1/√2, 1/√2) square-sum to 1, so they are cosα and sinα.
4. Recognise 1/√2 = cos45° = sin45°, so the equation is x cos45° + y sin45° = 1.
5. Hence α = 45° and p = 1. p = 1 is the perpendicular distance of the line from the origin — a bonus read straight off the normal form.
6. So the required normal form is x cos45° + y sin45° = 1.
  x + y = sqrt(2)     (A=1, B=1, C=sqrt2)
  divisor = sqrt(1+1) = sqrt(2)
  (1/sqrt2)x + (1/sqrt2)y = sqrt2/sqrt2 = 1
  1/sqrt2 = cos45 = sin45
  => x cos45 + y sin45 = 1  (p = 1)
⚡ JEE Trick To reach normal form, always divide by √(A2+B2) and keep the right side POSITIVE (flip all signs if C is negative). The constant you land on is p, the distance of the line from the origin — a free by-product.
Answer: x cos45° + y sin45° = 1 (with p = 1).

Concept The intercept (double-intercept) form of a line is x/a + y/b = 1, where a is the x-intercept (the point where the line cuts the x-axis) and b is the y-intercept (where it cuts the y-axis). This form is prized because it lets you sketch the line from just two points and reads off both intercepts at a glance. Any line Ax + By = C with a non-zero constant C is converted simply by dividing the whole equation through by that constant so the right-hand side becomes 1.
Formula Ax + By = C ⇒ divide by C ⇒ x/(C/A) + y/(C/B) = 1. Dividing by the constant term makes the right side equal to 1; the numbers that end up under x and y are then exactly the two axis intercepts a and b.
Solution
1. Begin with 2x + 3y = 12; the constant on the right is 12.
2. Divide every term by 12: 2x/12 + 3y/12 = 12/12.
3. Simplify each fraction: x/6 + y/4 = 1. 2x/12 = x/6 and 3y/12 = y/4, giving the clean intercept form.
4. Read off the intercepts: a = 6 (x-intercept) and b = 4 (y-intercept).
5. Check: at y = 0, x = 6; at x = 0, y = 4 — both satisfy 2x + 3y = 12. The line cuts the axes at (6, 0) and (0, 4).
6. Hence the intercept form is x/6 + y/4 = 1.
  2x + 3y = 12     (constant C = 12)
  divide by 12:  2x/12 + 3y/12 = 1
  => x/6 + y/4 = 1
  x-intercept a = 6,  y-intercept b = 4
  axis points: (6,0) and (0,4)
⚡ JEE Trick For intercept form, just divide the equation by its constant term so the right side is 1 — never rearrange term by term. The denominators you obtain are the intercepts, so the graph is a two-point sketch away.
Answer: x/6 + y/4 = 1 (x-intercept 6, y-intercept 4).

Concept A normal to the curve xy = 1 at a point (t, 1/t) is perpendicular to the tangent there. The tangent slope is −1/t2, so the normal slope is t2, which is always positive. Matching this to the slope −a/b of ax + by + c = 0 forces a and b to have opposite signs.
Formula tangent slope to xy = 1 is −1/t2; normal slope = t2 > 0. t2 ≥ 0, and for a proper normal t ≠ 0, so the normal slope is strictly positive.
Solution
1. On xy = 1 take the point (t, 1/t); differentiate: y = 1/x ⇒ dy/dx = −1/x2 = −1/t2.
2. The normal is perpendicular to the tangent, so its slope = t2. Negative reciprocal of −1/t2 is t2.
3. The line ax + by + c = 0 has slope −a/b, so −a/b = t2 > 0.
4. Thus a/b < 0: a and b have opposite signs.
5. So either a > 0, b < 0 (option b) or a < 0, b > 0 (option c). Both same-sign cases (a, d) are impossible since the normal slope cannot be negative.
6. Hence the answers are (b) and (c).
  xy=1 at (t, 1/t): tangent slope = -1/t^2
  normal slope = t^2 > 0
  line slope -a/b = t^2 > 0  ->  a/b < 0
  a>0,b<0 (b)  or  a<0,b>0 (c)
⚡ JEE Trick Normal slope to xy = c is always positive (it equals t2), so for ax + by + c = 0 to be a normal, its slope −a/b > 0 — meaning a and b must carry OPPOSITE signs. Same-sign options are automatically ruled out before any further work.
Answer: (b) and (c).

Then the points A, B, A′, B′ will be concyclic for: (a) only one value of k (b) two values of k (c) three values of k (d) if k = 12Concyclic axis-intercepts (power of a point)If the lines `2x +3y-1 =0 and kx -y +4 =0` intersect the coordinate axes in concyclic points🟢 Exactb, dtwo values of k; if k = 12⚠
Concept Two lines each cut both axes, giving four points: A, B from 2x + 3y = 6 and A′, B′ from kx + 8y = 11. Four points, two on the x-axis and two on the y-axis, are concyclic exactly when the power of the origin is the same along both axes: OA·OA′ = OB·OB′. This is a single linear equation in k, so it pins exactly ONE value.
Formula concyclic ⇔ OA·OA′ = OB·OB′ (power of the origin). For two chords of a circle through a common point O, the products of the signed segment lengths are equal.
Solution
1. 2x + 3y = 6 cuts the axes at A(3, 0) and B(0, 2).
2. kx + 8y = 11 cuts the axes at A′(11/k, 0) and B′(0, 11/8). Set y = 0 then x = 0 in each line.
3. Power of the origin along the x-axis: OA·OA′ = 3 · (11/k) = 33/k.
4. Along the y-axis: OB·OB′ = 2 · (11/8) = 11/4.
5. Concyclic ⇒ 33/k = 11/4, which is linear in k and gives exactly k = 12 — ONE value. So (a) only one value of k, together with (d) k = 12, is correct.
6. The key’s (b) ‘two values of k’ does not reconcile; only one value exists.
