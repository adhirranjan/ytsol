1 (i)
Fill in the blanks: Circle having same centre and different radii are called ______ circles.
M15L1VRecapQ1iConcentric circles
🟡 SimilarFinding centre and r…
Concentric
Concept A family of circles that share one common centre but have different radii. Only the size changes; the centre is fixed.
Formula x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0, centre (−g,−f). Two circles are concentric when (−g,−f) is identical and only c (hence r) differs.
Solution
1. A circle has a centre and a radius; concentricity is a statement about the centre only. Same centre is the defining word.
2. Keep g, f (so the centre −g,−f stays put) and change only the constant c. Changing c changes r2=g2+f2−c.
3. Example: x2+y2=4 and x2+y2=9 share centre (0,0), radii 2 and 3. Different radii, one centre.
4. Such a set of circles is called concentric. Latin con- (together) + centre.
5. They never intersect, so they nest one inside the other. No common point for distinct radii.
6. So the blank is filled by the word describing equal centre, unequal radius. Matches the given key.
⚡ JEE Trick To build a circle concentric with a given one, copy its x and y terms verbatim and only replace the constant — never touch g or f.
Answer: Concentric
1 (ii)
Fill in the blanks: If g2+f2−c>0 then we have a ______ circle.
M15L1VRecapQ1iiCondition for a real circle
🟡 SimilarHow to find the cent…
Real
Concept The quantity g2+f2−c equals the square of the radius, so its sign decides whether the equation is a genuine circle.
Formula r=√(g2+f2−c). Completing the square on x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0 gives (x+g)2+(y+f)2=g2+f2−c.
Solution
1. Write r2=g2+f2−c. The RHS is exactly the radius squared.
2. If g2+f2−c > 0 then r is a positive real number. A positive r means a curve of non-zero size.
3. That curve is a proper (real) circle. Every point is at distance r>0 from the centre.
4. If it equals 0, r=0 and the circle shrinks to a single point (point circle). Boundary case.
5. If it is < 0, r is imaginary and no real points satisfy the equation. Imaginary / virtual circle.
6. Given condition is > 0, so the blank is the first (positive) case. Matches the key.
⚡ JEE Trick Before drawing any general-form circle, test the sign of g2+f2−c first — a negative value means the circle has no real existence.
Answer: Real
1 (iii)
Fill in the blanks: The equation x2+y2+2x−4y+5=0 represents ______.
M15L1VRecapQ1iiig²+f²−c=0 ⇒ point circle
🟡 SimilarFinding centre and r…
A point circle
Concept Read g, f, c from the general form and test g2+f2−c. When it is zero the radius is zero — the circle is a single point.
Formula r2=g2+f2−c, centre (−g,−f). Standard reading of x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0.
Solution
1. Compare x2+y2+2x−4y+5=0 with the general form. 2g=2, 2f=−4, c=5.
2. So g=1, f=−2, c=5. Halve the x and y coefficients.
3. r2=g2+f2−c=1+4−5. Substitute.
4. r2=0, so r=0. Zero radius.
5. Centre =(−g,−f)=(−1,2). The lone point the equation describes.
6. A zero-radius circle is a point circle at (−1,2). Only (−1,2) satisfies the equation.
⚡ JEE Trick If g2+f2−c comes out 0, do not call it no circle — it is a legitimate point circle located exactly at (−g,−f).
Answer: A point circle

Concept The angle an arc subtends at the centre is twice the angle it subtends at any point on the remaining circumference (inscribed-angle theorem).

If the centre of a circle is (h, k) and the radius is r, its equation is: (a) x2+y2=r2   (b) (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2   (c) (x+h)2+(y+k)2=r2   (d) x2+y2+2hx+2ky=0
M15L1VSuggQ1Standard (centre–radius) equation
🟡 SimilarWriting standard equ…
(b) (x−h)²+(y−k)²=r²
Concept A circle is the locus of all points kept at a fixed distance r (the radius) from a fixed point (h, k) (the centre). Converting that constant-distance condition into coordinates gives the standard (centre–radius) equation.
Formula (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2. it is simply PC2=r2 from the distance formula, with P(x, y) a general point and C(h, k) the centre.
Solution
1. Let P(x, y) be any point on the circle; its defining property is PC=r. every point of a circle is equidistant from the centre.
2. Distance formula: PC=√[(x−h)2+(y−k)2].
3. Put PC=r and square both sides ⇒ (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2, which is option (b).
4. Reject the others: (a) is the special case with centre at the origin; (c) has the signs of h, k reversed. a bracket (x−h) forces the centre coordinate to be +h, so +h inside means a sign error.
5. Reject (d): x2+y2+2hx+2ky=0 has centre (−h,−k) and passes through the origin — a different circle altogether. its form matches the general equation, not the perfect-square standard form.
⚡ JEE Trick Read the centre straight off the brackets by flipping signs: a term (x+3) means the centre's x is −3, not +3. This single sign rule disposes of options (a), (c) and (d) instantly.
Answer: (b) (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2

M15L1VSuggQ2Centre & radius from general form
🟡 SimilarHow to find the cent…
Centre (3/4, −5/4); radius 3√10/4
Concept To read the centre and radius of a circle given in general form you must first make the coefficients of x2 and y2 equal to 1, then compare with x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0.
Formula centre (−g,−f), radius √(g2+f2−c). completing the square on x2+2gx and y2+2fy shifts the centre to (−g,−f).
Solution
Given the circle 2x² + 2y² = 3x − 5y + 7
Find the centre and the radius
1. Bring every term to one side: 2x2+2y2−3x+5y−7=0.
2. Divide through by 2 so the x2 coefficient is 1: x2+y2−(3/2)x+(5/2)y−(7/2)=0. the (−g,−f) rule only applies once x2 and y2 have coefficient 1.
3. Match coefficients: 2g=−3/2 ⇒ g=−3/4; 2f=5/2 ⇒ f=5/4; c=−7/2.
4. Centre =(−g,−f)=(3/4,−5/4).
5. g2+f2−c = 9/16+25/16+7/2 = 9/16+25/16+56/16 = 90/16. write 7/2 as 56/16 so all three terms share the denominator 16.
6. radius =√(90/16)=√90/4=3√10/4. √90=√(9·10)=3√10.
⚡ JEE Trick The classic slip is skipping the divide-by-2 step — that halves g and f and shrinks the radius you compute. Always normalise the x2/y2 coefficient to 1 before reading anything off.
Answer: Centre (3/4, −5/4); radius 3√10/4

🟡 SimilarCONCENTRIC CIRCLES C…
x²+y²−8x+6y−27=0
Concept Concentric circles share the same centre and differ only in radius. In general form, two circles are concentric exactly when their x and y terms are identical (same g and f) and only the constant c changes.
Formula family: x2+y2−8x+6y+c=0. keeping 2g=−8 and 2f=6 fixed pins the centre at (4,−3); only c (hence the radius) stays free.
Solution
Given the circle x² + y² − 8x + 6y − 5 = 0 and the point (−2, −7)
Find the equation of the circle concentric with it and passing through the point
1. Centre of the given circle: 2g=−8, 2f=6 ⇒ centre (−g,−f)=(4,−3).
2. Write any concentric circle as x2+y2−8x+6y+c=0 with c unknown. reusing the same linear terms guarantees the centre cannot move.
3. It must pass through (−2,−7), so substitute x=−2, y=−7.
4. (−2)2+(−7)2−8(−2)+6(−7)+c=0.
5. 4+49+16−42+c=0 ⇒ 27+c=0 ⇒ c=−27. a point lying on a circle must satisfy its equation.
6. Hence the circle is x2+y2−8x+6y−27=0.
⚡ JEE Trick Do not re-derive the centre or complete the square — copy the given circle's x and y terms verbatim and let a single unknown c absorb the new point. One substitution finishes the whole problem.
Answer: x2+y2−8x+6y−27=0

M15L1VSuggQ4Parametric form of a circle
🟡 SimilarParametric Form of C…
x=(p/2)(−1+√2 cosθ), y=(p/2)(−1+√2 sinθ)
Concept The parametric form describes a point on the circle through one angle θ: start at the centre and step out exactly one radius in the direction θ. So first extract the centre and radius, then substitute them in.
Formula x=−g+r cosθ, y=−f+r sinθ. (−g,−f) is the centre and r the radius, so (r cosθ, r sinθ) is the offset to a point on the rim.
Solution
1. Compare x2+y2+px+py=0 with x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0: 2g=p, 2f=p, c=0.
2. Centre =(−g,−f)=(−p/2,−p/2).
3. radius r=√(g2+f2−c)=√(p2/4+p2/4)=√(p2/2)=(p/2)√2. p2/4+p2/4=p2/2, and √(p2/2)=p/√2=(p/2)√2.
4. x=−p/2+r cosθ=−p/2+(p/2)√2 cosθ.
5. Factor out p/2: x=(p/2)(−1+√2 cosθ). pulling the common p/2 out of both terms matches the required answer form.
6. Likewise y=−p/2+(p/2)√2 sinθ=(p/2)(−1+√2 sinθ).
⚡ JEE Trick Write r as (p/2)√2 rather than p/√2 before substituting — the shared p/2 then combines cleanly with the −p/2 from the centre, collapsing everything into the compact (p/2)(−1+√2 cosθ) form in a single line.
Answer: x=(p/2)(−1+√2 cosθ), y=(p/2)(−1+√2 sinθ)

If the parametric form of a circle is given by (i) x=−4+5cosθ and y=−3+5sinθ; (ii) x=a cosα+b sinα and y=a sinα−b cosα, find its Cartesian form.
M15L1VSuggQ5Cartesian form from parametric
🟡 SimilarParametric form of c…
(i) (x+4)²+(y+3)²=25    (ii) x²+y²=a²+b²
Concept To return from parametric to Cartesian form, isolate the trig terms and eliminate the parameter using cos2θ+sin2θ=1. Any pair x(θ), y(θ) then collapses to one relation purely in x and y.
Formula cos2θ+sin2θ=1. it is the single identity that removes the angle once cos and sin are written through x and y.
Solution
Given (i) x = −4 + 5cos θ, y = −3 + 5sin θ; (ii) x = a cos α + b sin α, y = a sin α − b cos α
Find the Cartesian form of each
1. (i) x=−4+5cosθ ⇒ cosθ=(x+4)/5; y=−3+5sinθ ⇒ sinθ=(y+3)/5.
2. Square and add: [(x+4)/5]2+[(y+3)/5]2=cos2θ+sin2θ=1.
3. Multiply through by 25: (x+4)2+(y+3)2=25 — centre (−4,−3), radius 5. the +4 and +3 reveal a centre shifted away from the origin to (−4,−3).
4. (ii) x=a cosα+b sinα, y=a sinα−b cosα; here add the squares directly.
5. x2+y2=(a cosα+b sinα)2+(a sinα−b cosα)2.
6. Expanding, the 2ab cross terms cancel, leaving a2(cos2α+sin2α)+b2(sin2α+cos2α)=a2+b2. +2ab cosα sinα and −2ab sinα cosα sum to zero.
⚡ JEE Trick In part (ii) skip the term-by-term expansion — recognise (a cosα+b sinα) and (a sinα−b cosα) as a rotation of (a, b), and rotation preserves length, so x2+y2=a2+b2 drops out instantly.

Concept When A and B are the ends of a diameter, use the diameter form of the circle. The question hides the actual coordinates inside two quadratics, so Vieta's sum and product relations hand you exactly the symmetric combinations the diameter form needs.
Formula (x−x1)(x−x2)+(y−y1)(y−y2)=0. a point on the circle sees the diameter at 90°, so the lines to A and B are perpendicular — their product of slopes gives this relation.
Solution

Find the equation and radius of the circle with AB as diameter

3. Expand the diameter form: x2−(x1+x2)x+x1x2+y2−(y1+y2)y+y1y2=0.

⚡ JEE Trick You never need the individual roots — the diameter form uses only x1+x2, x1x2, y1+y2, y1y2, so read them straight off each quadratic by Vieta and skip the quadratic formula entirely.

4. (d) |z2| = |z|2 = 4, so z2 lies on the circle |w|=4 of radius 4. TRUE. Squaring doubles the argument but fixes |z2|=4.

Concept Read the two worded conditions off the general equation: “through the origin” forces c=0, and “centre on the x-axis” forces f=0. The radius then simplifies to |g|.

Solution
1. The circle x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0 has centre (−g, −f) and radius √(g2+f2−c). Standard form.

3. Centre on the x-axis: its y-coordinate −f=0 ⇒ f=0. (b) TRUE. x-axis means y=0.
4. Radius = √(g2+f2−c) = √(g2+0−0) = |g|. (c) TRUE. Take the positive root.
