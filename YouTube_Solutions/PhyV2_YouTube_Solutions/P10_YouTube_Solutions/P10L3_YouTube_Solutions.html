1What is the direction of centripetal acceleration in circular motion? (a) Away from the center (b) Tangential to the circle (c) Perpendicular to the radius (d) Towards the center along the radius — (Level-1)Direction of centripetal acceleration9.2 Uniform Circular Motion: Direction of the Acceleration🟡 SimilardTowards the center along the radius
Concept The speed on a circle may stay fixed, but the direction of the velocity turns every instant — and a turning vector is a changing vector. Acceleration is the rate of change of the velocity vector, so a body can be accelerated at constant speed. Split it into a tangential part (changes speed) and a perpendicular part; the second aims at the concave side of the path — the centre.
Formula a = dv/dt, split as at = dv/dt along the tangent and ac = v²/r along −r̂. The minus sign on the outward unit radial r̂ is the answer: ac is anti-parallel to it, i.e. centre-seeking.
Solution
1. The velocity on a circle is tangential, hence perpendicular to the radius drawn to that point.
2. Over a small dt it turns from v1 to v2 through dθ, both of length v.Equal lengths make that velocity triangle isosceles, forcing its base dv perpendicular to v as dθ → 0.
3. dv = v2 − v1 is that base, and it swings into the interior of the circle — straight at the centre.
4. |dv| = v·dθ ⇒ a = |dv|/dt = v(dθ/dt) = vω = v²/r.One triangle gives both the direction and the number v²/r.
5. Newton's second law then demands an inward net force — tension, orbital gravity, road friction — which is what "centripetal" names.
6. Options: (a) outward is the centrifugal pseudo-force, real only in the rotating frame; (b) tangential is at, zero at uniform speed; (c) "perpendicular to the radius" is the tangent again; only (d) names both the line and the sense.(c) is the sharp trap — a true statement about the velocity, sold as one about the acceleration.

Answer: (d) Towards the center along the radius.
2A particle moves in a circle of radius 20 cm. Its linear speed is given by υ = 2t, where t is in second and υ in metre/second. Concept When the speed on a circle is itself changing, the acceleration has two perpendicular parts. The tangential part is how fast the speedometer reading grows, at = dv/dt; the radial part bends the path, ar = v²/r, and cares only about the speed at that instant. With v = 2t given as a function of time, one differentiation gives at and one substitution gives ar.
Formula at = dv/dt, ar = v²/r = ω²r, net a = √(ar² + at²). Being perpendicular by construction, they combine by Pythagoras, never by simple addition.
Solution
1. Put the data in SI: r = 20 cm = 0.20 m, v = 2t with v in m/s and t in s.The cm → m step is the usual mark-loser; leaving r = 20 gives 1.8 instead of 180.
2. Speed at the required instant: v = 2t = 2(3) = 6 m/s.
3. at = dv/dt = d(2t)/dt = 2 m/s², the same at every instant.v is linear in t, so its slope — the tangential acceleration — is a constant, independent of t = 3 s.
4. ar = v²/r = (6)²/0.20 = 36/0.20 = 180 m/s², directed towards the centre.
5. Cross-check: ω = v/r = 6/0.20 = 30 rad/s, so ar = ω²r = (30)²(0.20) = 180 m/s² — same value.
6. Net magnitude: a = √(180² + 2²) ≈ 180.01 m/s², tilted only tan−1(2/180) ≈ 0.64° off the radius.The radial part outweighs the tangential 90:1, so the motion is near uniform-circular at that instant.

Concept A projectile has exactly one acceleration, g downward, at every instant. What changes is how that one vector splits against the velocity: the part along v is tangential (changes speed), the part across v is normal (bends the path). Any curve is locally a circle, so the normal part plays the role of v²/R.
Formula at = g·v̂, an = g sin(angle between g and v), and R = v²/an. R = v²/an is ac = v²/r read backwards, for the circle hugging the path there.
Solution
1. At t = 0, v0 points θ above the horizontal and g straight down, so the angle between them is 90° + θ.

5. At the top the vertical velocity is zero, so v is horizontal with magnitude v0cosθ and g is exactly perpendicular to it: at = 0, an = g.at = 0 there is exactly why the speed is minimum at the top.
6. R at the top: Rtop = (v0cosθ)²/g = v0²cos²θ/g.

5A table with smooth horizontal surface is turning at an angular speed ω about its axis. A groove is made on the surface along a radius and a particle is gently placed inside the groove at a distance a from the centre. Find the speed of the particle as its distance from the centre becomes L. — (Level-5)Bead in a rotating radial grooveA table with smooth horizontal surface is turning at an angular speed ω about its axis. A groove is made along a radius...🟢 Exactv = ω√(L² − a²)
Concept Ride with the table. In that rotating frame the bead only slides along the groove, and the price of the non-inertial frame is two pseudo-forces: centrifugal mω²r outward along the groove, Coriolis 2mωv across it. The walls supply a normal force cancelling the Coriolis term, so the motion is one-dimensional under a force that grows with r.
Formula Fcf = mω²r, Fcor = 2mωv, and the chain rule dv/dt = v(dv/dr). The last turns a problem with no known t into a clean r-integration.
Solution
1. "Gently placed" fixes the initial condition: v = 0 relative to the groove at r = a.
2. Across the groove, 2mωv is balanced by the walls' reaction, so it never enters the along-groove equation.Coriolis is always perpendicular to the relative velocity, so it does zero work and cannot change the speed.
3. Along the groove (smooth, so no friction): m dv/dt = mω²r ⇒ dv/dt = ω²r.
4. The acceleration depends on r, not t, so switch variables: v(dv/dr) = ω²r ⇒ v dv = ω²r dr.Standard escape when a = f(r) — no need to find r(t) at all.
5. Integrate from (a, 0) to (L, v): v²/2 = ω²(L² − a²)/2.
6. v = ω√(L² − a²), the sliding speed along the groove as measured on the table.
7. Checks: a = L gives v = 0 (not moved yet), a = 0 gives v = ωL; in the ground frame add the transverse ωL, giving ω√(2L² − a²).Radial sliding and transverse carry-along are perpendicular, so they add by Pythagoras.

1A particle is moving in a circle with uniform speed. It has: (a) constant kinetic energy (b) constant acceleration (c) constant velocity (d) constant displacementWhat is constant in UCMA particle moving with uniform speed in a circular path maintains: NEET 2024🟡 Similaraconstant kinetic energy
Concept "Uniform speed" locks the magnitude of the velocity and nothing else. Every vector quantity here keeps a fixed size but is dragged round with the particle, so its direction changes continuously — and a vector is constant only when both magnitude and direction hold still. So the survivors of this question are exactly the scalars built from speed, of which kinetic energy is one.
Formula KE = ½mv² (scalar, speed only), ac = v²/r (fixed size, inward direction rotates), s = 2r sin(θ/2) for the displacement from the start. Each formula is chosen to expose whether the quantity depends on direction at all.
Solution
1. Uniform speed ⇒ |v| = v is fixed; only the direction of v turns.
2. (a) KE = ½mv² involves v², a pure number with no direction, so it is constant.Squaring the speed erases every trace of direction, which is why energy survives where velocity does not.
3. (b) The acceleration has fixed magnitude v²/r but always points at the centre, and that inward direction sweeps round with the particle — so a⃗ is not constant.Constant vector means constant magnitude and direction; a constant a⃗ would force a parabolic path, not a circle.
4. (c) Velocity is tangential and turns every instant; a constant velocity means straight-line motion, the opposite of a circle. Out.
5. (d) Displacement from the starting point grows as s = 2r sin(θ/2), from 0 up to the diameter 2r and back — anything but constant.
6. Only (a) survives, and note it is the sole scalar in the list — the giveaway.

Concept A body on a circle has a velocity whose direction keeps turning, so the velocity vector changes even when the speed does not — and changing velocity is acceleration. Newton's second law then insists a real force act, and the geometry fixes where it points: straight at the centre. "Centripetal" names a role, not a new species of force; tension, friction and gravity take turns playing it.
Formula ac = v²/r = ω²r towards the centre, hence Fc = mv²/r. Written in the inertial ground frame, where only real forces may enter ΣF = ma.

1. Compare v at two nearby instants: |v| is unchanged but the directions differ, and Δv points inward ⇒ ac = v²/r towards the centre.Pure kinematics — true before any force is named.

5. (d) centrifugal force — outward pseudo-force of the rotating frame; an outward net force would widen the path, not bend it inward. Out.It has no reaction partner and vanishes the instant you stand on the ground.

1An object of mass 0.5 kg attached to a string of length 0.5 m is whirled in a horizontal circle at constant angular speed. If the maximum tension in the string is 5 kg wt. Calculate maximum speed of stone (in m/s). (g = 9.8 m/s²)Max speed from breaking tensionCircular motion Numerical part 2 | Class 11 | An object of mass 0.5 kg attached to a string of length 0.5 m whirled in a horizontal circle🟢 Exact7 m/s
Concept For a stone whirled in a horizontal circle the string tension is the only agent pointing at the centre, so it alone plays the centripetal role: T = mv²/r. The string can pull only up to a breaking value Tmax, and since v² rises with T, the fastest safe speed is the one that uses exactly that limit. The single trap is the unit "kg wt", a force disguised as a mass.
Formula T = mv²/r ⇒ vmax = √(Tmaxr/m), with 1 kg wt = 1 × g N. Ideal-model step: the circle is taken as truly horizontal, so weight is carried elsewhere and T is wholly centripetal.
Solution
1. Data: m = 0.5 kg, r = 0.5 m (string length = radius), Tmax = 5 kg wt, g = 9.8 m/s².
2. Convert the tension: Tmax = 5 kg wt = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N."kg wt" is the weight of that many kg, i.e. mg newtons — feeding 5 straight in gives 2.24 m/s, a classic wrong option.
3. Newton along the radius: T = mv²/r, the whole tension being centripetal for a horizontal circle.
4. Rearrange and substitute: v² = Tmaxr/m = (49)(0.5)/(0.5) = 49 m²/s².r and m are numerically equal here, so they cancel and v² is just Tmax — the arithmetic is one square root.
