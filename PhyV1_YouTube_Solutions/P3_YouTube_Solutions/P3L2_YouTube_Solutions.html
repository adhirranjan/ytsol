Concept The triangle law is the geometric rule for adding two vectors. Draw the first vector, then place the tail of the second at the head of the first (head-to-tail). The single vector that closes the triangle — drawn from the tail of the first to the head of the second — is their resultant. "Taken in order" means the two added vectors run the same way round the triangle (A then B); the resultant is "taken in reverse order" because it points back from the finish to the start. This is a definition question: choose the statement that captures exactly this closing-side idea.
Formula Statement form: if A and B are two sides of a triangle taken in order, then R = A + B is the third side taken in reverse order. Magnitude from the same triangle (cosine rule): R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ), θ = angle between A and B. The definition is purely geometric; the cosine rule only supplies the size of the closing side.
Solution
1. Test (a) "two vectors form sides of a triangle in opposite direction" — vague and wrong; for addition the two vectors are taken in order (head-to-tail), not opposite. ✗
2. Test (b) "added by forming a parallelogram" — that is the parallelogram law, a different (though equivalent) construction, not the triangle law. ✗
3. Test (c) "two sides of a triangle taken in order, sum is the third side taken in reverse order" — this is the exact textbook statement of the triangle law. ✓
4. Test (d) "vectors must be collinear for addition" — false; the triangle law adds vectors at any angle. ✗
5. Only (c) survives, so it is the answer.

Concept Two fixed vectors A and B at angle θ give a resultant R₁ by the cosine (parallelogram) law. Reversing B means replacing B by −B; the angle between A and −B becomes (180° − θ), so the cross-term 2AB cosθ simply flips sign. When you square each resultant and add, the +2AB cosθ and −2AB cosθ cross-terms cancel exactly, leaving a result that no longer depends on θ. That angle-independence is the whole trick — the answer is a fixed combination of A² and B².
Formula Resultant magnitude: R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ), so R² = A² + B² + 2AB cosθ. For the reversed vector use cos(180°−θ) = −cosθ. Only the sign of the cross-term distinguishes R₁² from R₂²; everything else is identical.
Solution
1. Original resultant of A + B: R₁² = A² + B² + 2AB cosθ.θ is the angle between A and B.
2. Reverse B → angle between A and −B is (180° − θ): R₂² = A² + B² + 2AB cos(180°−θ) = A² + B² − 2AB cosθ.
3. Add the two: R₁² + R₂² = (A²+B²+2AB cosθ) + (A²+B²−2AB cosθ).
4. The +2AB cosθ and −2AB cosθ cancel: R₁² + R₂² = 2A² + 2B² = 2(A² + B²).
5. Independent of θ — matches option (c).

It is largest when they point the same way (θ = 0°, they reinforce) and smallest when they oppose (θ = 180°, they partly cancel). 1. Write the maximum condition (forces parallel, θ = 0°): A + B = 11 N.cos0° = 1 makes the cross-term maximally positive.

Concept The triangle law of vector addition is the basic geometric rule for combining two vectors. It says that if two vectors are drawn head-to-tail as two sides of a triangle taken in order, the third side — drawn from the tail of the first to the head of the second — represents their sum in both magnitude and direction. It follows directly from the fact that displacement from O to P then P to Q is the same as the single displacement O to Q. This is a Board / NCERT-style descriptive question, so a complete statement plus the resultant formulae is expected.
Formula If A and B are the two sides taken in order and R is the closing side (reverse order), R = A + B. Magnitude: R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ); direction w.r.t. A: tanα = B sinθ / (A + B cosθ), θ = angle between A and B. Same triangle, cosine rule for size and the perpendicular construction for direction.
Solution
1. Statement: If two vectors are represented in magnitude and direction by two sides of a triangle taken in order, then their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the third side taken in reverse order.
2. Construction: place the tail of B at the head of A (O→P→Q); the resultant R = A + B is the arrow O→Q that closes the triangle.
3. Magnitude: from the cosine rule on that triangle, R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ).
4. Direction: the resultant makes angle α with A where tanα = B sinθ / (A + B cosθ).
5. Why it works: the net displacement O→P→Q equals the single displacement O→Q, so vectors add head-to-tail.

Concept The parallelogram law adds two vectors drawn from a common point (tail-to-tail). Completing the parallelogram, the diagonal through that point is the resultant. To find its size and direction, drop a perpendicular from the far corner onto the produced first side, forming a right triangle, then apply Pythagoras. This is a Board descriptive question, so a full statement, labelled proof, and both R and its direction are required.

Solution
1. Statement: if two vectors acting at a point are represented in magnitude and direction by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their resultant is represented by the diagonal passing through that point.

3. Right-triangle parts: AC = B, so CD = B sinθ and AD = B cosθ; hence OD = OA + AD = A + B cosθ.

Concept The polygon (or polygon law) of vectors extends the triangle law to any number of vectors. If several vectors are laid head-to-tail in order, they trace an open polygon; the single vector that closes the polygon (from the first tail to the last head) is their resultant. It is the natural generalisation: triangle law is the polygon law for two vectors. This Board question expects the statement plus the head-to-tail construction and the special closed-polygon case.
Formula For vectors A₁, A₂, …, An taken in order, R = A₁ + A₂ + … + An, the closing side taken in reverse order. Component form for computation: R = √((ΣAx)² + (ΣAy)²). If the polygon closes on itself, the resultant is zero.
Solution
1. Statement: if a number of vectors are represented in magnitude and direction by the sides of an open polygon taken in order, their resultant is represented by the closing side taken in the reverse order.
2. Construction: draw A₁, then A₂ from the head of A₁, then A₃ from the head of A₂, and so on head-to-tail.
3. The arrow from the tail of the first vector to the head of the last vector is the resultant R = A₁ + A₂ + … + An.
4. Closed-polygon case: if the last head coincides with the first tail, the polygon is closed and R = 0 — the vectors are in equilibrium.
5. For numbers, resolve every vector into x- and y-parts and sum separately, then recombine by Pythagoras.

4State the Properties of Vector AdditionProperties of vector additionProperties of Vector Addition – Class 11 Physics (all derivations) · Properties of Vector Addition, Special Cases – Class 11/JEE/NEET · Vectors 06 – Important Properties of Vector Addition (NEET/IIT)🟢 Exact—
Concept Vector addition obeys the same structural rules as ordinary addition of numbers, because it can be built up geometrically (head-to-tail) without reference to any coordinate axes. The key properties are commutativity, associativity, the existence of a zero (null) vector, and the existence of an additive inverse. This Board question asks you to list and briefly justify each property.
Formula Commutative: A + B = B + A. Associative: (A + B) + C = A + (B + C). Identity: A + 0 = A. Inverse: A + (−A) = 0. 0 is the null vector, of zero magnitude and no specified direction; −A is A reversed.
Solution
1. Commutative law: A + B = B + A. The parallelogram built on A and B has the same diagonal whichever side you start from, so order does not matter.
2. Associative law: (A + B) + C = A + (B + C). Chaining three vectors head-to-tail gives the same closing vector regardless of how you group them.
3. Additive identity: A + 0 = A, where 0 is the null vector (zero magnitude); adding it changes nothing.
4. Additive inverse: for every A there is −A (same magnitude, opposite direction) with A + (−A) = 0.
5. Closure (bonus): the sum of two vectors is itself a vector, so the set is closed under addition.
 property        rule                        geometric reason
 commutative     A + B = B + A               same parallelogram diagonal
 associative     (A+B)+C = A+(B+C)           same head-to-tail closure
 identity        A + 0 = A                   0 = null vector
 inverse         A + (-A) = 0                -A is A reversed
 closure         A + B is a vector           sum stays a vector
⚡ JEE Trick These four laws (commutative, associative, identity, inverse) make vectors an abelian group under addition — the same reason you may freely rearrange terms in a vector equation. Trap: commutativity and associativity hold for addition but not for the vector (cross) product, where A×B = −B×A — do not carry these properties over to cross products.
Answer: Vector addition is commutative (A+B=B+A), associative ((A+B)+C=A+(B+C)), has an identity (A+0=A) and an inverse (A+(−A)=0), and is closed.

Formula R² = P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ with P = A + B, Q = A − B, R² = A² + B². P² + Q² = (A+B)² + (A−B)² = 2(A² + B²); PQ = (A+B)(A−B) = A² − B².
Solution
1. Compute P² + Q² = (A + B)² + (A − B)² = 2(A² + B²).
2. Compute the product 2PQ = 2(A + B)(A − B) = 2(A² − B²).
3. Substitute into the law: A² + B² = 2(A² + B²) + 2(A² − B²)cosθ.
4. Isolate the cosine term: 2(A² − B²)cosθ = (A² + B²) − 2(A² + B²) = −(A² + B²).
5. Solve: cosθ = −(A² + B²)/[2(A² − B²)] = (A² + B²)/[2(B² − A²)] ⇒ θ = cos⁻¹((A² + B²)/(2(B² − A²))).Flipping the sign moves the minus onto the denominator, giving the (B² − A²) form.
