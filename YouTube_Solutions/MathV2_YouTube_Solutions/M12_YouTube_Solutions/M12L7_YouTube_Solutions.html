1 (i)Fill in the blanks: Sum of opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral is ______cyclic quadrilateral opposite anglesOpposite Angles of a Cyclic Quadrilateral add up to 180 Degrees - Proof | Don't Memorise🟡 Similar(i) 180°
Concept A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on one circle is cyclic. Each pair of opposite angles stands on the two arcs that together make the whole circle, and an inscribed angle is half its arc. So the pair always adds up to half of 360°. Trap: it is the opposite angles, not adjacent ones, that are supplementary.
Formula ∠A + ∠C = (1/2)(α + β) = (1/2)(360°) = 180° Inscribed-angle theorem: an angle on the circle is half the central angle of the arc it stands on.
Solution
1. Let ABCD be inscribed in a circle with centre O. ∠A = ∠DAB stands on arc BCD; ∠C = ∠BCD stands on arc DAB.
2. Call the central angles of these arcs α (arc BCD) and β (arc DAB). The two arcs are complementary pieces of the circle.
3. Inscribed-angle theorem: ∠A = α/2 and ∠C = β/2.
4. Whole circle: α + β = 360°, so ∠A + ∠C = α/2 + β/2 = 360°/2 = 180°.
5. Angle sum of a quadrilateral is 360° ⇒ ∠B + ∠D = 360° − 180° = 180° as well.
6. Trig form used in this lecture: C = 180° − A ⇒ cos C = −cos A and sin C = sin A. This is exactly what makes Brahmagupta's area √((s−a)(s−b)(s−c)(s−d)) and Ptolemy's theorem work.
7. Converse is also true: if one pair of opposite angles is supplementary, the four points are concyclic.
 arc BCD : central angle α  ->  ∠A = α/2
 arc DAB : central angle β  ->  ∠C = β/2
 α + β = 360°
 ∠A + ∠C = (α + β)/2 = 180°
 ∠B + ∠D = 360° - 180° = 180°
 -----------------------------------------

⚡ JEE Trick Sanity check: a rectangle is cyclic (its diagonals are diameters) and 90° + 90° = 180°. A rhombus that is not a square has opposite angles equal but not 90°, so they cannot add to 180°: it is never cyclic. Exterior angle at a vertex = interior opposite angle is the same fact restated. Quick use: if A = 70° in a cyclic quadrilateral, then C = 110° at once, and cos C = −cos 70°.
Answer: (i) 180° (opposite angles are supplementary).

Concept This is Ptolemy's theorem: for a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, AB·CD + BC·DA = AC·BD. The left side is the sum of the products of the two pairs of opposite sides, so it equals the product of the diagonals. Trap: it is a strict equality only for cyclic quadrilaterals; for any other quadrilateral the left side is larger (Ptolemy's inequality).
Formula AB·CD + BC·DA = AC·BD Proved by building two pairs of similar triangles that share the diagonal AC.
Solution
1. Pick E on diagonal BD such that ∠BAE = ∠CAD.
2. ∠ABE = ∠ABD = ∠ACD (same arc AD). So △ABE ~ △ACD (AA) ⇒ AB/AC = BE/CD ⇒ AB·CD = AC·BE.
3. Adding ∠EAC to both sides of ∠BAE = ∠CAD gives ∠BAC = ∠EAD.
4. ∠ADE = ∠ADB = ∠ACB (same arc AB). So △ADE ~ △ACB (AA) ⇒ AD/AC = DE/CB ⇒ AD·BC = AC·DE.
5. Add the two results: AB·CD + AD·BC = AC·(BE + DE) = AC·BD, because E lies on BD.
6. So the sum of products of opposite sides = product of the diagonals.

6. P/D = 2·(1 − t²)/2 = 1 − t² = 2/√5. Hence P : D = 2 : √5. Numerically tan18°/tan36° = 0.3249/0.7265 = 0.4472 = 1/√5 ✓.
