1Which correctly represents cos θ in a right triangle?Ratio definitionwatch?v=G4Dm8WYc73s🟡 SimilarbBase/Hypotenuse
Concept In a right triangle each trig ratio compares two specific sides taken relative to the acute angle θ. Naming the sides correctly is the whole task: the side touching θ (other than the hypotenuse) is the adjacent side (the base), the side facing θ is the opposite (perpendicular), and the longest side facing the right angle is the hypotenuse. cos θ is the ratio that pairs the adjacent side with the hypotenuse.
Formula cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse = base ÷ hyp. From SOH-CAH-TOA: CAH = Cosine–Adjacent–Hypotenuse, so cos always uses the leg next to θ over the hypotenuse.
Solution
1. Fix the reference angle θ and label the three sides: opposite (perpendicular), adjacent (base), hypotenuse.Every ratio is meaningless until "adjacent" and "opposite" are pinned to θ.
2. Apply the definition of cosine: cos θ = (side adjacent to θ) ÷ (hypotenuse).This is the C-A-H block of the mnemonic.
3. Since the adjacent side is the base, cos θ = base ÷ hypotenuse.
⚡ JEE Trick Keep the three CAH/SOH/TOA blocks straight: only cos uses adjacent/hyp; sin uses opposite/hyp; tan uses opposite/adjacent. Trap: options that swap opposite for adjacent give sin θ, not cos θ.
Answer: cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse (= base/hypotenuse).

Concept Almost every basic identity proof runs on one engine: convert every ratio to sin x and cos x, combine over a common denominator, and let sin²x + cos²x = 1 collapse the numerator. Here tan x + cot x merges into a single fraction whose numerator is exactly the Pythagorean identity, and the leftover 1/(sin x cos x) is precisely sec x·cosec x written as a product of reciprocals. The four companion identities in this exercise fall to the identical machine.
Formula tan x = sin x/cos x, cot x = cos x/sin x, sin²x + cos²x = 1, sec x·cosec x = 1/(cos x·sin x). Reciprocal + quotient definitions are the only tools needed.
Solution
1. Start from the LHS and switch to sin/cos: tan x + cot x = sin x/cos x + cos x/sin x.Quotient identities.
2. Combine over the common denominator sin x·cos x: LHS = (sin²x + cos²x)/(sin x·cos x).Cross-multiply each term up: sin x·sin x and cos x·cos x.
3. Apply the Pythagorean identity to the numerator: sin²x + cos²x = 1, so LHS = 1/(sin x·cos x).
4. Split the single fraction into a product of reciprocals: 1/(sin x·cos x) = (1/cos x)·(1/sin x) = sec x·cosec x = RHS. ∎
5. The remaining identities of this problem set are proved the same way: rewrite in sin/cos, take a common denominator, deploy sin²x + cos²x = 1, then re-name the result via the reciprocal definitions.

7Prove cosθ/(1−tanθ) + sinθ/(1−cotθ) = sinθ + cosθIdentity proofwatch?v=NaT8174VjPQ🟡 Similar—
Concept The trick with mixed tan/cot denominators is to convert everything to sin θ and cos θ first. Each denominator then becomes a single fraction, the two terms share the factor (cos θ − sin θ) up to sign, and a difference-of-squares collapses the whole expression.
Formula tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, cot θ = cos θ/sin θ, and a² − b² = (a−b)(a+b).
Solution
1. Rewrite denominators: 1 − tan θ = 1 − sin θ/cos θ = (cos θ − sin θ)/cos θ; likewise 1 − cot θ = (sin θ − cos θ)/sin θ.
2. Invert and multiply each term:Dividing by a fraction multiplies by its reciprocal. cos θ ÷ [(cos θ − sin θ)/cos θ] = cos²θ/(cos θ − sin θ); sin θ ÷ [(sin θ − cos θ)/sin θ] = sin²θ/(sin θ − cos θ).
3. Make denominators identical: sin²θ/(sin θ − cos θ) = −sin²θ/(cos θ − sin θ).(sin θ − cos θ) = −(cos θ − sin θ).
4. Add over the common denominator: LHS = (cos²θ − sin²θ)/(cos θ − sin θ).
5. Factor the numerator: cos²θ − sin²θ = (cos θ − sin θ)(cos θ + sin θ), then cancel (cos θ − sin θ): LHS = cos θ + sin θ.
6. This equals the RHS, so the identity is proved. ∎
⚡ JEE Trick For any "1 ± tan / 1 ± cot" pair, expect the shared factor (cos θ − sin θ) and a difference of squares — convert to sin/cos, don't rationalise blindly. Trap: dropping the minus sign in step 3 gives 0 instead of sin θ + cos θ.
Answer: identity verified — LHS = sin θ + cos θ = RHS. ∎
8Prove (1+cosecA+cotA)/(1+cosecA−cotA) = cosecA + cotAIdentity proofwatch?v=NaT8174VjPQ🟡 Similar—
Concept The engine of this proof is the Pythagorean identity in the form cosec²A − cot²A = 1. Replacing the lone "1" in the numerator by this difference of squares exposes the common factor (cosec A + cot A), which then cancels against the whole denominator.
Formula cosec²A − cot²A = 1, i.e. (cosec A − cot A)(cosec A + cot A) = 1.
Solution
1. In the numerator write 1 = cosec²A − cot²A = (cosec A − cot A)(cosec A + cot A).
2. Numerator = (cosec A − cot A)(cosec A + cot A) + (cosec A + cot A).The remaining +cosec A + cot A is untouched.
3. Factor out (cosec A + cot A): numerator = (cosec A + cot A)·[(cosec A − cot A) + 1].
4. Denominator = 1 + cosec A − cot A = (cosec A − cot A) + 1 — exactly the bracket in step 3.
5. Cancel the common bracket: ratio = (cosec A + cot A)·[(cosec A − cot A)+1] ÷ [(cosec A − cot A)+1] = cosec A + cot A.
6. Equals the RHS, so the identity holds. ∎
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a "1" sits beside a cosec/cot (or sec/tan) pair, rewrite it as the matching difference of squares — that is what unlocks the factorisation. Trap: trying to cross-multiply the whole thing is far messier than the one-line 1 = cosec²−cot² substitution.
Answer: identity verified — LHS = cosec A + cot A = RHS. ∎

This is a classic JEE-Main one-liner once the two power-reduction identities are known.
