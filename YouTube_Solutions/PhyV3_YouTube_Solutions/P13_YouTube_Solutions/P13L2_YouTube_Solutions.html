1The value of acceleration due to gravity is the same for all bodies because: (a) All bodies have same shape (b) It depends on mass of body (c) It is independent of mass of body (d) Air resistance is same — (Level-1)g independent of body massAcceleration due to gravity is the same for all objects || in HINDI for Class 9🟡 SimilarcIt is independent of mass of body
Concept The gravitational pull on a body is proportional to its own mass, and so is its inertia. Dividing force by mass, the body's mass cancels exactly, so every body at a given place falls with the same acceleration g. The trap is option (b): heavier bodies feel a bigger force, but not a bigger acceleration.
Formula a = F/m = (GMm/R²)/m = GM/R² = g Newton's law of gravitation gives the force; Newton's second law converts it to acceleration. Gravitational mass = inertial mass, so m drops out.
Solution
1. Force on a body of mass m at Earth's surface: F = GMm/R², directed towards Earth's centre.
2. Acceleration produced: a = F/m = (GMm/R²)/m = GM/R². The same m sits in the numerator (gravity) and the denominator (inertia).
3. The result GM/R² contains only Earth's mass M, Earth's radius R and G; nothing belonging to the falling body.
4. Numerically: g = GM/R² = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 6×10²⁴/(6.4×10⁶)² ≈ 9.8 m/s² for a pin or a truck alike.
5. Check the options: (a) shape does not enter GM/R²; (b) says g depends on mass, the opposite of step 2; (d) air resistance is not part of g at all (it is a separate drag force, and it is not the same for all bodies anyway).
6. Hence g is the same for all bodies because it is independent of the mass of the body, option (c).
 F   = G M m / R²       (pull on body)
 a   = F / m            (2nd law)
     = G M / R²         (m cancels)
 g   = 6.67e-11 x 6e24 / (6.4e6)² = 9.8 m/s²
 depends on : M, R of Earth only
 -----------------------------------------
 g independent of body mass m     <-- answer (c)
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a force is proportional to the mass it acts on (gravity, pseudo force), the acceleration is mass-free. Trap: (b) mixes up weight (mg, depends on m) with g (does not). (d) is tempting from feather-vs-coin demos, but air drag is why they fall differently in air, not why g is common.
Answer: (c) It is independent of mass of body.
2If the radius of the Earth were increased by a factor of 3, by what factor would its density have to be changed to keep 'g' the same? — (Level-2)g in terms of density radiusIf the radius of the earth were incresed by a factor of 3, by what factor would its density🟢 Exactρ' = ρ/3
Concept Write g in terms of density instead of mass: since M = ρ·(4/3)πR³, the surface field g = GM/R² becomes proportional to the product ρR. Keeping g fixed therefore means ρR must stay constant. The trap is to use g = GM/R² with M held fixed and answer "9 times"; but M itself grows when R grows at fixed density.
Formula g = GM/R² = G·(4/3)πR³ρ/R² = (4/3)πGρR Valid for a uniform sphere; the question fixes g and changes R, so ρ is the free variable.
Solution
1. Mass of a uniform sphere: M = ρ·V = ρ·(4/3)πR³.
2. Surface gravity: g = GM/R² = G·(4/3)πρR³/R² = (4/3)πGρR. One power of R survives, so g ∝ ρR.
3. Condition: g' = g ⇒ ρ'R' = ρR.
4. With R' = 3R: ρ' = ρR/R' = ρR/(3R) = ρ/3.
5. Check with masses: new mass M' = (ρ/3)·(4/3)π(3R)³ = 9M; then g' = G(9M)/(3R)² = GM/R² = g. ✓
6. Numerically for Earth: ρ = 5500 kg/m³ and R = 6400 km give g = (4/3)π·6.67×10⁻¹¹·5500·6.4×10⁶ ≈ 9.8 m/s²; with 3R and ρ/3 the product ρR is unchanged, so g is again ≈ 9.8 m/s². Only the product ρR matters.
7. So the density must be reduced to one-third of its value (changed by a factor 1/3).
 g  = (4/3) pi G rho R        =>  g ∝ rho.R
 g' = g  =>  rho'.R' = rho.R
 R' = 3R  =>  rho' = rho.R/(3R) = rho/3
 check: M' = (rho/3)(3R)³(4pi/3) = 9M
        g' = G.9M/(9R²) = g   ok

⚡ JEE Trick Memorise both forms: g ∝ M/R² at fixed mass, g ∝ ρR at fixed density. Pick the form whose "other" variable is the one being solved for. Trap: holding M constant and tripling R gives g/9, then people answer "density ×9"; that ignores that density, not mass, is the knob here. Sanity check: bigger planet at the same g must be less dense, so the factor must be below 1.
Answer: Density must become ρ/3 (reduced by a factor of 3).

This links three measurable things (g, R, G) to the one we cannot weigh directly, Earth's mass. Rearranging gives M = gR²/G. The trap is arithmetic: R must be squared in metres, and the powers of ten must be tracked carefully.
Formula mg = GMm/R² ⇒ M = gR²/G Earth treated as a uniform sphere, so its pull acts as if all mass were at the centre; rotation ignored.

1. Data: g = 9.81 m/s², R = 6.37 × 10⁶ m, G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².

A 10% drop in g means g' = 0.9g, so 2h/R = 0.1. The trap is writing h/R = 0.1 (that is the depth law) or setting g' = 0.1g.
Formula g' = g(1 − 2h/R) First-order binomial approximation of g R²/(R + h)², allowed because the question says h is small compared with R.
Solution
1. "Decreases by 10%" ⇒ g' = g − 0.10g = 0.90g, i.e. Δg/g = 0.10.
2. Exact form: g' = GM/(R + h)² = g(1 + h/R)⁻² ≈ g(1 − 2h/R) for h ≪ R.
3. Fractional decrease: Δg/g = 2h/R. The "2" comes from the square in the inverse-square law.
4. Set 2h/R = 0.10 ⇒ h = 0.05R.
5. h = 0.05 × 6400 km = 320 km.
6. Accuracy check with the exact law: (R/(R + h))² = (6400/6720)² = 0.907, a 9.3% drop. The exact height for 10% is R(1/√0.9 − 1) = 6400 × 0.0541 = 346 km; the small-h answer the book wants is 320 km.
7. Check with the linear law: g' = 9.8(1 − 2·320/6400) = 9.8 × 0.9 = 8.82 m/s², exactly 10% below 9.8 as required. Consistent within the approximation the question allows.

4At what distance below the surface of the Earth, the acceleration due to gravity decreases by 10% of its value at the surface, given radius of Earth is 6400 km. — (Level: Board)depth for 10% decrease in g#1 GRAVITATION At depth 'd', if g is decreases by 10% of its value at the surface, find 'd'🟢 Exact640 km
Concept Inside a uniform Earth only the mass of the inner sphere of radius (R − d) pulls on you; the outer shell exerts zero net force (shell theorem). The inner mass shrinks as r³ while the distance shrinks as r², so g falls linearly: g' = g(1 − d/R). This is an exact result for a uniform sphere, no approximation needed. The trap is borrowing the height law with its factor 2.
Formula g' = g(1 − d/R) From g(r) = GM r/R³ inside a uniform sphere, with r = R − d.
Solution
1. "Decreases by 10%" ⇒ g' = 0.90g, so Δg/g = 0.10.
2. Inside the Earth: g(r) = G·M(r)/r² with M(r) = M r³/R³ ⇒ g(r) = GMr/R³ = g·r/R. Shell theorem: the outer layer contributes nothing.
3. At depth d, r = R − d ⇒ g' = g(R − d)/R = g(1 − d/R).
4. Fractional decrease: Δg/g = d/R = 0.10 ⇒ d = 0.1R.
5. d = 0.1 × 6400 km = 640 km.
6. Check: g' = 9.8(1 − 640/6400) = 9.8 × 0.9 = 8.82 m/s², exactly 10% below 9.8. ✓
7. Compare with going up: for the same 10% drop the height is only 320 km, because above the surface the full mass M still pulls and only the distance grows, giving the faster 2h/R fall-off. Below, mass loss (r³) partly offsets the closer distance (r²).

Note the depth law is linear and exact for a uniform Earth, valid even for large depths, while the height law 1 − 2h/R is only a small-h approximation. At the centre (d = R) the formula correctly gives g = 0.
