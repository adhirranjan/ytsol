1The vector v(rm) in the context of rain and man motion represents: (c) Rain's velocity relative to the manv_rm definitionJEE Physics — Relative motion in 2-D: Rain Umbrella Problem · If Vr is velocity of rain and Vm is velocity of man...🟢 ExactcRain's velocity relative to the man
Concept The double subscript in v⃗rm is read left-to-right as "rain, as seen by man": the first letter names the object whose motion you are describing, the second names the observer/frame doing the watching. It is the velocity the man actually perceives for the rain, obtained by stepping out of the ground frame into the man's frame. Ordering matters because v⃗rm and v⃗mr are equal in size but opposite in direction.
Formula v⃗rm = v⃗r − v⃗m — velocity of rain relative to the man. To transfer any velocity into an observer's frame you subtract that observer's own ground velocity; this single rule generates every rain-umbrella result. No numbers are needed — this is a definition of the symbol itself.
Solution
1. Decode the subscript order: "rm" = object rain relative to observer man — object first, frame second.The universal convention v⃗AB = "velocity of A with respect to B" fixes which letter is which.
2. Apply the defining relation v⃗rm = v⃗r − v⃗m — subtract the man's velocity from the rain's.
3. Interpret it physically: this vector is the rain's apparent motion in the man's frame, and its direction is exactly where the umbrella must point.You always tilt the umbrella along v⃗rm, never along v⃗r.
4. Rule out the reverse: v⃗mr = v⃗m − v⃗r = −v⃗rm, the man seen from the rain — opposite arrow, so it is a different quantity.
5. Hence v⃗rm is precisely "rain's velocity relative to the man" — option (c).
   symbol      reads as                 formula
   ---------   ----------------------   ----------------
   v_rm        rain w.r.t. man          v_r - v_m   <-- answer (c)
   v_mr        man  w.r.t. rain         v_m - v_r  = -v_rm
   v_r         rain w.r.t. ground       (given)
⚡ JEE Trick Read any double subscript right-to-left: "velocity of the first, as seen by the second." Trap: students flip v⃗rm and v⃗mr and get an answer pointing the wrong way — remember the sign flip v⃗rm = −v⃗mr before committing to a direction.
Answer: (c) Rain's velocity relative to the man.
2Rain falling vertically downwards at 4 km/h. A girl moves on a straight road with velocity 3 km/h. Apparent velocity of rain w.r.t. girl? (5 km/h)Relative velocity (3-4-5 triangle)Rain falling vertically downward at a speed of 4 km/hr. A girl... · Rain falling vertically 4 m/s, man moving horizontally 3 m/s🟢 Exact5 km/h
Concept The girl sees the rain not as it moves over the ground but as it moves relative to her, so its apparent velocity is the vector difference v⃗r − v⃗g. Because the rain falls straight down and the girl walks horizontally, these two velocities are perpendicular, and the difference is the hypotenuse of a right triangle. The magnitude then comes straight from Pythagoras.
Formula v⃗rg = v⃗r − v⃗g, magnitude |v⃗rg| = √(vr² + vg²) when the two are ⟂. Square-root of (km/h)² + (km/h)² is again km/h — dimensionally a speed. Data: vr = 4 km/h (down), vg = 3 km/h (horizontal).
Solution
1. Set axes: horizontal = x (girl's road), vertical = y. Rain v⃗r = (0, −4) km/h; girl v⃗g = (3, 0) km/h.
2. Subtract to enter the girl's frame: v⃗rg = (0, −4) − (3, 0) = (−3, −4) km/h.The −3 means the rain appears to blow toward her front — the reason a walker feels rain on the chest.
3. Magnitude: |v⃗rg| = √((−3)² + (−4)²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 km/h.
4. Direction from the vertical: tan θ = 3/4 = 0.75 ⇒ θ = tan⁻¹(0.75) ≈ 36.9°, leaning forward into her walk.Not asked for the magnitude part, but this is where the umbrella points.
5. So the apparent (relative) velocity of the rain is 5 km/h — a textbook 3-4-5 triangle.

⚡ JEE Trick Spot the 3-4-5 Pythagorean triple on sight and skip the arithmetic. Trap: the answer is the resultant 5 km/h, not 4−3 = 1 or 4+3 = 7 — perpendicular velocities never add or subtract as plain numbers, they combine as a right triangle.
Answer: 5 km/h.

Formula v⃗rm = v⃗r − v⃗m. "Rain appears vertical" ⇒ horizontal part of v⃗rm is zero; "appears at θ to vertical" ⇒ tan θ = (horizontal)/(vertical) of v⃗rm. Let the ground rain velocity be v⃗r = (a, −b) with downward = −ĵ; solve for a and b.
