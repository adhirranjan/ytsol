1 (i)
Fill in the blank: 11.2 L of O2 at NTP is produced from ________ g of H2O2.
C9L4VRecapQ1iH2O2 mass for 11.2 L O2
🟡 SimilarThe volume of oxygen…
34
Concept H2O2 decomposes into water and oxygen, and the balanced equation fixes how many grams of peroxide release one mole of O2. The trap is reading the equation as 1 : 1: two molecules of H2O2 give only one molecule of O2.
Key relation 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2: 68 g H2O2 ↔ 22.4 L O2 at NTP One mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L at NTP, so the litres of O2 convert straight to moles.
Solution
1. Write the decomposition: 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2. Each O in H2O2 is −1; half go to −2 (water) and half to 0 (O2), a disproportionation.
2. Molar mass of H2O2 = 2(1) + 2(16) = 34 g/mol, so 2 mol = 68 g.
3. Moles of O2 wanted: n = V/22.4 = 11.2/22.4 = 0.5 mol.
4. Mole ratio from the equation: n(H2O2) = 2 × n(O2) = 2 × 0.5 = 1 mol. two peroxide molecules per O2
5. Mass of peroxide: m = n × M = 1 × 34 = 34 g.
6. Cross-check by proportion: 68 g gives 22.4 L, so x g gives 11.2 L ⇒ x = 68 × 11.2/22.4 = 34 g.
7. Link to volume strength: the same ratio gives strength (g/L) = (68/22.4) × V = 3.036 × V, the relation used for every “volume” label in this lecture.
⚡ JEE Trick Memorise “34 g H2O2 ↔ 11.2 L O2” (equivalently 17 g, one equivalent, ↔ 5.6 L). It is the root of V = 5.6 N = 11.2 M. The common slip is answering 17 g (taking 1 mol H2O2 per mol O2) or 68 g (forgetting that 11.2 L is only half a mole).
Answer: 34 g of H2O2.
1 (ii)
Fill in the blank: Hydrogen peroxide oxidise hydrogen sulphide to ________.
C9L4VRecapQ1iiH2O2 + H2S reaction
🟢 ExactHydrogen peroxide ox…
sulphur
Concept H2S is a reducing agent (S at −2, its lowest state) and H2O2 is an oxidant. With ordinary H2O2 the sulphide is oxidised only as far as elemental sulphur, which appears as a pale-yellow turbidity. The trap is writing H2SO4, which is the product with metal sulphides such as PbS, not with H2S gas.
Key relation H2S + H2O2 → S↓ + 2H2O S: −2 → 0 (oxidised, loses 2e−); O: −1 → −2 (reduced, 2 O atoms gain 1e− each).
Solution
1. Assign oxidation numbers in the reactants: S in H2S = −2; O in H2O2 = −1.
2. Oxidation half: H2S → S + 2H+ + 2e−. S rises from −2 to 0.
3. Reduction half: H2O2 + 2H+ + 2e− → 2H2O. Each O falls from −1 to −2. this is the oxidising half of H2O2
4. The electrons balance 2 : 2, so adding the halves gives H2S + H2O2 → S + 2H2O (atoms: 4 H, 1 S, 2 O on each side).
5. The product is colloidal sulphur, seen as a milky pale-yellow deposit; the book writes this very reaction in QR 3(vi) as evidence of the oxidising nature of H2O2.
6. Contrast: a metal sulphide is pushed all the way to sulphate, PbS + 4H2O2 → PbSO4 + 4H2O (S: −2 → +6), the reaction used to restore blackened paintings.
⚡ JEE Trick For H2S with a mild oxidant (H2O2, Cl2, SO2, Fe3+, acidified KMnO4) the answer is almost always S, the yellow precipitate. Reserve “sulphate” for the PbS → PbSO4 (black → white) case.
Answer: sulphur (S).
1 (iii)
Fill in the blank: Belching [bleaching] action of H2O2 is due to its ________ nature.
C9L4VRecapQ1iiiBleaching action of H2O2
🟢 ExactBleaching action of…
oxidising
Concept The book’s stem prints “Belching”, a misprint for bleaching. H2O2 bleaches because it releases nascent oxygen that oxidises the coloured matter (the chromophore) to a colourless product. It is therefore an oxidising bleach, unlike SO2, which bleaches by reduction.
Key relation H2O2 → H2O + [O];  coloured matter + [O] → colourless matter O goes from −1 to −2 in water, so the peroxide is reduced while the dye is oxidised.
Solution
1. Read the stem as intended: “Bleaching action of H2O2 is due to its ____ nature.”
2. H2O2 is unstable: H2O2 → H2O + [O]. The O.N. of O falls −1 → −2 in the water formed.
3. The nascent oxygen [O] attacks the conjugated chromophore of a dye and oxidises it to a colourless compound. this is oxidation of the dye, reduction of the peroxide
4. So the bleaching follows directly from the oxidising nature of H2O2.
5. Because the bleach is mild and leaves only water behind, it is used for delicate materials: silk, wool, hair, ivory, feathers and paper pulp.
6. Oxidative bleaching is permanent, whereas SO2 bleaches by reduction and the colour returns slowly when air re-oxidises the reduced dye.
⚡ JEE Trick Pair the bleaches with their mechanism: Cl2 (moist), H2O2, O3 bleach by oxidation (permanent); SO2 bleaches by reduction (temporary). Do not answer “reducing” just because H2O2 can also act as a reductant; that dual nature is not the source of its bleaching.
Answer: oxidising nature (the stem’s “Belching” is a misprint for bleaching).
1 (iv)
Fill in the blank: Hydrogen peroxide act as an ________ as well as a ________ agent.
C9L4VRecapQ1ivDual redox nature of H2O2
🟢 ExactHydrogen peroxide ac…
oxidising, reducing
Concept In H2O2 oxygen sits at −1, midway between its common states −2 (H2O, OH−) and 0 (O2). It can therefore move either way: down to −2 as an oxidising agent, or up to 0 as a reducing agent, in both acidic and alkaline media.
Key relation Oxidant: H2O2 + 2H+ + 2e− → 2H2O;  Reductant: H2O2 → O2 + 2H+ + 2e− An intermediate oxidation state is the signature of a species that can act both ways.
Solution
1. O.N. of O in H2O2: 2(+1) + 2x = 0 ⇒ x = −1, an intermediate state.
2. Oxidising (acid): 2Fe2+ + H2O2 + 2H+ → 2Fe3+ + 2H2O; also 2I− → I2 and PbS → PbSO4.
3. Oxidising (alkali): 2Cr(OH)3 + 3H2O2 + 4OH− → 2CrO42− + 8H2O; Mn2+ → MnO2. O drops to −2 in every case
4. Reducing (acid): 2MnO4− + 5H2O2 + 6H+ → 2Mn2+ + 8H2O + 5O2, the pink colour is discharged.
5. Reducing (alkali / neutral): I2 + H2O2 + 2OH− → 2I− + 2H2O + O2; Ag2O + H2O2 → 2Ag + H2O + O2.
6. Whenever H2O2 acts as a reductant, O2 gas is evolved; that is the quick diagnostic.
⚡ JEE Trick “O2 bubbles out ⇒ H2O2 was the reductant.” Against strong oxidants (KMnO4, O3, Cl2, Ag2O, PbO2) it reduces; against reductants (Fe2+, I−, sulphides) it oxidises.
Answer: an oxidising as well as a reducing agent.
1 (v)
Fill in the blank: The oxidation number of oxygen in hydrogen peroxide is ________.
C9L4VRecapQ1vOxidation number of O in H2O2
🟢 ExactThe oxidation number…
−1
Concept H2O2 contains an O–O single bond, the peroxide linkage. A bond between two identical atoms contributes nothing to oxidation number, so each oxygen only has to balance one hydrogen. The trap is applying the “oxygen is always −2” rule, which fails for peroxides, superoxides and OF2.
Key relation Σ O.N. = 0: 2(+1) + 2x = 0 ⇒ x = −1 H is +1 when bonded to a non-metal; the molecule is neutral.
Solution
1. Structure: H–O–O–H. Each O is bonded to one H and to the other O.
2. Algebraic method: 2(+1) + 2x = 0 ⇒ 2x = −2 ⇒ x = −1.
3. Bond-electron method: in each O–H bond the more electronegative O takes both electrons (−1 per bond); the O–O bond is split equally (0). Each O therefore gets −1. confirms that the algebra is not hiding unequal O atoms
4. The peroxide ion is O22− (2 × −1 = −2), as in Na2O2 and BaO2, which is why those salts give H2O2 with acid.
5. Compare: H2O (−2), KO2 superoxide (−1/2), O2 (0), OF2 (+2).
6. The value −1 is intermediate between −2 and 0, which explains why H2O2 is both an oxidant and a reductant and why it disproportionates to H2O + O2.
⚡ JEE Trick Spot an O–O bond and drop the −2 reflex. Na2O2, BaO2, H2SO5 and H2S2O8 all carry peroxide oxygens at −1, which is why S in H2S2O8 is +6, not +7, despite the formula count.
Answer: −1.
2 (i)
True or false: Hydrogen peroxide is a planar molecule.
C9L4VRecapQ2iStructure of H2O2
🟢 Exactthe structure of h2o…
False
Concept H2O2 is non-planar. The O–O bond is the “spine” of an open book and the two O–H bonds lie on two different pages. The repulsion between the lone pairs on adjacent O atoms twists the molecule out of a single plane.
Key relation dihedral angle (H–O–O plane vs O–O–H plane) = 111.5° (gas), 90.2° (solid) A planar molecule would need a dihedral of 0° (cis) or 180° (trans).
Solution
1. Each O is sp3 hybridised: two bond pairs (to H and to the other O) and two lone pairs.
2. Looking along the O–O bond, the lone pairs on the two oxygens repel one another, and the molecule rotates to a skew (gauche-like) conformation that minimises that repulsion. neither cis nor trans planar is the minimum
3. The result is the open-book structure: the two H–O–O planes meet along the O–O “spine” at a dihedral angle of 111.5° in the gas phase.
4. In the solid, hydrogen bonding changes the angle to 90.2°; still far from 0° or 180°, so the molecule is non-planar in every phase.
5. Other data: O–O = 147.5 pm (gas) / 145.8 pm (solid); H–O–O angle 94.8° (gas) / 101.9° (solid).
6. Since the dihedral angle is neither 0° nor 180°, the four atoms cannot be coplanar. The statement is False.
⚡ JEE Trick “Open book” = non-planar. The same question returns as “the two O–H bonds lie in the same plane” (QR 3(i)(b)), and that is the false option. Remember the two dihedral numbers 111.5° (gas) and 90.2° (solid); the gas value is the one usually asked.
Answer: False. H2O2 has a non-planar, open-book structure.
2 (ii)
True or false: The peroxo ion can be written as O22−
C9L4VRecapQ2iiPeroxide ion
🟡 SimilarWhat are Peroxide io…
True
Concept The peroxide (peroxo) ion is two oxygen atoms joined by a single bond and carrying two negative charges: O22−, i.e. [−O–O−]. It must not be confused with the superoxide ion O2− or the oxide ion O2−.
Key relation O22−: 18 electrons, bond order = (Nb bond order = (8 − 8)/2 + (2 − 0)... = 1minus; Na)/2 = (10 bond order = (8 − 8)/2 + (2 − 0)... = 1minus; 8)/2 = 1 Bond order 1 means a single O–O bond, exactly the peroxide link in H–O–O–H.
Solution
1. In H2O2 each O is −1; removing the two H+ leaves O22− with total charge 2 × (−1) = −2.
2. MO count: O22− has 16 + 2 = 18 electrons. Bonding 10, antibonding 8 ⇒ bond order = (10 − 8)/2 = 1. all π* filled, so it is diamagnetic
3. Salts containing this ion are true peroxides: Na2O2, BaO2. With dilute acid they give H2O2: BaO2 + H2SO4 → BaSO4↓ + H2O2.
4. Compare the family: O2 (BO 2), O2− superoxide (BO 1.5, paramagnetic, in KO2), O22− peroxide (BO 1, diamagnetic).
5. The ion also appears as a ligand: in peroxo-acids (H2SO5, H2S2O8), in CrO5 and in the yellow-orange Ti(IV) peroxo complex used to test for H2O2.
6. So writing the peroxo ion as O22− is correct: the statement is True.
⚡ JEE Trick “Per-oxide = O22−, super-oxide = O2−, oxide = O2−.” Only a true peroxide (an O–O bond, e.g. BaO2) gives H2O2 with acid; dioxides like PbO2 and MnO2 contain O2− and do not.
Answer: True.
2 (iii)
True or false: Hydrogen peroxide is neutral like water
C9L4VRecapQ2iiiAcidic nature of H2O2
🟡 SimilarJustify the statemen…
False
Concept H2O2 is a weak acid, noticeably more acidic than water. The electron-withdrawing second oxygen pulls charge from the O–H bond and helps delocalise the charge on HO2−, so it ionises more than water does. Pure H2O2 turns blue litmus faintly red.
Key relation H2O2 ⇌ H+ + HO2−, Ka ≈ 2.4 × 10−12 (pKa ≈ 11.6) Water’s own pKa is about 15.7 (14 on the Kw scale), so H2O2 is roughly 104 times stronger.
Solution
1. First ionisation: H2O2 ⇌ H+ + HO2− (hydroperoxide ion).
2. pKa ≈ 11.6 against about 15.7 for H2O (Kw = 10−14 at 25 °C), so the difference is ≈ 4 log units: H2O2 is ≈ 104 times more acidic. a measurable but weak acid
3. Chemical proof: it forms salts with strong bases, H2O2 + 2NaOH → Na2O2 + 2H2O, and with Ba(OH)2 gives BaO2·8H2O.
4. Pure H2O2 turns blue litmus red (weakly), which water does not.
5. The reverse is also true: Na2O2 and BaO2 are salts of this weak acid, which is why a stronger acid (H2SO4) releases H2O2 from them.
6. Since H2O2 is weakly acidic, not neutral, the statement is False.
⚡ JEE Trick Ask “does it form a salt with NaOH/Ba(OH)2?” H2O2 does (Na2O2, BaO2), so it is a weak dibasic-type acid. The tempting “True” comes from its water-like look (colourless liquid, H-bonded, miscible), but physical similarity is not neutrality.
Answer: False. H2O2 is a weak acid.
2 (iv)
True or false: Pure H2O2 can be prepared from BaO2 but not from MnO2 and PbO2.
C9L4VRecapQ2ivPreparation of H2O2 from peroxides
🟡 SimilarHow to prepare H2O2…
False ⚠
Concept Only a true peroxide, a salt of the O22− ion with an O–O bond, gives H2O2 with acid. BaO2 is one; MnO2 and PbO2 are dioxides (polyoxides), M4+ with two O2− ions and no O–O bond, so acid cannot produce H2O2 from them.
Key relation BaO2·8H2O + H2SO4 → BaSO4↓ + H2O2 + 8H2O Peroxide in, peroxide out: O stays at −1; the insoluble BaSO4 is filtered off.
Solution
1. O.N. test: in BaO2, Ba = +2 so each O = −1 (peroxide). In MnO2 and PbO2, the metal is +4 and each O is −2 (oxide).
2. BaO2 + dilute H2SO4 (ice-cold) → BaSO4↓ + H2O2. the O–O unit is simply protonated
3. MnO2 with acid gives Cl2 (with HCl) or O2 (hot H2SO4): MnO2 + 4HCl → MnCl2 + Cl2 + 2H2O. No H2O2.
4. PbO2 behaves the same way (2PbO2 + 2H2SO4 → 2PbSO4 + 2H2O + O2); it is a strong oxidant and even destroys H2O2.
5. So the chemistry of the statement is True. The only hook for “False” is the word pure: the BaO2 route gives a dilute aqueous solution that must still be concentrated.
6. The printed key lists five entries ((v) True) for four statements, so its (iv) is likely shifted. The cell keeps the key’s False with a flag.
⚡ JEE Trick “Peroxide vs dioxide”: check the O.N. of O. −1 (BaO2, Na2O2) gives H2O2 with acid; −2 (PbO2, MnO2, NO2) does not.
Answer: False per the key — my working gives True ⚠

Choose the correct option: What is false about H2O2?
(a) Act as both oxidising and reducing agent   (b) Two OH bonds lie in the same plane   (c) Pale blue liquid   (d) Can be oxidised by O3
C9L4VRecapQ3iProperties of H2O2
🟢 ExactWhich is false about…
(b) Two OH bonds lie in the same plane
Concept The question asks for the false statement. H2O2 has an open-book (skew) structure, so its two O–H bonds lie in different planes. The trap is option (d): “oxidised by O3” sounds wrong but is true, because against ozone H2O2 acts as a reductant.
Key relation H2O2 + O3 → H2O + 2O2 O in H2O2 goes −1 → 0 (oxidised); ozone is the stronger oxidant.
Solution
1. (a) O is at −1, between −2 and 0, so H2O2 acts as both oxidant and reductant. True.
2. (b) The two H–O–O planes are at a dihedral angle of 111.5° (gas) / 90.2° (solid), so the O–H bonds are not coplanar. False.
3. (c) Pure H2O2 is a very pale blue, syrupy liquid (dilute solutions look colourless). True.
4. (d) H2O2 + O3 → H2O + 2O2. Electron count: both O of H2O2 go −1 → 0 in one O2 (lose 2e−); one O of O3 goes 0 → −2 in H2O (gains 2e−). H2O2 is oxidised. True. atoms balance: H 2 = 2, O 5 = 5
5. Only (b) is false, so the answer is (b).
6. Structural reason for (b): lone-pair repulsion across the O–O bond twists the molecule into the skew form, so no planar conformation is the minimum.
⚡ JEE Trick In “which is false” questions on H2O2, the structure statement is the usual target: open book = non-planar. Option (d) is the decoy; whenever O2 is evolved, H2O2 has been oxidised, and O3, KMnO4, Cl2 and Ag2O all do this.
Answer: (b) Two OH bonds lie in the same plane.
3 (ii)
Choose the correct option: H2O2 acts as an oxidising agent in:
(a) neutral medium   (b) acidic medium only   (c) alkaline medium only   (d) both acidic and alkaline mediums
C9L4VRecapQ3iiOxidising action of H2O2
🟢 Exact, H_2O_2 acts as an…
(d) both acidic and alkaline mediums
Concept H2O2 oxidises in both acidic and alkaline solution; only the balancing species change (H+ consumed in acid, OH− produced/consumed in alkali). The words “only” in (b) and (c) are what make those options wrong.
Key relation Acid: H2O2 + 2H+ + 2e− → 2H2O;  Alkali: H2O2 + 2e− → 2OH− Same change in O (−1 → −2) in both media; E° = +1.78 V (acid) and +0.88 V (alkali, as HO2−).
Solution
1. Acidic medium: 2Fe2+ + H2O2 + 2H+ → 2Fe3+ + 2H2O; and 2I− + H2O2 + 2H+ → I2 + 2H2O.
2. Alkaline medium: 2Cr(OH)3 + 3H2O2 + 4OH− → 2CrO42− + 8H2O (green → yellow); Mn2+ + H2O2 + 2OH− → MnO2 + 2H2O.
3. In every case O goes from −1 to −2, the defining step of oxidising action. the medium only decides H+/OH− bookkeeping
4. Options (b) “acidic only” and (c) “alkaline only” each ignore one of the two sets of examples, so both fail.
5. Option (a) neutral medium is incomplete: H2O2 can oxidise there too (PbS → PbSO4 in a water suspension), but (a) does not capture the general behaviour that (d) states.
6. Hence the best answer is (d) both acidic and alkaline media. It is thermodynamically a stronger oxidant in acid (E° 1.78 V) but often kinetically faster in alkali.
⚡ JEE Trick Any option with “only” about H2O2 is usually a trap: it oxidises and reduces, in acid and in alkali (four combinations, all real).
Answer: (d) both acidic and alkaline mediums.
3 (iii)
Choose the correct option: The hybridization of oxygen atom in H2O2 is:
(a) sp3d   (b) sp   (c) sp2   (d) sp3
C9L4VRecapQ3iiiHybridisation of O in H2O2
🟢 ExactThe hybridization of…
(d) sp³
Concept Hybridisation follows from the steric number: bond pairs (σ bonds) plus lone pairs on the atom. Each O in H–O–O–H has two σ bonds and two lone pairs, a steric number of 4, i.e. sp3, just as in water.
Key relation steric number = σ bonds + lone pairs = 2 + 2 = 4 ⇒ sp3 Four electron domains arrange tetrahedrally; lone-pair compression then shrinks the bond angle below 109.5°.
Solution
1. Valence electrons on each O = 6. Two are used in the O–H and O–O single bonds; the remaining 4 form two lone pairs.
2. σ bonds on each O = 2 (one to H, one to the other O); no π bond is present. all single bonds in H2O2
3. Steric number = 2 + 2 = 4 ⇒ sp3 hybridisation, tetrahedral electron geometry, bent shape about each O.
4. Lone pair–bond pair repulsion closes the H–O–O angle from 109.5° to ≈ 94.8° (gas) / 101.9° (solid).
5. The lone pairs on neighbouring O atoms also repel across the O–O bond, which is what gives the non-planar open-book shape.
6. Rejecting the rest: sp (b) needs SN 2 (linear); sp2 (c) needs SN 3 (a double bond or planar O); sp3d (a) needs SN 5 and d-orbitals, impossible for a period-2 atom. So the answer is (d) sp3.
⚡ JEE Trick Oxygen with only single bonds is sp3: H2O, alcohols, ethers, H2O2. Period-2 elements can never be sp3d, so (a) is out at sight. The small bond angle (< 109.5°) is lone-pair compression, not a different hybridisation.
Answer: (d) sp3.
3 (iv)
Choose the correct option: The H–O–O bond angle in H2O2 is:
(a) 107.28°   (b) 109.28°   (c) 104.5°   (d) 97°
C9L4VRecapQ3ivH–O–O bond angle
🟢 Exact`O-O-H` bond angle i…
(d) 97°
Concept Each O in H2O2 is sp3 with two lone pairs, so the H–O–O angle is squeezed well below 109.5°, and even below water’s 104.5°. Experiment gives 94.8° in the gas and 101.9° in the solid; older texts quote ≈ 97°, which is the only option in that range.
Key relation sp3 + 2 lone pairs ⇒ angle < 109.5°; observed H–O–O = 94.8° (g) to 101.9° (s) Lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair repulsion compresses the bond angle.
Solution
1. Each O: 2 σ bonds + 2 lone pairs ⇒ sp3, ideal angle 109.5° (the book’s 109°28′, printed as “109.28°”).
2. Two lone pairs compress the angle, as in water (104.5°). so (b) 109.28° is the undistorted tetrahedral value, not H2O2
3. In H2O2 the neighbour of H–O is an O atom carrying its own lone pairs; the angle closes further to 94.8° in the gas phase.
4. In the crystal, hydrogen bonding opens it to 101.9°. Many textbooks quote a single figure of about 97°, between these two.
5. Reject the distractors: (a) 107.28° resembles NH3 (107°); (c) 104.5° is H2O; (b) 109.28° is the pure tetrahedral angle.
6. The only option in the 94.8–101.9° window is (d) 97°.
⚡ JEE Trick Each distractor is another molecule’s angle: 109.5° for CH4, 107° for NH3, 104.5° for H2O. H2O2 is smaller than water, so pick the value below 104.5°. Do not mix the bond angle with the dihedral angle (111.5°).
Answer: (d) 97°.
3 (v)
Choose the correct option: Hydrogen peroxide is not:
(a) a reducing agent   (b) a dehydrating agent   (c) an oxidising agent   (d) a bleaching agent
C9L4VRecapQ3vRoles of H2O2
🟢 Exact, Hydrogen peroxide…
(b) a dehydrating agent
Concept H2O2 is an oxidant, a reductant and a bleach, all through its −1 oxygen. It is not a dehydrating agent: it has no affinity to pull H2O out of other compounds and is itself always handled as an aqueous solution that releases water when it decomposes. The trap is confusing it with conc. H2SO4 or P4O10.
Key relation 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2 It produces water rather than removing it; a dehydrating agent does the opposite (C12H22O11 −11H2O with conc. H2SO4).
Solution
1. (a) reducing agent: 2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 + 5H2O2 → K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 8H2O + 5O2. It is one.
2. (c) oxidising agent: PbS + 4H2O2 → PbSO4 + 4H2O; 2Fe2+ → 2Fe3+. It is one.
3. (d) bleaching agent: H2O2 → H2O + [O]; nascent oxygen oxidises dyes (silk, wool, hair, ivory). It is one. bleaching is an application of (c)
4. (b) dehydrating agent: a dehydrator removes the elements of water from another substance (conc. H2SO4 chars sugar; P4O10 converts HNO3 to N2O5).
5. H2O2 shows no such reaction; it is miscible with water and decomposes to water. So it is not a dehydrating agent.
6. Hence the answer is (b).
⚡ JEE Trick Classic dehydrating agents: conc. H2SO4, P4O10, anhydrous CaCl2, Al2O3 (hot). H2O2 is never on that list; its three roles are oxidant, reductant and (oxidative) bleach.
Answer: (b) a dehydrating agent.
3 (vi)
Choose the correct option: The reaction, H2S + H2O2 → S + 2H2O manifests:
(a) acidic nature of H2O2   (b) alkaline nature of H2O2   (c) oxidising nature of H2O2   (d) reducing nature of H2O2
C9L4VRecapQ3viH2O2 oxidises H2S
🟢 ExactThe reaction, H2S#16…
(c) oxidising nature of H₂O₂
Concept To decide which “nature” of H2O2 a reaction shows, track oxidation numbers. If sulphur’s O.N. rises, something else must fall, and the only candidate is the peroxide oxygen. Do not mistake it for an acid–base reaction.
Key relation H2O2 + 2H+ + 2e− → 2H2O (O: −1 → −2) The peroxide oxygen gains electrons whenever H2O2 acts as an oxidant; the partner loses them.
Solution
1. Write the reaction: H2S + H2O2 → S + 2H2O. It is already balanced (4 H, 1 S, 2 O on each side).

Given x g H₂O₂, 100 mL of M/5 KMnO₄, pOH = 1.0
Find x and the products (check options a–d)
1. pOH = 1.0 ⇒ [OH−] = 10−1 M (pH = 13): strongly alkaline medium.
2. Reduction: MnO4− + e− → MnO42− (Mn +7 → +6), n = 1 ⇒ (c) correct.
3. Oxidation: H2O2 + 2OH− → O2 + 2H2O + 2e− (O −1 → 0), n = 2 ⇒ (d) correct.
4. Net: 2MnO4− + H2O2 + 2OH− → 2MnO42− + O2 + 2H2O (mole ratio 2 : 1).
5. eq KMnO4 = 0.100 L × (1/5) M × 1 = 0.02 eq = eq H2O2; mol H2O2 = 0.02/2 = 0.01 mol.
6. x = 0.01 × 34 = 0.34 g ⇒ (b) correct, (a) wrong.
7. (a) 1.7 g is the acidic-medium answer (n = 5: 0.1 eq → 0.05 mol × 34); with MnO2 (n = 3) it would be 1.02 g, not offered.
⚡ JEE Trick Always fix the medium before the n-factor: KMnO4 n = 5 / 3 / 1 for acid / neutral / strong alkali. Option (a) is the planted trap for anyone who uses n = 5 by habit — it is exactly 5 × 0.34.
Answer: (b), (c), (d).
