1 (i)Fill in the blank: The pH of a solution of a salt made from a weak acid and a strong base is ______ than 7.Weak acid strong base salt pHHydrolysis of Salts of Weak Acids and Strong Bases - Chemical Equilibrium - Chemistry Class 11🟡 SimilarGreater
Concept A salt of a weak acid and a strong base (CH3COONa, Na2CO3, KCN) gives an anion that is the conjugate base of a weak acid. That anion pulls H+ off water and leaves OH− behind, so the solution turns basic. The trap is to assume every salt is neutral.
Key relation A− + H2O ⇌ HA + OH−, Kh = Kw/Ka; pH = 7 + (1/2)pKa + (1/2)log C Only the anion hydrolyses; Na+ comes from a strong base and stays a spectator.
Solution
1. The salt dissociates completely: CH3COONa → CH3COO− + Na+.
2. Na+ is the conjugate acid of the strong base NaOH, so it is far too weak to react with water. Strong base ⇒ negligible conjugate acid.
3. CH3COO− is the conjugate base of the weak acid CH3COOH, so it hydrolyses: CH3COO− + H2O ⇌ CH3COOH + OH−.
4. Its constant is Kh = Kw/Ka = 10−14/(1.8×10−5) = 5.6×10−10 (Kw = 10−14 at 25 °C).
5. OH− accumulates, so [OH−] > [H+] and pH > 7.
6. Check with 0.1 M CH3COONa, pKa = 4.74: pH = 7 + (1/2)pKa + (1/2)log C = 7 + 2.37 + (1/2)log 0.1 = 8.87, which is above 7.
7. So the blank reads greater.
 CH3COONa -> CH3COO-  +  Na+  (spectator)
 CH3COO- + H2O <=> CH3COOH + OH-
 Kh = Kw/Ka = 1e-14/1.8e-5 = 5.6e-10

 pH > 7  ->  "greater"              <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick “The strong parent wins”: strong base + weak acid ⇒ basic, strong acid + weak base ⇒ acidic, strong + strong ⇒ neutral, weak + weak ⇒ decided by Ka vs Kb. The weaker the parent acid (larger pKa) or the more concentrated the salt, the higher the pH.
Answer: Greater (pH > 7).
1 (ii)Fill in the blank: The equation that relates the ion product of water (Kw), the acid ionization constant (Ka), and the hydrolysis constant (Kh) is Kw = ______.Kw Ka Kh relationRelationship between Kh ,Kw and Ka for salt of weak acid and strong base🟡 SimilarKa × Kh
Concept The hydrolysis constant Kh of the anion A− is simply the base constant of the conjugate base of HA. Multiplying the acid’s ionisation constant by it cancels every species except H+ and OH−, which leaves the ionic product of water.
Key relation Ka × Kh = ([H+][A−]/[HA]) × ([HA][OH−]/[A−]) = [H+][OH−] = Kw HA and A− are a conjugate pair, so their concentrations cancel exactly.
Solution
1. Acid ionisation: HA ⇌ H+ + A−, Ka = [H+][A−]/[HA].
2. Anion hydrolysis: A− + H2O ⇌ HA + OH−, Kh = [HA][OH−]/[A−]. [H2O] is constant and absorbed.
3. Multiply the two: [HA] and [A−] cancel, leaving [H+][OH−] = Kw.
4. Hence Kw = Ka × Kh, i.e. Kh = Kw/Ka.
5. Numerical check for acetate: Kh = Kw/Ka = 10−14/(1.8×10−5) = 5.6×10−10.
6. In log form: pKa + pKh = pKw = 14 at 25 °C.
7. Related forms: weak base + strong acid salt, Kw = Kb × Kh; weak acid + weak base salt, Kw = Ka × Kb × Kh.
 Ka = [H+][A-]/[HA]
 Kh = [HA][OH-]/[A-]
 Ka x Kh = [H+][OH-] = Kw
 acetate: Kh = 1e-14/1.8e-5 = 5.6e-10
 pKa + pKh = 14  (25 C)
 -----------------------------------------
 Kw = Ka x Kh                       <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick Write Kh as “Kw over whichever parent is weak”: Kw/Ka, Kw/Kb, or Kw/(KaKb). A common slip is to write Kh = Ka/Kw, which is upside down and gives a huge number.
Answer: Kw = Ka × Kh.

Concept A salt of a weak base and a strong acid (NH4Cl, CuSO4, FeCl3) supplies a cation that is the conjugate acid of a weak base. It reacts with water and releases H+, so the solution turns acidic. The anion (Cl−) comes from a strong acid and does nothing.

1 (v)Fill in the blank: The pH scale is a measure of the ______ ion concentration in a solution.pH scale meaningpH Scale, Relation Between pH and pOH - Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 6 | CBSE🟡 SimilarHydrogen
Concept The pH scale is Sørensen’s compact way of expressing how much H+ (really H3O+) a solution holds. Concentrations run from about 1 M down to 10−14 M, so a logarithmic scale turns that huge range into the handy numbers 0–14. (The key lists this as sub-part (iv) because the book skips (iv); it is the same blank.)
Key relation pH = −log[H+], so [H+] = 10−pH M The “H” in pH stands for hydrogen ion; “p” means −log.
Solution
1. The quantity being measured is the hydrogen ion (hydronium ion) concentration, in mol L−1.
2. In pure water at 25 °C, [H+] = √Kw = √(10−14) = 10−7 M, so pH = −log 10−7 = 7 (neutral).
3. [H+] = 10−3 M gives pH = −log 10−3 = 3 (acidic); [H+] = 10−11 M gives pH = 11 (basic).
4. Because the scale is logarithmic, a change of 1 pH unit means a tenfold change in [H+]. pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4.
5. Lower pH means higher hydrogen-ion concentration; the scale runs inversely.
6. The companion scale pOH = −log[OH−] measures hydroxide, and pH + pOH = 14 at 25 °C.
7. So the blank is hydrogen.
 [H+] (M)     pH = -log[H+]
 1e-3         3        acidic
 1e-7         7        neutral (25 C)
 1e-11        11       basic
 1 unit of pH = 10x change in [H+]
 -----------------------------------------
 pH measures [H+]  -> "hydrogen"   <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick Don’t write “hydroxide”: that is pOH. And pH is not confined to 0–14: 2 M HCl has pH = −log 2 = −0.30, and the neutral point is 7 only at 25 °C.
Answer: Hydrogen (H+/H3O+) ion.

2 (i)True or false: A solution of a salt made from a weak acid and a strong base will always be basic.Weak acid strong base saltSalt hydrolysis of weak acid strong base. Chemistry ionic equilibrium class 11th.🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept In a salt of a weak acid and a strong base, only the anion hydrolyses, and every hydrolysis step makes OH−. Nothing in the solution makes extra H+, so [OH−] always exceeds [H+]. The word “always” is the bait, but it holds here.
Key relation A− + H2O ⇌ HA + OH−; [OH−] = √(KhC) = √(KwC/Ka) Valid when h = √(Kh/C) ≪ 1, which is the usual case.
Solution
1. Example: CH3COONa → CH3COO− + Na+; Na+ does not hydrolyse.
2. CH3COO− + H2O ⇌ CH3COOH + OH− adds OH− on top of what water already supplies.
3. 0.1 M salt, Ka = 1.8×10−5: [OH−] = √(KwC/Ka) = √(10−14×0.1/1.8×10−5) = 7.5×10−6 M, pOH = 5.13, pH = 8.87.
4. On dilution the pH falls but only approaches 7; it never crosses it, because hydrolysis can only add OH−.
5. At other temperatures “basic” still means [OH−] > [H+], which still holds even though neutral pH is no longer 7.
6. Real examples: Na2CO3, KCN, CH3COOK, borax solutions are all alkaline to litmus.
7. So the statement is True.
 A- + H2O <=> HA + OH-   (only OH- made)
 0.1 M NaOAc, Ka = 1.8e-5
 [OH-] = sqrt(1e-14 x 0.1/1.8e-5) = 7.5e-6
 pOH = 5.13  ->  pH = 8.87
 dilution: pH -> 7 from above, never below
 -----------------------------------------
 always basic -> TRUE               <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick “Always” makes most statements false, but not this one: the direction of hydrolysis is fixed by which ion is weak. Contrast the weak acid + weak base salt (CH3COONH4), whose pH depends on Ka vs Kb and may be neutral.
Answer: True.
2 (ii)True or false: The pH of a solution can be calculated using the equation pH = −log [H+].pH equationpH scale| Hydrogen ion concentration| pOH| Acidity & Basicity of a solution🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept pH = −log[H+] is the definition of pH, so it applies to every aqueous solution: acid, base or salt. What changes from case to case is only how you find [H+]. The trap comes with very dilute acids, where the water’s own H+ must be counted.
Key relation pH = −log[H+]total; for bases [H+] = Kw/[OH−] [H+] must be the total hydrogen-ion concentration, including water’s share.
Solution
1. Strong acid: 0.01 M HCl ⇒ [H+] = 10−2 M ⇒ pH = −log 10−2 = 2.
2. Strong base: 0.001 M NaOH ⇒ [H+] = Kw/[OH−] = 10−14/10−3 = 10−11 ⇒ pH = 11.
3. Weak acid: 0.1 M CH3COOH, Ka = 1.8×10−5 ⇒ [H+] = √(KaC) = 1.34×10−3 ⇒ pH = 2.87.
4. Very dilute acid: 10−8 M HCl. Using 10−8 alone would give pH 8 (basic!), which is absurd. Water’s 10−7 M H+ is now larger than the acid’s.
5. Solve x(x − 10−8) = 10−14: x = 1.05×10−7 M ⇒ pH = 6.98, still from pH = −log[H+].
6. So the equation itself never fails; only a wrong [H+] does.
7. The statement is True.
 0.01 M HCl   : pH = -log 1e-2         = 2
 1e-3 M NaOH  : [H+] = 1e-14/1e-3      -> 11
 0.1 M HOAc   : [H+] = sqrt(1.8e-6)    -> 2.87
 1e-8 M HCl   : [H+] = 1.05e-7 (water) -> 6.98

⚡ JEE Trick If an acid’s computed pH comes out above 7 (or a base’s below 7), you forgot water’s 10−7 M. Add it in before taking the log.
Answer: True.
2 (iii)True or false: Kw is always equal to the product of Ka and Kh.Kw equals Ka times KhHYDROLYSIS CONSTANT|| RELATION BETWEEN Kh, Kw, Ka AND Kb || IONIC EQUILIBRIUM ||11 CLASS CHEMISTRY🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Here Kh is the hydrolysis constant of A−, the conjugate base of the acid whose constant is Ka. For any conjugate pair the product of the two constants is exactly Kw, at every temperature, because the defining expressions multiply out to [H+][OH−]. “Always” is safe within that meaning.
Key relation Ka × Kh = ([H+][A−]/[HA]) × ([HA][OH−]/[A−]) = Kw An identity, not an approximation: it does not depend on concentration.
Solution
1. HA ⇌ H+ + A− defines Ka; A− + H2O ⇌ HA + OH− defines Kh.
2. Adding the two equations gives H2O ⇌ H+ + OH−, and adding equations multiplies their constants: KaKh = Kw.
3. Acetate check: Kh = 5.6×10−10, so KaKh = 1.8×10−5 × 5.6×10−10 = 1.0×10−14 = Kw at 25 °C.
4. When temperature changes, Ka, Kh and Kw all shift, but the sum-of-reactions argument still holds, so the product still equals the new Kw.
5. Caveat: for a weak acid + weak base salt (CH3COONH4) both ions hydrolyse and Kh = Kw/(KaKb), so there Kw = KaKbKh. The Quick Recap means the conjugate-pair relation of Q1(ii).
6. Likewise for a weak base + strong acid salt the partner constant is Kb: KbKh = Kw.
7. Read as intended, the statement is True.
 HA       <=> H+ + A-          Ka
 A- + H2O <=> HA + OH-         Kh
 sum: H2O <=> H+ + OH-         Ka x Kh = Kw
 1.8e-5 x 5.6e-10 = 1.0e-14
 WA-WB salt: Kw = Ka x Kb x Kh  (caveat)
 -----------------------------------------
 TRUE (conjugate pair)              <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick Adding reactions multiplies K’s; that one line proves KaKb = Kw for any conjugate pair. In an MCQ, check which salt type is meant: for a weak-weak salt the “KaKh” form is the wrong option.
Answer: True.

2 (v)True or false: The ion product of water, Kw, changes with temperature.Kw temperature dependenceEffect of temperatures on Ionic product of water(Kw) or Dissociation Constant of water.🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Kw is an equilibrium constant, and every equilibrium constant depends on temperature. The self-ionisation of water absorbs heat (about +57 kJ mol−1), so by Le Chatelier and van’t Hoff Kw grows as the water is heated. Neutral water stays neutral, but its pH drops below 7.
Key relation H2O ⇌ H+ + OH−, ΔH > 0; log(K2/K1) = (ΔH/2.303R)(1/T1 − 1/T2) Endothermic ⇒ K rises with T.
Solution
1. Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1.0×10−14 at 25 °C (298 K).
2. Breaking the O–H bond to make ions needs energy, so the forward reaction is endothermic.
3. Raising T shifts the equilibrium forward, increasing both [H+] and [OH−].
4. Measured values: about 1.1×10−15 at 0 °C and about 5.5×10−13 at 100 °C.
5. At 100 °C neutral pH = (1/2)pKw = (1/2)(12.26) = 6.13, even though [H+] = [OH−] still.
6. So Kw is constant only at a fixed temperature; it is not a universal constant.
7. The statement is True.

 Kw changes with T -> TRUE          <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick “Neutral” means [H+] = [OH−], not pH = 7. Hot pure water at pH 6.13 is still neutral, a favourite assertion-reason trap. Concentration or added ions never change Kw; only temperature does.
Answer: True.

2If Ka for a weak acid is 10−5, pKb value of its conjugate base is: (Level-2)

Concept A weak acid HA and its conjugate base A− are linked through water: Ka × Kb = Kw. Taking −log turns this into pKa + pKb = 14 at 25 °C. The weaker the acid, the stronger its conjugate base, and vice versa.
Key relation pKb = pKw − pKa = 14 − (−log 10−5) = 14 − 5 = 9 Kb of A− is the same as the hydrolysis constant Kh of its salt.
Solution
1. Given Ka = 10−5, so pKa = −log 10−5 = 5.
2. HA ⇌ H+ + A− (Ka) and A− + H2O ⇌ HA + OH− (Kb) add up to H2O ⇌ H+ + OH−.
3. Adding reactions multiplies constants: KaKb = Kw = 10−14 at 25 °C.
4. Kb = Kw/Ka = 10−14/10−5 = 10−9.
