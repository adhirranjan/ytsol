1 (i)Fill in the blanks: Angle between the lines x + y = 2 and x − y = 1 is ______.Angle between two linesAngle between two lines | Straight lines | Grade 11 | Math | Khan Academy🟡 Similar90°
Concept Two lines cross at an angle governed only by their slopes. The quickest test is the product of the slopes: if it equals −1 the lines are perpendicular, so the angle between them is exactly 90° and no tangent formula is needed. Read each slope straight off the equation.
Formula line perpendicular ⇔ m1m2 = −1. When m1m2 = −1 the denominator 1 + m1m2 of the tan formula is zero, so tanθ is infinite and θ = 90°.
Solution
1. Write x + y = 2 as y = −x + 2, so its slope is m1 = −1.
2. Write x − y = 1 as y = x − 1, so its slope is m2 = 1. Solve each for y to expose the coefficient of x.
3. Form the product m1m2 = (−1)(1) = −1.
4. Since the product is −1, the two lines are perpendicular.
5. Hence the angle between them is θ = 90°. Perpendicularity is the whole content of the product being −1.
6. A check with tanθ = |(m1 − m2)/(1 + m1m2)| gives |−2/0|, which is undefined — the signature of a right angle.
  x+y=2  -> y=-x+2 -> m1 = -1
  x-y=1  -> y= x-1 -> m2 = +1
  m1*m2 = (-1)(1) = -1  -> perpendicular
  angle between the lines = 90 deg
⚡ JEE Trick Slopes 1 and −1 always meet at 90° — the +45° and −45° diagonals. Spot a slope product of −1 and quote a right angle instantly, skipping the tan computation.
Answer: 90°.

Concept The angle between two lines is fixed by the difference of their inclinations. Expanding tan of that difference in terms of the slopes gives the standard working formula, and the modulus is taken so the acute angle is reported regardless of which line is named first.
Formula tanθ = |(m1 − m2)/(1 + m1m2)|. If the inclinations are θ1, θ2 with m = tanθ, then θ = θ1 − θ2 and tan of a difference yields this ratio.
Solution
1. Let the inclinations be θ1, θ2 so that m1 = tanθ1, m2 = tanθ2.
2. The angle between the lines is θ = θ1 − θ2.
3. tanθ = tan(θ1 − θ2) = (tanθ1 − tanθ2)/(1 + tanθ1tanθ2). Direct use of the tangent subtraction identity.
4. Replace the tangents by the slopes: tanθ = (m1 − m2)/(1 + m1m2).
5. Up to sign this is the same as (m2 − m1)/(1 + m1m2), the blank asked for.
6. Taking the modulus gives the acute angle; a zero denominator signals perpendicular lines. 1 + m1m2 = 0 pushes θ to 90°.
  m1 = tan t1 ,  m2 = tan t2
  theta = t1 - t2
  tan theta = (tan t1 - tan t2)/(1 + tan t1 tan t2)

⚡ JEE Trick Memorise the shape difference-of-slopes over one-plus-product. If the product term equals −1 the lines are perpendicular; if the slopes are equal the numerator vanishes and the lines are parallel.

1 (iii)Fill in the blanks: Equation of a line parallel to x + 2y = 3 passing through (2, 1) is ______.Line parallel to a given lineEquation of a Line Parallel to a Given Line Through a Point🟡 Similarx + 2y = 4
Concept A line parallel to a given line shares its slope, so it has the same x- and y-coefficients; only the constant term is free to change. Writing the whole parallel family as x + 2y = λ and then fixing the single unknown λ from the point the line must pass through is far faster than computing a slope, forming y − y1 = m(x − x1) and simplifying back. The method works for any line and extends to perpendiculars by swapping the coefficients.
Formula parallel to ax + by = c ⇒ ax + by = λ. Parallel lines differ only by the constant term because equal coefficients of x and y force equal slope −a/b.
Solution
1. The given line is x + 2y = 3, so any parallel line has the form x + 2y = λ.
2. The required line must pass through (2, 1).
3. Substitute x = 2, y = 1: λ = 2 + 2(1) = 4. The point fixes the single unknown constant λ.
4. Hence the equation is x + 2y = 4.
5. Check the slope: both lines give y = −x/2 + const, slope −1/2, confirming they are parallel.
6. Check the point: 2 + 2(1) = 4 holds, so (2, 1) indeed lies on it. Both conditions — parallel and through the point — are satisfied.
  given line:  x + 2y = 3
  parallel family:  x + 2y = L
  through (2,1):  L = 2 + 2(1) = 4
  required line:  x + 2y = 4
⚡ JEE Trick For a parallel line, copy the left side untouched and solve for the new constant by plugging in the point — never recompute the slope. The same trick writes a perpendicular line by swapping coefficients and flipping one sign.
Answer: x + 2y = 4.

Concept Reflecting a point in the x-axis flips it to the opposite side of that axis while its horizontal position is unchanged. The mirror is the line y = 0, the perpendicular from the point to it is vertical, and the image sits an equal distance below (or above), which is precisely what flipping the sign of y achieves.
Formula image of (x, y) in the x-axis = (x, −y). The x-axis is the mirror y = 0; the foot of the perpendicular keeps x the same and the image sits an equal distance on the other side, so y → −y.

Concept One straight line quietly carries three readable numbers: its perpendicular distance from the origin, its slope and its y-intercept. The distance comes from the point-to-line formula applied at the origin, while the slope and intercept are read straight off the slope-intercept rearrangement y = mx + c. All three are obtained from the single equation without any diagram, and the arithmetic is especially tidy when the coefficients form a 3-4-5 pattern.
Formula p = |c|/√(a2+b2), q = −a/b, r = c/b for ax + by = c. Writing ax + by = c as y = (−a/b)x + c/b exposes both slope and intercept, and the distance of the origin is |0 + 0 − c|/√(a2+b2) = |c|/√(a2+b2).
Solution
1. The line is 3x + 4y = 60, so a = 3, b = 4, c = 60.
2. Perpendicular distance from the origin P = |60|/√(32+42) = 60/√25 = 60/5 = 12. √(9+16) = 5 makes the arithmetic clean.
3. Rearrange for slope: 4y = −3x + 60, so y = (−3/4)x + 15.
4. Slope q = −3/4.
5. y-intercept r: set x = 0 to get 4y = 60, y = 15, so r = 15. The constant term of the slope-intercept form is the y-intercept.
6. Hence p = 12, q = −3/4, r = 15.
  3x + 4y = 60  -> a=3, b=4, c=60
  P = 60/sqrt(9+16) = 60/5 = 12
  y = (-3/4)x + 15  -> q = -3/4
  x=0 -> y = 15  -> r = 15
⚡ JEE Trick On a 3-4-5 line the denominator √(a2+b2) is just 5, so the origin distance is the constant over 5. Spotting the Pythagorean coefficients turns the distance into mental arithmetic.
Answer: p = 12, q = −3/4, r = 15.

9All points lying inside the triangle formed by the points (1, 3), (5, 0) and (−1, 2) satisfy: (a) 3x + 2y ≥ 0 (b) 2x + y − 13 ≥ 0 (c) 2x − 3y − 12 ≤ 0 (d) −2x + y ≥ 0

Inequalities satisfied inside a triangle (multiple correct)Point Inside Triangle Trick | Straight Lines & Graphs | JEE Maths🟢 Exact(a), (c): 3x + 2y ≥ 0 and 2x − 3y − 12 ≤ 0
Concept An inequality holds throughout a triangle precisely when it holds at all three vertices, because a linear expression attains its extreme values at the corners. So each option is tested at the three vertices (1, 3), (5, 0) and (−1, 2).
Formula linear L on a triangle: extremes occur at the vertices. Since ax + by + c is linear, its maximum and minimum over the triangle are reached at vertices, so an inequality true at all three vertices is true everywhere inside.
Solution
1. (a) 3x + 2y at the vertices: 3 + 6 = 9, 15 + 0 = 15, −3 + 4 = 1; all ≥ 0, so (a) holds throughout. The minimum value 1 is still non-negative.
2. (b) 2x + y − 13 at (1, 3): 2 + 3 − 13 = −8 < 0, so (b) fails.
3. (c) 2x − 3y − 12 at the vertices: −19, −2, −20; all ≤ 0, so (c) holds throughout. The maximum value −2 is still non-positive.
4. (d) −2x + y at (5, 0): −10 < 0, so (d) fails.
5. Only (a) and (c) are satisfied at every vertex, hence inside the whole triangle.
6. Therefore the correct options are (a) and (c).
  vertices (1,3),(5,0),(-1,2)
  3x+2y: 9,15,1  -> all >=0   (a) OK
  2x+y-13: -8,...            (b) fails
  2x-3y-12: -19,-2,-20 <=0   (c) OK
  -2x+y: 1,-10,...           (d) fails -> (a),(c)
⚡ JEE Trick A linear inequality over a triangle is decided entirely at its three vertices; if all satisfy it, so does the interior. Test each option at the corners and keep those that pass all three.
Answer: (a), (c): 3x + 2y ≥ 0 and 2x − 3y − 12 ≤ 0.
10In a triangle ABC, AB = AC and the coordinates of B and C are (1, 3) and (−2, 7) respectively. The coordinates of A can be: (a) (−1/2, 5) (b) (−7, 1/8) (c) (1, 6) (d) (5/6, 6)Apex on the perpendicular bisector (multiple correct)Find Equation of Line Perpendicular to a Line Through a Given Point🟡 Similar(a), (b), (d)
Concept Since AB = AC, vertex A is equidistant from B and C, so it lies on the perpendicular bisector of BC. Finding that bisector and testing each option tells which candidate apex points are valid.
Formula A equidistant from B, C ⇒ A on the perpendicular bisector of BC. The locus of points equidistant from two fixed points is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them.
Solution
1. Midpoint of B(1, 3) and C(−2, 7) is (−1/2, 5); slope of BC = (7 − 3)/(−2 − 1) = −4/3. A is equidistant from B and C.
2. Perpendicular bisector has slope 3/4: y − 5 = (3/4)(x + 1/2).
3. (a) (−1/2, 5): this is the midpoint, which lies on the bisector — valid.
4. (b) (−7, 1/8): check y − 5 = 1/8 − 5 = −39/8 and (3/4)(−7 + 1/2) = (3/4)(−13/2) = −39/8 — valid.
5. (c) (1, 6): y − 5 = 1 but (3/4)(1 + 1/2) = 9/8 ≠ 1 — not on the bisector, rejected. The two sides do not match, so (c) fails.
6. (d) (5/6, 6): y − 5 = 1 and (3/4)(5/6 + 1/2) = (3/4)(4/3) = 1 — valid. So (a), (b), (d).
  B(1,3) C(-2,7): mid (-1/2,5), slope BC = -4/3
  perp bisector slope 3/4: y-5 = (3/4)(x+1/2)
  (a)(-1/2,5) OK ; (b)(-7,1/8): -39/8 = -39/8 OK
  (c)(1,6): 1 vs 9/8 NO ; (d)(5/6,6): 1 = 1 OK -> a,b,d
⚡ JEE Trick AB = AC pins the apex onto the perpendicular bisector of the base; just write that bisector and substitute each candidate point. Any option satisfying it is a valid apex, giving (a), (b), (d).
Answer: (a), (b), (d).
