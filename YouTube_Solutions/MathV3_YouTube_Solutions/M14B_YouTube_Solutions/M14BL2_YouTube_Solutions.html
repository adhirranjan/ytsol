Concept An equation is homogeneous when every term carries the same total degree. For a second-degree homogeneous equation in x and y each term must be of degree 2, so only the monomials x2, xy and y2 may appear — no first-degree (x, y) terms and no constant. Such an equation always passes through the origin and stands for a pair of straight lines through O.
Formula ax2+2hxy+by2=0. The three degree-2 monomials x2, xy, y2 are collected with coefficients a, 2h and b; the middle coefficient is written 2h so the symmetric determinant stays clean.
Solution
1. “Homogeneous of degree 2” forces every term to total degree 2 in x and y.
2. The only such monomials are x2, xy and y2; any x, y or constant term would break homogeneity.
3. Attaching coefficients gives ax2+2hxy+by2=0, the standard homogeneous pair.
4. Divide by x2 (x≠0) and set m=y/x: b m2+2h m+a=0, a quadratic in the slope m. Two roots m1, m2 mean two slopes, hence two lines through the origin.
5. So the equation always represents a pair of straight lines passing through O(0, 0).
6. Whether they are real-distinct, coincident or imaginary is decided by the sign of h2−ab. h2>ab real & distinct, h2=ab coincident, h2<ab imaginary.
  ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0        (all terms degree 2)
  put y = m x :  b m^2 + 2h m + a = 0
  roots m1, m2 -> two lines through the origin
⚡ JEE Trick Recognise a homogeneous second-degree equation at a glance: if x, y and the constant are all missing, it is instantly a pair of lines through the origin — no Δ test needed.
Answer: ax2+2hxy+by2=0.

Concept The general equation of the second degree keeps every possible term up to degree 2: the three quadratic terms, the two linear terms and the constant. It describes any conic — a circle, parabola, ellipse or hyperbola — and, under one special degenerate condition, a pair of straight lines.
Formula ax2+2hxy+by2+2gx+2fy+c=0. Six coefficients a, h, b, g, f, c; the 2's on h, g, f make the associated symmetric matrix [[a,h,g],[h,b,f],[g,f,c]] come out neatly.
Solution
1. List all monomials of degree ≤ 2 in x and y: x2, xy, y2 (degree 2); x, y (degree 1); 1 (degree 0).
2. Give them coefficients a, 2h, b, 2g, 2f, c respectively.
3. The sum set to zero is ax2+2hxy+by2+2gx+2fy+c=0.
4. The purely quadratic part ax2+2hxy+by2 fixes the directions (slopes); the linear part shifts the curve off the origin. Removing 2gx+2fy+c leaves the homogeneous pair through O.
5. This form represents a pair of lines precisely when abc+2fgh−af2−bg2−ch2=0.
6. Otherwise it is a circle, parabola, ellipse or hyperbola. The pair of lines is the degenerate conic.
  ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0
  quadratic part -> directions
  linear part    -> position (off origin)
⚡ JEE Trick Always read off (a, h, b, g, f, c) in this fixed order with the 2's attached — every pair-of-lines test (Δ, angle, intersection) plugs straight into these six letters.
Answer: ax2+2hxy+by2+2gx+2fy+c=0.

2. Comparing coefficients ties a, h, b, g, f, c to the line constants l, m, n.
3. Eliminating those six unknowns leaves one relation among a..c — the determinant of the symmetric matrix set to zero.
4. Expanding that 3×3 determinant along the first row gives a(bc−f2)−h(hc−fg)+g(hf−bg). Collecting terms yields abc+2fgh−af2−bg2−ch2.
5. Setting it to zero is the necessary and sufficient condition for a pair of lines.

2. Other lines perpendicular: m′m″=−1 ⇒ (2/(3m))(−2/(3m))=−4/(9m2)=−1.
3. So 9m2=4 ⇒ m2=4/9 ⇒ m=±2/3.
4. Slope sums: pair 1 m+m′=−p/3; pair 2 m+m″=q/3. So p=−3(m+m′) and q=3(m+m″). For 2x2+pxy+3y2, sum of slopes=−p/3; for −3y2, sum=q/3.
5. Use the common-line equations instead: from 2+pm+3m2=0, p=−10/(3m); from 2+qm−3m2=0, q=−2/(3m). So p+q=−12/(3m)=−4/m.
6. m=2/3 ⇒ p+q=−6; m=−2/3 ⇒ p+q=6. So options (a) 6 and (b) −6. Both signs of m are legitimate, giving a multiple-correct answer.
  m^2 = 4/9 -> m = +/- 2/3
  p = -10/(3m), q = -2/(3m)
  p + q = -4/m = -/+ 6
⚡ JEE Trick For “one common line, other two perpendicular”, the perpendicularity fixes m2, and both ±m feed back through the common-line equations — expect a ± pair of answers.
Answer: (a) 6 and (b) −6.
8The lines joining the origin to the point of intersection of 3x2+mxy−4x+1=0 and 2x+y−1=0 are at right angles. Then which of the following is a possible value of m? Concept Homogenise the second-degree curve using the given line to get the pair of lines joining the origin to the intersection points. The perpendicularity condition a+b=0 then turns out to hold for every m.
Formula make the curve homogeneous with 2x+y=1; perpendicular ⇒ (coeff x2)+(coeff y2)=0. Replace the constant and linear terms using the line =1 to force degree 2.
Solution
1. Line: 2x+y−1=0 ⇒ 2x+y=1. Curve: 3x2+mxy−4x+1=0.
2. Homogenise: write −4x as −4x(2x+y) and 1 as (2x+y)2.
3. 3x2+mxy−4x(2x+y)+(2x+y)2=0.
4. Expand: 3x2+mxy−8x2−4xy+4x2+4xy+y2=−x2+mxy+y2=0. −8x2+4x2+3x2=−x2; −4xy+4xy=0.
5. For the lines through O to be perpendicular: (coeff x2)+(coeff y2)=−1+1=0.
6. This is 0 for every m, so the lines are perpendicular regardless of m — options (a), (b), (c), (d) all qualify. The m-term is the xy coefficient, which does not affect a+b.
  3x^2 + mxy - 4x(2x+y) + (2x+y)^2 = 0
  = -x^2 + mxy + y^2 = 0
  a + b = -1 + 1 = 0 for all m
⚡ JEE Trick After homogenising, perpendicularity is just (x2 coeff)+(y2 coeff)=0 — if the m sits only in the xy term, it cannot break perpendicularity, so every value works.
Answer: (a), (b), (c), (d) — the lines are perpendicular for every value of m.
9If the equation ax2−6xy+y2+bx+cy+d=0 represents a pair of lines whose slopes are m and m2, then the value(s) of a is/are: (a) a=−8 (b) a=8 (c) a=27 (d) a=−27Slopes m and m² of a pair (multiple correct)If the equation ax^2-6xy+y^2+bx+cy+d=0 represents a pair of lines whose slopes are m and m^2🟢 Exact(b) a=8  and  (d) a=−27
Concept The slopes of the pair are m and m2. Use the sum and product of slopes from the second-degree part to find m, then the product m·m2=m3 gives a.
Formula for ax2−6xy+y2: m1+m2=−2h/b=6, m1m2=a/b=a. b=1, 2h=−6 ⇒ h=−3, so sum of slopes = 6 and product = a.
Solution
1. Slopes m and m2 satisfy m+m2=6 (sum) and m·m2=a (product).
2. From the sum: m2+m−6=0 ⇒ (m+3)(m−2)=0 ⇒ m=2 or m=−3.
3. Product a=m3.
4. m=2 ⇒ a=23=8.
5. m=−3 ⇒ a=(−3)3=−27. Both roots give valid slope pairs (m, m2).
6. So a=8 or a=−27 — options (b) and (d). The linear terms bx+cy+d only shift the pair and do not affect the slopes.
7. Both are genuine: for a=8 the slopes are 2 and 4; for a=−27 they are −3 and 9, each a valid (m, m2) pair. Checking m2 against the second root confirms consistency in both cases.
  m + m^2 = 6 -> m = 2 or -3
  a = m^3
  m=2 -> a=8 ;  m=-3 -> a=-27
⚡ JEE Trick “Slopes m and m2” means sum=−2h/b and product=a/b both in terms of one m — solve the sum for m, then compute a=m3 for each root; expect two valid answers here, namely a=8 and a=−27 both.
Answer: (b) a=8 and (d) a=−27.
10Two pairs of straight lines have the equations y2+xy−12x2=0 and ax2+2hxy+by2=0. One line will be common among them if: (a) a+8h−16b=0 (b) a−8h+16b=0 (c) a−6h+9b=0 (d) a+6h+9b=0Common line between two homogeneous pairs (multiple correct)If the pairs of lines ax^2+2hxy+by^2=0 and a'x^2+2h'xy+b'y^2=0 have one line in common, then🟡 Similar(b) a−8h+16b=0  and  (d) a+6h+9b=0
Concept Factor the first pair to get its two lines, then require each to also satisfy the second pair ax2+2hxy+by2=0 by substituting its slope. Each line gives one common-line condition.
Formula if y=mx lies on ax2+2hxy+by2=0 then a+2hm+bm2=0. Substitute the slope of each line of the first pair into the second.
Solution
1. y2+xy−12x2=0: treat as quadratic in y, y=(−x±√(x2+48x2))/2=(−x±7x)/2.
2. So y=3x or y=−4x, i.e. slopes m=3 and m=−4.
3. For a common line, one of these must satisfy a+2hm+bm2=0.
4. m=3: a+2h(3)+b(9)=a+6h+9b=0 — option (d).
5. m=−4: a+2h(−4)+b(16)=a−8h+16b=0 — option (b). Each line of the first pair gives its own condition.
6. So one line is common if a+6h+9b=0 or a−8h+16b=0 — options (b) and (d). Either shared line suffices, hence two valid conditions.
7. Options (a) a+8h−16b=0 and (c) a−6h+9b=0 have the wrong sign pairings and match neither slope. The sign of the 2hm term must follow the slope: +6h for m=3, −8h for m=−4.
  y^2 + xy - 12x^2 = 0 -> y = 3x or y = -4x
  a + 2hm + bm^2 = 0
  m=3  -> a + 6h + 9b = 0
  m=-4 -> a - 8h + 16b = 0
⚡ JEE Trick Factor the numeric pair first, then plug each slope into a+2hm+bm2=0 — one substitution per line yields each common-line condition directly.
Answer: (b) a−8h+16b=0 and (d) a+6h+9b=0.
