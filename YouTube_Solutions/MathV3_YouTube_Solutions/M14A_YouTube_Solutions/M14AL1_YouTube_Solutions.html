1 (i)Fill in the blanks: Equation of a line having slope 3 and y-intercept 2 is ______.Slope-intercept formGraph and write the equation a line with slope = 3 and y-intercept = -2.🟡 Similary = 3x + 2
Concept When the slope m and the y-intercept c of a line are both known, the line is fixed uniquely and its equation is written straight down in slope-intercept form. No two points and no extra working are needed — m controls the steepness and sign of the tilt while c fixes the single point (0, c) where the graph crosses the y-axis. Every non-vertical line can be put in this shape, which is why it is the first tool to reach for when a slope and a y-intercept are the data given.
Formula y = mx + c. c is the y-coordinate of the point where the line meets the y-axis, i.e. the value of y when x = 0, and m = tanθ is the tangent of the line's inclination.
Solution
1. Identify the data: the slope is given as m = 3.
2. The y-intercept is given as c = 2, so the line passes through (0, 2). Intercept 2 means it cuts the y-axis two units above the origin.
3. Substitute both values directly into y = mx + c.
4. y = (3)x + (2), that is y = 3x + 2.
5. Check the intercept: at x = 0, y = 2, which is the given crossing point. The constant term reproduces the required y-intercept.
6. Check the slope: the coefficient of x is 3, matching the given steepness, and a second point (1, 5) confirms a rise of 3 per unit run.
7. Hence the required equation is y = 3x + 2, valid for every x.
  given:  slope m = 3,  y-intercept c = 2
  form:   y = m x + c
  sub:    y = 3 x + 2
  check:  x=0 -> y=2 (intercept),  coeff x = 3 (slope)
  check:  x=1 -> y=5, rise 3 over run 1  OK
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a problem hands you a slope and a y-intercept, skip every other method — write y = mx + c on sight. The number multiplying x is always the slope and the lone constant is always the y-intercept, so you can also read a line's slope and intercept straight back off any equation already in this form, which saves time in match-column items.
Answer: y = 3x + 2.

Concept Two distinct points determine a unique line. The plan is a fixed three-step routine: compute the slope from the two points, substitute it with one point into the slope-point form, and then tidy the result into the compact general form printed in the key. This same routine solves every "line through two given points" question.
Formula m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1), then y − y1 = m(x − x1). The slope is rise over run between the two given points; the point form then pins the line to one of them so no unknown constant survives.
Solution
1. Take (x1, y1) = (2, 1) and (x2, y2) = (3, 2).
2. Slope m = (2 − 1)/(3 − 2) = 1/1 = 1. Equal rise and run give a 45° line of slope 1.
3. Use y − y1 = m(x − x1) with the point (2, 1): y − 1 = 1·(x − 2).
4. Expand: y − 1 = x − 2, so y = x − 1.
5. Rearrange to the key's form by taking variables to one side: x − y = 1. The general form Ax + By = C is the compact way the answer key prints lines.
6. Verify both data points: (3, 2) gives 3 − 2 = 1 ✓ and (2, 1) gives 2 − 1 = 1 ✓.
7. So the single line through the two points is x − y = 1.
  m = (2-1)/(3-2) = 1
  y - 1 = 1*(x - 2)
  y = x - 1  ->  x - y = 1
  check (3,2): 3-2=1 OK
  check (2,1): 2-1=1 OK
⚡ JEE Trick With two lattice points one unit apart diagonally, the slope is obviously 1 — jump straight to y = x + (constant) and fit the constant from one point. Here y = x − 1 drops out in a single line, and the same shortcut (y = x + k) handles any slope-1 line the moment you spot equal rise and run.
Answer: x − y = 1.
1 (iii)Fill in the blanks: Equation of line passing through (4, 0) having slope −1 is ______.Slope-point formHow to Find the Equation of a Line Given its Slope and a Point it passes through🟡 Similarx + y = 4
Concept A slope together with one point on the line fixes the line completely. The slope-point form builds the equation immediately from exactly these two pieces of data. A negative slope means the line falls as x increases, so a slope of −1 produces the familiar downward 45° line whose equation always collapses to x + y = constant.
Formula y − y1 = m(x − x1). This form needs only one point and the slope, which are exactly what the question supplies, so no second point has to be found first.
Solution
1. Given point (x1, y1) = (4, 0) and slope m = −1.
2. Substitute: y − 0 = −1·(x − 4).
3. Simplify the right side: y = −(x − 4) = −x + 4. Distributing the −1 flips both signs inside the bracket.
4. Move the x-term across to the left: x + y = 4.
5. Check the given point (4, 0): 4 + 0 = 4 ✓. The line really passes through (4, 0), so the x-intercept is 4.
6. Slope check: writing y = −x + 4, the coefficient of x is −1, matching the data, and the y-intercept is 4.
7. Therefore the required line is x + y = 4.
  y - 0 = -1 * (x - 4)
  y = -x + 4
  x + y = 4
  check (4,0): 4+0=4 OK
  slope read back = -1 OK,  y-int = 4
⚡ JEE Trick A slope of −1 always gives a line of the shape x + y = constant; just add the coordinates of the given point to get the constant. Here 4 + 0 = 4, so x + y = 4 with no algebra at all. The mirror rule is that slope +1 gives x − y = constant.
Answer: x + y = 4.
1 (iv)Fill in the blanks: Equation of line having 2, 3 as x & y intercept respectively is ______.Double-intercept formEquation of a Line given the x- and y-intercepts | Two-Intercept Form | Grade 8🟡 Similar3x + 2y = 6
Concept When the x-intercept a and the y-intercept b are both given, the double-intercept form writes the line at once because it is built precisely from those two crossing points (a, 0) and (0, b). Clearing the denominators then turns it into the standard integer form Ax + By = C that the answer key uses.
Formula x/a + y/b = 1. a is where the line meets the x-axis and b where it meets the y-axis; dividing each coordinate by its own intercept makes the two fractions sum to 1 exactly on the line.
Solution
1. Here the x-intercept is a = 2 and the y-intercept is b = 3.
2. Substitute into x/a + y/b = 1: x/2 + y/3 = 1.
3. The least common denominator of 2 and 3 is 6; multiply every term by 6. Clearing fractions avoids a messy final answer and gives integer coefficients.
4. 6·(x/2) + 6·(y/3) = 6·1 gives 3x + 2y = 6.
5. Check x-intercept: put y = 0 ⇒ 3x = 6 ⇒ x = 2 ✓.
6. Check y-intercept: put x = 0 ⇒ 2y = 6 ⇒ y = 3 ✓. Both intercepts come back exactly, confirming the line is the right one.
7. Hence the line through (2, 0) and (0, 3) is 3x + 2y = 6.
  x/2 + y/3 = 1   (x-int 2, y-int 3)
  multiply by 6:  3x + 2y = 6
  y=0 -> 3x=6 -> x=2 OK
  x=0 -> 2y=6 -> y=3 OK
⚡ JEE Trick For a line with intercepts a and b, the integer form is (b)x + (a)y = ab. Here b = 3, a = 2, ab = 6, giving 3x + 2y = 6 directly — note the coefficients swap with the intercepts. This one-line template beats clearing fractions every time.
Answer: 3x + 2y = 6.

5. Collect the triple: p = 2, q = 5, r = −10.

1Find the slope of a line which bisects the first quadrant. (Level-1)Slope of the first-quadrant bisectorThe slope of the line which bisects the angles in the first and third quadrants is🟢 Exact1
Concept The bisector of the first quadrant is the ray on which the x- and y-coordinates are always equal, i.e. the line y = x. Its slope is the tangent of the angle it makes with the positive x-axis, and that angle is exactly half of the 90° first quadrant.
Formula m = tanθ, with θ the inclination; for y = x, θ = 45°. The bisector splits the 90° between the axes into two 45° halves, so its inclination is 45°.
Solution
1. The first quadrant is the 90° region between the positive x-axis and positive y-axis.
2. Its bisector makes equal angles with both axes, so its inclination is 90°/2 = 45°. Bisecting means cutting the angle into two equal 45° parts.
3. Slope m = tan 45° = 1.
4. Equivalently, every point on the bisector has y = x, so the line is y = x with slope 1.
5. A check: the point (1, 1) lies on it and the rise 1 over run 1 gives slope 1. Equal coordinates force equal rise and run.
6. Hence the required slope is 1.
  first quadrant angle = 90 deg
  bisector inclination = 90/2 = 45 deg
  m = tan 45 = 1
  line y = x,  point (1,1): rise/run = 1/1 = 1
⚡ JEE Trick Memorise the two quadrant bisectors: the first/third quadrant bisector is y = x with slope +1, and the second/fourth quadrant bisector is y = −x with slope −1. Then any "bisector of a quadrant" question is answered without drawing, and the same idea extends to angle bisectors between any two given lines once their inclinations are known.
Answer: slope of the first-quadrant bisector = 1.

Concept The slope of the line through two given points is their rise divided by their run. Here the two points are supplied directly, so a single application of the two-point slope formula gives the answer; the order of the points does not matter as long as it is consistent in numerator and denominator.
Formula m = (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1). This is the change in y divided by the change in x as you move from one point to the other along the line.
Solution

4. Slope m = rise/run = 2/4 = 1/2.
5. Re-order check: using the points the other way, (2 − 4)/(−3 − 1) = −2/−4 = 1/2 ✓. Swapping both points flips both signs, leaving the ratio unchanged.

  points (-3, 2) and (1, 4)

The same discipline rescues the distance and section formulas, where negative coordinates bite just as often.
Answer: m = 1/2.

2. With Q(1, 5), R(7, 2), area = (1/2)|3xP + 6yP − 33| (after expanding the determinant). The coefficients 3, 6, −33 come from (yQ−yR), etc.
3. Substitute: 3xP + 6yP = (39λ − 9)/(λ + 1), so 3xP + 6yP − 33 = (6λ − 42)/(λ + 1).
4. Area = 2 gives |(6λ − 42)/(λ + 1)| = 4.
5. Case +: 6λ − 42 = 4(λ + 1) ⇒ 2λ = 46 ⇒ λ = 23. Case −: 6λ − 42 = −4(λ + 1) ⇒ 10λ = 38 ⇒ λ = 19/5. The modulus splits into two sign cases, each giving a value.
6. So λ = 23 or 19/5, options (a) and (c).
  P = ((3L-5)/(L+1), (5L+1)/(L+1))
  area = (1/2)|3xP + 6yP - 33| = 2
  |(6L-42)/(L+1)| = 4
  6L-42 = 4(L+1) -> L = 23
  6L-42 = -4(L+1) -> L = 19/5
⚡ JEE Trick An area-equals condition carries a modulus, so expect two values of the parameter — solve both sign cases. Here that yields 23 and 19/5, both listed, confirming a multiple-correct question.
Answer: (a) and (c): λ = 19/5 or 23.

Concept From Q(4, 1) along a 30° line, the two points exactly 6 units away are found with the distance (parametric) form of a line. A line "can pass through either point" if it contains one of them; test each option.
Formula point = (x0 + r cosθ, y0 + r sinθ), r = ±6, θ = 30°. The parametric form steps a signed distance r along the line's direction from Q.

9A straight line 3x + 4y = 12 cuts the coordinate axes and forms an intercepted segment between the x-intercept and y-intercept. Lines passing through the origin divide this intercepted segment into three equal parts. Which of the following can represent such lines? (a) 2x − 5y = 0 (b) 3x − 8y = 0 (c) 3x − 2y = 0 (d) 3x − y = 0Lines through origin trisecting a segment (multiple correct)Let two straight lines drawn from the origin O intersect the line 3x + 4y = 12 at the points P🟢 Exactb, c3x − 8y = 0 and 3x − 2y = 0
Concept The line 3x + 4y = 12 cuts the axes, and its intercepted segment is divided into three equal parts by two lines through the origin. Find the two trisection points of that segment and join each to the origin.
Formula trisection = A + t(B − A), t = 1/3, 2/3; origin line: (y-coord)x − (x-coord)y = 0. Lines through the origin have no constant term, so a single point fixes each.
Solution
1. Intercepts of 3x + 4y = 12: A(4, 0) (y = 0) and B(0, 3) (x = 0).
2. First trisection point (t = 1/3): (4, 0) + (1/3)[(0, 3) − (4, 0)] = (8/3, 1).
3. Second trisection point (t = 2/3): (4, 0) + (2/3)[(0, 3) − (4, 0)] = (4/3, 2). These split AB into three equal lengths.
4. Line from O to (8/3, 1): slope = 1/(8/3) = 3/8 ⇒ y = 3x/8 ⇒ 3x − 8y = 0.
5. Line from O to (4/3, 2): slope = 2/(4/3) = 3/2 ⇒ y = 3x/2 ⇒ 3x − 2y = 0. Each origin line passes through one trisection point.
6. So the two lines are 3x − 8y = 0 and 3x − 2y = 0, options (b) and (c).
  3x+4y=12 -> A(4,0), B(0,3)
  P1 = (8/3, 1): slope 3/8 -> 3x - 8y = 0
  P2 = (4/3, 2): slope 3/2 -> 3x - 2y = 0
⚡ JEE Trick To trisect the intercept segment, step 1/3 and 2/3 of the way from one axis point to the other; a line through the origin to each is just (y-coord)x − (x-coord)y = 0. No constant term means no intercept hunting, and both trisection lines come out in one pass. Expect two correct options whenever the segment is split into equal parts by origin lines.
Answer: (b) and (c): 3x − 8y = 0 and 3x − 2y = 0.
10A straight line cuts the coordinate axes at points whose intercepts a and b satisfy: a + b = 5, ab = 6. Which of the following can be the equation of such a line? (a) 2x + 3y − 10 = 0 (b) 3x + 2y − 10 = 0 (c) 2x + 3y − 6 = 0 (d) 3x + 2y − 6 = 0Intercepts from sum and product (multiple correct)The intercepts a, b of a stline on the co-ordinate axes are given by a + b-5, ab= 6 then The i...🟢 Exactc, d2x + 3y − 6 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 6 = 0
Concept The intercepts a and b satisfy a given sum and product, so they are the roots of a quadratic. Solve for the two intercepts, then write the line in double-intercept form for each assignment to the axes — giving two possible lines.
Formula a + b = 5, ab = 6 ⇒ t2 − 5t + 6 = 0; line x/a + y/b = 1. The intercepts are roots of a quadratic whose sum is 5 and product 6.
Solution
1. a and b are roots of t2 − 5t + 6 = 0.
2. Factor: (t − 2)(t − 3) = 0 ⇒ t = 2 or t = 3.
3. So the intercepts are 2 and 3, assignable two ways. Either axis can take either root.
4. Case a = 3, b = 2: x/3 + y/2 = 1 ⇒ 2x + 3y = 6, i.e. 2x + 3y − 6 = 0.
5. Case a = 2, b = 3: x/2 + y/3 = 1 ⇒ 3x + 2y = 6, i.e. 3x + 2y − 6 = 0. Swapping which root is the x-intercept gives the second line.
6. These are options (c) and (d). Options (a), (b) have the constant 10, not 6, so they are wrong.
7. Hence the lines are 2x + 3y − 6 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 6 = 0.
  a+b=5, ab=6 -> t^2 - 5t + 6 = 0
  (t-2)(t-3)=0 -> t = 2, 3
  a=3,b=2: x/3+y/2=1 -> 2x + 3y - 6 = 0
  a=2,b=3: x/2+y/3=1 -> 3x + 2y - 6 = 0
⚡ JEE Trick Sum-and-product of intercepts always means a quadratic t2 − (sum)t + (product) = 0, and two intercepts give two lines (swap the axes). The constant term of each line is the product ab = 6, which instantly rejects the options showing 10.
Answer: (c) and (d): 2x + 3y − 6 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 6 = 0.
