★ Core ideaThe COM is the mass-weighted average position. Internal forces (springs, explosions, a man walking on a plank) can never move it — only a net external force accelerates it, as if the whole mass were a single particle there. If Fext = 0 and the system starts at rest, the COM stays put: ΣmiΔxi = 0.
L-1

Centre of Mass of a Two-Particle & n-Particle System

  • The centre of mass is the point that represents the average position of all the mass; the whole mass may be treated as concentrated there.
  • It need not lie inside the body — it can be on its surface or even outside (ring, L-shape, boomerang).
  • For symmetric uniform bodies (sphere, cylinder, cuboid) it coincides with the geometric centre.
  • Under external forces the COM moves as a single particle would: a spinning cricket ball wobbles on its surface but its COM follows a smooth path.

Two-particle system

distances inversely proportional to massesr₁/r₂ = m₂/m₁  ⇒  m₁r₁ = m₂r₂  ·  r₁ = m₂d/(m₁+m₂), r₂ = m₁d/(m₁+m₂)
  • m₁ = m₂ ⇒ COM at the midpoint. m₁ < m₂ ⇒ r₁ > r₂ — the COM sits closer to the heavier particle.

n-particle system

mass-weighted average of position vectorsr⃗cm = (m₁r⃗₁ + m₂r⃗₂ + … + mnr⃗n)/(m₁ + m₂ + … + mn) = Σmir⃗i / Σmi
component formxcm = Σmixi/Σmi  ·  ycm = Σmiyi/Σmi  ·  zcm = Σmizi/Σmi
Terminology
Σmir⃗i is the first moment of mass (a vector) about the chosen origin. r⃗cm = first moment ÷ total mass. The COM is a physical point — its location does not depend on the choice of axes, only its coordinates do. About the COM itself, the first moment Σmir⃗i′ is always zero.
💡 Speed trickPut the origin on the heaviest particle (or on a symmetry line) so that its term vanishes; handle x and y separately — each axis is an independent weighted average.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example 1 · L-1
Two particles of masses 1 kg and 2 kg are located at x = 0 and x = 3 m. Find the position of their centre of mass.
Let the COM be at x: r₁ = x, r₂ = 3 − x. r₁/r₂ = m₂/m₁ ⇒ x/(3 − x) = 2/1 ⇒ x = 6 − 2x ⇒ 3x = 6.
Check: xcm = (1·0 + 2·3)/(1 + 2) = 6/3 = 2.
xcm = 2 m (1 m from the 2 kg particle)
Book Example 2 · L-1
In a carbon monoxide molecule the C and O atoms are 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m apart. Find the distance of the centre of mass from the carbon atom.
mC = 12 u, mO = 16 u, d = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.
rC = mOd/(mC + mO) = (16 × 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰)/28 = 0.686 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.
≈ 0.68 × 10⁻¹⁰ m from the carbon atom (closer to the heavier O)
Book Example 3 · L-1
Particles of masses 1 kg, 2 kg, 3 kg have position vectors r⃗₁ = (î + 4ĵ + k̂) m, r⃗₂ = (î + ĵ + k̂) m, r⃗₃ = (2î − ĵ − 2k̂) m. Find the position vector of their centre of mass.
Σmir⃗i = 1(î + 4ĵ + k̂) + 2(î + ĵ + k̂) + 3(2î − ĵ − 2k̂) = (1 + 2 + 6)î + (4 + 2 − 3)ĵ + (1 + 2 − 6)k̂ = 9î + 3ĵ − 3k̂.
r⃗cm = (9î + 3ĵ − 3k̂)/6.
r⃗cm = ½(3î + ĵ − k̂) m
Book Example 4 · L-1
Particles A, B, C, D of masses m, 2m, 3m, 4m are placed in order at the corners of a square of side a (A at the origin, B = (a, 0), C = (a, a), D = (0, a)). Locate the centre of mass.
xcm = Σmixi/Σmi = [m(0) + 2m(a) + 3m(a) + 4m(0)]/10m = 5a/10 = a/2.
ycm = Σmiyi/Σmi = [m(0) + 2m(0) + 3m(a) + 4m(a)]/10m = 7a/10.
COM = (a/2, 7a/10)
⚠ Book misprintThe module swaps the labels: it writes the x-coordinate sum (0 + 2a + 3a + 0)/10 under “yCOM = a/2” and the y-coordinate sum (3a + 4a)/10 under “xCOM = 7a/10”, i.e. it effectively prints (7a/10, a/2). Correct: xcm = a/2, ycm = 7a/10 — the COM is pulled toward the heavy top edge (C, D = 7m), not the right edge.
L-2

Centre of Mass of a Rigid Body · Rod, Arc & Sector of a Disc

  • For a continuous body replace the sum by an integral over small mass elements dm located at (x, y, z).
continuous mass distributionxcm = ∫x dm / ∫dm  ·  ycm = ∫y dm / ∫dm  ·  zcm = ∫z dm / ∫dm
choice of elementline: dm = λ dx  ·  area: dm = σ dA  ·  volume: dm = ρ dV  (λ, σ, ρ may vary with position)

Standard results (derived in the module)

uniform rod, 0 → L, λ = M/Lxcm = (1/M)∫₀L(M/L)x dx = L/2  ·  ycm = zcm = 0
uniform arc, radius R, total angle φ, symmetric about x-axisxcm = ∫R cosθ·λR dθ / ∫λR dθ (−φ/2 → φ/2) = 2R sin(φ/2) / φ
uniform sector of a disc, radius R, total angle 2θstrips = arcs of radius r, mass 2σrθ dr, COM r sinθ/θ ⇒ xcm = 2R sinθ / 3θ
Body (from the centre of curvature)COM distance
Semicircular ring (φ = π)2R/π
Quarter ring (φ = π/2)2√2R/π
Semicircular disc (2θ = π)4R/3π
Quarter disc (2θ = π/2)4√2R/3π
Full ring / full disc (φ = 2π)0 (at the centre — sin π = 0)
⚠ TrapThe arc formula uses the full angle φ with sin(φ/2); the sector formula is written with the half-angle θ. Before substituting, decide which one the question gives you — mixing them turns a quarter-disc answer into a semicircle answer (see Example 4).
💡 Symmetry firstAny axis of symmetry contains the COM — for arcs and sectors set the symmetry line as the x-axis so only one integral is needed. For a density that depends only on x, use full-height strips so the y-integral collapses.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example 1 · L-2
Find the centre of mass of a non-uniform rod of length L whose mass per unit length is λ = kx²/L, x measured from one end.
dm = λ dx = (kx²/L) dx.
xcm = ∫₀L x·(kx²/L)dx / ∫₀L(kx²/L)dx = (k/L)(L⁴/4) / (k/L)(L³/3) = 3L/4.
xcm = 3L/4 from the light end
Book Example 2 · L-2
Find the centre of mass of a uniform annular half-disc of inner radius R₁ and outer radius R₂.
Element: half-ring of radius x, width dx ⇒ dm = σπx dx; its COM is at 2x/π on the symmetry axis.
ycm = ∫(2x/π)σπx dx / ∫σπx dx = 2σ[x³/3]R₁R₂ / σπ[x²/2]R₁R₂ = 4(R₂³ − R₁³)/3π(R₂² − R₁²).
Check: R₁ → 0 gives 4R₂/3π (semicircular disc) ✓.
ycm = 4(R₂³ − R₁³) / [3π(R₂² − R₁²)]
Book Example 3 · L-2
Find the centre of mass of a uniform arc of radius R whose ends subtend an angle π/3 at the centre.
Arc of total angle φ = π/3: xcm = 2R sin(φ/2)/φ = 2R sin(π/6)/(π/3) = 2R(½)·(3/π).
xcm = 3R/π (≈ 0.955R) from the centre, on the bisector
Book Example 4 · L-2
Find the centre of mass of a uniform sector (segment) of a disc whose ends subtend an angle π/2 at the centre.
Total angle 2θ = π/2 ⇒ half-angle θ = π/4.
xcm = 2R sinθ/(3θ) = 2R sin(π/4)/(3π/4) = 2R(1/√2)(4/3π) = 8R/(3√2π) = 4√2R/3π.
xcm = 4√2R/3π ≈ 0.600R from the centre, on the bisector (quarter disc)
⚠ Book misprintThe module substitutes θ = π/2 (sin π/2 over 3π/2) and prints 4R/3π — but with its own definition (2θ = total angle) θ = π/2 describes a semicircular disc. For ends subtending π/2, θ = π/4 and the correct answer is 4√2R/3π ≈ 0.600R.
L-3

Centre of Mass of a Composite / Remaining Body

  • For a uniform lamina, mass ∝ area, so areas can replace masses in the COM formula (volumes for uniform solids).
composite body (pieces added)r⃗cm = (A₁r⃗₁ + A₂r⃗₂ + … + Anr⃗n)/(A₁ + A₂ + … + An)
portion removed (cavity / cut-out)xcm = (A₁x₁ − A₂x₂)/(A₁ − A₂)  ·  ycm = (A₁y₁ − A₂y₂)/(A₁ − A₂)
  • A₁, (x₁, y₁) belong to the whole body; A₂, (x₂, y₂) to the removed part. Treat the hole as a piece of negative mass.
💡 ShortcutPut the origin at the centre of the whole body (x₁ = 0). Then xcm = −A₂x₂/(A₁ − A₂): the COM shifts away from the hole, along the line joining the two centres.
⚠ TrapSubtract in the denominator too — (A₁ − A₂), not A₁. And if the removed piece is re-attached elsewhere, treat it as “remove at old place + add at new place”.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example 1 · L-3
From a uniform disc of radius a (centre O at the origin), a disc of radius a/2 touching the rim is cut out, its centre at (a/2, 0). Find the centre of mass of the remaining lamina.
A₁ = πa² at (0, 0); A₂ = π(a/2)² = πa²/4 at (a/2, 0).
xcm = (A₁x₁ − A₂x₂)/(A₁ − A₂) = [0 − (πa²/4)(a/2)] / (πa² − πa²/4) = (−πa³/8)/(3πa²/4) = −a/6.
Both centres lie on the x-axis ⇒ ycm = 0.
COM = (−a/6, 0) — a/6 from O, on the side opposite the hole
Book Example 2 · L-3
Find the centre of mass of the uniform L-shaped block: a square of side L (corner at the origin) from which the top-right square of side L/2 has been removed.
A₁ = L² at (L/2, L/2); A₂ = L²/4 at (3L/4, 3L/4).
xcm = [L²(L/2) − (L²/4)(3L/4)] / (L² − L²/4) = (L³/2 − 3L³/16)/(3L²/4) = (5L³/16)(4/3L²) = 5L/12.
By symmetry about the line y = x, ycm = xcm = 5L/12.
COM = (5L/12, 5L/12)
L-4

Motion of the Centre of Mass · Momentum · Newton's 2nd Law for Systems

  • The COM's motion is decided by external forces only. A shell that explodes in flight: the fragments scatter, but their COM continues on the original parabola (until a fragment lands).

Displacement of the COM

mass-weighted displacementsΔr⃗cm = (m₁Δr⃗₁ + m₂Δr⃗₂ + … + mnΔr⃗n)/(m₁ + m₂ + … + mn)
  • If Fext = 0 and the system starts from rest, Δr⃗cm = 0 ⇒ ΣmiΔxi = 0 (man–plank, boat, block-on-wedge problems).

Velocity of COM & total linear momentum

differentiate Mr⃗cm = Σmir⃗iv⃗cm = Σmiv⃗i/M  ·  P⃗ = p⃗₁ + p⃗₂ + … + p⃗n = Mv⃗cm

Acceleration of COM & Newton's 2nd law for a system

differentiate once morea⃗cm = Σmia⃗i/M  ·  F⃗net,ext = Σmia⃗i = Ma⃗cm
Why internal forces drop out
Σmia⃗i = ΣF⃗i = ΣF⃗ext + ΣF⃗int. Internal forces come in Newton's-third-law pairs, so ΣF⃗int = 0, leaving F⃗ext = Ma⃗cm. Consequently F⃗ext = 0 ⇒ v⃗cm constant ⇒ P⃗ conserved.
★ Explosion from rest0 = m₁v⃗₁ + m₂v⃗₂ ⇒ equal and opposite momenta ⇒ K = p²/2m ⇒ K₁/K₂ = m₂/m₁: the lighter fragment carries more kinetic energy.
⚠ TrapZero external torque does not mean constant vcm — gravity acting at the COM gives zero torque about it yet accelerates it. Only zero external force keeps vcm constant.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example 1 · L-4
Particles of mass 2 kg and 1 kg are some distance apart. If the 2 kg particle is moved 1 m towards the other particle, find the shift of the centre of mass.
Only the 2 kg particle moves: Δxcm = (m₁Δx₁ + m₂Δx₂)/(m₁ + m₂) = (1·0 + 2·1)/(1 + 2).
Δxcm = 2/3 m, towards the 1 kg particle
Book Example 2 · L-4
A girl of mass 40 kg walks from one end to the other of a plank of mass 80 kg (length 3 m) lying on a smooth surface. Find the displacement of the plank.
No external horizontal force ⇒ COM stays fixed: mgΔxg + mpΔxp = 0.
Let the plank move d backward; the girl moves (3 − d) forward relative to ground: 40(3 − d) − 80d = 0 ⇒ 120 = 120d.
d = 1 m (opposite to the girl); general result d = mL/(m + M)
Book Example 3 · L-4
A 1 kg block at x = 10 m moves in −x at 6 m/s; a 2 kg block at x = 12 m moves in +x at 4 m/s at the same instant. Find the position of their centre of mass after 2 s.
vcm = (1(−6) + 2(4))/(1 + 2) = 2/3 m/s (+x).
xcm,i = (1·10 + 2·12)/3 = 34/3 m ⇒ xcm,f = 34/3 + (2/3)(2) = 38/3 m.
xcm = 38/3 ≈ 12.67 m
Book Example 4 · L-4
A stationary particle explodes into two fragments of masses m₁ and m₂ moving in opposite directions with speeds v₁ and v₂. Find the ratio of their kinetic energies K₁ : K₂.
Momentum: 0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ ⇒ |m₁v₁| = |m₂v₂| = p.
K₁/K₂ = (p²/2m₁)/(p²/2m₂) = m₂/m₁.
K₁ : K₂ = m₂ : m₁
Book Example 5 · L-4
Blocks A (2 kg) and B (1 kg) hang over a light frictionless pulley by a light inextensible string. Find the acceleration of the centre of mass of the two blocks.
A is heavier ⇒ A moves down, B up: a = (mA − mB)g/(mA + mB) = (2 − 1)g/3 = g/3.
Taking downward +ve: acm = (mAa − mBa)/(mA + mB) = (2a − a)/3 = a/3 = g/9.
Check via Newton II for the system: external forces = 3g (down) − 2T (up), T = 2mAmBg/(mA+mB) = 4g/3 ⇒ acm = (3g − 8g/3)/3 = g/9 ✓.
acm = g/9, directed downward
⚠ Book misprintThe module's text says “block A moving up and B moving down”, yet uses (mAg − mBg) — with mA = 2 kg > mB = 1 kg, A moves down and B moves up. The numerical answer g/9 (downward) is unaffected.
Book Example 6 · L-4
A body of mass 2.5 kg is acted on by 5 N at 37° (up-right), 6 N at 53° below +x, 4 N at 60° (up-right) and 6 N along −x. Find the acceleration of its centre of mass.
Fx = 5cos37° + 6cos53° + 4cos60° − 6 = 4 + 3.6 + 2 − 6 = 3.6 N.
Fy = 5sin37° − 6sin53° + 4sin60° = 3 − 4.8 + 2√3 = 1.66 N.
a⃗cm = F⃗/M = (3.6î + 1.66ĵ)/2.5 = 1.44î + 0.67ĵ; |a| ≈ 1.59 m/s².
a⃗cm ≈ (1.44î + 0.67ĵ) m s⁻² (book rounds Fy to 1.7 N ⇒ 0.68ĵ)

Worked examples from the module
Strong Box · PYQ 1 · JEE Advanced 2024 P2
Particles 1 and 2 (mass m each), joined by a light spring on a smooth plane, oscillate about their COM x₀: x₁(t) = (x₀ + d) + a sinωt, x₂(t) = (x₀ − d) − a sinωt (d > 2a). Particle 3 (mass m) moving at u₀ = aω/2 collides elastically with particle 2 at t₀. Afterwards 1 and 2 have COM speed vcm and oscillate with amplitude b at the same ω. If t₀ = π/(2ω), find 4b²/a². Options: 4.25, 3.25, 4.50, 3.50.
At t₀ = π/2ω: sinωt₀ = 1, cosωt₀ = 0 ⇒ v₁ = aωcosωt = 0, v₂ = −aωcosωt = 0; spring at maximum stretch.
Equal-mass elastic collision exchanges velocities: v₂′ = u₀ = aω/2, v₃′ = 0. Positions unchanged.
Relative coordinate s = x₁ − x₂ − 2d = 2a sinωt ⇒ just after: s = 2a, ṡ = v₁ − v₂′ = −aω/2.
New relative amplitude S² = s² + (ṡ/ω)² = 4a² + a²/4 = 17a²/4. Each particle's amplitude about the COM is b = S/2 ⇒ b² = 17a²/16.
4b²/a² = 17/4 = 4.25 — option (a)
Strong Box · PYQ 2 · JEE Advanced 2024 P2
Same system as PYQ 1. If the collision occurs at t₀ = 0, find vcm/(aω). Options: 0.75, 0.71, 0.79, 0.77.
At t = 0: v₁ = aωcos0 = +aω, v₂ = −aω (spring passing through natural length, both at maximum speed).
Elastic, equal masses ⇒ exchange: v₂′ = u₀ = +aω/2, v₃′ = −aω.
vcm = (v₁ + v₂′)/2 = (aω + aω/2)/2 = 3aω/4.
vcm/(aω) = 0.75 — option (a)
Strong Box · PYQ 3 · JEE Advanced 2017 P1 (multiple correct)
A block M with a frictionless quarter-circle cut (radius R) rests on a smooth table, its right edge at x = 0. A point mass m is released from rest at the top of the cut and slides down; when it leaves the block its position is x and velocity v. Which are correct? (a) v = √(2gR/(1 + m/M)) (b) velocity of M = −(m/M)√(2gR) (c) x = −√2·mR/(m + M) (d) x-displacement of the COM of M = −mR/(m + M).
No horizontal external force ⇒ horizontal momentum 0 and xcm fixed. m leaves at the bottom of the arc moving horizontally: mv + MV = 0.
Energy: mgR = ½mv² + ½MV² = ½mv²(1 + m/M) ⇒ v = √(2gR/(1 + m/M)) ⇒ (a) ✓.
V = −(m/M)v = −(m/M)√(2gR/(1 + m/M)) ≠ −(m/M)√(2gR) ⇒ (b) ✗.
m moves R relative to M: Δxm − ΔxM = R and mΔxm + MΔxM = 0 ⇒ ΔxM = −mR/(m + M) ⇒ (d) ✓.
x of m: starts at −R, Δxm = MR/(m + M) ⇒ x = −R + MR/(m + M) = −mR/(m + M), no √2 ⇒ (c) ✗.
Correct: (a) and (d)
Strong Box · PYQ 4 · JEE Advanced 2007 P2
Statement I: If there is no external torque on a body about its centre of mass, the velocity of the centre of mass remains constant. Statement II: The linear momentum of an isolated system remains constant.
Statement I: a freely falling ball has zero torque about its COM (gravity acts there) but acm = g ⇒ vcm changes ⇒ false. vcm is governed by Fext = Macm, not by torque.
Statement II: isolated ⇒ Fext = 0 ⇒ dP⃗/dt = 0 ⇒ true.
Option (d) — Statement I false, Statement II true
Strong Box · PYQ 5 · JEE Main 2025 (Shift 1)
Two 1 kg particles: r⃗A = (α₁t²î + α₂tĵ + α₃tk̂) m, r⃗B = (β₁tî + β₂t²ĵ + β₃tk̂) m with α₁ = 1, α₂ = 3n, α₃ = 2, β₁ = 2, β₂ = −1, β₃ = 4p (SI). At t = 1 s, |v⃗A| = |v⃗B| and v⃗A ⟂ v⃗B. The angular momentum of A about the position of B at t = 1 s is √L kg m² s⁻¹. Find L.
At t = 1: v⃗A = (2α₁t, α₂, α₃) = (2, 3n, 2); v⃗B = (β₁, 2β₂t, β₃) = (2, −2, 4p).
Equal speeds: 8 + 9n² = 8 + 16p² ⇒ 9n² = 16p². Perpendicular: 4 − 6n + 8p = 0 ⇒ 3n = 2 + 4p.
(2 + 4p)² = 16p² ⇒ 4 + 16p = 0 ⇒ p = −1/4, n = 1/3 ⇒ v⃗A = (2, 1, 2), v⃗B = (2, −2, −1) (check: 4 − 2 − 2 = 0 ✓).
r⃗A(1) = (1, 1, 2), r⃗B(1) = (2, −1, −1) ⇒ r⃗A − r⃗B = (−1, 2, 3).
L⃗ = (r⃗A − r⃗B) × m v⃗A = (−1, 2, 3) × (2, 1, 2) = (4 − 3, 6 + 2, −1 − 4) = (1, 8, −5) ⇒ |L⃗|² = 1 + 64 + 25.
L = 90
Strong Box · PYQ 6 · JEE Main 2025 (Shift 1)
A thin rectangular plate occupies 0 ≤ x ≤ a, 0 ≤ y ≤ b, with mass per unit area σ = σ₀x/(ab). Its centre of mass is: (a) (2a/3, b/2) (b) (a/2, b/2) (c) (a/3, b/2) (d) (2a/3, 2b/3).
σ is independent of y ⇒ ycm = b/2 by symmetry.
Vertical strip: dm = σ b dx = (σ₀/a)x dx ⇒ xcm = ∫₀ax² dx / ∫₀ax dx = (a³/3)/(a²/2) = 2a/3.
(2a/3, b/2) — option (a)
Strong Box · PYQ 7 · JEE Main 2022 (Shift 1)
Blocks of 10 kg and 30 kg are at (0, 0) cm and (x, 0) cm. The 10 kg block is moved 6 cm towards the other. How far must the 30 kg block move to keep the COM unchanged? (a) 4 cm towards the 10 kg block (b) 2 cm away from it (c) 2 cm towards it (d) 4 cm away from it.
COM fixed ⇒ m₁Δx₁ + m₂Δx₂ = 0 ⇒ 10(+6) + 30Δx₂ = 0 ⇒ Δx₂ = −2 cm.
Negative x is toward the origin, i.e. toward the 10 kg block (x itself drops out).
2 cm towards the 10 kg block — option (c)
★ Recurring tricksNo horizontal external force ⇒ ΣmiΔxi = 0 (block-on-wedge, plank, boat). Equal-mass elastic collision ⇒ velocities swap. Spring-coupled pair: vcm from the instant's velocities; amplitude from S² = s² + (ṡ/ω)² of the relative coordinate. Density varying in x only ⇒ ycm is the geometric midpoint.
