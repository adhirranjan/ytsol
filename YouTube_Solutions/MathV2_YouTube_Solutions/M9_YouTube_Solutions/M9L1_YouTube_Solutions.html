1 (i)i^30 + i^60 = ____Powers of iotaComplex Numbers 01 | Integral powers of iota (i) | Imaginary number | JEE/BITSAT/CET/ Class 11🟡 Similar0
Concept Iota is defined by the single relation i² = −1, and that one fact makes every higher power collapse. From i² = −1 follow i³ = i²·i = −i and i⁴ = (i²)² = 1, and the moment a power of i equals 1 the whole ladder starts over. So i raised to any whole number is one of only four values, selected by the remainder of the exponent on division by 4. Evaluating i³⁰ + i⁶⁰ is therefore two remainder calculations followed by one addition — no expansion is ever needed.
Formula iⁿ = i^(n mod 4), with i⁰ = 1, i¹ = i, i² = −1, i³ = −i. Because i⁴ = 1, stripping any multiple of 4 out of the exponent cannot change the value.
Solution
1. Reduce the first exponent: 30 = 4(7) + 2, so i³⁰ = (i⁴)⁷ · i² = 1 · (−1) = −1.
2. Reduce the second: 60 = 4(15) + 0, so i⁶⁰ = (i⁴)¹⁵ = 1. An exponent divisible by 4 always gives 1, the identity of the cycle.
3. Add the two reduced values: i³⁰ + i⁶⁰ = (−1) + 1 = 0.
4. A faster way to get the remainders: 4 divides 100, so only the last two digits of the exponent matter — here 30 and 60 themselves. This is what lets you handle exponents like i²⁰²&sup0; at sight.
5. Cross-check by squaring: i⁶⁰ = (i³⁰)² = (−1)² = 1, which agrees with step 2.
6. Both terms are real and opposite, so the blank is the real number 0. Whenever two exponents differ by an odd multiple of 2, the powers are negatives of each other and cancel.
 n mod 4 :   0     1     2     3
 i^n     :   1     i    -1    -i
 --------------------------------------------
 30 = 4(7)  + 2   ->  i^30 = i^2 = -1
 60 = 4(15) + 0   ->  i^60 = i^0 =  1
 sum = (-1) + 1 = 0
⚡ JEE Trick The trap is arithmetic on the exponents instead of on their remainders: 30 + 60 = 90 and i⁹⁰ = −1 is a tempting but wrong shortcut, because iⁿ + iᵐ is not iⁿ⁺ᵐ. Reduce each term separately, then add. A second slip is reading 30 as odd-looking and guessing ±i; only odd exponents can produce i or −i, and 30 and 60 are both even, so the answer had to be real from the start.
Answer: 0.

Concept A complex number carries two independent pieces of information, a real part and an imaginary part, so it behaves exactly like a vector with two components. Addition therefore happens component by component: real adds to real, imaginary adds to imaginary, and the two channels never mix. This is why the sum of two complex numbers can turn out purely real — the imaginary parts may cancel. Nothing here needs i² = −1; that relation is only required once multiplication enters.
Formula (a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i. It is just collecting like terms, treating i as a symbol that is never combined with the pure numbers.
Solution
1. The printed z₁ = z + i is a misprint — no z is defined anywhere in the question. Read as z₁ = 2 + i, the only value that reproduces the book's own answer 5.
2. Separate the parts: z₁ = 2 + i gives a = 2, b = 1; z₂ = 3 − i gives c = 3, d = −1.
3. Real channel: a + c = 2 + 3 = 5.
4. Imaginary channel: b + d = 1 + (−1) = 0. The coefficient is 1 and −1, not i and −i; add the coefficients only.
5. Reassemble: z₁ + z₂ = 5 + 0i = 5, a purely real number.
6. Structural check: 2 + i and 3 − i have opposite imaginary parts, so their sum was bound to land on the real axis. On the Argand plane the two arrows point one up and one down by the same amount, leaving a horizontal resultant.
            Re      Im
 z1 =  2 + i     2      +1
 z2 =  3 - i     3      -1
 -----------------------------
 sum              5       0
 z1 + z2 = 5 + 0i = 5
⚡ JEE Trick The classic error is writing the answer as 5 + 0i and then calling it "complex, not real"; a complex number with zero imaginary part is a real number, and 5 is the expected form of the blank. The second trap is treating −i as the quantity being added rather than the coefficient −1, which produces the nonsense 5 − i. Keep the two columns separate and the cancellation is obvious in one glance.
Answer: 5.

Concept Subtraction of complex numbers is addition of the negative, and negating a complex number negates both of its parts. So z₁ − z₂ is again a two-channel operation: subtract real from real and imaginary from imaginary, independently. The result is a complex number that is real exactly when the two imaginary parts are equal, and purely imaginary exactly when the two real parts are equal. Recognising which of those two situations you are in usually settles the answer before any arithmetic.
Formula (a + bi) − (c + di) = (a − c) + (b − d)i. The minus sign distributes over both terms of the bracket, so the sign of d flips as well as the sign of c.
Solution
1. Read off the parts: z₁ = 3 + i has a = 3, b = 1; z₂ = 2 + i has c = 2, d = 1.
2. Real channel: a − c = 3 − 2 = 1.
3. Imaginary channel: b − d = 1 − 1 = 0. Both numbers carry exactly one unit of i, so that unit is removed completely.
4. Assemble: z₁ − z₂ = 1 + 0i = 1, a real number.
5. Verify by expanding in full: (3 + i) − (2 + i) = 3 + i − 2 − i = (3 − 2) + (i − i) = 1.
6. Geometric reading: the two points sit on the same horizontal line of the Argand plane, so the vector joining them is horizontal of length 1. Equal imaginary parts always force a real difference; that is the whole content of this blank.

 z2 =  2 + i     2      +1
 -----------------------------
 diff             1       0
 z1 - z2 = 1 + 0i = 1
⚡ JEE Trick The one place marks are lost is forgetting to distribute the minus sign across the whole second bracket, which turns the answer into 1 + 2i. Write the subtraction as z₁ + (−z₂) = (3 + i) + (−2 − i) and the sign takes care of itself. Note also that 1 is a perfectly acceptable complex number here — the real numbers sit inside the complex numbers as the case b = 0.

1 (iv)(2 + i)(2 - i) = ____Multiplication of complex numbersChapter 5 - Complex Numbers Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, Division|CBSE Class 11 Maths|GS🟡 Similar5
Concept Multiplication of complex numbers is ordinary bracket expansion followed by one substitution, i² = −1. The two factors 2 + i and 2 − i differ only in the sign of the imaginary part, so they are conjugates of each other. A number times its conjugate is a difference of two squares in which the second square carries i², and that i² turns a subtraction into an addition. The product is therefore always real and never negative — it is precisely the square of the modulus.
Formula (a + bi)(a − bi) = a² − (bi)² = a² + b² = |z|². Because (bi)² = b²i² = −b², the minus in the identity is cancelled by the minus hidden in i².
Solution
1. Recognise the pattern: with a = 2 and b = 1, the product is z·z̄ for z = 2 + i.
2. Apply the identity directly: (2 + i)(2 − i) = 2² + 1² = 4 + 1 = 5.
3. Confirm by longhand expansion: 2(2) + 2(−i) + i(2) + i(−i) = 4 − 2i + 2i − i².
4. The cross terms −2i and +2i cancel, leaving 4 − i² = 4 − (−1) = 5. The cross terms must cancel for conjugates; if they do not, a sign was copied wrongly.
5. Modulus check: |2 + i| = √(2² + 1²) = √5, and (√5)² = 5. z·z̄ = |z|² is the fastest route and needs no expansion at all.
6. So the blank is the real number 5. Positive and real is the only possible shape of a conjugate product, a useful one-second sanity test.
 (2 + i)(2 - i)
   = 2*2 + 2*(-i) + i*2 + i*(-i)
   = 4 - 2i + 2i - i^2
   = 4 - (-1)
   = 5
 shortcut: a^2 + b^2 = 2^2 + 1^2 = 5
⚡ JEE Trick The trap is applying a² − b² mechanically and answering 4 − 1 = 3; the identity for conjugates is a² + b², because i² supplies an extra minus sign. The other slip is writing i·(−i) = −1 instead of −i² = +1. Whenever you see a bracket pair identical except for the sign of i, jump straight to a² + b² and move on.
Answer: 5.

Concept Two complex numbers are equal only when their real parts agree and their imaginary parts agree separately, so a single complex equation is really two real equations. That principle, called comparing real and imaginary parts, is what lets you solve for the unknowns p, q, r here. The numbers given, 2 + i and 2 − i, are conjugates, and conjugate pairs have a memorable signature: their sum is twice the real part and purely real, their difference is twice the imaginary part and purely imaginary. Spotting that structure gives all three answers before any expansion.
Formula z + z̄ = 2·Re(z) and z − z̄ = 2i·Im(z). Both follow instantly from (a + bi) ± (a − bi); the b-terms cancel in the sum and the a-terms cancel in the difference.
Solution
1. Add: z₁ + z₂ = (2 + i) + (2 − i) = (2 + 2) + (1 − 1)i = 4 + 0i.
2. Compare with the given form p + qi: p = 4 and q = 0. The imaginary coefficient is genuinely 0, not "absent" — q must be written as 0.
3. Subtract: z₁ − z₂ = (2 + i) − (2 − i) = (2 − 2) + (1 + 1)i = 0 + 2i = 2i.
4. Compare with the given form ir: ir = 2i, so r = 2. Here r is a real number multiplying i, so match the coefficients, not the whole expression.
5. Check against the conjugate identities: 2·Re(z₁) = 2(2) = 4 = p, and 2·Im(z₁) = 2(1) = 2 = r.
6. Hence p = 4, q = 0, r = 2. Because z₂ = z̄₁, the answer could have been read off in one line without adding anything.

 z2         2      -1

 z1 - z2    0      +2    ->  ir = 2i, r = 2
⚡ JEE Trick The difference is written as ir, not r + ir, so do not carry a real part into it; matching 2i with ir gives r = 2, while carelessly matching 2i with r gives the wrong r = 2i. The second trap is leaving q blank or calling it undefined when the sum is real — the correct entry is q = 0. Conjugate pairs are worth memorising: sum is real, difference is purely imaginary, product is the square of the modulus.
Answer: p = 4, q = 0, r = 2.

Concept The complex numbers were built to give √(−1) a home, and the object created for that purpose is i, with i² = −1. Every complex number is then a real number plus a real multiple of i, and that description is exhaustive: the set is C = {a + bi : a, b ∈ R}. The pair (a, b) is unique for a given number, which is precisely why comparing real and imaginary parts is legitimate. Writing a number as a + bi is called putting it in standard, or rectangular, form, and the two slots are named Re(z) = a and Im(z) = b.
Formula z = a + bi, a, b ∈ R, i = √(−1). Im(z) is the real coefficient b, not bi — a distinction the paper tests constantly.
Solution
1. Option (a) a + b contains no i, so it is just a real number and cannot represent a general complex number.
2. Option (b) a + bi has a real part a and an imaginary part b attached to i — exactly the definition. Taking b = 0 recovers every real number, so the reals sit inside C as a special case.
3. Option (c) ab + 1 is a product of two reals plus 1, again purely real, and it cannot produce a non-real value.
4. Option (d) a + b + i forces the imaginary part to be exactly 1, so it cannot express 3 + 5i or any number with Im(z) ≠ 1. A standard form must cover every complex number, not a single slice of them.
5. Uniqueness settles it: if a + bi = c + di with a, b, c, d real, then a = c and b = d, so the representation in (b) labels each complex number exactly once.
6. Hence the standard form is a + bi, option (b). On the Argand plane this is the Cartesian pair (a, b); the polar form r(cosθ + i sinθ) is the same point written differently.
