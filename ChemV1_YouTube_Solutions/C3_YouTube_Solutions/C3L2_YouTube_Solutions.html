1Number of g-atom of S present in 49 g H₂SO₄ are: (a) 0.5 (b) 1 (c) 0.2 (d) 0.3g-atoms of an elementNumber of g-atom of S in 49 g H₂SO₄🟢 Exacta0.5
Concept A g-atom (gram-atom) of an element is simply one mole of atoms of that element. To count g-atoms of S locked inside a compound, first convert the compound's mass to moles, then multiply by the number of S atoms each molecule carries (the subscript). H₂SO₄ has exactly one sulphur per molecule, so the g-atoms of S equal the moles of acid — the whole question reduces to a single mass-to-mole division once that is seen.
Formula g-atom of S = (m ÷ M) × (subscript of S): m = mass of H₂SO₄, M = its molar mass. m ÷ M gives moles of molecules; the subscript converts moles of molecules into moles (g-atoms) of that element. Data: H = 1, S = 32, O = 16.
Solution
1. Molar mass of H₂SO₄: M = 2×1 + 32 + 4×16 = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 g mol⁻¹.
2. Moles of H₂SO₄ = m ÷ M = 49 ÷ 98 = 0.5 mol.49 is exactly half of 98, so the division is instant.
3. Each H₂SO₄ molecule contains 1 S atom, so moles of S atoms = 0.5 × 1 = 0.5 mol S.The subscript on S in H₂SO₄ is 1.
4. Moles of S atoms = g-atoms of S, so the sample holds 0.5 g-atom of S — option (a).
5. Check the distractors: 1 would need 98 g of acid; 0.2 and 0.3 correspond to no simple fraction of 98 g.
 quantity          value
 M(H₂SO₄)          2 + 32 + 64 = 98 g/mol

 S per molecule    1
 g-atom of S       0.5 × 1 = 0.5   <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick Spot that 49 = 98/2 — half a mole of acid, and with one S per molecule the answer is 0.5 immediately. Trap: "g-atom" panics students into converting to grams of sulphur (16 g here); it is only a mole count of atoms. Had the question asked g-atoms of O you would multiply by 4 (⇒ 2 g-atom).
Answer: (a) 0.5.
2How many mole of atoms are in a mole of CH₃COOH molecule?Moles of atoms in a moleculeHow many atoms in a mole of acetic acid (CH₃COOH)?🟡 Similara2 mol C, 4 mol H, 2 mol O atoms
Concept A chemical formula is also a mole recipe: the subscripts that count atoms in one molecule become the moles of atoms in one mole of that molecule. Because one mole always contains the same NA molecules, "how many moles of each kind of atom" is read straight off the formula — you only have to count the atoms of each element correctly, watching for atoms that appear in more than one part of a condensed formula.
Formula moles of atoms of an element = (subscript of that element) × (moles of molecules). 1 molecule of formula CₓHᵧO_z holds x atoms of C, y of H, z of O; scaling by NA to reach 1 mole multiplies every count by the same NA, so the per-mole answer equals the subscript. Acetic acid CH₃COOH expands to the empirical count C₂H₄O₂.
Solution
1. Expand the condensed formula CH₃COOH group by group so no atom is missed:condensed formulas hide atoms that recur in different groups.
  • C: 1 (in CH₃) + 1 (in COOH) = 2 carbon atoms
  • H: 3 (in CH₃) + 1 (in the O–H of COOH) = 4 hydrogen atoms
  • O: 2 (both in COOH) = 2 oxygen atoms
2. So one molecule = C₂H₄O₂ (2 C, 4 H, 2 O), a total of 2 + 4 + 2 = 8 atoms.
3. Scale to one mole of molecules — multiply each atom count by NA, i.e. take each as "moles":1 mol molecules contains NA molecules, so it contains 2·NA C atoms = 2 mol C atoms, and so on.
4. Result: 2 mol C, 4 mol H, 2 mol O atoms (8 mol atoms in all) — option (a).
 element   atoms per molecule   × N_A   moles of atoms per mole
 C          2                   →       2 mol
 H          4                   →       4 mol
 O          2                   →       2 mol
 total      8                   →       8 mol atoms
⚡ JEE Trick Read moles of atoms straight off the subscripts — no NA arithmetic needed for a "per mole of molecule" question. Trap: CH₃COOH looks like it has few atoms; the two carbons and the two separate O's (C=O and O–H) are easy to undercount. Always expand to the empirical formula (C₂H₄O₂) first.
Answer: (a) 2 mol C, 4 mol H, 2 mol O atoms.

4Which gas has least volume if 10 g of each is taken at same T and P? CO₂ / N₂ / CH₄ / HClVolume ∝ moles at same T,PWhich gas has least volume — 10 g each at same T&P🟢 ExactaCO₂
Concept By Avogadro's law, at the same temperature and pressure the volume of a gas depends only on its number of moles, never on which gas it is: V ∝ n. Equal 10 g masses therefore give different volumes because each gas packs a different number of moles into 10 g — n = 10 ÷ M. The heaviest molecules give the fewest moles, hence the least volume.
Formula V ∝ n = m ÷ M at fixed T, P. With m fixed at 10 g, n is inversely proportional to molar mass M — so least volume ⇔ largest M. Data: C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, H = 1, Cl = 35.5.
Solution
1. Molar masses: M(CO₂) = 12 + 32 = 44; M(N₂) = 28; M(CH₄) = 12 + 4 = 16; M(HCl) = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5 g mol⁻¹.
2. Moles in 10 g: n(CO₂) = 10/44 = 0.227; n(N₂) = 10/28 = 0.357; n(CH₄) = 10/16 = 0.625; n(HCl) = 10/36.5 = 0.274 mol.
3. Since V ∝ n at the same T and P, rank the volumes by moles: CH₄ > N₂ > HCl > CO₂.More moles ⇒ proportionally more volume, whatever the gas.
4. The smallest mole count belongs to CO₂ (0.227 mol), so CO₂ occupies the least volume — option (a).
5. Sanity check: CO₂ also has the largest molar mass (44), confirming the inverse rule without any division.
 gas    M(g/mol)   n = 10/M (mol)   volume rank
 CH₄    16         0.625            largest
 N₂     28         0.357            2nd
 HCl    36.5       0.274            3rd
 CO₂    44         0.227            least   <-- answer
⚡ JEE Trick Equal masses at the same T, P: least volume = heaviest gas — just compare molar masses, no division needed. Trap: students instinctively pick the "biggest molecule by size" or the gas with most atoms (CH₄ has 5 atoms but is the lightest, so it actually has the most volume). Only molar mass matters.
Answer: (a) CO₂.

1Explain the term: Average atomic massAverage atomic massAverage atomic mass — Basic Concepts of Chemistry🟡 Similar—
Concept The single atomic-mass value printed for an element is not the mass of any one atom. Most elements occur naturally as a mixture of isotopes — atoms of the same element (same protons) but different numbers of neutrons, and hence different masses. The average atomic mass is the abundance-weighted mean of those isotopic masses, so it reflects both how heavy each isotope is and how common it is in nature. This is the number the periodic table quotes.
Key relation average atomic mass = Σ (fractional abundance × isotopic mass), in u, where each fractional abundance = (percentage ÷ 100) and all fractions sum to 1. Weighting by abundance means a rare heavy isotope shifts the mean only slightly, while an abundant isotope dominates it.
Solution
1. Recognise that an element is a mixture of isotopes with different masses and natural abundances.Only the neutron count differs, so chemistry is identical but mass is not.
2. Multiply each isotopic mass by its fractional abundance, then add the products — a weighted mean, not a plain average.
3. Worked example (chlorine): 75% (0.75) of mass 35 u and 25% (0.25) of mass 37 u.
  • 0.75 × 35 = 26.25
  • 0.25 × 37 = 9.25
  • sum = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 uNearer 35 than 37 because ³⁵Cl is three times more abundant.
 isotope   mass(u)   abundance   mass × abundance
 ³⁵Cl       35        0.75        26.25
 ³⁷Cl       37        0.25         9.25
                      ─────       ──────
 total                1.00        35.50 u   ← weighted average
⚡ JEE Trick It is a weighted mean — the result always lands nearer the more abundant isotope, and must lie between the lightest and heaviest isotope. Trap: (35+37)/2 = 36 is the tempting wrong "plain average"; the correct 35.5 leans toward the 75%-abundant ³⁵Cl.
Key point: average atomic mass = Σ(fractional abundance × isotopic mass) — the abundance-weighted mean of an element's isotopic masses, expressed in u.

Concept Molar mass is the mass of one mole (NA = 6.022×10²³ particles) of a substance. The question asks for the molar mass of oxygen atoms (O), not oxygen gas (O₂) — a distinction that trips many students. The atomic-mass value 16 u for a single O atom becomes 16 g mol⁻¹ when scaled to one mole, because the u→g switch and the ×NA exactly cancel, leaving the same number.
Key relation molar mass = (atomic mass in u) expressed in g mol⁻¹ = mass of 1 mole of atoms. 1 u = 1/NA g, so NA atoms of 16 u weigh NA × 16 × (1/NA) g = 16 g — the NA cancels, so the number is unchanged.
Solution
1. Read the atomic mass of oxygen: 16 u per atom.
2. One mole of O atoms = NA atoms; multiply the per-atom mass by NA and convert u→g:this scales a single atom up to a weighable, mole-sized amount.
3. molar mass of O atoms = 16 u × NA (in g) = 16 g mol⁻¹.Numerically identical to the atomic mass — only the unit changes from u to g mol⁻¹.
4. Contrast with O₂ gas: molar mass = 2 × 16 = 32 g mol⁻¹, because a molecule holds two atoms.
 species    atoms   atomic/molecular mass   molar mass
 O (atom)   1       16 u                    16 g mol⁻¹
 O₂ (gas)   2       32 u                    32 g mol⁻¹
⚡ JEE Trick Molar mass (g mol⁻¹) is numerically the same as the atomic mass (u) — never recompute, just swap the unit. Trap: "oxygen" in a question can mean the atom (O, 16 g mol⁻¹) or the gas (O₂, 32 g mol⁻¹); here it says "oxygen atoms," so the answer is 16 g mol⁻¹, not 32.
Key point: molar mass of oxygen atoms = mass of 1 mole of O atoms = 16 g mol⁻¹ (numerically equal to the atomic mass 16 u).
3Give one example of each: homogeneous mixture / heterogeneous mixture / element / compoundClassification of matterElements, compounds, homogeneous & heterogeneous mixtures🟡 Similar—
Concept All matter is first split into pure substances (fixed composition) and mixtures (variable composition). Pure substances divide into elements (one kind of atom) and compounds (two or more elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio). Mixtures divide into homogeneous (uniform throughout, single visible phase) and heterogeneous (non-uniform, distinct regions visible). Placing an example correctly means testing composition and uniformity, not appearance alone.
Key relation matter → pure substance (element | compound) OR mixture (homogeneous | heterogeneous). Element = one type of atom; compound = fixed ratio of bonded elements separable only chemically; homogeneous mixture = uniform, one phase; heterogeneous mixture = visibly different parts. Mixtures are separated by physical means, keeping component identities.
Solution
1. Homogeneous mixture — pick something uniform with variable composition: salt dissolved in water (brine), or air. One phase, no visible boundaries.Composition can change (more or less salt) yet it looks uniform, so it is a mixture, not a compound.
2. Heterogeneous mixture — pick something with visibly distinct parts: sand mixed with iron filings (or oil + water). Different regions are seen.You can point to separate components, so it is non-uniform.
3. Element — one kind of atom only: oxygen (O₂), iron (Fe), or gold (Au). Cannot be broken into simpler substances.
4. Compound — elements bonded in a fixed ratio: water (H₂O) or carbon dioxide (CO₂). Separable only by chemical reaction.
 category                 example              key test
 homogeneous mixture      salt in water        uniform, variable composition
 heterogeneous mixture    sand + iron filings  visibly distinct parts
 element                  oxygen (O₂)          one kind of atom
 compound                 water (H₂O)          fixed ratio, chemically bonded
⚡ JEE Trick Two quick tests settle every case: (1) fixed vs variable composition — fixed ⇒ pure substance, variable ⇒ mixture; (2) uniform vs not — for mixtures, uniform ⇒ homogeneous. Trap: an element can be a molecule (O₂, N₂) — "made of molecules" does not make it a compound; a compound needs two or more different elements.
Key point: homogeneous mixture — salt in water; heterogeneous mixture — sand + iron filings; element — oxygen (O₂); compound — water (H₂O).

Concept A single atom is far too light to weigh, but its mass follows directly from the mole link: one mole of H atoms (NA = 6.022×10²³ atoms) weighs the molar mass, 1.008 g. Formula mass of one atom = M ÷ NA: M = molar mass in g mol⁻¹, NA = 6.022×10²³ mol⁻¹. M grams contain NA identical atoms, so one atom carries M/NA grams. For hydrogen M = 1.008 g mol⁻¹ (≈ 1 for quick work).
Solution

2. Mass of one atom = M ÷ NA = 1.008 ÷ (6.022×10²³).
