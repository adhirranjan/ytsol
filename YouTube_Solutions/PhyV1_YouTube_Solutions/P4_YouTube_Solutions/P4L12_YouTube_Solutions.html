1In river boat problems, which direction is the river assumed to flow? (a) Along y-direction (b) Diagonal to the river bank (c) Along x-direction (d) Along negative x-directionRiver-boat setup conventionRiver Boat Problem || Relative Velocity in 2D || River Man Problem || Motion in a Plane || JEE NEET🟡 SimilarcAlong x-direction
Concept This is a convention question, not a calculation. Before any river-boat problem can be written down, we must fix axes. A river's banks are two parallel straight lines and the water moves along those banks — never across them. So the natural, universal choice is to lay the x-axis along the flow (parallel to the banks) and the y-axis across the river (perpendicular to the banks, along the width d). Every formula in this lesson is built on that choice.
Formula With the standard axes, the river velocity has no y-component: v⃗_r = v_r î + 0 ĵ (m s⁻¹). The boat's velocity relative to water, steered at angle θ from the y-axis (the normal to the bank), is v⃗_br = v_br sinθ î + v_br cosθ ĵ (m s⁻¹). This is exactly why the lesson's crossing time is t = d ÷ (v_br cosθ) — only the y-component (the cosθ one) carries the boat across the width d.
Solution
1. Note the physical fact: river water flows parallel to its banks, along the river's length.If it had a component across the banks the river would be flooding sideways, not flowing.
2. Set the axes to match that fact: x-axis along the banks / along the flow, y-axis across the river (along the width d).
3. The river's velocity therefore lies entirely on the x-axis: v⃗_r = v_r î, with v_ry = 0 m s⁻¹.This single simplification is what makes drift purely an x-quantity and crossing time purely a y-quantity.
4. So the river is assumed to flow along the x-direction — option (c).Not (a): y is across the river, the direction the boat must travel. Not (b): a diagonal flow would contradict the banks being parallel. Not (d): −x is merely the same axis with a sign flip, i.e. a flow chosen to run "backwards"; the standard convention takes +x.

2In aircraft problems, which condition must the net velocity vector satisfy? (a) Perpendicular to wind (b) Parallel to ground (c) Along the path AB (d) Opposite to windAircraft-wind problem setupRelative Velocity Problem - Plane and Wind🟡 SimilarcBe along the path AB
Concept An aeroplane's engines push it through the air, but the air itself is moving over the ground. The plane therefore has two different velocities at once: v⃗_aw (relative to the wind/air — the "airspeed", the direction the nose points) and v⃗_a (relative to the ground — the direction it actually travels). A pilot ordered to fly from A to B does not care where the nose points; he cares that the ground velocity ends up pointing straight at B. That is the one condition that defines every wind-aeroplane problem.
Formula v⃗_a = v⃗_aw + v⃗_w (km h⁻¹ or m s⁻¹) — ground velocity = airspeed vector + wind velocity vector. All three are velocities in the same units, so they add as a closed vector triangle. The "reach B directly" condition is v⃗_a ∥ AB: the resultant must lie along the path AB, and the pilot then solves the triangle backwards for the heading v⃗_aw.
Solution
1. Write the vector sum: the ground velocity is the airspeed vector plus the wind: v⃗_a = v⃗_aw + v⃗_w.The plane is carried bodily by the air mass, so the wind's velocity adds on top of the plane's motion through that air.
2. Ask what "reach B" means geometrically: the plane's actual displacement must run from A to B in a straight line.
3. Displacement is along the ground velocity, so v⃗_a must point from A to B, i.e. the net (resultant) velocity must lie along the path AB — option (c).If v⃗_a had any component perpendicular to AB, the plane would steadily slide off the track and miss B.
4. Reject the distractors: (a) the resultant perpendicular to the wind is an accident of geometry, not a requirement; (b) "parallel to ground" is true of every level flight and fixes nothing; (d) "opposite to wind" describes at most a pure headwind, one special case.It is the HEADING v⃗_aw that gets tilted into the wind — the resultant stays on AB. Students routinely swap these two.

Concept "Velocity of the boat with respect to the water" means: sit on the water and watch the boat. Mathematically that is a vector subtraction, v⃗_bw = v⃗_b − v⃗_w, where both v⃗_b and v⃗_w are measured in the same ground frame. Here the water is drifting with exactly the reverse of the boat's ground velocity, so subtracting it doubles the boat vector — the trap is to add the two (giving 0) or to keep only one component.
Formula v⃗_bw = v⃗_b − v⃗_w (m s⁻¹) relative velocity of A w.r.t. B is always v⃗_A − v⃗_B in a common frame. Data: v⃗_b = 3î + 4ĵ, v⃗_w = −3î − 4ĵ.

4A river 400 m wide flows at 2.0 m/s. A boat sails at 10 m/s relative to water, perpendicular to the river. (i) Find the time to reach the opposite bank. (ii) How far from the point directly opposite does the boat reach the bank?Perpendicular crossing — time & driftDC Pandey Physics Class 11 — river 400 m wide, 2.0 m/s, boat 10 m/s · HCV: river 400 m wide, 2.0 m/s, boat 10 m/s🟢 Exact(i) 40 sec (ii) 80 m
Concept The boat is steered straight across (perpendicular to the banks), so its whole 10 m/s relative-to-water speed goes into the width — the current cannot help or hinder the crossing. The two axes decouple: the y-motion (across) fixes the TIME, and the x-motion (along the bank) fixes the DRIFT. The trap is to combine 10 and 2 into a resultant speed before doing either part.
Formula t = d ÷ v_br (crossing time) and x = v_r · t (downstream drift). Because the heading is perpendicular, the across-component is the full v_br and the drift is the current acting for the whole crossing time. Data: d = 400 m, v_br = 10 m/s, v_r = 2.0 m/s.
Solution
1. Resolve the boat's velocity: across = 10 m/s (all of it), along-bank = 0 — it is aimed straight across.
2. (i) Time to reach the far bank depends only on the across-component: t = d ÷ v_br = 400 ÷ 10 = 40 s.The 2 m/s current is parallel to the bank, so it adds nothing to the y-direction and cannot change t.
3. (ii) During those 40 s the current sweeps the boat downstream at 2.0 m/s: x = v_r · t = 2.0 × 40 = 80 m.
4. Cross-check the landing: the boat's ground speed is √(10² + 2²) = √104 ≈ 10.2 m/s and its path length √(400² + 80²) ≈ 408 m, covered in 408 ÷ 10.2 ≈ 40 s — consistent.
        across (y): 10 m/s  -> t = 400/10 = 40 s
        along  (x):  2 m/s  -> x = 2 x 40 = 80 m
   -----------------------------------------------
   time = 40 s , drift = 80 m           <-- answers
⚡ JEE Trick For a perpendicular crossing, time uses only v_br and drift uses only v_r — never a Pythagorean resultant. Trap: candidates who compute the resultant speed √104 and divide 400 by it get the wrong time, because that resultant points along the slanted path, not across the width.

Concept The pilot ignored the wind, so his nose stayed locked due north the whole flight — that is the key simplification. His airspeed vector was therefore a known constant, and any discrepancy between where he meant to be and where he is must have been produced entirely by the wind carrying him. So this is a vector subtraction run in reverse: take the actual position, subtract the position the airspeed alone would have produced, and the leftover displacement divided by the time is the wind velocity.
