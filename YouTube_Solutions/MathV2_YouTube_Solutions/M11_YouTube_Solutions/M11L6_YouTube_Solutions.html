Concept An inequality sin x > k is read off the graph: mark where the sine curve crosses the horizontal line y = k and keep the arc of the curve that lies above the line. The trap is the strict sign: the end points themselves give equality and are excluded, but a + b only needs the two end values.

Concept Since cos²x = 1 − sin²x, the second power is 16/16sin²x. Putting t = 16sin²x turns the equation into a quadratic in t. Each root gives a value of sin²x, i.e. sin x = ±k, and each ±k with 0 < k < 1 gives four angles in [0, 2π]. The trap is forgetting the ± or one of the two t-roots.
Formula 16cos²x = 161 − sin²x = 16/t, with t = 16sin²x ∈ [1, 16] sin²x ∈ [0, 1], so both roots of the quadratic must lie in [1, 16].
Solution
1. t + 16/t = 10 ⇒ t² − 10t + 16 = 0 ⇒ (t − 2)(t − 8) = 0 ⇒ t = 2 or 8, both in [1, 16].
2. t = 2: 16sin²x = 2 = 161/4 ⇒ sin²x = 1/4 ⇒ sin x = ±1/2.2 = 2¹ and 16 = 2⁴, so the exponent is 1/4.
3. t = 8: 16sin²x = 8 = 163/4 ⇒ sin²x = 3/4 ⇒ sin x = ±√3/2.

5. sin x = ±√3/2 in [0, 2π]: π/3, 2π/3, 4π/3, 5π/3 ⇒ 4 roots.

Concept Write the sum of cosines as a product. Setting each factor to zero gives two families, θ = (2n + 1)π/(p + q) and θ = (2n + 1)π/(p − q). Each family is an A.P., and their common differences are 2π/(p + q) and 2π/(p − q), compactly 2π/(p ± q). The trap is reading the coefficient π/(p ± q) as the step.
Formula cos C + cos D = 2 cos((C + D)/2) cos((C − D)/2), cos t = 0 ⇒ t = (2n + 1)π/2 Consecutive odd integers differ by 2, so the step is twice the coefficient.
Solution
