L-1

Sub-atomic Particles & Atomic Models

  • Electron — discovered by J.J. Thomson from cathode rays (gas at ≈10⁻⁴ atm, ≈10,000 V). Cathode rays: travel straight, cast shadows, rotate a mica wheel (material particles), deflect toward the + plate (negative charge). Universal to all atoms.
  • Proton — discovered by Goldstein from anode / canal rays (positive rays). Smallest charge +1 unit, mass ≈ H-atom.
  • Neutron — discovered by Chadwick bombarding Be/B with α-rays. No charge, mass ≈ proton.

Atomic models

  • Thomson (1904): sphere of positive charge with embedded electrons — the raisin pudding / watermelon model (radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m). Could not explain scattering.
  • Rutherford's α-scattering (gold foil): most α pass undeflected (empty space); few deflected (near the positive body); ≈1 in 20,000 bounce back (heavy, tiny nucleus).
Drawbacks of Rutherford's model
By Maxwell's theory an accelerating charged electron must continuously radiate energy, spiral in and collapse into the nucleus — so the model cannot explain the stability of the atom. It also fails to explain the arrangement of electrons and the discontinuous (line) atomic spectrum.

Nature of Light · Spectra · Planck's Theory

  • EM radiation = oscillating electric & magnetic fields, mutually ⊥ and ⊥ to propagation; needs no medium; all travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m·s⁻¹ in vacuum.
wave relationsc = ν·λ · ν̄ = 1/λ · ν = c·ν̄ · T = 1/ν
units1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m ; 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m ; 1 pm = 10⁻¹² m ; wave number ν̄ in cm⁻¹
💡 EM spectrum orderIncreasing λ: Cosmic < γ-rays < X-rays < UV < Visible < Infrared < Microwaves < Radio. Visible = VIBGYOR (violet 400 nm → red 700 nm).

Atomic spectrum

  • Emission spectrum = bright lines on a dark background (excited atoms). Absorption spectrum = dark lines in an otherwise continuous spectrum.
  • Atomic spectra are discontinuous (line) spectra — the "finger-prints" of atoms (e.g. Na always gives lines at 589 & 589.6 nm).

Planck's quantum theory

quantum of energyE = hν = hc/λ · E = nhν (n = integer)
  • Energy is emitted / absorbed in discrete packets called quanta (of light = photon). E ∝ ν.
  • 1 Einstein = energy of 1 mole of photons = (hν) × NA.

L-3

Particle Nature of Light · Photoelectric Effect

  • Ejection of electrons from a metal surface when illuminated by light of suitable frequency (Hertz/Lenard). Ejected electrons = photoelectrons.
  • Number of photoelectrons ∝ intensity; their max K.E. depends only on frequency, not intensity. No time lag. Evidence of the particle nature of light.
Einstein photoelectric eqnhν = W₀ + ½mv² ⇒ ½mv² = h(ν − ν₀) · W₀ = hν₀
stopping potential½mv² = eV₀
  • ν₀ = threshold frequency (K.E. = 0 just at ejection); W₀ = hν₀ = work function (depends on the metal).
  • A K.E.–ν graph is a straight line of slope h (Planck's constant), intercept on ν-axis = ν₀.
💡 TipOnly frequencies with ν > ν₀ eject electrons — a bright beam below ν₀ ejects none.

Book Example · L-3
Radiation of wavelength 242 nm is just sufficient to ionize the sodium atom. Calculate the ionization energy of sodium in kJ·mol⁻¹.
W₀ = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸)/(242 × 10⁻⁹) = 8.214 × 10⁻¹⁹ J per atom.
Per mole: E = 8.214 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.022 × 10²³ = 494.647 × 10³ J.
≈ 494.65 kJ·mol⁻¹
Book Example · L-3
Calculate the minimum energy needed to eject electrons from cesium (threshold frequency 4.6 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹).
W₀ = hν₀ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 4.6 × 10¹⁴ = 30.498 × 10⁻²⁰ J.
= 3.05 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
Book Example · L-3
Light of wavelength 5000 Å falls on a metal of work function 1.9 eV. Find (a) energy of the photons, (b) K.E. of the photoelectrons.
E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸)/(5000 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 3.978 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 2.486 eV.
K.E. = E − W₀ = 2.486 − 1.9.
(a) 2.486 eV (b) 0.586 eV
L-4

Bohr's Atomic Model · Radius of Orbit

  • Electrons revolve in fixed circular stationary orbits; force of attraction = centrifugal force; no energy radiated while in an orbit.
  • Angular momentum is quantized. Energy is absorbed (inner → outer) or emitted (outer → inner) only on a jump.
quantizationmvr = n·h/2π · ΔE = Ehigh − Elow = hν
Deriving the radius
Equate Coulomb attraction to centrifugal force: KZe²/r² = mv²/r ⇒ v² = KZe²/mr. Put v = nh/2πmr into it: r = n²h²/(4π²mKZe²). Substituting h, π, m, e, K gives r = 0.529 × n²/Z Å.
Bohr radiusrₙ = 0.529 × n²/Z Å · for H: rₙ = r₁·n² with r₁ = 0.529 Å
★ Scalingr ∝ n² (grows with orbit) and r ∝ 1/Z (shrinks with nuclear charge).

rₙ = 0.529 × n²/Z; for He⁺, Z = 2, n = 2 ⇒ r = 0.529 × 4/2.
= 1.058 Å

Calculate the radius of Bohr's 3rd orbit in the Li²⁺ ion.
rₙ = 0.529 × n²/Z; n = 3, Z = 3 ⇒ r₃ = 0.529 × 9/3 = 3 × 0.529.
= 1.587 Å
Book Example · L-4
Calculate the ratio of the radii of the fifth orbits of He⁺ and Li²⁺.

L-5

Energy of an Electron

total energyE = −KZe²/2r = −2π²mK²Z²e⁴/n²h²
working formsEₙ = −13.6 Z²/n² eV = −21.79 × 10⁻¹⁹ Z²/n² J = −1312 Z²/n² kJ·mol⁻¹
hydrogen-likeEₙ = E₁ × Z²/n² (E₁ = energy of H first orbit)
K.E., P.E. & T.E. relations
K.E. = KZe²/2r, P.E. = −KZe²/r, T.E. = −KZe²/2r. Hence P.E. = 2·T.E., K.E. = −T.E., P.E. = −2·K.E. The negative total energy means the electron is bound; energy → 0 as n → ∞ (electron free).
★ DefinitionsIonization energy (from ground state), separation energy (from an excited state) and excitation energy are all positive.

Velocity, Orbital Frequency & Time Period

velocityv = 2πKZe²/nh = 2.18 × 10⁶ (Z/n) m·s⁻¹ ⇒ v ∝ Z/n
time period & frequencyT = 2πr/v · f = v/2πr ∝ Z²/n³
★ Proportionalitiesv ∝ Z/n (falls with n) · r ∝ n²/Z · f ∝ Z²/n³ (falls sharply with n). K.E. ∝ Z²/n².

Hydrogen Spectrum & Rydberg Formula

Rydbergν̄ = 1/λ = R·Z²·(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²) · R = 109677 cm⁻¹
Seriesn₁n₂Region
Lyman12,3,4…Ultraviolet
Balmer23,4,5…Visible
Paschen34,5,6…Infrared
Brackett45,6,7…Infrared
Pfund56,7,8…Infrared
Humphrey67,8,9…Far IR
  • Series limit (last line): n₂ = ∞ ⇒ λmin, νmax, highest energy. First line: n₂ = n₁+1 ⇒ λmax, lowest energy.
counting lineslines (n₂ → n₁) = (n₂ − n₁)(n₂ − n₁ + 1)/2 · (any n → 1) = n(n − 1)/2
Bohr's drawbacks
Bohr fails for multi-electron atoms; does not justify why angular momentum = nh/2π; mixes quantum & classical ideas; cannot explain the fine structure, nor line splitting in a magnetic field (Zeeman effect) or electric field (Stark effect).

L-8

Dual Nature (de-Broglie) & Uncertainty Principle

de-Broglieλ = h/mv = h/p · λ = h/√(2Ekm)
  • Every moving particle has an associated matter wave. Momentum (particle) ∝ 1/wavelength (wave).
  • Derived by equating E = hc/λ (Planck) with E = mc² (Einstein) ⇒ λ = h/mc, generalized to any particle.
HeisenbergΔx·Δp ≥ h/4π · Δx·mΔv ≥ h/4π · ΔE·Δt ≥ h/4π
⚠ TrapIt is impossible to know exact position AND momentum simultaneously. Significant only for tiny particles (electrons); negligible for macroscopic bodies.

Book Example · L-8

Book Example · L-8
Particles A and B are in motion. The momentum of B is half that of A, and λA = 5 × 10⁻⁸ m. Find λB.
λ = h/p ⇒ λB/λA = pA/pB = 2 ⇒ λB = 2λA = 2 × 5 × 10⁻⁸.

L-10

Rules for Electronic Configuration

  • Aufbau principle — subshells fill in increasing energy: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p, 7s.
  • (n+ℓ) rule — lower (n+ℓ) fills first; if equal, the one with lower n fills first.
  • Pauli exclusion — an orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with opposite spins.
  • Hund's rule — orbitals of a subshell fill singly (parallel spins) before pairing — maximum multiplicity.
Why Cr and Cu are exceptions
Exactly half-filled and completely-filled subshells are extra stable (symmetry + exchange energy). So Cr (Z=24) is [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ (not 3d⁴4s²) and Cu (Z=29) is [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ (not 3d⁹4s²) — one 4s electron shifts to 3d.
★ Exchange energyMaximum number of exchanges (and hence stability) occurs for exactly half-filled or fully-filled degenerate orbitals; e.g. d⁵ allows 4+3+2+1 = 10 exchanges.

L-11

Configurations of Elements & Orbitals

  • First transition series Sc(21)→Zn(30) fill 3d after 4s; Cr = [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ and Cu = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ (stability exceptions).
  • Orbital wave function ψ = ψ(r)·ψ(θ,φ) — radial part (size) × angular part (shape). ψ² = probability density.
nodesangular nodes = ℓ · radial nodes = (n − ℓ − 1) · total nodes = (n − 1)
radial probability4πr²ψ²·dr (probability in a shell of radius r → r+dr)
Reading the plots
For 1s, ψ decreases smoothly with r (no node); for 2s, ψ crosses zero (a node) then goes negative. The radial-probability curve 4πr²ψ² is zero at the nucleus, peaks at rmax (= a₀ = 0.529 Å for 1s of H), and shows (n−ℓ−1) minima. s-orbitals are spherical; p are dumb-bells (with a nodal plane); d are four-lobed.
