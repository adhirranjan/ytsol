Concept A general second-degree equation represents two parallel lines only when h2=ab. In that case the two lines share the common direction √a x+√b y, differing just in their constant terms, so the gap between them is a fixed perpendicular distance expressible straight from the coefficients a, g, c.
Formula ax2+2hxy+by2+2gx+2fy+c=0 splits as (√a x+√b y+c1)(√a x+√b y+c2)=0. Matching x-terms gives c1+c2=2g/√a and the constant gives c1c2=c, so (c1−c2)2 is known.
Solution
1. With h2=ab write the pair as (√a x+√b y+c1)(√a x+√b y+c2)=0.
2. Compare x-coefficients: √a(c1+c2)=2g, so c1+c2=2g/√a.
3. Compare constant: c1c2=c. These two symmetric functions fix the difference of the intercepts.
4. Distance between parallel lines √a x+√b y+c1=0 and √a x+√b y+c2=0 is |c1−c2|/√(a+b).
5. (c1−c2)2=(c1+c2)2−4c1c2=4g2/a−4c, so D=√(4g2/a−4c)/√(a+b).
6. Simplify: D=2√((g2−ac)/(a2+ab)). The same working with f, b gives the identical value, since g2b=f2a for a genuine parallel pair.
  h^2 = ab  ->  (Va x + Vb y + c1)(Va x + Vb y + c2) = 0
  c1 + c2 = 2g/Va ,  c1 c2 = c
  (c1 - c2)^2 = 4g^2/a - 4c
  D = |c1 - c2| / V(a+b)
  D = 2 V( (g^2 - ac) / (a^2 + ab) )
⚡ JEE Trick First confirm h2=ab (else the lines are not parallel and the formula is meaningless), then plug straight into D=2√((g2−ac)/(a2+ab)). No factorising needed in the exam — read a, g, c off the equation and substitute.
Answer: D = 2√((g2−ac)/(a2+ab)).

Concept The angle bisectors of a pair of lines through the origin are themselves a pair of lines through the origin, lying symmetrically between the two given lines and perpendicular to each other. Their combined equation comes directly from the coefficients a, h, b of the given homogeneous pair.
Formula pair ax2+2hxy+by2=0 ⇒ bisectors (x2−y2)/(a−b)=xy/h. The bisectors make equal angles with both lines; equating the two angle-tangents yields this single symmetric relation.
Solution
1. Let the pair be ax2+2hxy+by2=0 with lines y=m1x, y=m2x.
2. m1+m2=−2h/b and m1m2=a/b from the coefficients.
3. A bisector is the locus of points equidistant (in angle) from both lines; this gives the standard result below. The derivation equates the two tangents of the angles the bisector makes with each line.
4. The combined equation of the two bisectors is (x2−y2)/(a−b)=xy/h.
5. This is a homogeneous equation of second degree, so it represents two lines through the origin.
6. Coefficient of x2 plus coefficient of y2 in h(x2−y2)−(a−b)xy=0 is h+(−h)=0, confirming the bisectors are perpendicular. Angle bisectors of any two lines are always mutually perpendicular.
  pair:  a x^2 + 2h xy + b y^2 = 0
  bisectors:  (x^2 - y^2)/(a - b) = xy / h
  i.e.  h(x^2 - y^2) - (a - b) xy = 0
  coeff x^2 + coeff y^2 = h + (-h) = 0  -> perpendicular

Answer: (x2−y2)/(a−b) = xy/h.

Solution
1. Let (α,β) be the point of intersection of the two lines of the pair.
2. Shift the origin to (α,β): set X=x−α, Y=y−β.
3. The pair reduces to the homogeneous form aX2+2hXY+bY2=0. The linear and constant terms vanish because (α,β) is the common point.
4. Its bisectors are (X2−Y2)/(a−b)=XY/h.
5. Replace X by x−α and Y by y−β.
6. Result: ((x−α)2−(y−β)2)/(a−b)=(x−α)(y−β)/h. The bisectors pass through (α,β), the common point, as bisectors always do.

  pair -> a X^2 + 2h XY + b Y^2 = 0
  bisectors: (X^2 - Y^2)/(a - b) = XY / h
  back-substitute X, Y:
  ((x-a)^2 - (y-b)^2)/(a - b) = (x-a)(y-b)/h
⚡ JEE Trick The second-degree coefficients a, h, b control direction, the linear terms only locate the vertex. So any formula about angles (bisectors, angle between lines) uses only a, h, b — shift to the vertex and reuse the homogeneous result.
Answer: ((x−α)2−(y−β)2)/(a−b) = (x−α)(y−β)/h, with (α,β) the point of intersection.

10Area of the triangle formed by the line x+y=3 and the angle bisectors of the pairs of straight lines x2−y2+2y=1 is: (a) 2 sq. units (b) 4 sq. units (c) 6 sq. units (d) 8 sq. units

Angle bisectors of a shifted pair; triangle areaArea of triangle formed by line x+y=3 angle bisectors x^2-y^2+2y=1🟢 Exacta2 sq. units
Concept Identical setup to Level-6 Q4: the pair x2−y2+2y=1 factors into two perpendicular lines, their bisectors are a horizontal and a vertical line, and the triangle cut by x+y=3 is a right triangle whose area is required as an option.
Formula x2−y2+2y−1=x2−(y−1)2; area=(1/2)×base×height. Perpendicular lines of slopes +1, −1 have axis-parallel bisectors through their meeting point.
Solution
1. x2−y2+2y−1=x2−(y−1)2=0 ⇒ (x−y+1)(x+y−1)=0.
2. Lines x−y+1=0 and x+y−1=0 meet at (0, 1), with perpendicular slopes +1 and −1.
3. Their bisectors are the axis-parallel lines through (0, 1): x=0 and y=1. Bisectors of two perpendicular lines run horizontally and vertically through the vertex.
4. The triangle is bounded by x=0, y=1 and x+y=3; vertices (0, 1), (0, 3), (2, 1).
5. Legs along x=0 and y=1 are each 2 units long.
6. Area=(1/2)·2·2=2 sq. units — option (a). The same configuration as Q4, so the area is again 2.
7. The right angle at (0, 1) makes this a clean leg×leg computation: both legs run from (0, 1) — one up to (0, 3), one across to (2, 1) — each of length 2, so no determinant is needed. Perpendicular bisectors guarantee a right-angled triangle, the easiest area case.
  x^2 - (y-1)^2 = 0 -> (x-y+1)(x+y-1)=0
  meet (0,1) ; bisectors x=0 , y=1
  with x+y=3 -> (0,1),(0,3),(2,1)
  area = (1/2)(2)(2) = 2
⚡ JEE Trick Recognise a repeat: this is Level-6 Q4 restated as an MCQ. The pair x2−(y−1)2=0 always yields bisectors x=0, y=1 and a right triangle of area 2 with x+y=3.
Answer: (a) 2 sq. units.
