1Which statement correctly describes electromagnetic radiation?EM radiation natureA-EMRAD🟡 SimilarbElectric and magnetic fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.
Concept Electromagnetic radiation is not a mechanical vibration of matter but a self-propagating disturbance of two coupled fields — an electric field and a magnetic field. Maxwell showed that a changing electric field generates a magnetic field and vice-versa, so the two regenerate each other and travel together through empty space at the speed of light, needing no medium. The defining geometry is that both fields, and the direction of travel, are mutually perpendicular.
Key idea The E field and the B field oscillate in phase, each at right angles to the other, and the wave advances along the third perpendicular axis (a transverse wave). This right-handed E ⊥ B ⊥ propagation arrangement is exactly what "electromagnetic" means, and it is why light needs no medium — unlike sound.
Solution
1. Recall the two ingredients of EM radiation: oscillating electric and magnetic fields, coupled by Maxwell's laws.
2. Fix their geometry: E is perpendicular to B, and both are perpendicular to the direction the wave moves — a transverse wave.A longitudinal (compression) picture would describe sound, which does need a medium — not light.
3. Test the options against this geometry: only (b) states both fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to propagation.
4. Eliminate the rest: options implying a required medium, parallel fields, or a longitudinal wave all contradict Maxwell's picture.
        E (electric field)
        ↑
        |
        |———→  direction of propagation
       /
      B (magnetic field, into/out of page)
   E ⊥ B ⊥ propagation, in phase, speed = c
⚡ JEE Trick Three key facts settle almost every "nature of EM radiation" MCQ: (i) it is transverse, (ii) E ⊥ B ⊥ direction of travel, (iii) it needs no medium and moves at c in vacuum. Trap: any option that says light requires a medium, or that E and B are parallel, is automatically wrong.
Answer: (b) Electric and magnetic fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.
2Calculate the frequency of light of wavelength 600 nmFrequency (c = νλ)watch?v=GaO4iVWN-w4🟢 Exactc5.0×10¹⁴ s⁻¹
Concept Light is an electromagnetic wave, and every wave obeys one master relation: speed = frequency × wavelength. For light in vacuum the speed is fixed at c = 3×10⁸ m s⁻¹, so once the wavelength is known the frequency is forced — the two are inversely locked together. The only real work in such problems is the unit conversion: wavelength is quoted in nanometres but the formula demands metres, and forgetting the 10⁻⁹ factor is the classic slip. A useful extra fact: when light passes into glass or water its speed and wavelength drop but its frequency stays fixed — frequency is the invariant fingerprint of a radiation, which is why examiners so often ask for ν rather than λ.
Formula ν = c ÷ λ rearranged from c = νλ, valid for all EM waves in vacuum. Data: c = 3×10⁸ m s⁻¹, λ = 600 nm, and 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m.
Solution
1. Convert the wavelength to SI: λ = 600 nm = 600 × 10⁻⁹ m = 6.0×10⁻⁷ m.c is in m s⁻¹, so λ must be in metres for ν to come out in s⁻¹.
2. Apply the wave relation: ν = c/λ = (3×10⁸ m s⁻¹) ÷ (6.0×10⁻⁷ m).
3. Divide the mantissas: 3 ÷ 6.0 = 0.5; subtract the exponents: 10⁸ ÷ 10⁻⁷ = 10⁸⁻⁽⁻⁷⁾ = 10¹⁵.
4. Combine: ν = 0.5×10¹⁵ = 5.0×10¹⁴ s⁻¹.Standard form keeps one non-zero digit before the decimal point.
5. Sanity check: 600 nm is orange-red visible light, and visible frequencies run ≈ 4–7.5×10¹⁴ Hz — 5.0×10¹⁴ sits comfortably inside, so the magnitude is right.

3Light of wavelength 480 nm absorbed by an atom — energy of each photonPhoton energywatch?v=QNvaDPcQZyE🟡 Similarb4.14×10⁻¹⁹ J
Concept Light delivers its energy in indivisible packets called photons. The energy carried by a single photon is fixed entirely by its frequency (or, equivalently, its wavelength) — brighter light means more photons, not more energetic ones. To find the energy of one photon from its wavelength you combine Planck's relation E = hν with the wave relation c = νλ, which merges into the single working formula E = hc/λ.
Formula E = hc ÷ λ: photon energy = (Planck constant h × speed of light c) ÷ wavelength λ. Since ν = c/λ, substituting into E = hν eliminates frequency and leaves wavelength directly. Use h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3×10⁸ m s⁻¹ (so hc = 1.9878×10⁻²⁵ J m), and λ in metres.
Solution
1. Convert the wavelength to metres: λ = 480 nm = 480 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.80×10⁻⁷ m.SI formula needs SI units; 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m.
2. Form the product hc = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸ = 1.9878×10⁻²⁵ J m.6.626 × 3 = 19.878, and 10⁻³⁴ × 10⁸ = 10⁻²⁶, giving 19.878×10⁻²⁶ = 1.9878×10⁻²⁵.
3. Divide by λ: E = 1.9878×10⁻²⁵ ÷ 4.80×10⁻⁷.
  • mantissa: 1.9878 ÷ 4.80 = 0.41413
  • powers of ten: 10⁻²⁵ ÷ 10⁻⁷ = 10⁻¹⁸
4. Combine: E = 0.41413×10⁻¹⁸ = 4.14×10⁻¹⁹ J.

 hc         1.9878×10⁻²⁵ J m

⚡ JEE Trick Memorise hc = 1.9878×10⁻²⁵ J m ≈ 1240 eV·nm — then E(in eV) = 1240 ÷ λ(nm) gives 1240/480 ≈ 2.58 eV, and 2.58 × 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 4.14×10⁻¹⁹ J on sight. Trap: forgetting the nm→m conversion shifts the answer by 10⁹.

4Why the H emission spectrum shows discrete lines, not a continuous spread — best explanationAtomic (line) spectrumA-ATOMSPEC🟡 SimilarbElectrons in atoms can occupy only discrete energy levels, giving fixed energy transitions.
Concept A hot gas of hydrogen atoms emits light only at a few sharp wavelengths (a "line" spectrum), not the smooth rainbow a hot solid gives. The reason is quantum: an electron in an atom cannot have just any energy — it is restricted to a set of discrete energy levels. Light is emitted when an electron falls from a higher level to a lower one, and since only fixed level-to-level gaps are allowed, only photons of fixed energies (hence fixed wavelengths) appear.
Key idea ΔE = Ehigh − Elow = hν. Because the levels E are quantized, the differences ΔE take only certain values; each allowed ΔE gives one spectral line at frequency ν = ΔE/h. A continuous range of energies would be needed to give a continuous spectrum.
Solution
1. Note the observation: hydrogen's emission is a set of separate coloured lines, with dark gaps between them.
2. Ask what could produce only certain photon energies: only a set of fixed energy differences ΔE can, since Ephoton = ΔE.
3. Fixed ΔE values require the electron's own energies to be quantized (discrete levels) — exactly option (b).
4. Eliminate distractors: if electrons could take any energy, the gaps ΔE would form a continuum and the spectrum would be continuous, not lined.

 discrete levels ✓     only fixed gaps      sharp lines  ← observed
⚡ JEE Trick "Line spectrum ⇒ quantized energy levels" is a one-way tell in every atomic-structure MCQ. Continuous spectrum ⇒ hot solid / all energies; line spectrum ⇒ isolated atoms with discrete levels. Trap: options blaming "collisions" or "impurities" for the lines miss the real cause — the quantization of the atom's own energy.
Answer: (b) Electrons in atoms can occupy only discrete energy levels, giving fixed energy transitions.

The question wants the tiny energy gap between the two photons. Solution

1What are isotones? / Define the term isotonesIsotones (definition)A-ISOTONE🟡 Similar—
Concept Atoms are labelled by two counts: the number of protons Z (atomic number) and the number of neutrons N; their sum A = Z + N is the mass number. Chemists group nuclei by which of these counts they share. Isotones are the family defined by a shared neutron number — atoms of different elements (different Z) that nonetheless contain the same number of neutrons N. They sit on the same "neutron column" of the chart of nuclides.
Key relation isotones ⇒ same N = A − Z, different Z. Because Z differs they are chemically different elements; because N is identical the label is "iso-tone" (same neutrons), by analogy with iso-tope (same protons) and iso-bar (same A).
Solution
1. Write each atom's neutron count as N = A − Z (mass number minus atomic number).
2. Isotones are atoms for which this N is the same, even though Z (and hence the element) differs.
3. Example: ¹⁴₆C has N = 14 − 6 = 8; ¹⁶₈O has N = 16 − 8 = 8 — same neutrons, different elements ⇒ isotones.

2Why was it necessary to account for the wave–particle duality of EM radiation?Dual nature of radiationA-DUAL🟡 Similar—
Concept No single model of light explains everything it does. Some experiments only make sense if light is a wave; others only make sense if light is a stream of particles. Since both sets of experiments are real, physics had to accept that EM radiation carries both characters — this is the "dual nature." Which face you see depends on the experiment you run, not on the light changing.
Key relation wave side: c = νλ, interference/diffraction ; particle side: E = hν (photons). The wave picture ties frequency to wavelength and explains spreading and overlap; the photon picture ties energy to frequency in discrete packets and explains threshold effects like the photoelectric effect.
Solution
1. List phenomena needing the wave model: interference, diffraction, polarisation — light bends around edges and overlaps to give bright/dark fringes, which particles cannot do.
2. List phenomena needing the particle model: the photoelectric effect and black-body radiation — energy arrives in fixed quanta E = hν, with a sharp frequency threshold a wave cannot explain.
3. Because both classes of experiment are valid, neither model alone suffices; a complete description must include both.
4. Hence duality is a necessity forced by experiment, not a convenience — light behaves as wave or particle depending on how it is probed.
 behaviour            model required   examples
 spreading/overlap    wave            interference, diffraction
 discrete energy      particle        photoelectric effect, E = hν
 ⇒ need BOTH ⇒ wave–particle duality
⚡ JEE Trick Sort the evidence: diffraction/interference ⇒ wave; photoelectric effect / black-body / E = hν ⇒ particle. Any exam question asking "why duality" is really asking you to name one phenomenon from each column. Trap: don't say light is "sometimes a wave and sometimes a particle" as if it switches — it always has both aspects; the experiment selects which one shows.
Key point: some experiments (interference, diffraction) reveal light's wave nature while others (photoelectric effect, E = hν) reveal its particle nature, so a complete description of EM radiation must include both — hence its dual character.
3Explain λ, frequency, wave number, amplitude & velocity of EM radiationWave characteristicsA-WAVECHAR🟡 Similar—
Concept A travelling wave is described by a small set of measurable quantities. Four of them — wavelength, frequency, wave number and velocity — describe the wave's timing and spacing, while amplitude describes its strength. Knowing what each means and how they interlock (through c = νλ and ν̄ = 1/λ) lets you convert between any of them, which is the backbone of every EM-radiation numerical.
Key relations c = νλ and ν̄ = 1/λ. The first says velocity = frequency × wavelength; the second defines wave number as waves per unit length. Together they connect all four timing/spacing quantities.
Solution
1. Wavelength (λ) — the distance between two consecutive crests (or troughs); SI unit metre. It sets the spatial size of one full wave.
2. Frequency (ν) — the number of complete waves passing a point per second; unit hertz (Hz = s⁻¹). It sets how fast the wave oscillates in time.
3. Wave number (ν̄) — the number of waves per unit length, ν̄ = 1/λ; unit m⁻¹ (often cm⁻¹). It is the reciprocal of wavelength.
4. Velocity (c) — the distance the wave front travels per second, c = νλ; in vacuum all EM radiation moves at c = 3×10⁸ m s⁻¹.Because c is fixed, ν and λ are inversely linked: raise one and the other falls.
5. Amplitude (A) — the maximum displacement of the field from its mean; it fixes the wave's intensity/brightness (∝ A²) but not its colour or energy per photon.
 quantity     symbol   meaning                    unit
 wavelength   λ        crest-to-crest distance    m
 frequency    ν        cycles per second          Hz (s⁻¹)
 wave number  ν̄        1/λ, waves per metre        m⁻¹
 velocity     c        νλ, speed of the wave       m s⁻¹
 amplitude    A        peak displacement (∝ √I)    field units
⚡ JEE Trick Keep the two links at your fingertips: c = νλ ties speed–frequency–wavelength, and ν̄ = 1/λ ties wave number to wavelength — every L-2 numerical is one of these rearranged. Trap: amplitude changes brightness, never frequency or photon energy — don't let an option pair "bigger amplitude" with "more energetic photons."
Key point: λ = crest-to-crest distance (m), ν = cycles per second (Hz), ν̄ = 1/λ = waves per metre (m⁻¹), velocity c = νλ (3×10⁸ m s⁻¹ in vacuum), and amplitude = peak displacement, which fixes intensity (∝ A²) but not frequency.
4How are wavelength, frequency and velocity of light interrelated?c = νλ relationA-FREQ🟡 Similar—
Concept For any wave, the speed at which it travels is set by how many waves pass each second and how long each wave is. Multiply "waves per second" (frequency) by "length of one wave" (wavelength) and you get "length travelled per second" (velocity). For light in vacuum this velocity is a fixed constant, c, so frequency and wavelength are locked in an inverse relationship.
Key relation c = ν × λ, where c = speed of light (3×10⁸ m s⁻¹ in vacuum), ν = frequency (Hz), λ = wavelength (m). Rearranged: ν = c/λ and λ = c/ν — knowing any two fixes the third.
Solution
1. Frequency ν = number of waves passing a point each second (unit s⁻¹).
2. Wavelength λ = length of one complete wave (unit m).
3. Distance covered per second = (waves per second) × (length per wave) = ν × λ — and that distance per second is the velocity, so c = νλ.Units check: s⁻¹ × m = m s⁻¹, a speed. ✓
4. Because c is constant for light, ν ∝ 1/λ: a shorter wavelength means a higher frequency, and vice-versa.
 rearrangement   solves for   used when you know
 c = νλ          velocity     ν and λ
 ν = c/λ         frequency    λ (and c)
 λ = c/ν         wavelength   ν (and c)
⚡ JEE Trick Commit c = νλ and both rearrangements — three of the four Board/Level items on light are just this equation solved for a different letter. Trap: mixing units — convert nm and Å to metres before dividing, and remember c is only 3×10⁸ m s⁻¹ in vacuum (slower in a medium).
Key point: velocity, frequency and wavelength obey c = νλ; since c is constant for light in vacuum, frequency and wavelength are inversely related (ν = c/λ, λ = c/ν).

4Who proposed the concept of energy quantization?Planck's theoryA-PLANCK🟡 SimilarcPlanck

3. Therefore the proposer is Planck, option (c).

5What is the relationship for energy of a quantum? (E = hν)Quantum energyA-PLANCK🟡 SimilarbE = hν
Concept Planck's central result states exactly how much energy one quantum of radiation carries.
