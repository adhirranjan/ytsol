1 (i)Fill in the blank: The equilibrium constant Kc is a measure of the ______ of a reaction at equilibrium.What K measuresEqulibrium | Chemical Equilibrium 02 | Equilibrium Constant Kp and Kc | IIT JEE MAINS /NEET🟡 Similarextent
Concept K is a products-over-reactants ratio at equilibrium, so its size is a direct readout of how far the reaction has gone before stopping. That is what the word extent names.
Key relation K >> 1 ⇒ products dominate; K << 1 ⇒ reactants dominate K says nothing about speed — a huge K with a huge activation energy still gives a reaction that takes years.
Solution
1. From the previous lecture: Kc = [C]c[D]d/([A]a[B]b), with products in the numerator.
2. A large K therefore means the product concentrations dominate at equilibrium: the reaction has gone nearly to completion.
3. A small K means the reverse: very little product forms before the rates equalise.
4. So the single number K measures how far the reaction proceeds — its extent. Column-II entry (t) of the L-2 Quick Recap says the same thing.
5. Do not write “rate” or “speed”. Those are kinetic quantities governed by the activation energy, and K contains no information about them.
6. Nor “yield” on its own: the yield also depends on how much you started with and on the volume, whereas K is fixed at a given temperature.
7. Contrast the two neighbouring blanks: (i) asks what K measures (extent), while (iii) asks which symbol is used for the concentration form (Kc). Both appear in the same Quick Recap, and answering one in place of the other is the easiest mark to lose here.
 K >> 1 : nearly complete
 K ~ 1  : comparable amounts
 K << 1 : hardly proceeds

 K measures HOW FAR, never HOW FAST
⚡ JEE Trick Two questions look alike but have different answers: “what does K measure?” → extent; “what does K depend on?” → temperature. Read which one is being asked before filling the blank.
Answer: Kc is a measure of the extent of a reaction at equilibrium.

Concept A catalyst lowers the activation energy of the forward and reverse steps by the same amount, so it multiplies kf and kb by the same factor and leaves their ratio — which is K — unaffected.
Key relation Keq(catalysed) = (kf×x)/(kb×x) = kf/kb = Keq(uncatalysed) This is the algebra printed on book p.28, characteristic (iv), with x the common speed-up factor.
Solution
1. Characteristic (iv) of the equilibrium constant on p.28: “Value of equilibrium constant is not affected by catalyst. Catalyst simply helps in attaining equilibrium earlier.”
2. The book then makes it explicit: the relative increase in the rate of the forward as well as the backward reaction remains the same on using the catalyst.
3. Write it out: uncatalysed K = kf/kb; catalysed K = (kfx)/(kbx), and the x cancels.
4. Structurally, forward and reverse reactions share one transition state, so lowering its energy helps both directions equally.
5. What does change is the time: the graph on p.28 marks t′eq (catalysed) to the left of teq (uncatalysed), with both curves flattening at the same height.
6. So the blank is unaffected.
 uncatalysed : K = kf/kb ,  reaches eqm at t(eq)
 catalysed   : K = (kf x)/(kb x) = kf/kb
               reaches eqm at t'(eq) < t(eq)

 same K, same plateau height, earlier arrival
⚡ JEE Trick The catalyst graph on p.28 is worth memorising as a picture: two pairs of curves converging to the same horizontal line at two different times. It answers every catalyst question in this chapter at a glance.

1 (iii)Fill in the blank: ______ is the equilibrium constant expressed in terms of the concentrations of the reactants and products.KcRelation Between Kc and Kp - Chemical Equilibrium - Chemistry Class 11🟡 SimilarKc
Concept The subscript names the quantity substituted. Concentrations → Kc; partial pressures → Kp. The stem specifies concentrations, so the blank is Kc.
Key relation Kc = [C]c[D]d/([A]a[B]b); Kp = Kc(RT)Δng The two are the same equilibrium measured two ways, which is exactly what the first section of this lecture derives.
Solution
1. The stem says “expressed in terms of the concentrations of the reactants and products”.
2. Square brackets denote molar concentrations, and the constant built from them is written Kc.
3. So the blank is Kc.
4. Scope: Kc is defined for gas-phase and solution-phase equilibria, because both have concentrations. Kp exists only for gases.
5. Units: Kc = MΔn, with Δn = (moles of product) − (moles of reactant) in the balanced equation.
6. The bridge derived on p.27 of this lecture: Kp = Kc(RT)Δng, so the two coincide when Δng = 0.
7. One further consequence of the definition: because Kc is built from concentrations, its numerical value changes if the balanced equation is rewritten — reversed, halved or doubled. Kc belongs to an equation, not merely to a set of chemicals, which is the point factors (A) and (B) on book pp.28–29 develop.
 Kc : uses [ ] in mol/L    gas AND solution
 Kp : uses p in atm        gas only

 Kp = Kc (RT)^dn(g)
⚡ JEE Trick If a question offers Kp for a solution-phase or all-liquid equilibrium, that option is wrong before any arithmetic — only gases have partial pressures.
Answer: Kc is the equilibrium constant expressed in terms of concentrations.
1 (iv)Fill in the blank: If the value of Kc is much larger than 1, the ______ are favoured at equilibrium.Magnitude of KEquilibrium Constant (Kc and Kp) - Problem 1 - Chemical Equilibrium - Chemistry Class 11🟡 Similarproducts
Concept Products sit in the numerator of K, so a K much larger than 1 can only mean that the product concentrations dominate the reactant concentrations at equilibrium.
Key relation Kc = [products]ν/[reactants]ν >> 1 ⇒ numerator >> denominator The ratio is fixed at a given temperature, so a large value is a statement about the composition, not about the starting amounts.
Solution
1. Write the definition: for aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, Kc = [C]c[D]d/([A]a[B]b).
2. Kc >> 1 forces the numerator to be much larger than the denominator.
3. So at equilibrium the mixture is mostly product: the products are favoured. That is the blank.
4. Mirror case: Kc << 1 means the reactants dominate and very little product ever forms.
5. A worked illustration from Level-4 of this lecture: 2NO ⇌ N2 + O2 has K = 2.2×1030, so NO is almost completely decomposed at equilibrium — the products are overwhelmingly favoured.
6. Note again that this says nothing about how quickly equilibrium is reached; NO is in fact kinetically persistent despite that enormous K.
7. A caution on wording: “products are favoured” describes the equilibrium position, not the yield you will actually obtain. The yield also depends on the starting amounts and on the volume, whereas Kc is fixed once the temperature is fixed.
 Kc >> 1 : products favoured
 Kc << 1 : reactants favoured
 Kc ~ 1  : comparable amounts

 large K =/= fast reaction
⚡ JEE Trick Rules of thumb worth carrying: K > 103 means the reaction is effectively complete; K < 10−3 means it barely proceeds; in between, both sides are present in appreciable amounts.
Answer: if Kc is much larger than 1, the products are favoured at equilibrium.
1 (v)Fill in the blank: The calculation of equilibrium involves solving for the ______ of the reactants and products at equilibrium.Equilibrium calculationsChemical Equilibrium Constant K - Ice Tables - Kp and Kc🟡 Similarconcentrations
Concept An equilibrium calculation means building an ICE table and solving the K expression for the unknown amount — that is, solving for the equilibrium concentrations of the species.
Key relation write ICE → substitute into K → solve for x → report the equilibrium concentrations K is the given; the concentrations are what the algebra produces, which is why they are the object of the calculation.
Solution
1. In a typical problem the balanced equation, the starting amounts and the value of K are given.
2. Set out an ICE table: Initial, Change (±x with the stoichiometric coefficients) and Equilibrium rows.
3. Substitute the equilibrium row into the K expression, which produces an equation in the single unknown x.
4. Solve it — often a quadratic, as in Level-6 Q1 of this lecture where x²/(3−x) = 1.80.
5. Feed x back into the equilibrium row to obtain each species’ concentration. Those numbers are the answer.
6. So the blank is concentrations. Column-II entry (q) of the match-the-column below phrases it identically: “solving for the concentrations of the reactants and products at equilibrium”.
7. Note the direction of the calculation. K is the input and the concentrations are the output; questions that hand you the concentrations and ask for K — such as Level-2 Q1 of this lecture — run the same machinery backwards and need no ICE table at all.
 given : balanced equation, initial amounts, K
 build : I / C / E table
 solve : K expression -> x
 report: equilibrium CONCENTRATIONS
⚡ JEE Trick Always write the ICE table in concentrations, not moles, unless Δn = 0. When Δn ≠ 0 the volume does not cancel, and substituting moles is the single commonest source of a factor-of-V error.
Answer: the calculation of equilibrium involves solving for the concentrations of the reactants and products at equilibrium.
2 (i)True or false: Kc and Kp are always equal to each other.Kp = Kc(RT)ΔnRelation Between Kp and Kc - Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 6🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept The relation derived at the top of this lecture is Kp = Kc(RT)Δng. The two are equal only in the special case Δng = 0, so “always equal” is false.
Key relation Kp = Kc(RT)Δng, Δng = (moles of gaseous product) − (moles of gaseous reactant) (RT)0 = 1, so equality needs the exponent to vanish — or the freak case T = 1/R.
Solution
1. Book p.27 lists the cases. Case-I: Δng = 0 gives Kp = Kc, e.g. N2 + O2 ⇌ 2NO.
2. Case-II: Δng > 0, e.g. PCl5 ⇌ PCl3 + Cl2 with Δng = +1. Then Kp = Kc(RT), which exceeds Kc whenever RT > 1.
3. Case-III: Δng < 0, e.g. N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 with Δng = −2. Then Kp = Kc(RT)−2, well below Kc.
4. Since two of the three cases give unequal values, the statement is False.
5. The book also notes Case-IV, a curiosity: at T = 1/R ≈ 12.2 K, RT = 1 and Kp = Kc whatever Δng is.
6. And a solution-phase equilibrium has no Kp at all, so the two cannot even be compared there.
 dn(g) = 0  : Kp = Kc         (N2 + O2 <=> 2NO)
 dn(g) > 0  : Kp > Kc if RT>1 (PCl5 dissoc.)
 dn(g) < 0  : Kp < Kc if RT>1 (Haber)

 T = 1/R = 12.2 K : Kp = Kc for any dn(g)
⚡ JEE Trick Compute Δng before anything else in a Kp/Kc question, and count gases only. In C(s) + CO2(g) ⇌ 2CO(g) it is +1, not 0 — the solid does not enter the count.
Answer: False — Kp = Kc only when Δng = 0.
2 (ii)True or false: The equilibrium constant is dimensionless.Units of KShort trick for unit of "Kc" and "Kp"| ETEA part 44🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept The unit of K is MΔn (or atmΔng), so it is dimensionless only when the mole counts on the two sides happen to match. Book p.27 says so explicitly.
Key relation unit of Kc = MΔn; unit of Kp = atmΔng “The unit of Kp and Kc are not fixed and depend on stoichiometry of the reaction” — the book’s own sentence.
Solution
1. Book p.27: “In case the number of moles of the reactant and that of the product are same, Kp and Kc do not have any unit.” The proviso is doing the work.
2. Dimensionless example: H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI, Δn = 2 − 2 = 0, so K is a bare number.
3. Counter-example with units: PCl5 ⇌ PCl3 + Cl2, Δn = +1, so Kc is in mol L−1 and Kp in atm.
4. Another: N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, Δn = −2, giving Kc in M−2 and Kp in atm−2.
5. One counter-example is enough to defeat a universal claim, so the statement is False.
6. Footnote worth knowing: in strict thermodynamics K is built from activities and is dimensionless, but this chapter works with concentrations and pressures, where the units follow Δn.
 H2 + I2 <=> 2HI    dn = 0   -> no unit
 PCl5 <=> PCl3+Cl2  dn = +1  -> mol/L , atm
 N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3  dn = -2  -> M^-2 , atm^-2
⚡ JEE Trick Quote the unit on every numerical K you write. Level-6 of this lecture prints options that differ only in their units (mol2 L−2 and so on), so Δn often decides the answer on its own.
Answer: False — K is dimensionless only when Δn = 0.
2 (iii)True or false: Changes in temperature will affect the value of the equilibrium constant.Temperature dependenceThe effect of conditions on the equilibrium constant🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Temperature is the one variable that genuinely changes the number K, because it changes the two rate constants by different factors. Factor (E) on book p.29 states it outright.
Key relation K = kf/kb, k = Ae−Ea/RT, and Ea(f) − Ea(b) = ΔH Unequal activation energies mean the two rate constants can never respond to heating by the same factor.
Solution
1. Factor (E) on p.29: “The equilibrium constant of a particularly balanced reaction depends only on temperature. It is independent of all other factors like amount of components, concentration, pressure, volume etc.”
2. Characteristic (iii) on p.28 adds the same proviso: K has one unique value for a particular balanced equation at a given temperature.
3. Mechanism: K = kf/kb, and substituting the Arrhenius form gives K ∝ e−ΔH/RT. Only T survives.
4. Direction: heating raises K for an endothermic reaction (larger forward activation energy speeds up more) and lowers it for an exothermic one.
5. So a change of temperature does change the value of K: the statement is True.
6. Contrast: pressure, volume, concentration, inert gas and catalyst can move the position of an equilibrium, but never the number K.
 K ∝ e^(-dH/RT)

 endothermic (dH > 0) : T up -> K up
 exothermic  (dH < 0) : T up -> K down

 only T changes the NUMBER K
⚡ JEE Trick Keep two boxes: “changes K” contains temperature and rewriting the equation; “shifts the position” contains concentration, pressure and volume; “neither” contains catalyst and inert gas at constant volume.
Answer: True — changes in temperature do affect the value of the equilibrium constant.
2 (iv)True or false: The equilibrium constant for the reverse reaction is the reciprocal of the equilibrium constant for the forward reaction.Reversing a reactionHow Modifying a Reaction Affects Equilibrium Constant, K🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Reversing a reaction swaps the numerator and denominator of K, which inverts the number. Factor (A) on book p.28 derives it in two lines.
Key relation A + B ⇌ C + D has Kc = [C][D]/([A][B]); C + D ⇌ A + B has K′c = 1/Kc The same equilibrium mixture is being described, so the same concentrations appear — only the side they are written on changes.
Solution
1. Forward: A + B ⇌ C + D, Kc = [C][D]/([A][B]).
2. Reversed: C + D ⇌ A + B, K′c = [A][B]/([C][D]).
3. The two expressions are reciprocals of one another, so K′c = 1/Kc. Book p.28: “If the reaction is reversed, the value of the equilibrium constant is inversed.”
4. Worked case from p.35: with Kc = 100 for N2 + 2O2 ⇌ 2NO2, the reverse 2NO2 ⇌ N2 + 2O2 has K′c = 1/100 = 0.01.
5. Sanity check on direction: if the forward reaction is nearly complete (large K), the reverse must be nearly impossible (small K). The reciprocal captures exactly that.
6. So the statement is True.
7. A practical use of the rule: a tabulated K is nearly always quoted for the formation reaction, so any question about a decomposition needs the reciprocal taken first. Suggested Problem 5 of this lecture is built on exactly that step.
 forward  : Kc  = [C][D] / ([A][B])
 reversed : Kc' = [A][B] / ([C][D]) = 1/Kc

 Kc = 100  ->  Kc' = 0.01
⚡ JEE Trick Three manipulations, three rules: reverse → take the reciprocal; multiply the equation by n → raise K to the n-th power; add two equations → multiply the K values. Almost every “relation between K1 and K2” question is one of these three.
Answer: True — K for the reverse reaction is the reciprocal of K for the forward reaction.
2 (v)True or false: The equilibrium constant includes the concentration of pure solids and liquids.Pure solids and liquidsExplain why pure liquids and solids can be ignored while writing th…🟢 ExactFalse
Concept The molar concentration of a pure solid or pure liquid is d/M, a constant, so it carries no information and is set to unity. Pure solids and liquids therefore do not appear in the equilibrium expression.
Key relation [pure solid] = [pure liquid] = 1, so they drop out of K Doubling the amount of solid doubles both mass and volume, leaving the density — and hence the “concentration” — unchanged.
Solution
1. Book p.34, on heterogeneous equilibria: “It is customary not to include the concentrations of pure solids in equilibrium expressions.”
2. The worked case there: for 2NaHCO3(s) ⇌ Na2CO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(g), the expression collapses to Kc = [CO2][H2O], with both solids gone.
3. Same for CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g), which reduces to Kc = [CO2] or Kp = pCO2.
4. The reason is that a pure phase’s molar concentration is W/(M×V) = d/M, and both density and molar mass are fixed properties.
5. The same argument covers a solvent present in large excess, e.g. water in NH3(aq) + H2O(l) ⇌ NH4+ + OH− on p.34, where [H2O] is absorbed into K.
6. So the statement is False.
 CaCO3(s) <=> CaO(s) + CO2(g)
 Kc = [CO2]        both solids omitted

 2NaHCO3(s) <=> Na2CO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(g)
 Kc = [CO2][H2O]
⚡ JEE Trick The practical consequence: adding more of a pure solid shifts nothing. That is why the CO2 pressure over a CaCO3/CaO mixture is fixed at a given temperature regardless of how much limestone is present.
Answer: False — pure solids and pure liquids are taken as unity and omitted from K.

2For hypothetical reactions, the equilibrium constant (k) values are given: A ⇌ B, k1 = 2.0; B ⇌ C, k2 = 4.0; C ⇌ D, k3 = 3.0. The equilibrium constant for the reaction A ⇌ D is (Level-2)Adding equilibria → multiply KFinding Equilibrium Constants Through Addition of Reactions🟡 SimilarK = 24
Concept Three consecutive equilibria add up to the overall one, and factor (C) on book p.29 gives the rule: when reactions are added, their equilibrium constants are multiplied.
Key relation A ⇌ B, B ⇌ C, C ⇌ D added ⇒ A ⇌ D with K = k1k2k3 Writing the three expressions and multiplying makes the intermediates B and C cancel algebraically.
Solution
1. Write the three constants: k1 = [B]/[A], k2 = [C]/[B], k3 = [D]/[C].
2. Multiply them: k1k2k3 = ([B]/[A])×([C]/[B])×([D]/[C]).
3. The [B] terms cancel and the [C] terms cancel, leaving [D]/[A].
4. But [D]/[A] is precisely K for the overall reaction A ⇌ D.
5. So K = k1k2k3 = 2.0 × 4.0 × 3.0 = 24.
