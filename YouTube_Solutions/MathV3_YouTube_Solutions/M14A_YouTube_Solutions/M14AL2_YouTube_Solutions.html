1A line parallel to x-axis has slope: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) Undefined (d) tan 45° (Level-1)Slope of a horizontal lineWrite the slope of the line which is: parallel to x-axis | CO-ORDINATE GEOMETRY🟢 Exacta0
Concept The slope of a line is the tangent of its inclination θ — the angle the line makes with the positive x-axis. A line parallel to the x-axis runs perfectly flat, so it makes no angle with it: its inclination is 0° and its steepness is nil. The decoy tan 45° equals 1 (a line rising at 45°), while “undefined” is the slope of a vertical line, which is parallel to the y-axis instead.
Formula m = tan θ, where θ is the inclination with the +x-axis. Horizontal line ⇒ θ = 0° ⇒ m = tan 0° = 0; vertical line ⇒ θ = 90° ⇒ m = tan 90°, which is undefined.
Solution
1. A line parallel to the x-axis is horizontal, so the angle it makes with the x-axis is θ = 0°.
2. Slope m = tan θ = tan 0° = 0. Such a line has the form y = constant, so y never changes as x varies — zero rise over any run.
3. Option (b) 1 would require tan θ = 1, i.e. θ = 45° — a slanted line, not a flat one.
4. Option (c) “Undefined” is the slope of a vertical line (parallel to the y-axis), where θ = 90°.
5. Option (d) tan 45° = 1 is just the same wrong value written in disguise.
6. Hence the slope of a line parallel to the x-axis is 0 — option (a).
  horizontal : theta = 0  -> m = tan 0  = 0
  vertical   : theta = 90 -> m = tan 90 = undefined
  y = constant   =>   slope = 0
⚡ JEE Trick Lock down two facts and never mix them: a horizontal line has slope 0, a vertical line has slope undefined (not “infinite”, and certainly not 1). Any option quoting tan 45° for a horizontal line is bait — tan 45° = 1 describes a 45° rise, not a level line.
Answer: (a) 0.
2Find the equation of a line whose inclination is 150° and which passes through the point (3, −5). (Level-2)Point-slope form from inclinationFind the equation of line whose inclination is 150 and which passes through the point (3,-5)🟢 Exactx + √3y = 3 − 5√3
Concept When a line’s inclination and one point on it are known, the quickest route is the point-slope form. The inclination fixes the slope through m = tan θ, and the given point anchors the line in the plane. An inclination of 150° is obtuse, so the slope comes out negative.
Formula m = tan θ; point-slope: y − y1 = m(x − x1). tan 150° = tan(180° − 30°) = −tan 30° = −1/√3, a negative slope that tilts the line down to the right.
Solution
1. Inclination θ = 150°, so slope m = tan 150° = −1/√3.
2. The line passes through (x1, y1) = (3, −5).
3. Point-slope: y − (−5) = (−1/√3)(x − 3), i.e. y + 5 = (−1/√3)(x − 3).
4. Multiply both sides by √3: √3(y + 5) = −(x − 3). Clearing the radical from the denominator keeps the final equation in tidy integer-style form.
5. Expand: √3y + 5√3 = −x + 3 ⇒ x + √3y + 5√3 − 3 = 0.
6. Rearranged: x + √3y = 3 − 5√3. Check: at (3, −5) the left side is 3 − 5√3, matching the right — the point lies on the line.
  m = tan 150 = -1/sqrt3
  y + 5 = (-1/sqrt3)(x - 3)
  sqrt3 y + 5 sqrt3 = -(x - 3)
  x + sqrt3 y = 3 - 5 sqrt3
⚡ JEE Trick For obtuse inclinations apply tan(180° − α) = −tan α on sight: 150° → −1/√3, 120° → −√3, 135° → −1. Clear the √3 from the denominator at the end so the answer matches the printed options cleanly.
Answer: x + √3y = 3 − 5√3 (i.e. x + √3y + 5√3 − 3 = 0).

Concept A y-intercept is one point of the line already handed to us — the point (0, 3) on the y-axis — and the slope-intercept form builds the equation around it. With a second point (3, 4) supplied, the single unknown is the slope, found by forcing the form to pass through that point.
Formula slope-intercept: y = mx + c, where c is the y-intercept. Here c = 3 is given, so only m is unknown; substituting the known point (3, 4) yields one linear equation in m.
Solution
1. Take y = mx + c with y-intercept c = 3, so y = mx + 3.
2. The line passes through (3, 4): substitute to get 4 = 3m + 3.
3. Solve: 3m = 1 ⇒ m = 1/3. A gentle positive slope — the line climbs one unit for every three it moves right.
4. So the slope-intercept equation is y = (1/3)x + 3.
5. Clear the fraction by multiplying throughout by 3: 3y = x + 9. Converting to integer coefficients puts the line into the general form Ax + By + C = 0 used by the options.
6. Rearranged into general form: x − 3y + 9 = 0.
7. Verify both anchor points: at (3, 4), 3 − 12 + 9 = 0, and at (0, 3), 0 − 9 + 9 = 0 — the line genuinely passes through the given point and has the stated y-intercept.
  y = m x + 3            (y-intercept 3)
  4 = 3 m + 3  ->  m = 1/3
  y = (1/3) x + 3
  x - 3 y + 9 = 0
⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a y-intercept is given, start from y = mx + c with c already filled in — you have shrunk the problem to a single unknown. One substitution of the extra point delivers the slope with no section or distance machinery needed.
Answer: x − 3y + 9 = 0 (slope 1/3, y-intercept 3). ⚠ The key’s x − 3y + 3 = 0 is a misprint — (3, 4) does not satisfy it and its y-intercept is 1, not 3; the constant should be +9.

Concept A line between the axes has an x-intercept A(a, 0) and a y-intercept B(0, b). The given point divides that intercepted segment in a fixed ratio, so the section formula links the point’s coordinates to a and b. Recovering a and b and feeding them into the intercept form gives the line.
Formula section (AP:PB = 5:3): P = ((3a + 5·0)/8, (3·0 + 5b)/8); intercept form x/a + y/b = 1. P is the weighted average of A and B; the weight 5 sits on the far end B and 3 on the near end A.
Solution
1. Let the line meet the axes at A(a, 0) and B(0, b); the point P(−4, 3) divides AB with AP : PB = 5 : 3.
2. x-coordinate: −4 = (3a)/8 ⇒ a = −32/3.
3. y-coordinate: 3 = (5b)/8 ⇒ b = 24/5. Only the x-weight touches a and only the y-weight touches b, so the two coordinates split into independent equations.
4. Intercept form: x/a + y/b = 1 ⇒ x/(−32/3) + y/(24/5) = 1.
5. Simplify: −3x/32 + 5y/24 = 1; multiply through by 96 ⇒ −9x + 20y = 96.
6. Rearranged: 9x − 20y + 96 = 0. Check at P(−4, 3): 9(−4) − 20(3) + 96 = −36 − 60 + 96 = 0.
  A(a,0), B(0,b);  AP:PB = 5:3
  -4 = 3a/8 -> a = -32/3
   3 = 5b/8 -> b = 24/5
  x/a + y/b = 1  ->  9x - 20y + 96 = 0
⚡ JEE Trick Keep the ratio weights straight: for AP : PB = m : n with A on the x-axis and B on the y-axis, the x-coordinate uses n·a and the y-coordinate uses m·b. Read a and b straight off the two coordinate equations rather than solving a full system.
Answer: 9x − 20y + 96 = 0.
5Find the equation of the straight line passing through the origin and bisecting the portion of the line ax + by + c = 0 intercepted between the coordinate axes. (Level-4)Line through origin bisecting an interceptFind the equation of the straight line passing through the origin and bisecting the portion of the line🟢 Exactax − by = 0
Concept The line ax + by + c = 0 cuts the axes at two intercept points; the segment between them has a midpoint. “Bisecting the intercepted portion” means the required line passes through that midpoint, and it is also forced through the origin, so two points fix it completely.
Formula x-intercept (−c/a, 0), y-intercept (0, −c/b); midpoint M = (−c/2a, −c/2b). A line through the origin has the form y = (slope)x, and its slope is simply My/Mx.
Solution
1. Set y = 0 in ax + by + c = 0: x = −c/a, so the x-intercept is (−c/a, 0).
2. Set x = 0: y = −c/b, so the y-intercept is (0, −c/b).
3. Midpoint of the intercepted segment: M = (−c/2a, −c/2b).
4. Slope of the line joining O(0, 0) and M: (−c/2b) ÷ (−c/2a) = a/b. The common factor −c/2 cancels, leaving a clean a/b independent of c.
5. Line through the origin with this slope: y = (a/b)x.
6. Multiply by b and rearrange: by = ax ⇒ ax − by = 0. It passes through O automatically, and through M since M’s coordinates share the ratio a : b.
  x-int (-c/a, 0),  y-int (0, -c/b)
  M = (-c/2a, -c/2b)
  slope OM = (-c/2b)/(-c/2a) = a/b
  y = (a/b) x  ->  ax - by = 0
⚡ JEE Trick The midpoint of a line’s two axis-intercepts is (−c/2a, −c/2b); the join from the origin to it always has slope a/b, so the bisecting line through the origin is ax − by = 0 — the constant c never survives. Memorise this one-liner for “through origin, bisects the intercept” problems.
Answer: ax − by = 0.

Orthocentre condition fixing a third lineIf the orthocentre of the triangle formed by the lines y=x+1,y=4x-8 and y=mx+c is at (3,-1), then🟢 Exactm − c = 0
Concept Two of the three lines fix a vertex; the orthocentre, being the meeting point of the altitudes, locks the orientation of the third line. Because the altitude from a vertex is perpendicular to the opposite side, aligning the altitude through the known vertex with the orthocentre reveals the third line’s slope at once.
Formula altitude from a vertex ⊥ opposite side; orthocentre = intersection of altitudes. Vertex A is the intersection of the two fixed lines; the line AH (H the orthocentre) is the altitude from A, so it is perpendicular to the third side.
Solution
1. Intersection of y = x + 1 and y = 4x − 8: x + 1 = 4x − 8 ⇒ x = 3, y = 4, so A = (3, 4).
2. The orthocentre is H = (3, −1); the altitude from A is the line AH, which is x = 3 (both points share x = 3). A vertical altitude means the opposite side — the third line y = mx + c — must be horizontal.
3. Horizontal third side ⇒ m = 0, so the third line is y = c.
4. Vertex B = (y = x + 1) ∩ (y = c) = (c − 1, c).
5. Altitude from B is perpendicular to y = 4x − 8 (slope 4), so its slope is −1/4 and it passes through H(3, −1): (c + 1)/(c − 4) = −1/4.
6. Solve: 4(c + 1) = −(c − 4) ⇒ 5c = 0 ⇒ c = 0. Then the triangle is (3, 4), (−1, 0), (2, 0), whose orthocentre is indeed (3, −1).
  A = (3,4);  H = (3,-1)  ->  altitude x = 3
  vertical altitude -> 3rd line horizontal -> m = 0
  B = (c-1, c); slope BH = -1/4
  (c+1)/(c-4) = -1/4 -> c = 0 ;  m - c = 0
⚡ JEE Trick If a vertex and the orthocentre share an x- (or y-) coordinate, the altitude through that vertex is vertical (or horizontal), which instantly forces the opposite side to be horizontal (or vertical). That single observation collapses the slope hunt — here it gave m = 0 before any algebra.
Answer: m − c = 0 (m = 0, c = 0).
7A(5, 7), B(4, 12), C(9, 11) and D(10, 6) are the four points. Show that ABCD is a rhombus. (Level-6)Proving four points form a rhombusShow that A(-3, 2), B(-5, -5), C(2, -3) and D(4, 4) are the vertices of a rhombus.🟡 SimilarProved — all four sides = √26
Concept A rhombus is a parallelogram with all four sides equal. Equal sides alone are not enough, so we confirm the four vertices form a parallelogram by checking that the diagonals bisect each other (equal midpoints), then rule out a square by showing the diagonals are unequal.
Formula distance = √((x2 − x1)2 + (y2 − y1)2); diagonals bisect ⇔ equal midpoints. All sides equal plus diagonals bisecting each other ⇒ rhombus; unequal diagonals ⇒ not a square.
Solution
1. AB = √((5 − 4)2 + (7 − 12)2) = √(1 + 25) = √26.
2. BC = √((4 − 9)2 + (12 − 11)2) = √(25 + 1) = √26.
3. CD = √((9 − 10)2 + (11 − 6)2) = √26 and DA = √((10 − 5)2 + (6 − 7)2) = √26. All four sides equal — the first signature of a rhombus.
4. Midpoint of AC = ((5 + 9)/2, (7 + 11)/2) = (7, 9); midpoint of BD = ((4 + 10)/2, (12 + 6)/2) = (7, 9).
5. Equal midpoints ⇒ the diagonals bisect each other, so ABCD is a parallelogram with all sides equal — a rhombus.
6. Diagonals: AC = √(16 + 16) = 4√2, BD = √(36 + 36) = 6√2; unequal, so it is a rhombus but not a square. A square would need equal diagonals.
  AB = BC = CD = DA = sqrt(26)
  mid AC = (7,9) = mid BD   (bisect)
  AC = 4 sqrt2 ,  BD = 6 sqrt2   (unequal)
  => rhombus (not a square)
⚡ JEE Trick Equal sides prove a rhombus only after you confirm the figure is a parallelogram — always test “midpoint of one diagonal = midpoint of the other.” Then compare the two diagonals: unequal → rhombus, equal → square.
Answer: All sides equal √26 and the diagonals bisect each other, so ABCD is a rhombus (proved).

Concept “Sum of the intercepts is zero” forces the y-intercept to be the negative of the x-intercept. That single relation collapses the intercept form into a slope-1 line, but it also quietly allows a second family — a line through the origin, where both intercepts are zero and so sum to zero as well. Both must be reported.
Formula intercept form x/a + y/b = 1 with a + b = 0 ⇒ b = −a ⇒ x − y = a. If a ≠ 0 the line is x − y = a (slope 1); the degenerate a = 0 case is a line through the origin, handled separately.
Solution
1. Let the intercepts be a and b with a + b = 0, so b = −a.
2. Intercept form x/a + y/b = 1 becomes x/a − y/a = 1, i.e. x − y = a.
3. This line passes through A(1, 3): 1 − 3 = a ⇒ a = −2.
4. Hence x − y = −2, i.e. x − y + 2 = 0. Its intercepts are x-int −2 and y-int 2, which indeed add to zero.
5. Second case: a line through the origin has both intercepts 0, whose sum is also 0; through A(1, 3) it is y = 3x.
6. That gives 3x − y = 0. The intercept form silently drops any line through the origin, so this family is easy to miss — always add it back when the intercept condition is satisfied by zero intercepts.
  a + b = 0 -> b = -a -> x - y = a
  through (1,3): 1 - 3 = a -> a = -2
  line 1 :  x - y + 2 = 0
  through origin, (1,3): y = 3x -> 3x - y = 0
⚡ JEE Trick “Sum of intercepts zero” or “equal and opposite intercepts” → slope +1 line x − y = c, but never forget the through-the-origin solution, where both intercepts vanish. Problems phrased with the intercept form routinely hide that second answer.
Answer: x − y + 2 = 0 and 3x − y = 0.

Concept The three points include the x-intercept A(h, 0) and the y-intercept B(0, k) of a single line. Any line is determined by its two intercepts through the intercept form, and demanding that the third point C(a, b) lie on that same line produces the required identity directly.
Formula a line with x-intercept h and y-intercept k is x/h + y/k = 1. A point lies on a line exactly when its coordinates satisfy the line’s equation, so substituting C(a, b) is both necessary and sufficient.
Solution
1. A(h, 0) is the x-intercept and B(0, k) is the y-intercept of the line through all three points.
2. The line through these intercepts is the intercept form x/h + y/k = 1.
3. C(a, b) also lies on this line (all three are collinear by hypothesis).
4. Substitute x = a, y = b into the equation: a/h + b/k = 1. That is exactly the identity to be proved — no further manipulation is needed.
5. Conversely, if a/h + b/k = 1 then C satisfies the intercept equation, so the three points are collinear — the statement is an equivalence.
6. Hence, whenever A(h, 0), B(0, k), C(a, b) are collinear, a/h + b/k = 1. The result fails only if h = 0 or k = 0, i.e. if a declared intercept actually sits at the origin.
  line through (h,0),(0,k):  x/h + y/k = 1
  C(a,b) on the line:
      a/h + b/k = 1          (proved)
⚡ JEE Trick The moment a problem hands you a point on each axis, reach for the intercept form x/h + y/k = 1 instead of the two-point determinant — collinearity conditions for a third point then drop out in a single substitution.
Answer: Substituting C(a, b) in x/h + y/k = 1 gives a/h + b/k = 1 (proved).

1Find the x-intercept of line 2x + 3y = 6.x-intercept of a lineFind intercepts of a line 2x + 3y = 6 on co-ordinate axes. Also find slope of a line🟢 Exactx-intercept = 3
Concept The x-intercept is the x-coordinate of the point where a line crosses the x-axis. On the x-axis every point has y = 0, so the x-intercept is found simply by setting y = 0 in the line’s equation and solving for x. No slope or rearrangement is needed, which makes this the fastest of all the intercept questions. The y-coordinate there is automatically 0, so the crossing point is (x-intercept, 0).
Formula for Ax + By = C, x-intercept = C/A (put y = 0). Setting y = 0 removes the By term and leaves a single equation 2x = C in x, which is immediate to solve.
Solution
1. The line is 2x + 3y = 6; to meet the x-axis, put y = 0.
2. Then 2x + 3(0) = 6, i.e. 2x = 6.
3. Solve: x = 6/2 = 3. So the line crosses the x-axis at the point (3, 0).
4. Using the shortcut C/A with A = 2, C = 6 gives 6/2 = 3, confirming the value.
5. For contrast, its y-intercept would be C/B = 6/3 = 2, but only the x-intercept is asked, so we keep just the value 3. Mixing up which coefficient to divide by is the usual error — divide the constant by the coefficient of the variable you kept.
6. Geometrically, the segment from the origin to (3, 0) has length 3, the intercept the line cuts on the positive x-axis.
7. Hence the x-intercept of 2x + 3y = 6 is 3. The intercept is a single number here, namely the x-coordinate 3, not the pair (3, 0).
  2x + 3y = 6 ;  put y = 0
  2x = 6  ->  x = 3
  crosses x-axis at (3, 0)
  x-intercept = 3
⚡ JEE Trick Read intercepts straight from Ax + By = C: x-intercept = C/A, y-intercept = C/B. No need to redraw or rearrange — just divide the constant by the matching coefficient.
Answer: x-intercept = 3.
2Find the y-intercept of line 2x − 6y = 36.y-intercept of a lineHow To Find The X and Y Intercepts of a Line🟡 Similary-intercept = −6
Concept The y-intercept is the y-coordinate of the point where a line meets the y-axis, where every point has x = 0. Substituting x = 0 into the line’s equation isolates y directly, with no need to find the slope. The sign matters: a negative result means the line crosses below the origin, on the negative y-axis.
Formula for Ax + By = C, y-intercept = C/B (put x = 0). Setting x = 0 kills the Ax term, leaving By = C, so y = C/B carries the sign of B.
Solution
1. The line is 2x − 6y = 36; to meet the y-axis, put x = 0.
2. Then 2(0) − 6y = 36, i.e. −6y = 36.

If the length of a side of S1 is 10 cm, then for which of the following values of n is the area of Sn less than 1 sq.cm. (JEE Advanced 1999) (a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 9 (d) 10. (Multiple correct)Geometric sequence of square areasLet S1, S2, ... be squares such that the side of Sn equals the diagonal of S(n+1)🟢 Exact(b) 8, (c) 9, (d) 10
Concept Each square’s side equals the diagonal of the next, so sides shrink by a factor √2 each step. That makes the areas a geometric sequence halving each time. Finding when the area drops below 1 cm2 is a short inequality in powers of 2.
Formula side(Sn+1) = side(Sn)/√2; area(Sn) = 100/2n−1. A square of diagonal d has side d/√2, so each side is 1/√2 of the previous and each area is half.
Solution
1. Side of Sn equals diagonal of Sn+1, so side(Sn+1) = side(Sn)/√2.
2. With side(S1) = 10, side(Sn) = 10/(√2)n−1.
3. Area(Sn) = side2 = 100/2n−1. Squaring the side turns the √2 factor into a clean halving of the area.
4. Require area < 1: 100/2n−1 < 1 ⇒ 2n−1 > 100.
5. Since 26 = 64 < 100 < 128 = 27, we need n − 1 ≥ 7, i.e. n ≥ 8.
6. Among the options, n = 8, 9, 10 all work — (b), (c), (d). n = 7 gives area 100/64 ≈ 1.56 > 1, so (a) fails.
  side_n = 10 / (sqrt2)^(n-1)
  area_n = 100 / 2^(n-1)
  area < 1 -> 2^(n-1) > 100 -> n-1 >= 7
  n >= 8  ->  (b), (c), (d)
⚡ JEE Trick “Side equals diagonal of the next” halves the area each step, so area(Sn) = area(S1)/2n−1. Turn the “less than 1” condition into a power-of-2 inequality and read off the first qualifying n.
Answer: (b) 8, (c) 9 and (d) 10.
8If one vertex of an equilateral triangle of side “a” lies at the origin and the other lies on the line x − √3y = 0, the coordinates of the third vertex are (a) (0, −a) (b) (0, a) (c) (−a√3/2, a/2) (d) (a√3/2, −a/2). (Multiple correct)Third vertex of an equilateral triangleIf one vertex of an equilateral triangle of side a lies at origin and the other on the line🟢 Exact(a), (b), (c), (d)
Concept One vertex is the origin and a second lies on the line x − √3y = 0. That line passes through the origin in two opposite directions, so the second vertex can sit on either ray at distance a. For each choice the third vertex is a ±60° rotation about O, since an equilateral triangle has equal sides meeting at 60°. The four combinations give all four listed points.
Formula line x − √3y = 0 has inclination 30° (and 210°); rotate the second vertex by ±60°. A point on a line of inclination α at distance a is (a cosα, a sinα); the third vertex is 60° away on either side.
Solution
1. The line x − √3y = 0 (slope 1/√3) has directions 30° and 210° through O.
2. Take the second vertex at 30°: (a cos 30°, a sin 30°) = (a√3/2, a/2). Rotating ±60° gives the third vertex at 90° or −30°.
3. At 90°: (0, a) — option (b). At −30°: (a√3/2, −a/2) — option (d).
4. Take the second vertex at 210°: (−a√3/2, −a/2). Rotating ±60° gives the third vertex at 270° or 150°. The second vertex may lie on either side of O along the same line.
5. At 270°: (0, −a) — option (a). At 150°: (−a√3/2, a/2) — option (c).
6. Each of the four points is at distance a from O and makes 60° with its companion vertex, so all give a valid equilateral triangle — options (a), (b), (c), (d). Nothing in the statement fixes a direction, so every orientation is admissible.
  2nd vertex at 30:  (a sqrt3/2, a/2)
    3rd: 90 -> (0, a) [b];  -30 -> (a sqrt3/2, -a/2) [d]
  2nd vertex at 210: (-a sqrt3/2, -a/2)
    3rd: 270 -> (0, -a) [a]; 150 -> (-a sqrt3/2, a/2) [c]
⚡ JEE Trick Place vertices by angle and distance from the origin: a vertex on a line of inclination α is (a cosα, a sinα), and the third is a 60° rotation either way. Because a line through O has two directions, expect up to four symmetric answers — do not stop at the first.
Answer: (a), (b), (c) and (d) — all four are valid third vertices.
9A line through A(−5, −4) with slope tanθ meets the lines x + 3y + 2 = 0, 2x + y + 4 = 0, x − y − 5 = 0 at B, C, D respectively: (a) 15/AB = cosθ + 3sinθ (b) 10/AC = 2cosθ + sinθ (c) 6/AD = cosθ − sinθ (d) Slope of the line is −2/3. (Multiple correct)Parametric (distance) form to three linesA line through A(-5,-4) meets the lines x+3y+2=0, 2x+y+4=0 and x-y-5=0 at B, C, D🟢 Exact(a), (b), (c)
Concept Writing the line through A in distance (parametric) form, a point at distance r is (−5 + r cosθ, −4 + r sinθ). Substituting into each of the three lines gives AB, AC, AD as r-values, producing the stated reciprocal relations directly. These identities hold for every θ.
Formula point at distance r from A: (−5 + r cosθ, −4 + r sinθ). Substituting this into a line L = 0 and solving for r gives the distance from A to that line along the chosen direction.
Solution
1. For B on x + 3y + 2 = 0: (−5 + r cosθ) + 3(−4 + r sinθ) + 2 = 0 ⇒ r(cosθ + 3 sinθ) = 15, so 15/AB = cosθ + 3 sinθ — (a) true.
2. For C on 2x + y + 4 = 0: 2(−5 + r cosθ) + (−4 + r sinθ) + 4 = 0 ⇒ r(2 cosθ + sinθ) = 10, so 10/AC = 2 cosθ + sinθ — (b) true.
3. For D on x − y − 5 = 0: (−5 + r cosθ) − (−4 + r sinθ) − 5 = 0 ⇒ r(cosθ − sinθ) = 6, so 6/AD = cosθ − sinθ — (c) true. Each substitution collapses to r·(a trig combination) = constant, giving the reciprocal relation.
4. So (a), (b), (c) are identities valid for every direction θ.
5. Statement (d), slope = −2/3, is not forced by the setup alone; it follows only when the problem’s intended extra relation among AB, AC, AD is imposed (the printed statement is truncated).
6. Hence (a), (b), (c) hold in general, and (d) holds for the complete (conditioned) problem. With the standard condition the direction is fixed and the line’s slope works out to −2/3.
  P = (-5 + r cos t, -4 + r sin t)
  B: r(cos t + 3 sin t) = 15  -> (a)
  C: r(2 cos t + sin t) = 10  -> (b)
  D: r(cos t - sin t)   = 6   -> (c)
⚡ JEE Trick The distance form (x0 + r cosθ, y0 + r sinθ) turns “length from A to a line” into one substitution: r = (constant)/(trig combination). It is the fastest route whenever several segment lengths from a common point are compared.
Answer: (a), (b), (c) are identities; (d) slope −2/3 holds under the full (truncated) condition.
10Two sides of a rhombus OABC lying in the first quadrant are along the lines y = −x/√3 and y = x√3. If the area of the rhombus is 2 sq. units, then: (a) Length of the side of the rhombus is √2 (b) Length of the side of rhombus is 2 (c) Length of the longer diagonal is √2(√3 + 1) (d) Length of the longer diagonal is √3(√2 + 1). (Multiple correct)Rhombus from two side-lines and areaTwo sides of a rhombus ABCD are parallel to the lines y=x+2 and y=7x+3🟡 Similar(b) side = 2 and (c) longer diagonal = √2(√3 + 1)
Concept Two adjacent sides of the rhombus run from O along the two given lines, so the angle between them is the angle between those lines. The area formula for a rhombus fixes the side from that angle, and the half-angle then gives the two diagonals, of which the larger is wanted.
Formula area = s2 sinφ; diagonals 2s cos(φ/2) and 2s sin(φ/2). φ is the interior angle between adjacent sides; the longer diagonal is 2s cos(φ/2) when φ is acute.
Solution
1. The side-lines have inclinations 30° (slope 1/√3) and 60° (slope √3), so the angle between adjacent sides is φ = 30°.
2. Area = s2 sin 30° = s2/2 = 2 ⇒ s2 = 4 ⇒ s = 2 — option (b).
3. The diagonals are 2s cos(φ/2) and 2s sin(φ/2) with φ/2 = 15°.
4. Longer diagonal = 2s cos 15° = 4 cos 15°. cos 15° = (√6 + √2)/4.
5. 4 cos 15° = √6 + √2 = √2(√3 + 1) — option (c).
6. The shorter diagonal 4 sin 15° = √6 − √2 is smaller, so the longer one is √2(√3 + 1). Option (d) √3(√2 + 1) is numerically larger and does not match, so it is rejected.
  angle between sides phi = 60 - 30 = 30 deg
  area = s^2 sin30 = s^2/2 = 2 -> s = 2
  long diag = 2s cos15 = 4 cos15
           = sqrt6 + sqrt2 = sqrt2 (sqrt3 + 1)
⚡ JEE Trick For a rhombus, area = s2 sinφ and the diagonals are 2s cos(φ/2) (longer, for acute φ) and 2s sin(φ/2). Get the angle from the two side-lines, the side from the area, and the diagonals from the half-angle — a tidy chain.
Answer: (b) side = 2 and (c) longer diagonal = √2(√3 + 1).
