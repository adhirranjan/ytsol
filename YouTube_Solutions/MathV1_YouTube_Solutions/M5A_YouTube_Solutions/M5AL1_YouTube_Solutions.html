1 (i)Fill in the blank: The algebraic sums of two or more angles are generally called ______ angles.Compound angle definitionCompound Angles Class 11 — 12+ Solved Questions🟡 SimilarCompound
Concept When two or more angles are combined by addition or subtraction, the result gets a special name. A single angle like A is "simple"; the instant we form A+B or A−B the trigonometric ratios can no longer be found by adding the ratios of the parts — they need the expansion formulae. That is exactly why this family of angles is singled out.
Formula A compound angle = algebraic sum of two or more angles, e.g. A ± B, A + B − C. "Algebraic sum" allows signs, so both A+B and A−B qualify.
Solution
1. Read the sentence: it describes "the algebraic sums of two or more angles."Key words: "algebraic sum" and "two or more angles."
2. By definition this combination is called a compound angle.e.g. sin(A+B), cos(A−B) are ratios of compound angles.
3. Contrast the vocabulary: a single angle A is simple; 2A, 3A are multiple angles; A±B is compound.
⚡ JEE Trick Classify fast: one letter = simple, nA = multiple/submultiple, A±B (different letters) = compound. The whole chapter is built on expanding these. Trap: 2A is a multiple angle, not compound — different formula set.
Answer: compound (angles).

Concept Two sine values of compound angles pin down α and β completely, because sin(α+β)=1 forces α+β to be exactly π/2 on the given interval. Once α and β are known numbers, the two tangents are just standard-angle values. The trap is quadrant care: α+2β and 2α+β land outside the first quadrant, so both tangents come out negative — and the product of two negatives is positive.
Formula sinθ = 1 ⇒ θ = π/2, sinθ = ½ ⇒ θ = π/6 (principal, θ small). Standard values: tan120° = −√3, tan150° = −1/√3. With α,β ∈ [0,π/2], α+β ∈ [0,π] and α−β ∈ [−π/2,π/2], so the principal solutions are the only ones.

2. sin(α−β) = ½ ⇒ α−β = π/6 (30°).α−β ∈ [−π/2,π/2]; the only value there with sine ½ is π/6.

Concept All four expansions come from just two source formulae — sin(A±B) and cos(A±B) — by dividing. tan(A±B) is sin over cos with everything divided by cosA·cosB; cot(A±B) is cos over sin with everything divided by sinA·sinB. That is why tan formulae carry 1 in the denominator while cot formulae carry 1 in the numerator — the classic matching trap.
Formula tan(A±B) = (tanA ± tanB)/(1 ∓ tanA tanB); cot(A±B) = (cotA cotB ∓ 1)/(cotB ± cotA) note the sign in a tan denominator is OPPOSITE to the sign between the angles; in cot both numerator sign and denominator order flip.
Solution
1. (A) tan(A+B) = sin(A+B)/cos(A+B); divide top and bottom by cosA cosB ⇒ (tanA + tanB)/(1 − tanA tanB) → matches (r).
2. (B) tan(A−B): same division on sin(A−B)/cos(A−B) ⇒ (tanA − tanB)/(1 + tanA tanB) → matches (s).Only the two middle signs flip.
3. (C) cot(A+B) = cos(A+B)/sin(A+B); divide top and bottom by sinA sinB ⇒ (cotA cotB − 1)/(cotB + cotA) → matches (q).
4. (D) cot(A−B): divide cos(A−B)/sin(A−B) by sinA sinB ⇒ (cotA cotB + 1)/(cotB − cotA) → matches (p).Denominator is cotB − cotA, in that order — B first.
5. Verify one numerically: A = 60°, B = 30°: tan90° should blow up; formula (r) gives (√3+1/√3)/(1−1) = ∞. ✓ cot90° = 0; formula (q) gives (√3·(1/√3)−1)/(...) = 0. ✓

Concept The target angle 2B is a combination of the two given compound angles: 2B = (A + B) − (A − B). So tan2B follows from the tangent subtraction formula with tan(A+B) = m and tan(A−B) = n as ready-made inputs, and cot2B is just its reciprocal. The trap is decomposing wrongly — (A+B) + (A−B) gives 2A, not 2B; the plus-combination answers a sibling question. Once tan2B is in hand, flipping to the cotangent is a single reciprocal — no fresh expansion needed.
Formula tan(X − Y) = (tanX − tanY)/(1 + tanX·tanY) with X = A + B, Y = A − B; then cot2B = 1/tan2B.
Solution
1. Write the target from the data: 2B = (A + B) − (A − B).Subtracting kills A and doubles B — the key structural observation.
2. Apply the subtraction formula: tan2B = [tan(A+B) − tan(A−B)]/[1 + tan(A+B)·tan(A−B)].m and n enter as opaque symbols — the formula never needs A or B individually.
3. Substitute m and n: tan2B = (m − n)/(1 + mn).
4. Invert for the cotangent: cot2B = (1 + mn)/(m − n) — option (b).cot is the reciprocal of tan; flip the fraction, no sign changes.
5. Check with A = 60°, B = 15°: m = tan75° ≈ 3.732, n = tan45° = 1; (1 + 3.732)/(3.732 − 1) = 4.732/2.732 ≈ 1.732 = √3 = cot30° = cot(2×15°) ✓.
 2B = (A+B) − (A−B)
 tan2B = (m − n)/(1 + m·n)
 cot2B = (1 + m·n)/(m − n)            <-- answer (b)
 A=60°, B=15°: (1+3.732)/(2.732) ≈ √3 = cot30°  ✓

Option (a) is tan2A and option (d) is a sign-mangled tan2B — the exam counts on you grabbing the wrong twin. Ten-second verify: pick easy angles (B = 15°, A = 60°) and match against cot30° = √3.
Answer: (b) (1 + mn)/(m − n).

Ignoring the obtuse condition and taking cosA = +4/5 leads straight to the distractor 33/65, option (a).

8If cos(θ−α) = a and cos(θ−β) = b, then sin²(α−β) + 2ab cos(α−β) is equal to: (a) a² + b² (b) a² − b² (c) b² − a² (d) −a² − b²cos(θ−α), cos(θ−β) eliminationIf cos(θ−α)=a, cos(θ−β)=b — value of sin²(α−β)+2ab cos(α−β)🟢 Exactaa² + b²
Concept The two given cosines share the angle θ; the target expression involves only α − β. The bridge: (θ − β) − (θ − α) = α − β, so cos(α−β) expands in terms of cos(θ−α), cos(θ−β) and their sines — i.e. in terms of a, b, √(1−a²), √(1−b²). Substituting and simplifying eliminates every square root, leaving the clean symmetric answer a² + b². This is an elimination problem: kill θ, keep a and b.
Formula Put x = θ − α, y = θ − β, so cosx = a, cosy = b and α − β = y − x. Then cos(α−β) = cos(x−y) = cosx cosy + sinx siny = ab + sinx siny, and sin²(α−β) = 1 − cos²(α−β).
Solution
1. Let C = cos(α−β) = ab + s, where s = sinx siny.Compact names keep the algebra readable.
2. Target: E = sin²(α−β) + 2ab·C = (1 − C²) + 2abC.
3. Expand with C = ab + s: E = 1 − (ab + s)² + 2ab(ab + s) = 1 − a²b² − 2abs − s² + 2a²b² + 2abs.
4. The ±2abs terms cancel: E = 1 + a²b² − s².
5. Now s² = sin²x sin²y = (1 − a²)(1 − b²) = 1 − a² − b² + a²b².This is where θ finally disappears.
6. Substitute: E = 1 + a²b² − 1 + a² + b² − a²b² = a² + b² — option (a). Check θ = α = β: a = b = 1, E = 0 + 2·1·1 = 2 = 1² + 1² ✓.

 E = 1 − (ab+s)² + 2ab(ab+s) = 1 + a²b² − s²
 s² = (1−a²)(1−b²)

9If A and B are acute angles such that A+B and A−B satisfy tan²θ − 4tanθ + 1 = 0, then: (a) A = π/3, B = π/12 (b) A = π/4, B = π/6 (c) A = π/6, B = π/4 (d) A = π/6, B = π/12Roots as tan(A+B), tan(A−B)Find A, B from tan(A+B) and tan(A−B)🟡 SimilarbA = π/4, B = π/6
Concept A+B and A−B are told to be the two roots of tan²θ − 4tanθ + 1 = 0. Solving that quadratic gives two tangent values that correspond to the standard angles 75° and 15°. Assigning the larger to A+B (since B > 0) and solving two linear equations recovers A and B.
Formula Quadratic roots tanθ = (4 ± √(16−4))/2 = 2 ± √3; and tan75° = 2 + √3, tan15° = 2 − √3.
Solution
1. Solve tan²θ − 4tanθ + 1 = 0: tanθ = (4 ± √12)/2 = 2 ± √3.
2. Identify angles: tanθ = 2 + √3 ⇒ θ = 75°; tanθ = 2 − √3 ⇒ θ = 15°.
3. Since A and B are acute with B > 0, take A + B = 75° and A − B = 15°.The larger compound angle must be the sum.
4. Add: 2A = 90° ⇒ A = 45° = π/4. Subtract: 2B = 60° ⇒ B = 30° = π/6.
⚡ JEE Trick The roots 2 ± √3 are the fingerprint of tan75° and tan15°; spotting them skips inverse-tan work. Solve A and B by adding/subtracting the sum and difference equations. Trap: match the bigger root (75°) to A+B, not A−B, so B stays positive.
Answer: (b) A = π/4, B = π/6.
10If α + β = π/2 and β + γ = α, then tanα = ? (a) 2(tanβ + tanγ) (b) tanβ + tanγ (c) tanβ + 2tanγ (d) 2tanβ + tanγα+β = π/2, β+γ = α relationIf α+β=π/2 and β+γ=α, find tanα🟢 Exactctanβ + 2tanγ
Concept Two constraints tie the three angles: α + β = π/2 makes α and β complementary (so cosβ = sinα, sinβ = cosα), and β + γ = α expresses γ as α − β. The neat route: start from γ = α − β, expand sinγ = sin(α−β), divide by cosα cosβ to manufacture tanα − tanβ, then use complementarity to show the leftover factor is exactly 2tanγ.
Formula tanα − tanβ = sin(α−β)/(cosα·cosβ) write each tan as sin/cos and combine over the common denominator. Complementary swaps: cosβ = sinα; also γ = α − β = 2α − π/2, so cosγ = cos(2α − π/2) = sin2α.
Solution
1. From β + γ = α: γ = α − β, hence sin(α − β) = sinγ.
2. Divide by cosα cosβ: tanα − tanβ = sinγ/(cosα cosβ).Standard identity from step-combining sin(α−β) = sinα cosβ − cosα sinβ.
3. Use β = π/2 − α: cosβ = sinα, so cosα cosβ = sinα cosα = ½·sin2α.
4. Also γ = α − (π/2 − α) = 2α − π/2, so cosγ = cos(2α − π/2) = sin2α.cos(x − π/2) = sinx.
5. Combine: tanα − tanβ = sinγ/(½ sin2α) = 2sinγ/cosγ = 2tanγ.
6. Hence tanα = tanβ + 2tanγ — option (c). Check α = 60°, β = 30°, γ = 30°: tan60° = √3; tan30° + 2tan30° = 3/√3 = √3 ✓.
