1What is the dot product of two perpendicular vectors? (0)Dot product propertiesProperties of scalar / dot product — Class 11th Physics vectors🟡 Similarc0
Concept The scalar (dot) product measures how much of one vector lies along the other. It is built from the projection of B onto the direction of A, and that projection shrinks as the two vectors swing apart. When the vectors are at right angles neither has any component along the other, so the projection — and hence the whole product — collapses to zero. This is why "A·B = 0" is the standard test for perpendicularity throughout mechanics (zero work by a centripetal force, zero work by the normal reaction, etc.).
Formula A·B = |A| |B| cos θ, where |A|, |B| are the magnitudes (units of A × units of B, e.g. m × m = m²) and θ is the angle between the two vectors when drawn tail-to-tail. The cos θ factor is exactly the fraction of B that survives when projected onto A, so the whole product is (length of A) × (projection of B on A). The result is a scalar, carrying the product of the two units and no direction.
Solution
1. State what "perpendicular" fixes: the angle between the vectors is θ = 90°.Perpendicular means at right angles, which is 90° by definition.
2. Substitute θ = 90° into the definition: A·B = |A| |B| cos 90°.
3. Evaluate the trigonometric factor: cos 90° = 0.On the unit circle the horizontal (adjacent) component at 90° is zero — a vector at 90° casts no shadow on the other.
4. Complete the multiplication: A·B = |A| × |B| × 0 = 0, whatever the magnitudes are.Any finite number multiplied by zero is zero, so the result does not depend on how long the vectors are.
5. Compare with the printed options: (a) 1, (b) the product of their magnitudes, (c) 0, (d) undefined. Only (c) 0 matches."Product of their magnitudes" is the value at θ = 0° (parallel), not at 90°; "undefined" would need a zero vector.

1Distinguish between dot product and cross productDot vs cross product6 Differences between Dot Product and Cross Product🟢 Exact—
Concept Both the scalar (dot) and vector (cross) products take two vectors as input, but they answer opposite questions. The dot product asks "how much do they point the same way?" and returns a direction-free scalar built on cos θ. The cross product asks "how much do they fail to point the same way — and in what perpendicular sense?" and returns a vector built on sin θ whose direction is set by the right-hand rule. Knowing which is which decides whether a quantity (work, torque, flux, area) is a number or an arrow.
Formula A·B = |A||B| cos θ (scalar, maximal when parallel) versus A×B = |A||B| sin θ n̂ (vector, maximal when perpendicular). cos θ measures the projection of one vector along the other; sin θ measures the perpendicular "spread" that a parallelogram's area depends on — hence one gives a length-like scalar and the other an area-like vector.
Solution
1. Nature of the result: dot product → a scalar (a plain number); cross product → a vector (has magnitude and direction).
2. Trig factor: dot uses cos θ, so it is largest at θ = 0° (parallel) and zero at 90°; cross uses sin θ, so it is zero at 0° (parallel) and largest at 90°.
3. Order (symmetry): the dot product is commutative, A·B = B·A; the cross product is anti-commutative, A×B = −(B×A).Reversing the order flips the right-hand-rule direction, so only the cross product changes sign.
4. Geometric meaning: A·B is |A| times the projection of B on A; |A×B| is the area of the parallelogram with sides A and B, directed along the normal.
5. Physics examples: dot → work W = F·s, power P = F·v, flux Φ = B·A (all scalars); cross → torque τ = r×F, angular momentum L = r×p, force on a charge F = qv×B (all vectors).
 feature          DOT  (scalar)          CROSS (vector)
 result           a number               a vector
 trig factor      cos θ                  sin θ
 parallel (0°)    maximum |A||B|         zero
 perp (90°)       zero                   maximum |A||B|
 order            A·B = B·A              A×B = -(B×A)
 geometry         projection             parallelogram area (normal)
 example          work  W = F·s          torque  τ = r×F
⚡ JEE Trick For any physical formula, ask one question — "is the result a number or an arrow?" Work, power, energy, flux ⇒ dot; torque, angular momentum, magnetic force, area ⇒ cross. Trap: torque (N·m) and work (J = N·m) share a unit, yet torque is a vector and work a scalar — the unit alone never tells you which product to use.
Answer: Dot product → scalar, uses cos θ, commutative, gives projection/work; Cross product → vector, uses sin θ, anti-commutative, gives area/torque with right-hand-rule direction. (Descriptive — no key value.)

1The dot product of vectors A and B is maximum when the angle between them is: (0°)Dot product — maximum valueProperties of dot product / scalar product of two vectors — Class 11🟡 Similarb0°
Concept Since A·B = |A||B| cos θ and the magnitudes |A| and |B| are fixed positive numbers, the only thing that can vary the dot product is cos θ. So asking "when is A·B maximum?" is exactly the same as asking "when is cos θ maximum?" — a pure trigonometry question. Physically, the dot product is largest when the two vectors point the same way, because then the whole of B projects onto A with nothing wasted sideways.
Formula A·B = |A| |B| cos θ, with |A|, |B| in their own units (their product is the unit of the answer) and θ ∈ [0°, 180°] the angle between them. Over 0° to 180°, cos θ falls monotonically from +1 to −1, so its single maximum sits at the left-hand end, θ = 0°.
Solution
1. Hold |A| and |B| constant — they are given magnitudes and cannot change.Only the angle is free, so the extremum must come from the cos θ factor.
2. Maximise cos θ over the allowed range 0° ≤ θ ≤ 180°: the largest value is cos θ = +1, reached at θ = 0°.
3. Substitute back: (A·B)max = |A| |B| × 1 = |A| |B|, achieved when the vectors are parallel.
4. Test the other printed options by substitution:

 (b)        0 deg    +1         +|A||B|             MAXIMUM  <-- answer
 (c)      180 deg    -1         -|A||B|             minimum (most negative)
 (d)       45 deg    +0.707     +0.707|A||B|        less than (b)

The cross product is the mirror image: |A×B| is maximum at 90° and zero at 0°/180°. Trap: 180° gives the largest magnitude of a negative number — but "maximum" means greatest signed value, so it is 0°, not 180°.

Concept An angle between two vectors is only meaningful if both vectors actually point somewhere. The null (zero) vector has magnitude 0 and no defined direction — you cannot draw an arrow of zero length and say which way it aims. Consequently the angle between a zero vector and anything else is undefined. This also shows up algebraically: the standard formula for θ divides by |A||B|, and that denominator vanishes when either magnitude is zero, giving the indeterminate form 0/0.
Formula θ = cos⁻¹( (A·B) ÷ (|A| |B|) ), with A·B in (unit A)(unit B) and |A||B| in the same (unit A)(unit B), so the ratio is dimensionless — as any argument of cos⁻¹ must be. If |A| = 0 or |B| = 0, then A·B = 0 too, and the fraction becomes 0/0 — mathematically indeterminate, so no angle can be extracted.
Solution
1. Write the defining relation for the angle: cos θ = (A·B) ÷ (|A| |B|).
2. Suppose A = 0 (the null vector). Then |A| = 0 and A·B = 0 as well.Every component of A is zero, so every term AxBx + AyBy + AzBz is zero.
3. Substitute: cos θ = 0 ÷ (0 × |B|) = 0/0 — indeterminate, so θ cannot be evaluated.
4. Confirm geometrically: a zero vector is a point, not an arrow, so it has no direction to measure an angle from.An angle needs two directions; with only one direction available, the angle is meaningless.

5The dot product is commutative. This means: (A·B = B·A)Commutative propertyProperties of dot product / scalar product of two vectors — Class 11🟡 SimilarcA · B = B · A
Concept "Commutative" means the order of the two operands does not change the result. For the dot product this follows immediately from its definition, because the two ingredients it uses — the magnitudes and the angle between the vectors — are both symmetric in A and B. The angle from A to B is the same angle as from B to A, so nothing in the formula can notice which vector was written first. (The cross product is deliberately not commutative: A×B = −B×A, because its direction depends on the right-hand-rule order.)
Formula A·B = |A| |B| cos θ = B·A. Component form: A·B = AxBx + AyBy + AzBz (each term in unit-A × unit-B, e.g. N·m = J for force × displacement). Ordinary real-number multiplication is commutative (AxBx = BxAx), so every term is unchanged when the vectors swap places.

1. Read the statement: "the dot product is commutative" means swapping the operands leaves the answer alone, i.e. A·B = B·A.
2. Prove it from the definition: A·B = |A||B| cos θ and B·A = |B||A| cos θ.The angle between A and B is a single geometric quantity — it does not depend on which vector you name first.
3. Since |A||B| = |B||A| (scalars multiply commutatively), the two expressions are identical: A·B = B·A. That is option (c).

4Distinguish between dot product and cross productDot vs cross product6 Differences between Dot Product and Cross Product🟢 Exact—
Concept The two products answer opposite questions about the same pair of vectors. The dot product asks "how much do they point the same way?" and returns a plain number (scalar) built from cos θ. The cross product asks "how perpendicular are they?" and returns a whole new vector built from sin θ, aimed normal to their plane. One collapses the pair to a scalar projection; the other lifts it to an oriented area.
Formula A·B = |A||B| cos θ (scalar) and A×B = |A||B| sin θ n̂ (vector). cos θ peaks when the vectors are parallel; sin θ peaks when they are perpendicular — the single fact from which every difference below follows.
Solution
1. Nature of the result: the dot product is a scalar; the cross product is a vector.
2. Trig factor: dot uses cos θ, cross uses sin θ.So the dot product is maximum at 0°, the cross product is maximum at 90°.
3. Order (symmetry): the dot product is commutative, A·B = B·A; the cross product is anti-commutative, A×B = −B×A.
4. Special angles: for parallel vectors A·B = |A||B| (max) while A×B = 0; for perpendicular vectors A·B = 0 while |A×B| = |A||B| (max).
5. Geometry: A·B is the projection of one vector on the other times its length; |A×B| is the area of the parallelogram they span, direction along its normal.
6. Physics: work W = F·d and power P = F·v are dot products; torque τ = r×F and angular momentum L = r×p are cross products.

8Write all the properties of the cross product of two vectorsProperties of cross productProperties of Vector Product / Cross Product🟢 Exact—
Concept The cross product A × B is the vector whose magnitude is the area |A||B| sin θ of the parallelogram spanned by A and B and whose direction is the normal n̂ to that plane, fixed by the right-hand rule. Concept The magnitude of a cross product is |A||B| sin θ, so the whole answer is controlled by the angle between the vectors.
