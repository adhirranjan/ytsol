1 (i)Fill in the blank: The equation a sinx + b cosx = c, where |c| > √(a²+b²), has ______ (number of solutions).Amplitude bound, no solutionMax/min & range of a cosθ + b sinθ + c🟡 SimilarNo solution
Concept Any expression of the form a sinx + b cosx can be compressed into a single sine wave R sin(x+φ) whose amplitude is fixed. Because a sine wave never leaves the band between −R and +R, the left side can only ever take values inside [−R, R]. Asking it to equal a constant c that lies outside that band is asking for something the function can never produce, so the count of solutions is what we must decide.
Formula a sinx + b cosx = R sin(x+φ) with R = √(a²+b²). R is the amplitude, so the whole expression is confined to the closed interval [−R, R].
Solution
1. Amplitude of the left side: R = √(a²+b²), so a sinx + b cosx always lies in [−√(a²+b²), √(a²+b²)].This is the entire range of the combined sinusoid — it physically cannot go higher or lower.
2. We are told |c| > √(a²+b²), i.e. c sits strictly beyond both ends of that band.
3. For a solution we would need R sin(x+φ) = c, i.e. sin(x+φ) = c/R with |c/R| > 1 — impossible, since |sin| ≤ 1.No angle has a sine larger than 1 in magnitude.
4. Therefore the equation is satisfied by no value of x.
⚡ JEE Trick Compare |c| with √(a²+b²) on sight: |c| > √(a²+b²) ⇒ no solution; |c| = √(a²+b²) ⇒ a unique family; |c| < √(a²+b²) ⇒ infinitely many. Never try to solve the trig equation directly — the amplitude test settles it instantly.
Answer: no solution (the equation has zero solutions).

1 (iii)Maximum value of sinθ is ______Range of sinθWrite the maximum & minimum values of sinθ🟢 Exact1
Concept On the unit circle, sinθ is simply the y-coordinate of the point sitting at angle θ. That point rides around a circle of radius 1, so its height above the centre can never exceed the radius itself. The question is therefore asking: how high can a point on a radius-1 circle get? The trap is mixing this up with related quantities — the maximum of 2 sinθ is 2, of sin²θ is 1, of cosecθ does not even exist — here we want the bare sine.
Formula −1 ≤ sinθ ≤ 1 for every real θ. The sine is the y-coordinate of a point on the unit circle, and no coordinate of such a point can exceed the radius 1 in magnitude.
Solution
1. Definition: place θ in standard position; the terminal ray meets the unit circle at P(cosθ, sinθ).This is the modern definition of sine — it works for every angle, not just acute ones.
2. Bound: P lies on x² + y² = 1, so sin²θ ≤ sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 ⇒ −1 ≤ sinθ ≤ 1.
3. Attainability: at θ = 90° the point is exactly (0, 1), so sinθ = 1 is actually reached — the bound is not just theoretical.A supremum only counts as a maximum if some angle achieves it; θ = 90° does.
4. In fact sinθ = 1 for the whole family θ = 90° + 360°n (n ∈ ℤ), and never for any other angle. Hence maximum value = 1.
