✓		A particle moves in a circle of radius 2 m with a constant speed of 4 m/s. Calculate the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration. (A) 8 m/s² (B) 4 m/s² (C) 5 m/s² (D) 6 m/s²Centripetal acceleration v²/rA body of mass 5 kg revolving along a circle of radius 2 m with a uniform speed of 4 m/s🟡 SimilarA8 m/s²similarP10 · L3 · SolvedEx · Ex-2
Concept At constant speed the tangential acceleration is zero, so the whole acceleration is the centripetal (radial) part. It always points to the centre and changes only the direction of the velocity. Its magnitude depends on the speed and the radius, not the diameter.
Formula ac = v²/r = rω². The velocity vector of fixed length v turns at rate ω = v/r, so its rate of change is vω = v²/r.
Solution
1. Data: r = 2 m, v = 4 m/s (constant), so at = dv/dt = 0.Only the radial component survives.
2. ac = v²/r = (4)²/2 = 16/2 = 8 m/s².
3. Cross-check with ω: ω = v/r = 4/2 = 2 rad/s, ac = rω² = 2 × 4 = 8 m/s².
4. Total acceleration a = √(ac² + at²) = √(64 + 0) = 8 m/s², so the centripetal part is the whole answer.With at = 0 nothing else adds to it.
5. Direction: along the radius towards the centre; magnitude 8 m/s² → option (A).
 v = 4 m/s, r = 2 m
 a_c = v^2/r = 16/2 = 8 m/s^2
 check: w = v/r = 2 rad/s, r*w^2 = 2*4 = 8
⚡ JEE Trick Use the radius, never the diameter: putting r = 4 m gives 16/4 = 4 m/s², which is exactly distractor (B). And “constant speed” never means zero acceleration in a circle — it only kills the tangential part.
Answer: (A) 8 m/s².

2. Check: momentum m(10) + 0 = 0 + m(10) ✓; separation 10 = approach 10, so e = 1 ✓.
3. B now travels at 10 m/s and hits C (at rest): vB = 0, vC = 10 m/s.
4. A and B are both at rest and C moves away, so no further collision occurs.Nothing is moving towards anything else.

Concept When two bodies stick together the collision is perfectly inelastic (e = 0). Kinetic energy is not conserved, but linear momentum always is, because the collision forces are internal. So the compound body’s velocity is just the total momentum divided by the total mass. Momentum is a vector: equal speeds in opposite directions carry opposite signs.
Formula v = (m1u1 + m2u2)/(m1 + m2). Momentum before = momentum after, and after the collision both move with one common velocity.
Solution
1. Take the first body’s direction as positive: u1 = +V, u2 = −V, masses m and m.
2. Total momentum before = mV + m(−V) = 0.The two momenta are equal and opposite.
3. After sticking: (m + m)v = 0 ⇒ v = 0/(2m) = 0.
4. KE before = 2 × (1/2)mV² = mV²; KE after = 0, so all the KE is lost (heat, sound, deformation) — allowed, since e = 0.
5. The compound body stays at rest at the point of impact → option (D).
 p_before = m(+V) + m(-V) = 0
 p_after  = 2m * v = 0   ->  v = 0
 KE lost  = mV^2 (100 %)
⚡ JEE Trick Equal masses, equal and opposite velocities ⇒ the CM is already at rest, and sticking together leaves the body moving with the CM, i.e. at rest. Option (C) V/2 is the trap for anyone who forgets the second body is moving (mV/2m = V/2), (B) V comes from adding the momenta as magnitudes, (mV + mV)/2m, and (A) 2V is only the relative speed of approach.
Answer: (D) zero.

2. ω18 = (ω³)6 = 16 = 1.
3. (ω²)18 = ω36 = (ω³)12 = 1.
4. Sum = 1 + 1 = 2.Polar check: ω = cis(2π/3), ω18 = cis(12π) = 1.
5. Contrast: if the exponent were not a multiple of 3, e.g. ωn + ω2n with 3 ∤ n, the sum would be −1 (from 1 + ω + ω² = 0).
 w^18      = (w^3)^6  = 1
 (w^2)^18  = (w^3)^12 = 1
 sum = 2      (3 | n gives 2; otherwise -1)
⚡ JEE Trick ωn + ω2n equals 2 when n is a multiple of 3 and −1 otherwise. 18 is a multiple of 3, so the answer is 2 without expanding anything. Writing −1 is the trap for those who apply 1 + ω + ω² = 0 blindly.
