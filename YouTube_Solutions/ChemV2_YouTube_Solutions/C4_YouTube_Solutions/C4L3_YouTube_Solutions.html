1Which of the following species has the largest anionic radius? (Level-1)   (a) F⁻   (b) O²⁻   (c) S²⁻   (d) Cl⁻Comparing anion sizesIonic radius trends and the sizes of isoelectronic ions🟡 SimilarcS²⁻
Concept When anions come from different periods the number of occupied shells decides everything — a period-3 anion is always bigger than a period-2 anion. Only when two ions have the same electron count do you fall back on nuclear charge. Here F⁻ and O²⁻ both live in period 2 (10 electrons) while S²⁻ and Cl⁻ live in period 3 (18 electrons), so the winner must come from the period-3 pair, and within that pair the one with the smaller nuclear charge wins.
Key relation r ↑ as n ↑ (new shell) and, for a fixed electron count, r ↓ as Z ↑. Two rules, applied in that order: shells first, nuclear charge second. Reversing the order is the commonest source of wrong orderings.
Solution
1. Count electrons: F⁻ = 10, O²⁻ = 10, S²⁻ = 18, Cl⁻ = 18.
2. The 18-electron ions have three occupied shells against two, so both S²⁻ and Cl⁻ beat both F⁻ and O²⁻.
3. Between S²⁻ (Z = 16) and Cl⁻ (Z = 17): same 18 electrons, so the smaller nuclear charge holds them more loosely ⇒ S²⁻ is larger.Shannon radii: S²⁻ = 184 pm, Cl⁻ = 181 pm — a small but real gap, and the same reasoning that makes O²⁻ > F⁻.
4. Eliminate the distractors: (a) F⁻ 133 pm and (b) O²⁻ 140 pm are both period-2; (d) Cl⁻ 181 pm loses to S²⁻ on nuclear charge.

 O2-    10     2       8    140

 S2-    18     3      16    184     <-- answer (c)

2Arrange the following cations in increasing order of size: Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, Na⁺ (Level-2)   (a) Mg²⁺ < Al³⁺ < Na⁺   (b) Al³⁺ < Mg²⁺ < Na⁺   (c) Na⁺ < Mg²⁺ < Al³⁺   (d) Al³⁺ < Na⁺ < Mg²⁺Isoelectronic cationsIncreasing ionic radii for Na⁺, Mg²⁺, F⁻ and O²⁻🟡 SimilarbAl³⁺ < Mg²⁺ < Na⁺
Concept Na⁺, Mg²⁺ and Al³⁺ are the classic isoelectronic trio: each has lost its entire valence shell and is left with the neon configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. With the electron cloud held fixed at 10 electrons, the only variable is the number of protons pulling on it. More protons ⇒ tighter grip ⇒ smaller ion. So size falls exactly as Z rises.
Key relation All three have σ = 7(0.35) + 2(0.85) = 4.15, so Z* = Z − 4.15: Na⁺ 6.85, Mg²⁺ 7.85, Al³⁺ 8.85. Because σ is literally identical, the ranking of Z* — and hence the reverse ranking of size — is fixed by Z alone. No arithmetic is really needed.
Solution
1. Electron counts: Na⁺ 11 − 1 = 10, Mg²⁺ 12 − 2 = 10, Al³⁺ 13 − 3 = 10. All isoelectronic with Ne.
2. Nuclear charges: 11 < 12 < 13.
3. Higher Z ⇒ smaller radius, so size order is Al³⁺ < Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ ⇒ option (b).
4. Eliminate the distractors:
  • (a) and (c) run the trend backwards, treating higher charge as bigger.
  • (d) puts Na⁺ below Mg²⁺, contradicting Z = 11 < 12.
 ion    Z    e-   sigma   Z* = Z - 4.15   r / pm
 -----------------------------------------------------
 Na+   11    10   4.15        6.85          102
 Mg2+  12    10   4.15        7.85           72
 Al3+  13    10   4.15        8.85           53.5

⚡ JEE Trick For an isoelectronic series, remember one sentence: "same electrons, so more protons means smaller". Then read the charges left-to-right — the most positive ion is always the smallest, the most negative always the largest. The order N³⁻ > O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺ is worth memorising outright; almost every isoelectronic question in this lecture is a slice of it.
Answer: (b) Al³⁺ < Mg²⁺ < Na⁺.
3For the isoelectronic species O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, which has the smallest radius? (Level-3)   (a) O²⁻   (b) F⁻   (c) Na⁺   (d) Mg²⁺Smallest of an isoelectronic setSize of isoelectronic species — which of O²⁻, F⁻, Mg²⁺ is smallest?🟢 ExactdMg²⁺
Concept All four species carry ten electrons, so the electron–electron repulsion and the shielding are identical for every one of them. The nucleus is the only thing that changes, and it changes monotonically: O (8) < F (9) < Na (11) < Mg (12). The species with the most protons compresses its ten-electron cloud hardest and is therefore the smallest.
Key relation r ∝ 1 / Z* with Z* = Z − 4.15 constant-σ across the series. Z* values: O²⁻ 3.85, F⁻ 4.85, Na⁺ 6.85, Mg²⁺ 7.85. The nearly two-fold rise in Z* from O²⁻ to Mg²⁺ produces the near two-fold fall in radius, 140 pm → 72 pm.
Solution
1. Confirm isoelectronicity: O²⁻ 8 + 2 = 10, F⁻ 9 + 1 = 10, Na⁺ 11 − 1 = 10, Mg²⁺ 12 − 2 = 10. ✓
2. Rank nuclear charge: 8 < 9 < 11 < 12; the maximum is Mg (12).
3. Maximum Z ⇒ maximum Z* ⇒ minimum radius: Mg²⁺.
4. Eliminate the distractors:
  • (a) O²⁻ is the largest (140 pm) — lowest Z of the four.
  • (b) F⁻ (133 pm) is second largest.
  • (c) Na⁺ (102 pm) is small but Mg²⁺ (72 pm) is smaller.
 species  Z    e-   Z* = Z - 4.15   r / pm
 --------------------------------------------
 O2-      8    10       3.85         140   (largest)
 F-       9    10       4.85         133
 Na+     11    10       6.85         102
 Mg2+    12    10       7.85          72   <-- answer (d)  smallest
⚡ JEE Trick Ask only one question of an isoelectronic list: "which has the most protons?" That species is the smallest; the one with the fewest is the largest. You never need radii tables. The trap the paper sets is the word "smallest" versus "largest" — read it twice, because both extremes are always sitting in the option list, here as (a) and (d).
Answer: (d) Mg²⁺.
4Which statement explains why atomic radius decreases across a period? (Level-4)   (a) Number of shells decreases   (b) Nuclear charge increases while electrons enter same shell   (c) Effective nuclear charge decreases   (d) Electron shielding increasesPeriod trend — causeAtomic radius and its periodic trends explained🟡 SimilarbNuclear charge increases while electrons enter same shell
Concept Moving left to right along a period, each step adds one proton and one electron — but the electron goes into the same principal shell. A same-shell electron screens poorly (Slater gives it only 0.35), so it cancels barely a third of the added proton. The net effect is that Z* climbs by about 0.65 per element while n stays put, and the valence cloud is pulled inward. No shell is ever removed, which is what makes option (a) nonsense.
Key relation Per step across a period: ΔZ = +1, Δσ = +0.35, ΔZ* = +0.65, Δn = 0 ⇒ r ↓. Contrast this with a group step, where n increases by 1 and the new core shell screens almost perfectly, so Z* barely moves and the radius grows.
Solution
1. Identify what changes across a period: Z increases; the added electron enters the same shell.
2. Because same-shell screening is weak (0.35), Z* rises sharply — the exact wording of option (b).
3. Confirm with period 2: Li 152 → Be 111 → B 88 → C 77 → N 74 → O 66 → F 64 pm.
4. Eliminate the distractors:
  • (a) The number of shells is constant along a period, never decreasing.
  • (c) Z* increases, not decreases — this is the exact reversal of the truth.
  • (d) Shielding grows only by 0.35 per step, far too little to matter; and if shielding dominated, the atom would grow, not shrink.

 Z              3     4     5     6     7     8     9
 shells (n)     2     2     2     2     2     2     2   (unchanged)
 Z* approx    1.30  1.95  2.60  3.25  3.90  4.55  5.20  (+0.65 each)
 r / pm        152   111    88    77    74    66    64  (falls)
                                              <-- answer (b)
⚡ JEE Trick Reduce both periodic-size trends to a single contest: "across a period Z* wins (no new shell); down a group n wins (new shell)." Every explanation option is then graded in one second — anything claiming shells are lost across a period, or that Z* falls across a period, is out. Note that (c) and (d) are the same error stated two ways, a common examiner device to make the correct option look lonely.
Answer: (b) Nuclear charge increases while electrons enter same shell.

1Define ionic radius.Definition — ionic radiusIonic radius — what it is and how it is measured🟡 Similar—
Concept Just like atomic radius, ionic radius cannot be read off a single ion — you measure the internuclear distance in an ionic crystal and then partition it between the cation and the anion. Because the two ions differ in size, you cannot simply halve; instead one radius is fixed by an independent method (Pauling's or Goldschmidt's set) and the rest follow by subtraction. The result is a self-consistent table in which cation + anion radii reproduce the observed lattice spacing.
Definition Ionic radius is the effective distance from the nucleus of an ion up to which it exerts its influence on the electron cloud — operationally, the share of the internuclear distance between a cation and its neighbouring anion in an ionic crystal that is assigned to that ion. d(M⁺–X⁻) = rM⁺ + rX⁻. For NaCl the observed Na–Cl distance is 276 pm, and it is split as r(Na⁺) = 95 pm and r(Cl⁻) = 181 pm — not 138 each, because the anion is far bigger.
Solution
1. State the definition in one sentence, as above, and note that it is measured, like all radii, from an internuclear distance.
2. Give the additivity relation d = rcation + ranion and stress that the split is not equal.
3. Add the two standard consequences, which boards almost always want stated:
  • A cation is smaller than its parent atom (electrons lost, often a whole shell lost, and the remaining electrons feel a higher Z*).
  • An anion is larger than its parent atom (electrons gained, extra inter-electronic repulsion, unchanged Z).
4. Quote a worked illustration: Na 186 pm → Na⁺ 95 pm; Cl 99 pm → Cl⁻ 181 pm.The near halving on cation formation and near doubling on anion formation are the numbers examiners look for.
 NaCl crystal      Na+ ------ 276 pm ------ Cl-
                      |<-95->|<---181--->|

 d(Na+ - Cl-) = r(Na+) + r(Cl-) = 95 + 181 = 276 pm

 Na  186 pm  ->  Na+   95 pm     (cation shrinks)
 Cl   99 pm  ->  Cl-  181 pm     (anion swells)
⚡ JEE Trick A "define" question is worth full marks only if you supply definition + relation + one numerical example. Memorise the NaCl split 95 / 181 / 276 pm; it doubles as evidence for cation shrinkage and anion swelling, so one example serves three parts of the answer. Do not write "half the distance between two nuclei" — that is the covalent radius, and using it here is the single most common board-level error.
Answer: Descriptive — ionic radius is the share of the cation–anion internuclear distance in an ionic crystal attributed to that ion; d = rcation + ranion.
2What are isoelectronic species? Give examples.Definition — isoelectronicWhat are isoelectronic species?🟢 Exact—
Concept "Isoelectronic" strips away the identity of the element and keeps only the electron count. Because chemical size, shielding and orbital arrangement are all set by the electrons, isoelectronic species share an identical electron configuration — and therefore an identical shielding constant. What they do not share is nuclear charge, and that single difference is what generates the whole family of size-ordering questions in this lecture.
Definition Isoelectronic species are atoms, ions or molecules that possess the same number of electrons (and hence the same electronic configuration). Note the definition says nothing about protons — that is exactly why isoelectronic species have different sizes despite identical configurations.
Solution
1. State the definition as above.
2. Give the 10-electron family (neon core, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶): N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺.
3. Give the 18-electron family (argon core): P³⁻, S²⁻, Cl⁻, Ar, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Sc³⁺.
4. Mention molecular examples if asked to go further: CO, CN⁻, N₂ and NO⁺ are all 14-electron species; CH₄, NH₄⁺ and BH₄⁻ all have 10 electrons.Molecular isoelectronic sets are also isostructural — CO, CN⁻, N₂ and NO⁺ are all linear with a triple bond and bond order 3.
5. Close with the size consequence: within a family, radius falls as Z rises, e.g. N³⁻ (171) > O²⁻ (140) > F⁻ (133) > Na⁺ (102) > Mg²⁺ (72) > Al³⁺ (53.5) pm.

⚡ JEE Trick Build isoelectronic sets by walking outward from a noble gas: take Ne (10 e⁻) and step left adding negative charge, step right adding positive charge. This generates N³⁻ … Al³⁺ in seconds and never mis-counts. Distinguish carefully from isosteres (same number of atoms and electrons, e.g. N₂ and CO) and isodiaphers (same neutron−proton difference) — boards like to test the vocabulary, not just the idea.
Answer: Descriptive — species with the same number of electrons; e.g. N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ (10 e⁻) and P³⁻, S²⁻, Cl⁻, Ar, K⁺, Ca²⁺ (18 e⁻).
3Explain, why the size of isoelectronic species decreases with the increase in atomic number.Isoelectronic size trend — reasonAtomic radius across an isoelectronic series🟡 Similar—
Concept Fix the electron count and you fix the shielding. Slater's constant σ depends only on how many electrons sit in which groups, so every member of an isoelectronic series has exactly the same σ. The effective nuclear charge Z* = Z − σ then varies one-for-one with Z. Each extra proton is felt in full by every one of the ten (or eighteen) electrons, which contract towards the nucleus. Hence size falls monotonically as atomic number rises.
Key relation Z* = Z − σ with σ constant ⇒ ΔZ* = ΔZ, and r ∝ 1/Z*. Equivalently, the Z/e ratio rises. For the 10-electron set, Z/e goes 0.7 (N³⁻), 0.8 (O²⁻), 0.9 (F⁻), 1.1 (Na⁺), 1.2 (Mg²⁺), 1.3 (Al³⁺) — the higher the ratio, the more protons there are per electron and the tighter the cloud.
Solution
1. State that all members have the same number of electrons and hence the same electronic configuration and the same screening constant σ.
2. Since σ is constant while Z increases, Z* = Z − σ increases step for step with Z.
3. A larger Z* means a stronger net inward pull on every electron, so the electron cloud contracts and the radius falls.
4. Equivalent phrasing accepted by boards: the nuclear charge per electron (Z/e) increases, so the electrons are held more tightly.
5. Illustrate with the standard series and its numbers.Quoting at least three radii turns a two-mark answer into a full-mark answer; examiners want to see that the trend is monotonic, not just "decreases".
 10-electron series : sigma = 7(0.35) + 2(0.85) = 4.15  for every member

 species   N3-   O2-    F-    Na+   Mg2+   Al3+
 Z          7     8      9     11    12     13
 sigma    4.15  4.15   4.15   4.15  4.15   4.15   (identical)
 Z*       2.85  3.85   4.85   6.85  7.85   8.85   (rises with Z)
 r / pm    171   140    133    102    72     53.5 (falls)
 Z/e       0.7   0.8    0.9    1.1   1.2    1.3
⚡ JEE Trick The examiner is testing one sentence: "same electrons ⇒ same shielding ⇒ Z* tracks Z ⇒ size falls". Write it explicitly. Do not say "because the charge increases" without saying why that matters — the marks sit on the constancy of σ. This is also the answer to the fill-in-the-blank "Ca²⁺ has a smaller ionic radius than K⁺ because it has ______": a larger positive charge, i.e. a higher Z/e value.
Answer: Descriptive — identical electron count fixes σ, so Z* = Z − σ rises with Z; the stronger net nuclear pull contracts the cloud and the radius decreases.
4Explain: The ionic radii of F⁻ and Na⁺ are 133 and 98 pm, respectively.F⁻ vs Na⁺ radiiWhat determines the size of the isoelectronic species F⁻, Ne and Na⁺🟡 Similar—
Concept F⁻ and Na⁺ are the cleanest possible demonstration of the isoelectronic rule, because they sit on opposite sides of neon. Both end up with exactly ten electrons in the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶, so shielding is identical at σ = 4.15. Fluorine, however, has only 9 protons to hold those ten electrons while sodium has 11. Two extra protons spread over the same cloud is a large relative change, and the radius drops by roughly a quarter.
Key relation Z*(F⁻) = 9 − 4.15 = 4.85 versus Z*(Na⁺) = 11 − 4.15 = 6.85. Z*(Na⁺)/Z*(F⁻) ≈ 1.41, and indeed 133/98 ≈ 1.36 — the inverse proportionality r ∝ 1/Z* is quantitatively respectable here.
Solution
1. Establish isoelectronicity: F (9 e⁻) + 1 e⁻ = 10; Na (11 e⁻) − 1 e⁻ = 10. Both are [Ne].
2. Because the configurations match, σ is the same (4.15) for both.
3. Compare nuclear charges: F has Z = 9, Na has Z = 11. Sodium has two more protons pulling on the same ten electrons.
4. Therefore Z*(Na⁺) > Z*(F⁻), the electron cloud of Na⁺ is held far more tightly, and r(Na⁺) = 98 pm < r(F⁻) = 133 pm.
5. Add the complementary observation boards expect: F⁻ is also larger than the F atom (72 pm) because it gained an electron, while Na⁺ is much smaller than the Na atom (186 pm) because it lost its whole 3s shell.Mentioning the parent atoms shows you understand both effects — charge within an isoelectronic set, and shell loss/gain relative to the neutral atom.

 Na+       10    [Ne]       11   4.15   6.85      98

⚡ JEE Trick Whenever a board question hands you two numbers and asks "explain", the marks are for naming the controlling variable, not for repeating the numbers. Answer: Descriptive — F⁻ and Na⁺ are isoelectronic (10 e⁻, [Ne]) so σ is identical; Na⁺ has 11 protons against fluorine's 9, giving a larger Z* and hence the smaller radius (98 pm vs 133 pm).

Concept Cation formation removes electrons while leaving the nucleus untouched. Two things follow. First, the surviving electrons share the same number of protons among fewer of them, so each is pulled harder — Z* rises. Second, in many cases (Na → Na⁺, Mg → Mg²⁺) the electron removed was the only occupant of the outermost shell, so that shell vanishes entirely. Both consequences are captured by the single statement "loss of electrons reduces size", which is why (c) is the general answer.
Key relation Z unchanged, e⁻ decreased ⇒ σ decreased ⇒ Z* = Z − σ increased ⇒ r decreased. Na: σ falls from 8.80 (3s probe) to 4.15 (2p probe) and Z* leaps from 2.20 to 6.85, which is why the radius collapses from 186 pm to 95 pm.
Solution
1. Removing electrons leaves the proton count fixed, so the nucleus now controls fewer electrons — inter-electronic repulsion drops and Z* per electron rises.
2. The cloud contracts; if the last electron of a shell was removed, that shell disappears too and the drop is dramatic.
3. Option (c) states the cause in its general form and is correct for every cation.

Concept Four species built from two elements. The anions form an isoelectronic pair (both 10 electrons) and the neutral atoms form a period-2 pair. Anions always dwarf neutral atoms of the same period, so the two anions occupy the top two places and the two atoms the bottom two. Inside each pair, the lower nuclear charge wins — oxygen over fluorine both times.
Key relation Anions (10 e⁻): Z* = Z − 4.15 ⇒ O²⁻ 3.85 < F⁻ 4.85, so O²⁻ > F⁻. Neutrals: Z*(O) = 4.55 < Z*(F) = 5.20, so O > F. Radii: O²⁻ 140, F⁻ 133, O 66, F 64 pm. The gap between the anion block and the atom block is enormous — roughly a factor of two.

1. Split into blocks: anions {O²⁻, F⁻} both far larger than neutrals {O, F}.
2. Within the anions (isoelectronic): O (Z = 8) < F (Z = 9) ⇒ O²⁻ > F⁻.
3. Within the neutrals (period trend): O is left of F ⇒ O > F.
4. Concatenate: O²⁻ > F⁻ > O > F ⇒ option (b).
5. Eliminate the distractors:
  • (a) reverses the anion pair, putting F⁻ above O²⁻.
  • (c) and (d) put the neutral atoms above the anions, which is backwards by roughly a factor of two in radius.Options (c) and (d) can be struck without any calculation — no neutral period-2 atom is larger than a period-2 anion.

Concept Unlike the assertion–reason item in L-2 Level 6, here the reason is exactly the mechanism that produces the assertion. R states the general isoelectronic rule; A is that rule applied to a specific four-member set, with Mg²⁺ having the highest nuclear charge. When the reason is the general law and the assertion is its direct instance, option (a) is correct.
Statement A: r(Mg²⁺) is smallest among O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺. R: in an isoelectronic series, Z ↑ ⇒ r ↓. R holds because σ is fixed by the electron count, so Z* = Z − σ tracks Z one-for-one; that is the causal chain A depends on.
Solution
1. Test A. All four are 10-electron species; nuclear charges 8, 9, 11, 12. Mg has the most protons, so Mg²⁺ (72 pm) is indeed the smallest. A is TRUE.
2. Test R. With the electron count fixed, σ is constant and Z* = Z − σ rises with Z; the cloud contracts. R is TRUE.
3. Test causation. A is nothing more than R evaluated on this particular set — R names the variable (nuclear charge), the direction (increases) and the consequence (size decreases), which is precisely why Mg²⁺ is smallest. R explains A.
4. Therefore option (a).
5. Eliminate: (b) denies a causal link that plainly exists; (c) and (d) each declare a true statement false.

Key relation Group: n ↑ ⇒ r ↑. Period: Z* ↑ at fixed n ⇒ r ↓. Ionisation: cation < atom < anion. The last chain has no exceptions anywhere in the periodic table, which is what makes S3 unambiguously false.

1. S1 — radius increases down a group (Li 152 → Cs 265 pm). TRUE.

3. S3 — cations are smaller, not larger (Na 186 → Na⁺ 95 pm). FALSE.
4. S4 — anions are larger (Cl 99 → Cl⁻ 181 pm). TRUE.
5. So the correct set is S1, S2, S4 ⇒ option (a).

6Which of the following statements is correct? (a) A cation is always larger than its parent atom   (b) An anion is always smaller than its parent atom   (c) A cation is smaller and an anion is larger than their respective atoms   (d) Both cation and anion are larger than their parent atomsCation/anion size ruleWhy cations are smaller and anions larger in radii than their parent atoms🟡 SimilarcA cation is smaller and an anion is larger than their respective atoms
Concept One rule, stated four ways, only one of them right. Cations form by electron loss, so the surviving electrons share a fixed nuclear charge among fewer of them — repulsion drops, Z* rises, the ion contracts, and often a whole shell disappears. Anions form by electron gain, so repulsion and shielding both rise at constant Z, Z* falls and the ion expands. The two effects are exact mirror images.
Key relation r(cation) < r(atom) < r(anion), always, for every element. Slater view: removing an electron lowers σ (Z* up, size down); adding one raises σ (Z* down, size up). The nucleus is untouched in both cases.
Solution
1. Cation: fewer electrons at fixed Z ⇒ higher Z* per electron ⇒ smaller.
2. Anion: more electrons at fixed Z ⇒ lower Z* per electron ⇒ larger.
3. Option (c) states both halves correctly.
4. Eliminate the distractors:
  • (a) reverses the cation rule (Na 186 → Na⁺ 95 pm refutes it).
  • (b) reverses the anion rule (Cl 99 → Cl⁻ 181 pm refutes it).
  • (d) gets the anion right but the cation wrong — a half-truth, which is why it is offered.Half-true options like (d) catch candidates who verify only one clause before selecting. Always check both halves of a compound statement.

⚡ JEE Trick Carry the chain cation < atom < anion as a single unbreakable fact and test each compound option against both of its clauses. One refuting example per clause — Na/Na⁺ and Cl/Cl⁻ — is enough to demolish (a), (b) and (d) in seconds. The word "always" in an option is not a warning here: this rule genuinely has no exceptions.
Answer: (c) A cation is smaller and an anion is larger than their respective atoms.
