1State the de-Broglie equation and explain its significance (λ=h/p)de-Broglie conceptA-DUAL🟡 Similaraλ = h/p (de-Broglie relation)
Concept Louis de-Broglie proposed that every moving particle behaves as a wave — matter, like light, has a dual particle–wave nature. He reasoned by analogy with a photon: for light a photon of momentum p has wavelength λ = h/p, and de-Broglie asserted the same relation must hold for electrons, protons and any moving mass. The significance is huge: it explains electron diffraction, underpins the electron microscope, and gives Bohr's quantised orbits (2πr = nλ) a physical reason to exist.
Formula λ = h/p = h/mv: λ = wavelength (m), h = Planck's constant = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s, p = momentum = mv (kg m s⁻¹), m = mass (kg), v = speed (m s⁻¹). λ is inversely proportional to momentum, so heavy/fast objects have immeasurably small wavelengths while light electrons show observable wave effects.
Solution
1. Photon analogy: a photon has energy E = hν and, from relativity, E = pc, so equating gives hν = pc ⇒ p = hν/c = h/λ.ν/c = 1/λ, since c = νλ for a wave.
2. de-Broglie's leap: the relation p = h/λ is not special to light — rearranged, λ = h/p should apply to any particle of momentum p.
3. Writing momentum as p = mv gives the working form λ = h/mv.
4. Match to options: the de-Broglie relation is λ = h/p, which is option (a).
 quantity        symbol   meaning                unit
 wavelength       λ       matter-wave length     m
 Planck const.    h       6.626×10⁻³⁴            J s
 momentum         p=mv    mass × velocity        kg m s⁻¹
 ⇒ λ = h/p = h/mv
⚡ JEE Trick Remember de-Broglie in three equivalent skins — λ = h/p = h/mv = h/√(2mEk) — and pick whichever matches the data (momentum, speed, or kinetic energy) given. Trap: options may quote λ = h·p or λ = p/h; the correct form is h over momentum, so λ falls as the particle speeds up.
Answer: (a) λ = h/p (de-Broglie relation).

4Compare Δv to confine electron vs proton in Δx=1×10⁻¹⁵ m — which is feasibleUncertainty (confinement)watch?v=PxolIA_tGns🟡 SimilarbElectron 5.80 × 10¹⁰ m/s, Proton 3.16 × 10⁷ m/s → Proton feasible
Concept Confining a particle to a tiny region Δx forces a minimum spread in its momentum through Heisenberg's principle, and hence a minimum spread in velocity Δv = Δp/m. Squeeze the box to nuclear size (10⁻¹⁵ m) and this minimum speed becomes enormous. Because the electron is ~1800× lighter than the proton, the same Δp demands a far larger Δv for the electron — so large it exceeds the speed of light, which is physically impossible. The heavier proton, however, gets a sub-light Δv, so it can be confined.
Formula Δp ≥ h/(4π·Δx) and Δv = Δp/m: h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s, Δx = 1×10⁻¹⁵ m, me = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, mp = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg. A common Δp is set by Δx alone; dividing by each mass then gives each particle's Δv.
Solution
1. Minimum momentum spread: Δp = h/(4π·Δx) = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ ÷ (4π × 1×10⁻¹⁵).4π = 12.566, so denominator = 12.566×10⁻¹⁵ = 1.2566×10⁻¹⁴.
2. Δp = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ ÷ 1.2566×10⁻¹⁴ = 5.273×10⁻²⁰ kg m s⁻¹ (same for both particles).
3. Electron: Δv = Δp/me = 5.273×10⁻²⁰ ÷ 9.11×10⁻³¹ = 5.79×10¹⁰ m/s ≈ 5.80×10¹⁰.This is ~190× the speed of light (3×10⁸ m/s) — impossible, so an electron cannot be confined to 10⁻¹⁵ m.
4. Proton: Δv = Δp/mp = 5.273×10⁻²⁰ ÷ 1.67×10⁻²⁷ = 3.157×10⁷ m/s ≈ 3.16×10⁷.About 0.1c — large but sub-light, so confining a proton is feasible.

5Electron confined to Δx=1.00×10⁻¹⁵ m — estimate Δp; can it stay in nucleus?Uncertainty (nucleus)watch?v=BmMoxplNY2Q🟡 Similara5.27 × 10⁻²⁰ kg·m/s; electron cannot be localized inside the nucleus
Concept This is the classic "why electrons are not in the nucleus" argument. Pinning an electron inside a nucleus means fixing its position to within the nuclear radius (Δx ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m). Heisenberg's principle then forces a minimum momentum uncertainty Δp = h/(4πΔx). The resulting Δp implies a velocity spread far greater than light speed and an energy far above what nuclear binding can supply — so an electron simply cannot be localized inside the nucleus.
Formula Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π ⇒ Δpmin = h/(4π·Δx): h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s, Δx = 1.00×10⁻¹⁵ m. The equality gives the smallest momentum spread compatible with that confinement; any real state has at least this Δp.
Solution
1. Write the minimum: Δp = h/(4π·Δx) = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ ÷ (4π × 1.00×10⁻¹⁵).
2. Evaluate the denominator: 4π = 12.566, so 4π·Δx = 12.566×10⁻¹⁵ = 1.2566×10⁻¹⁴.
3. Divide: Δp = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ ÷ 1.2566×10⁻¹⁴ = 5.273×10⁻²⁰ kg m s⁻¹ ≈ 5.27×10⁻²⁰, option (a).
4. Interpret: Δv = Δp/me = 5.27×10⁻²⁰ ÷ 9.11×10⁻³¹ ≈ 5.8×10¹⁰ m/s ≫ c.A velocity spread far above light speed is impossible, so the electron cannot be confined to nuclear dimensions.

3Uncertainties associated with (a) a 1 mg mass (b) an electronUncertainty principleA-HUPNUM🟡 Similar—
Concept The question contrasts how tightly the uncertainty principle constrains a heavy object versus a light one. Writing Δp = m·Δv, the relation becomes Δx·Δv ≥ h/(4πm): the product of position and velocity uncertainties is inversely proportional to mass. 4What is the quantum mechanical model of an atom?Quantum mechanical modelA-QMODEL🟡 Similar—
Concept The quantum mechanical model is the modern picture of the atom that replaces Bohr's fixed circular orbits. It grows directly out of this lesson's two ideas — de-Broglie's matter waves (the electron is a wave, not a tiny planet) and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (its exact path cannot be known). Erwin Schrödinger combined these into a wave equation whose solutions describe the electron not by a trajectory but by a probability distribution in space.
Key relation Ĥψ = Eψ (Schrödinger equation), where ψ is the wave function and |ψ|² gives the probability of finding the electron per unit volume. Because uncertainty forbids a definite orbit, the model reports where the electron is likely to be (an orbital), not where it is.
Solution
1. Treat the electron as a standing matter wave bound to the nucleus (de-Broglie).Only certain wave patterns "fit," which is what quantises the energy.
2. Solve the Schrödinger equation Ĥψ = Eψ for the atom to obtain allowed wave functions ψ and their energies E.
3. Interpret |ψ|² as the electron's probability density; a region where this is high is an orbital.Heisenberg's principle rules out a precise path, so probability is the most we can state.
4. Each orbital is labelled by quantum numbers (n, l, m, s) that fall out of the solution and fix its size, shape and orientation.

Key point: the quantum mechanical model describes the electron by Schrödinger's wave function ψ; |ψ|² gives the probability of finding it, defining orbitals (not fixed orbits) labelled by quantum numbers n, l, m, s.

1What does Heisenberg's uncertainty principle state?Uncertainty statementA-HUP🟡 SimilarcΔx × Δp ≥ h/4π
Concept Heisenberg's uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to know a particle's exact position and exact momentum simultaneously. The more precisely one is fixed, the more the other must blur — this is not a measurement flaw but a fundamental property of matter waves. Mathematically the product of the two uncertainties has a lower bound, so it can never be driven to zero.
Formula Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π: Δx = uncertainty in position (m), Δp = uncertainty in momentum (kg m s⁻¹), h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s. The "≥" is essential — it sets a floor the product cannot fall below, so shrinking Δx forces Δp to grow.
Solution
1. State the relationship it quantifies: position and momentum cannot both be exact at once.
2. The exact statement bounds their product: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π.A product bound means precision in one variable is bought at the cost of the other.
3. Compare with options: only (c) gives the product form with the correct constant h/4π.
4. Reject look-alikes: h/2π, h/4πm or an equality "=" all misstate the bound.The constant is h/4π and the sign is ≥, not =.
 option           form              verdict
 (c) Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π  product ≥ h/4π    ✓ correct statement
 Δx·Δp ≥ h/2π      wrong constant    ✗
 Δx·Δp = h/4π      equality, not ≥   ✗

⚡ JEE Trick Lock in the exact triple: product, ≥ (not =), constant h/4π. Distractors change one of these three. Trap: h/4πm (with a stray mass) is the classic wrong option — the standard position–momentum relation has no mass in it (mass only appears in the Δx·Δv form).
Answer: (c) Δx × Δp ≥ h/4π.

3. Therefore Δp = m × Δv, which is option (a).
4. This is why substituting into Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π gives the working velocity form Δx·Δv ≥ h/4πm.
