1 (i)Fill in the blank: In a chemical reaction, the state of ______ is achieved when the concentrations of reactants and products remain ______ over time.Definition of equilibriumExpression For Equilibrium Constant From Law of Mass Action - Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Ch 6🟡 Similarequilibrium, constant
Concept The opening definition of the lecture: a reaction reaches the state of equilibrium when the rates of the forward and reverse reactions become equal, and the observable signature of that state is that the concentrations stop changing — they become constant.
Key relation rf = rb ⇒ [reactants], [products] constant with time The rate equality is the cause; the constancy of concentrations is the effect you can actually measure.
Solution
1. Read the sentence structure: “the state of ______ is achieved when the concentrations… remain ______ over time”. The first blank names a state, the second describes what the concentrations do.
2. Book p.16, first paragraph: “When the rates of the forward and reverse reactions become equal, the concentrations of the reactants and the products become constant. This is the stage of chemical equilibrium.”
3. So the first blank is equilibrium and the second is constant.
4. Do not write “equal” in the second blank. Equal concentrations require K = 1 and are an accident; constancy holds at every equilibrium whatever K is.
5. Nor is the answer “zero” for the second blank: nothing is consumed away at equilibrium, the amounts simply hold steady.
6. The three concentration–time graphs on pp.16–17 show all three possibilities — [A]eq > [B]eq, [A]eq = [B]eq and [A]eq < [B]eq — and in every one the curves flatten out. That flattening is the word “constant”.
 before eqm : [A] falling , [B] rising
 at eqm     : rf = rb
 after      : [A], [B] CONSTANT (flat curves)

 constant =/= equal
⚡ JEE Trick The pair “equilibrium … constant” is the standard fill-in of this chapter. Whenever a blank follows “concentrations remain ______”, the answer is constant, never equal.
Answer: the state of equilibrium is achieved when the concentrations remain constant over time.
1 (ii)Fill in the blank: The ______ states that at equilibrium, the rate of forward reaction equals the ______ of the reverse reaction.Law of chemical equilibriumLaw of Mass Action or Law of Chemical Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Ch 7 | CBSE/JEE/NEET🟡 Similarlaw of chemical equilibrium, rate
Concept The heading on book p.18 reads “THE LAW OF CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM (APPLICATION OF LAW OF MASS ACTION)”. That law is what asserts the equality of the two rates at equilibrium, and what the forward rate equals is the rate of the reverse reaction — not its rate constant.
Key relation kf[A][B] = kb[C][D]  ⇒  Kc = kf/kb = [C][D]/([A][B]) The left-hand side is the forward rate, the right-hand side the backward rate — two rates, set equal.
Solution
1. The first blank must name a law. The candidates in this lecture are the law of mass action and the law of chemical equilibrium.
2. The law of mass action (p.17) is a statement about rates in general: the rate is proportional to the product of the active masses raised to their stoichiometric powers. It says nothing about equilibrium on its own.
3. Applying it to a reversible reaction at equilibrium gives the law of chemical equilibrium, which is exactly the statement that the forward rate equals the reverse rate. That is the first blank.
4. The second blank: rate of forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction. Writing “rate constant” would be wrong — kf and kb are equal only if K = 1.
5. Check with the algebra: kf[A][B] = kb[C][D] is an equality of rates. Dividing gives kf/kb = Kc, which is 1 only in a special case.
6. Hence the answer: law of chemical equilibrium, rate.
 law of mass action : rate = k [A]^m [B]^n   (any time)
 applied at equilibrium:
      kf[A][B] = kb[C][D]    (rate = rate)
      => Kc = kf/kb = [C][D]/[A][B]
 that consequence is the LAW OF CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM
⚡ JEE Trick Keep the two laws apart: mass action describes a rate at any instant; chemical equilibrium describes the constant ratio at equilibrium. The second is derived from the first, so they are not synonyms — a fact the very next true/false in this Quick Recap tests.
Answer: the law of chemical equilibrium states that at equilibrium the rate of forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.
1 (iii)Fill in the blank: The numerical value of equilibrium constant depends upon the ______ at which the equilibrium is established.What K depends onThe effect of conditions on the equilibrium constant🟡 Similartemperature⚠
Concept K is a constant with respect to almost everything: concentrations, catalysts, inert gases and the direction from which equilibrium was approached. The one thing it does depend on is the temperature at which the equilibrium is set up.
Key relation Kc = kf/kb, and kf, kb are Arrhenius functions of T alone The two activation energies differ by ΔH, so heating changes kf and kb by different factors and their ratio moves.
Solution
1. The grammar of the blank settles it: “the ______ at which the equilibrium is established” must be a condition of the experiment, not a property of the reaction.
2. Book p.19 lists the factors K is independent of: initial concentrations of the reactants, presence of a catalyst, direction from which equilibrium was attained, and presence of inert materials.
3. That leaves only temperature. The book states on p.18 that Kc “has a definite value for every chemical reaction at a given temperature regardless of the concentration of the reactants”.
4. Mechanistically: K = kf/kb, and each rate constant obeys k = Ae−Ea/RT. The forward and backward activation energies differ by ΔH, so heating multiplies them by different factors and the ratio changes.
5. Cross-check inside this very Quick Recap: item 2(vi), “A change in temperature changes the value of the equilibrium constant”, is keyed True.
6. So the blank is temperature. “Extent” is not a condition at all — it is what a large or small K tells you, and it is the wording of Column-II entry (t) further down this same Quick Recap.
 K independent of : initial conc., catalyst,
                    direction of approach, inert gas
 K depends on     : TEMPERATURE  (and the equation
                    as written)

 K = kf/kb ; both k's = A e^(-Ea/RT)
⚡ JEE Trick Only two things change a numerical K: the temperature, and rewriting the balanced equation (reversing it, halving it, adding two equilibria). Everything else on an exam list — pressure, catalyst, inert gas, starting amounts — leaves K alone.
Answer: the numerical value of the equilibrium constant depends upon the temperature at which the equilibrium is established. ⚠ The key prints “extent”.
1 (iv)Fill in the blank: For a gaseous reaction, the equilibrium constant in terms of partial pressure is denoted by ______.KpRelation Between Kp and Kc - Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 6🟡 SimilarKp
Concept Book p.19 defines two flavours of the equilibrium constant by the quantity used to express the amounts: Kc when molar concentrations are used, Kp when partial pressures are used. Only gases have partial pressures, so Kp is defined for gas-phase reactions.
Key relation for m1A + m2B ⇌ n1C + n2D:   Kp = (pC)n1(pD)n2 / [(pA)m1(pB)m2] The subscript p is a reminder of the quantity substituted, not of a different physical law.
Solution
1. The blank follows “For a gaseous reaction, the equilibrium constant in terms of partial pressure is denoted by…”, so it wants a symbol.
2. Section “Types of equilibrium constant” on p.19 gives exactly two: (i) KC, in terms of concentration, defined for gas phase and solution phase; (ii) KP, in terms of partial pressure, defined for reactions in the gas phase.
3. Since the stem says partial pressure, the answer is Kp.
4. Its unit follows the same rule as Kc: (atm)Δng, where Δng = moles of gaseous product − moles of gaseous reactant.
5. Worth carrying forward: the two constants are linked by Kp = Kc(RT)Δng, the Board question at the end of this lecture.

 Kc : concentrations  (gas OR solution)
 Kp : partial pressures (gas only)

 Kp = Kc (RT)^dn(g)
 dn(g) = 0  =>  Kp = Kc
⚡ JEE Trick A solution-phase equilibrium has no Kp at all. If a question offers Kp for an aqueous or all-liquid system, that option is wrong before you do any arithmetic.
Answer: Kp — the equilibrium constant written in terms of partial pressures.
1 (v)Fill in the blank: For a reaction, if the concentration of reactants decreases over time, the reaction is ______.Direction of net reactionChemical Equilibria and Reaction Quotients🟡 Similarproceeding (in the forward direction)
Concept A falling reactant concentration means the forward reaction is outrunning the backward one, so a net change is still going on. The system has not yet reached equilibrium — the reaction is still proceeding in the forward direction.
Key relation d[reactant]/dt < 0 ⇒ rf > rb ⇒ net forward reaction, Q < K At equilibrium the derivative would be exactly zero, so a non-zero slope is proof that equilibrium has not arrived.
Solution
1. Net rate of change of a reactant = −(rf − rb). If the concentration decreases, rf − rb > 0.
2. So the forward reaction dominates and product is accumulating: the reaction is running forward.
3. Look at the graphs on pp.16–17: the [A] curve slopes downwards up to the dashed line marked “Eq.”, and is flat after it. The sloping part is the “proceeding” stage.
4. So the blank is proceeding — i.e. the reaction is still going forward and has not attained equilibrium.
5. In the language of the reaction quotient (taken up in the later lectures of C-7), this is the region Q < K.
6. The moment the reactant concentration stops falling, the two rates have matched and the system is at equilibrium.
 [R] falling  -> rf > rb -> net forward, Q < K
 [R] flat     -> rf = rb -> EQUILIBRIUM, Q = K
 [R] rising   -> rf < rb -> net backward, Q > K
⚡ JEE Trick The sign of the slope of any concentration–time curve tells you the direction of net reaction instantly. A flat curve is the only signature of equilibrium — height and crossing points tell you nothing.
Answer: the reaction is proceeding (running in the forward direction; equilibrium has not yet been reached).
1 (vi)Fill in the blank: If the equilibrium constant Kc for a reaction is significantly greater than 1, it means the ______ are favoured at equilibrium.Magnitude of KEqulibrium | Chemical Equilibrium 02 | Equilibrium Constant Kp and Kc | IIT JEE MAINS /NEET🟡 Similarproducts
Concept K is a products-over-reactants ratio, so its size is a direct readout of which side the equilibrium mixture sits on. Kc >> 1 means the numerator dominates, i.e. the products are favoured.
Key relation Kc = [products]ν/[reactants]ν; K >> 1 ⇒ product side, K << 1 ⇒ reactant side Because the ratio is fixed at a given temperature, a big number can only mean a big numerator relative to the denominator.
Solution
1. Write the definition from p.18: Kc = [C][D]/([A][B]) for A + B ⇌ C + D — products on top.
2. If Kc is much larger than 1, the product concentrations must be much larger than the reactant concentrations at equilibrium.
3. So the reaction has gone far towards completion, and the blank is products.
4. The mirror case: Kc << 1 means the reactants dominate and very little product forms.
5. K near 1 means comparable amounts of both, which is the graph (b) case on p.16 where [A]eq = [B]eq.
6. This is what characteristic (6) of physical equilibria on the earlier lecture called “the magnitude of the equilibrium constant represents the extent to which the process proceeds”.
 Kc >> 1  : products dominate  (near completion)
 Kc ~ 1   : comparable amounts
 Kc << 1  : reactants dominate (barely proceeds)

 K measures EXTENT, not speed
⚡ JEE Trick K tells you how far, never how fast. A huge K with a huge activation energy gives a reaction that will eventually be almost complete but may take years — the classic diamond-to-graphite example.
Answer: if Kc >> 1, the products are favoured at equilibrium.

1 (viii)Fill in the blank: The value of the equilibrium constant is ______ by the presence of a catalyst.Catalyst and KWhy equilibrium constant of a reaction does not change in the presence of a catalyst ?🟢 Exactunaffected
Concept A catalyst provides a new, lower-energy path for the reaction — but it provides that path in both directions equally. It multiplies kf and kb by the same factor, so their ratio, which is K, is unaffected.
Key relation K = kf/kb; catalyst: kf → xkf, kb → xkb ⇒ K unchanged The catalyst lowers the activation energy of the forward and reverse steps by the same amount, because they share one transition state.
Solution
1. Book p.19 lists the presence of a catalyst among the factors the value of the equilibrium constant is independent of.
2. Characteristic (vi) on p.17 says the same in words: “Equilibrium is not affected by the presence of catalyst. The catalyst only helps in attaining equilibrium rapidly.”
3. The reason is structural. Forward and reverse reactions pass through the same transition state, so a catalyst that lowers its energy lowers Ea for both by the same amount.
4. Both rate constants are therefore multiplied by the same factor eΔEa/RT, and K = kf/kb is untouched.
5. What a catalyst does change is the time taken to reach equilibrium — the plateau appears sooner, at the same height.
6. Hence the blank is unaffected.
 without catalyst : Ea(f) , Ea(b)
 with catalyst    : Ea(f)-x , Ea(b)-x   (same x)

 kf and kb both multiplied by e^(x/RT)
 K = kf/kb  ->  UNCHANGED
⚡ JEE Trick “Catalyst” in an equilibrium question is nearly always a distractor. It changes only the time axis, never the equilibrium position and never K — and this lecture asks it four separate times (QR 1(viii), QR 2(vii), L4 Q10, L5 Q1).
Answer: the value of the equilibrium constant is unaffected by the presence of a catalyst.

Concept By convention the equilibrium expression is written with pure solids and pure liquids omitted, because their active masses are fixed constants. The book calls this the standard way of writing K, and sets [solid] = 1 and [pure liquid] = 1.
Key relation molar concentration of a pure phase = w/(M×V) = d/M = constant ⇒ taken as unity Density and molar mass are both fixed for a pure substance, so its “concentration” cannot change however much of it you have.
Solution
1. Book p.17 under “Pure Solid & Pure Liquid”: molar concentration M = w/(M×V) = d/M = constant, since the density and molar mass of a pure substance are constants.
2. Convention 1 on p.19: “the concentration of all solids are taken as unity, i.e. [Solid] = 1”. Convention 2 on p.20 does the same for a pure liquid in equilibrium with a gas.
3. Worked example on p.19: for CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g), Kc = [CaO][CO2]/[CaCO3] collapses to Kc = [CO2], and Kp = pCO2.
4. So the expression from which pure solids and liquids have been dropped is the standard (conventional) form of the equilibrium constant. Answers such as “simplified” or “conventional” carry the same meaning.
5. Convention 3 extends the same reasoning to a solvent present in large excess — [H2O] in an aqueous equilibrium is absorbed into K.
6. Note the practical consequence: adding more solid CaCO3 to the vessel does not shift the equilibrium at all, because it does not appear in K.
 CaCO3(s) <=> CaO(s) + CO2(g)

1 (x)Fill in the blank: A ______ in the concentration of a reactant or product will cause the system to shift in order to restore equilibrium.Disturbing an equilibriumLe Chatelier's Principle | Effect of Concentration | Effect of Pressure | Effect of Temperature🟡 Similarchange
Concept Any change in the concentration of a species destroys the equality rf = rb. The system then runs net forward or net backward until the rates match again — the seed of Le Chatelier’s principle.
Key relation disturb a concentration ⇒ Q ≠ K ⇒ net reaction until Q = K again K is fixed at that temperature, so the mixture has no choice but to move back onto the value of K.
Solution
1. The blank sits in “A ______ in the concentration of a reactant or product will cause the system to shift…”, so it must be a noun describing a disturbance.
2. Add reactant, and the forward rate jumps above the backward rate; net forward reaction follows until the two match again.
3. Remove product, and the backward rate falls below the forward rate; again a net forward reaction restores the balance.
4. Either way the trigger is a change in a concentration, so that is the answer. “Increase” alone would be too narrow — a decrease works just as well.
5. Crucially the system settles at new concentrations, but on the same K, because the temperature did not change.
6. That is why concentration changes shift the position of an equilibrium while leaving its constant alone — a distinction this lecture keeps testing.
 add reactant   : Q < K -> net forward
 add product    : Q > K -> net backward
 remove product : Q < K -> net forward

 in every case K itself is unchanged (T fixed)
⚡ JEE Trick Separate “shifts the equilibrium” from “changes K”. Concentration, pressure and catalyst can shift the position; only temperature changes the number K.
Answer: a change in the concentration of a reactant or product causes the system to shift so as to restore equilibrium.
2 (i)True or false: The equilibrium state can only be achieved in a closed system.Closed-system requirementEquilibrium can be achieved only in open vessel. | 11 | CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM | CHEMISTRY | CENGA…🟢 ExactTrue
Concept Characteristic (ii) of chemical equilibrium on book p.17 is stated flatly: “Equilibrium is possible only in closed system.” If matter can leave the vessel, the backward reaction is starved and the rates can never equalise.
Key relation closed system ⇒ no species escapes ⇒ rb can grow until rb = rf “Closed” forbids matter crossing the boundary but still allows heat in or out, so heating a sealed tube is fine.
Solution
1. Equilibrium needs both directions running. The backward reaction can only run on the products that are still in the vessel.
2. In an open vessel a gaseous product diffuses away, so its concentration is pinned near zero and rb stays far below rf.
3. With rf permanently greater than rb, a net forward reaction continues until the reactant is exhausted — completion, not equilibrium.
4. The previous lecture’s worked case is the standard illustration: CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g)↑ in an open vessel goes to completion, while the same change in a closed container reaches equilibrium.
5. So the statement matches the book’s own characteristic and is True.
6. Strictly, the requirement bites hardest when a gas is involved; a purely condensed-phase equilibrium such as an esterification in a flask loses nothing to the air. The chapter states the rule in the blanket form, and that is the sense in which it is True.
 open   : product escapes -> rb pinned low
          rf > rb always  -> COMPLETION
 closed : product stays   -> rb rises to meet rf
                          -> EQUILIBRIUM
⚡ JEE Trick Scan any equilibrium stem for the words “open vessel”. For a gas-producing reaction that phrase alone rules out equilibrium, whatever the rest of the question says.
Answer: True — the equilibrium state can only be achieved in a closed system.
2 (ii)True or false: The equilibrium constant for a reaction is dependent on the concentration of the reactants and products.K independent of concentrationsWhich of the following statements are correct regarding the equilibrium constant? (A) Its value…🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept The equilibrium constant is fixed by the temperature alone. Start with any amounts you like — the individual concentrations at equilibrium will differ, but the particular combination that makes up K comes out the same every time.
Key relation Kc = kf/kb, a ratio of two temperature-dependent constants Neither rate constant contains a concentration, so K cannot contain one either.
Solution
1. Book p.18: “Kc has a definite value for every chemical reaction at a given temperature regardless of the concentration of the reactants.”
2. Book p.19 repeats it in list form: the value of the equilibrium constant is independent of the initial concentrations of the reactants involved.
3. The algebra shows why. Equating rates gives kf[A][B] = kb[C][D], so K = kf/kb = [C][D]/([A][B]). The right-hand side is built out of concentrations, but it is forced to equal the left-hand side, which is not.
4. So changing the starting amounts changes each individual equilibrium concentration but leaves the ratio unmoved.

6. Hence the statement is False — K depends on temperature, not on the concentrations.

 start 6 M A -> eqm 2 M A , 4 M B  -> ratio 2
 start pure B (3 M) -> 1 M A , 2 M B -> ratio 2

 concentrations differ ; K does not
⚡ JEE Trick Distinguish “K depends on the concentrations” (false) from “K is calculated from the equilibrium concentrations” (true). The first is about causation, the second about arithmetic, and exam statements exploit the gap.
Answer: False — K is independent of the initial or equilibrium concentrations; it depends only on temperature.
2 (iii)True or false: In an equilibrium mixture, the concentration of reactants is always equal to the concentration of products.Constant vs equalThe concentrations of reactants and products become equalhen equilibrium is attained.🟢 ExactFalse
Concept The perennial constant-versus-equal confusion. At equilibrium the concentrations become constant; whether they are also equal depends entirely on the value of K, and equality is the exception, not the rule.
Key relation [products]/[reactants] = K, so equality requires K = 1 The word “always” in the statement is what makes it false — one counter-example is enough.
Solution
1. For A ⇌ B, equilibrium means kf[A] = kb[B], so [B]/[A] = kf/kb = K.
2. [A] = [B] therefore holds only when K = 1, which is one particular number out of infinitely many.
3. The book draws all three cases explicitly on pp.16–17: graph (a) with [A]eq > [B]eq and K < 1; graph (b) with [A]eq = [B]eq and K = 1; graph (c) with [A]eq < [B]eq and K > 1.

5. A real example: for the Haber process at 500 K the equilibrium mixture is overwhelmingly N2 and H2, with only a little NH3. Concentrations nowhere near equal, and yet a perfectly good equilibrium.
6. So the statement is False. What is always true is that the concentrations stop changing.

 graph (b) : Keq = 1 , [A] = [B]
 graph (c) : Keq > 1 , [A] < [B]

2 (v)True or false: The law of mass action is another name for the law of chemical equilibrium.Law of mass actionLaw of Mass Action or Law of Chemical Equilibrium | Class 11 Chemistry Ch 7 | CBSE/JEE/NEET🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept The two laws are related but not identical. The law of mass action is a statement about the rate of a reaction at any instant; the law of chemical equilibrium is what you get by applying it to a reversible reaction at equilibrium.
Key relation mass action: rate = k[A]m[B]n  →  at equilibrium: Kc = [C]p[D]q/([A]m[B]n) One is a rate law, the other a constant-ratio law; the arrow between them is the derivation on p.18.
Solution
1. The law of mass action (Guldberg and Waage, 1864, p.17): at a given temperature the rate of a reaction is proportional to the product of the active masses of the reactants, each raised to its stoichiometric power.
2. Notice what it is about — a single rate, at any moment, equilibrium or not. It applies just as well one second after mixing.
3. The book’s own heading on p.18 is “THE LAW OF CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM (APPLICATION OF LAW OF MASS ACTION)”. The bracket says it plainly: one is applied to obtain the other.
4. The derivation: write the mass-action rate for each direction, set them equal at equilibrium, and rearrange to get Kc = [C][D]/([A][B]). That constant-ratio statement is the law of chemical equilibrium.
5. So they stand in the relation general-principle → special consequence, not synonym → synonym.
6. Hence the statement is False.
 LAW OF MASS ACTION
   rate(f) = kf [A][B]      any instant
   rate(b) = kb [C][D]

 set equal at equilibrium  ->
 LAW OF CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM
   Kc = [C][D]/[A][B] = kf/kb
⚡ JEE Trick Ask “when does this law apply?”. Mass action: always. Chemical equilibrium: only at equilibrium. Two laws with different domains cannot be the same law.
Answer: False — the law of chemical equilibrium is an application of the law of mass action, not another name for it.
2 (vi)True or false: A change in temperature changes the value of the equilibrium constant.Temperature dependence of KThe effect of conditions on the equilibrium constant🟡 SimilarTrue
Concept Temperature is the one variable that genuinely changes the number K. It does so because it changes kf and kb by different factors, and K is their ratio.
Key relation k = Ae−Ea/RT, with Ea(forward) − Ea(backward) = ΔH Because the two activation energies are unequal whenever ΔH ≠ 0, heating cannot scale them by the same factor.
Solution
1. Book p.19 lists what K is independent of: initial concentrations, catalyst, direction of approach, inert materials. Temperature is conspicuously absent from that list.
2. Book p.18 makes it explicit: Kc has a definite value “at a given temperature”. The proviso implies a different value at a different temperature.

Concept A three-part answer: define active mass, state the law, then write it mathematically for a general reaction. The statement is on book p.17 and the mathematical form on p.18.
Key relation for m1A + m2B → products: rate = k[A]m1[B]m2 The exponents are the stoichiometric coefficients of the balanced equation, which is what “raised to powers numerically equal to the number of molecules” means.
Solution
1. Active mass. The mass of a substance which affects the rate of reaction, i.e. which takes an active part in it. For a solution or a gas it is the molar concentration, written in square brackets: [A] = W/(MW×V).
2. Statement (Guldberg and Waage, 1864). At a given temperature, the rate of a reaction at a particular instant is proportional to the product of the active masses of the reactants at that instant, each raised to a power equal to the number of its molecules in the balanced equation.
3. Mathematical form. For m1A + m2B → products, rate ∝ [A]m1[B]m2, i.e. rate = k[A]m1[B]m2, where k is the rate (velocity) constant.

5. Application. Applying the law separately to the forward and backward reactions of A + B ⇌ C + D and equating the rates at equilibrium gives Kc = [C][D]/([A][B]) = kf/kb. This is the law of chemical equilibrium.

Concept An ICE table in solution. FeCl3 and NH4SCN are the sources of Fe3+ and SCN−, and the amount of complex formed tells you how much of each ion was consumed.

Concept An assertion–reason pair in which both statements are true, so the whole question turns on whether the second explains the first. Statement-2 defines equilibrium; Statement-1 is about the stage before it.
Key relation at t = 0: [product] = 0 ⇒ rb = 0 ⇒ rf > rb The real reason for Statement-1 is the absence of product at the start, not the definition of equilibrium.
Solution
1. Statement-1. At the initial stage rf > rb. Starting from pure reactants, the forward rate is at its maximum while the backward rate is zero, since no product yet exists. Book p.16 says exactly this. True.
2. Statement-2. When rf = rb, the reaction is at equilibrium. That is the definition on p.16. True.
3. Now the explanation test. Statement-2 tells you what happens at the end — the condition that defines equilibrium.
4. Statement-1 is about the beginning, and its cause is that [product] = 0 initially, so kb[product] = 0.
5. Knowing that equal rates define equilibrium does not tell you why the forward rate starts out larger; you need the extra fact that the vessel began with no product.
6. So both statements are true but Statement-2 does not explain Statement-1: option (b).
7. Rejecting the rest: (a) requires an explanatory link that is absent; (c) and (d) each declare one of the two statements false, and both are true. Note that Statement-1 would also fail if the vessel had been charged with pure product — then rb > rf initially — which is a further sign that the two claims are logically independent.
