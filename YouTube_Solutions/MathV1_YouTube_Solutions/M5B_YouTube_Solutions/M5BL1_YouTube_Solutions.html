2Match Column-I (2sinAcosB, 2cosAsinB, 2cosAcosB, 2sinAsinB) with Column-II (their product-to-sum expansions).Product-to-sum formulae (match)Product formulae: 2sinAcosB, 2cosAsinB, 2cosAcosB, 2sinAsinB🟡 SimilarA→s, B→r, C→q, D→p
Concept All four product-to-sum formulae come from adding or subtracting the two compound-angle expansions of the same pair. Write sin(A+B) and sin(A−B) side by side and add/subtract; do the same for cos(A+B) and cos(A−B). Nothing needs to be memorised separately.
Formula sin(A±B) = sinA cosB ± cosA sinB; cos(A±B) = cosA cosB ∓ sinA sinB.
Solution
1. Write the sine pair: sin(A+B) = sinA cosB + cosA sinB, and sin(A−B) = sinA cosB − cosA sinB.
2. Add them: sin(A+B) + sin(A−B) = 2 sinA cosB, so 2 sinA cosB = sin(A+B) + sin(A−B).The cosA sinB terms cancel, leaving twice the sinA cosB term.
3. Subtract them: sin(A+B) − sin(A−B) = 2 cosA sinB, so 2 cosA sinB = sin(A+B) − sin(A−B).Now the sinA cosB terms cancel instead.
4. Write the cosine pair: cos(A+B) = cosA cosB − sinA sinB, and cos(A−B) = cosA cosB + sinA sinB.
5. Add them: cos(A+B) + cos(A−B) = 2 cosA cosB, so 2 cosA cosB = cos(A+B) + cos(A−B).
6. Subtract (difference minus sum, to keep the sign positive): cos(A−B) − cos(A+B) = 2 sinA sinB, so 2 sinA sinB = cos(A−B) − cos(A+B).Note the reversed order — this is the one sign students most often get wrong.

Concept Convert both given sums into products. Each factorises into the same half-angle factor sin((α+β)/2), so dividing one equation by the other kills that unknown factor and leaves a pure tangent of the half-difference.
Formula sinC + sinD = 2 sin((C+D)/2) cos((C−D)/2); cosC − cosD = −2 sin((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2).
Solution
1. Transform the first datum: sinα + sinβ = 2 sin((α+β)/2) cos((α−β)/2) = a.
2. Transform the second datum: cosα − cosβ = −2 sin((α+β)/2) sin((α−β)/2) = b.Mind the minus sign — cosC − cosD carries a leading −2.
3. Divide equation (2) by equation (1): b/a = [−2 sin((α+β)/2) sin((α−β)/2)] / [2 sin((α+β)/2) cos((α−β)/2)].
4. The common factor 2 sin((α+β)/2) cancels top and bottom: b/a = −sin((α−β)/2)/cos((α−β)/2) = −tan((α−β)/2).This is why the half-sum angle never appears in the answer.

Answer: tan((α−β)/2) = −b/a, i.e. p = −1, q = b, r = a.
4Match Column-I (cosC−cosD, tanC−tanD, sinC−sinD, cotC+cotD) with Column-II (their sum-to-product forms).Sum-to-product formulae (match)Sum-to-product identity proof — sinA+sinB🟡 SimilarA→q, B→s, C→p, D→r
Concept Two of these (cosC−cosD, sinC−sinD) are genuine sum-to-product results, obtained by substituting C = A+B, D = A−B into the product formulae. The other two (tanC−tanD, cotC+cotD) are not sum-to-product forms at all — they are handled by taking a common denominator, which produces sin(C∓D) over a product.
Formula cosC − cosD = −2 sin((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2); sinC − sinD = 2 cos((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2); tanC − tanD = sin(C−D)/(cosC cosD); cotC + cotD = sin(C+D)/(sinC sinD).
Solution
1. Set C = A+B and D = A−B, so that A = (C+D)/2 and B = (C−D)/2.This substitution converts every product formula into a sum-to-product formula.
2. cosC − cosD: from 2 sinA sinB = cos(A−B) − cos(A+B) we get cosD − cosC = 2 sinA sinB, so cosC − cosD = −2 sinA sinB = −2 sin((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2).
3. sinC − sinD: from 2 cosA sinB = sin(A+B) − sin(A−B) we get sinC − sinD = 2 cosA sinB = 2 cos((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2).
4. tanC − tanD: take a common denominator — tanC − tanD = sinC/cosC − sinD/cosD = (sinC cosD − cosC sinD)/(cosC cosD).
5. The numerator is exactly the sin(C−D) expansion, so tanC − tanD = sin(C−D)/(cosC cosD).
6. cotC + cotD: similarly cosC/sinC + cosD/sinD = (cosC sinD + sinC cosD)/(sinC sinD), and the numerator is the sin(C+D) expansion, so cotC + cotD = sin(C+D)/(sinC sinD).Note it is sin(C+D) on top, not cos — adding cotangents still produces a sine.
⚡ JEE Trick Do not force tan or cot differences into the sum-to-product mould — they never fit. The rule is: sin/cos sums → half-angle products; tan/cot combinations → common denominator, giving sin(C∓D) over the product of the two denominators.
Answer: cosC−cosD = −2 sin((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2); tanC−tanD = sin(C−D)/(cosC cosD); sinC−sinD = 2 cos((C+D)/2) sin((C−D)/2); cotC+cotD = sin(C+D)/(sinC sinD).
