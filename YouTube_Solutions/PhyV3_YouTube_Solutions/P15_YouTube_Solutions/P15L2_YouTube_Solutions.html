1On the surface of the liquid in equilibrium, molecules of the liquid possess (a) maximum potential energy (b) minimum potential energy (c) maximum kinetic energy (d) minimum kinetic energy (Level-1)Energy of surface moleculesDerivation of surface tension and surface energy | Mechanical Properties of Fluids 🟡 Similaramaximum potential energy
Concept A molecule in the bulk is pulled equally in all directions by its neighbours, so the net cohesive force is zero. A molecule at the surface has neighbours only on the liquid side, so a net inward pull acts on it and it sits in a state of higher energy.
Formula Work done against the inward pull to bring a molecule up to the surface = increase in its potential energy. Temperature (hence mean kinetic energy) is the same throughout the liquid in equilibrium.
Solution
1. Take a molecule deep inside: forces from all sides cancel, net force = 0, so no work is needed to move it within the bulk.
2. Take a molecule in the surface layer: the half-sphere of neighbours above it is missing, so the net force is directed into the liquid.
3. To bring a bulk molecule to the surface, work must be done against this inward force, so W = ΔU > 0: the surface molecule has greater potential energy.
4. This extra potential energy per unit area is the surface energy, U/A = S; the liquid lowers its energy by shrinking its surface (minimum area).
5. Kinetic energy depends only on temperature; in equilibrium it is the same for surface and interior molecules, so (c) and (d) are wrong.
6. Surface molecules have the maximum PE, not the minimum, so (b) is wrong and (a) is right.
⚡ JEE Trick Surface = extra energy (surface energy). Anything about 'surface' and 'energy' means potential energy is maximum there.
Answer: (a) maximum potential energy.

Concept Surface tension is force per unit length acting along a line in the surface, so its unit is the unit of force divided by the unit of length. The same quantity is also energy per unit area.

3What is surface energy? Establish the relation between surface tension and surface energy.Surface energy ↔ surface tensionDerivation of surface tension and surface energy | Class 11 🟡 SimilarSurface energy = work to create unit area; T = ΔW/ΔA, so T equals the surface energy per unit area
Concept Molecules at the surface have fewer neighbours, so they have extra potential energy. Surface energy is the work needed to create new surface, by bringing molecules from the interior to the surface.
Formula T = F/L, W = F × d, ΔA = L × d
Solution
1. Definition: surface energy is the work done (isothermally) to increase the surface area of a liquid.
2. Take a film on a U-frame with a slider of length L pulled outward by F against surface tension.
3. For one free surface, F = T L; moving the slider through d gives work W = F d = T L d.
4. New area created ΔA = L d, so W = T ΔA (for two surfaces, F = 2TL and ΔA = 2Ld, same result).
5. Stored as surface energy: U = T ΔA, hence T = ΔW/ΔA = surface energy per unit area.
6. So T in N/m is numerically the same as energy per unit area in J/m2.
⚡ JEE Trick Tension and energy per unit area are the same number: T (N/m) = E/A (J/m2).
Answer: Surface energy is the work to create unit area; T = ΔW/ΔA, so T equals the surface energy per unit area.
