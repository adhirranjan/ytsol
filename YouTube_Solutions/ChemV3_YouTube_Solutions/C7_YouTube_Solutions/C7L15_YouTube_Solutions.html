Concept Once a sparingly soluble salt is in equilibrium with its saturated solution, the extent of dissolution is fixed by one equilibrium constant: the solubility product, Ksp. 1 (ii)Fill in the blank: The ______ is the phenomenon which describes the decrease in the solubility of an ionic precipitate when a soluble compound containing one of the ions of the precipitate is added to the solution in equilibrium with the precipitate.Common ion effect definitionCommon ion effect | Class 11 | Ionic equilibrium | IIT JEE & NEET | Vineet Khatri Sir| ATP STAR Kota🟡 Similarcommon ion effect
Concept Adding a soluble salt that shares an ion with a sparingly soluble precipitate pushes the dissolution equilibrium back (Le Chatelier). Ksp stays fixed, so the extra common ion forces the other ion’s concentration, and hence the solubility, down. This is the common ion effect.
Key relation Ksp = s′(s′ + c) ≈ s′c ⇒ s′ = Ksp/c For an AB salt in c M of common ion, with s′ ≪ c.
Solution
1. The phenomenon described is a decrease in solubility of an ionic precipitate caused by adding a soluble compound that contains one of its ions.
2. Take AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag+ + Cl− with Ksp = 1.8 × 10−10; in pure water s = √(1.8 × 10−10) = 1.34 × 10−5 M.
3. Now add NaCl to make [Cl−] = 0.1 M. NaCl is fully dissociated, so Cl− is the common ion.
4. Ksp = s′(s′ + 0.1) ≈ 0.1 s′ ⇒ s′ = 1.8 × 10−10/0.1 = 1.8 × 10−9 M. s′ ≪ 0.1, so dropping it is safe.
5. The ratio s/s′ = 1.34 × 10−5/1.8 × 10−9 ≈ 7.4 × 103: the solubility falls about 7,000-fold.
6. The name of this suppression by a shared ion is the common ion effect.

 0.1 M NaCl : s' = Ksp / 0.1      = 1.8e-9  M
 s / s'     = 1.34e-5 / 1.8e-9    = ~7400 x lower

Concept The ionic product (Qsp) is the same expression as Ksp but uses the actual ion concentrations, whether or not they are at equilibrium. Comparing Qsp with Ksp says whether a solution is unsaturated, saturated or supersaturated.

Concept Ksp is the equilibrium constant of the dissolution equilibrium, and like every equilibrium constant it depends only on temperature. Added ions (common ion, H+, NH3) shift the position of the equilibrium and so change the solubility s, while Ksp itself stays the same. The trap is confusing “solubility” with “solubility product”.

2 (ii)True or false: The common ion effect can increase the solubility of a solute.Common ion effect directionThe Common Ion Effect🟡 SimilarFalse
Concept The common ion effect is, by definition, the decrease in solubility caused by adding an ion already present in the salt. A common ion pushes the dissolution equilibrium to the left, so it can never raise solubility through that mechanism.
Key relation AB in c M of B−: s′ = Ksp/(c + s′) < √Ksp = s Any c > 0 makes the denominator larger.
Solution
1. For AgCl, Ksp = 1.8 × 10−10; in pure water s = 1.34 × 10−5 M.
2. In 10−3 M NaCl: s′ ≈ Ksp/c = 1.8 × 10−10/10−3 = 1.8 × 10−7 M, which is lower.
3. In 10−1 M NaCl: s′ = 1.8 × 10−10/10−1 = 1.8 × 10−9 M, lower still. The more common ion, the lower s.
4. Le Chatelier: AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag+ + Cl−. Extra Cl− on the right pushes the equilibrium left, so solid re-forms.
5. Exception that is not the common ion effect: in very concentrated Cl−, AgCl forms the complex [AgCl2]− and s rises again. That rise is complex formation, a different phenomenon.
6. So the common ion effect cannot increase solubility: the statement is False.

                 (complexation, not common ion)

⚡ JEE Trick Sort effects by direction: common ion ↓ solubility; complexing agent ↑; non-common inert salt slightly ↑ (salt effect); acid ↑ for salts of weak-acid anions (CaCO3, CaF2). If a statement credits an increase to the common ion, it is false.

Concept A Lewis base is an electron-pair donor; a Lewis acid is an electron-pair acceptor. In ammonia complexes the N atom of NH3 donates its lone pair into vacant orbitals of the metal cation (Ag+, Cu2+, Zn2+), forming a coordinate bond. So NH3 is the Lewis base and the metal ion the Lewis acid.
Key relation Ag+ (acceptor) + 2 :NH3 (donor) ⇌ [H3N→Ag←NH3]+ Each arrow is a dative bond from N to the metal.
Solution
1. NH3 has one lone pair on N (3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair, sp3).
2. Ag+ ([Kr]4d10) has empty 5s and 5p orbitals; it uses sp hybrids to accept two lone pairs.
3. Each NH3 donates its pair, giving linear [Ag(NH3)2]+. Donor = Lewis base, so NH3 is the Lewis base.
4. Similarly Cu2+ + 4NH3 → [Cu(NH3)4]2+ (deep blue): four N→Cu dative bonds.
5. This is what dissolves AgCl in aqueous NH3: removing Ag+ as the complex lets more AgCl dissolve (overall K = Ksp × Kf).
6. NH3 is also a Brønsted base (NH3 + H2O ⇌ NH4+ + OH−), but in complexation its role is Lewis base. Statement: True.
