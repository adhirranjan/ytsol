L-1

Force, Free-Body Diagrams, Weight & Gravitation

  • Force = a push or pull; an interaction between two objects. Can change an object's speed, direction, or both. SI unit = newton (N).
  • Contact force — bodies must touch (bat–ball). Non-contact / long-range force — acts without touching (gravity, magnetism).

Free-Body Diagram (FBD)

  • An FBD shows only the external forces acting on a body "freed" from its surroundings.
  • Center of mass — the point where all the mass is treated as concentrated.
  • Normal force Fn — perpendicular to a surface; always a push (surfaces cannot pull).
  • Applied force Fa, and Friction Ff — parallel to the surface, opposing motion.
💡 TipIn an FBD the length of each arrow represents the magnitude of that force. Show forces acting on the body only — never the forces it exerts on others.

Weight & Universal Gravitation

weightW = m·g (force of gravity; unit = newton)
Newton's law of gravitationF = G · m₁m₂ / r² (G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²·kg⁻²)
  • Gravity grows with mass and falls with distance² between centres. Assume each body's mass is at its geometric centre.
  • Gravity is a very weak force unless a huge mass (or a tiny separation) is involved.
Why gravity feels one-sided
The Earth pulls you with F = GMEm/r², and you pull the Earth with an equal and opposite force. You accelerate a lot (small mass) while the Earth's acceleration is immeasurably tiny (huge mass) — same force, wildly different accelerations, exactly as in Example-4.

Book Example · L-1
In a Cavendish balance a small sphere m₁ = 0.0100 kg sits r = 0.0500 m (centre-to-centre) from a large sphere m₂ = 0.500 kg. Find the gravitational force on each sphere.
Fg = G·m₁m₂/r² = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)(0.01)(0.5) / (0.05)².
Fg ≈ 1.334 × 10⁻¹⁰ N (equal & opposite on each)
Book Example · L-1
Two particles of masses 1.0 kg and 2.0 kg are 50 cm apart. Taking only mutual gravitation, find the initial acceleration of each.
F = G·m₁m₂/r² = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)(1)(2)/(0.5)² = 5.336 × 10⁻¹⁰ N (same on both).
a₁ = F/m₁ = 5.336 × 10⁻¹⁰ m·s⁻²; a₂ = F/m₂ = 2.668 × 10⁻¹⁰ m·s⁻².
a₁ = 5.336 × 10⁻¹⁰ m·s⁻², a₂ = 2.668 × 10⁻¹⁰ m·s⁻²
Book Example · L-1
A body has mass 60 kg and g = 9.8 m·s⁻². Find its weight on Earth.
W = mg = 60 × 9.8.
W = 588 N
Book Example · L-1
Find the ratio of the electric force to the gravitational force between two protons (mp = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, q = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, k = 9 × 10⁹, G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹).
Both go as 1/r², so r cancels: Fe/Fg = kq² / (G·mp²).
= (9 × 10⁹)(1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / [(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)(1.67 × 10⁻²⁷)²].
Fe/Fg ≈ 1.24 × 10³⁶
⚠ Book typoThe module prints this ratio as "1.23 × 10⁹". The coefficient is right but the exponent is wrong — evaluating kq²/(G·mp²) gives ≈ 1.24 × 10³⁶ (the famous "gravity is ~10³⁶ times weaker than electricity" result).
L-2

Normal Force, Tension & String–Pulley Systems

Normal contact force

  • Acts perpendicular to the contact surface and is always a push (Newton's third law: equal & opposite pair).
  • On a sphere, the normal reaction always passes through the centre (surface is tangential).
Stacked blocks — normals add up
For blocks A (top), B, C (bottom) stacked on the ground: N below A = wA; N below B = wA + wB; N below C (from ground) = wA + wB + wC. Each surface carries the weight of everything above it.

Tension force

key rulesTension always pulls AWAY from the body · a massless, frictionless string ⇒ SAME tension throughout · force applied directly to a string ⇒ tension = applied force
  • If the string has mass, or there is friction at the pulley, the tension changes from point to point.

String & pulley (Atwood)

two masses over a smooth pulleya = (m₂ − m₁)/(m₁ + m₂) · g · T = 2m₁m₂g / (m₁ + m₂)
★ Force on the pulleyFor a light pulley the hinge force = T + T = 2T (both string segments pull down). If equal masses m hang on both sides, thrust on pulley = 2mg.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-2
A sphere of mass 10 kg rests on the ground while a man pushes it horizontally with force F. What is the ground's normal reaction N₁? (g = 9.8 m·s⁻²)
Vertical balance on the sphere: N₁ = Mg (the horizontal push F and the wall reaction N₂ are horizontal and do not affect the vertical equation).
N₁ = (10)(9.8).
N₁ = 98 N
Book Example · L-2
A 2 kg block hangs by a string from a 5 kg block, which hangs by another string from the ceiling. Find the tensions T₁ (top) and T₂ (between). (g = 9.8 m·s⁻²)
Lower string carries only the 5 kg block: T₂ = m₂g = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N.
Top string carries both blocks: T₁ = (m₁ + m₂)g = 7 × 9.8.
T₁ = 68.6 N, T₂ = 49 N
Book Example · L-2
Blocks of 4 kg and 2 kg hang one below the other from a light string and are pulled vertically up by F = 120 N. Find (a) the acceleration, (b) the string tension. (g = 10 m·s⁻²)
(a) a = (F − m₁g − m₂g)/(m₁ + m₂) = (120 − 40 − 20)/6 = 60/6 = 10 m·s⁻².
(b) For the lower 2 kg block: T − m₂g = m₂a ⇒ T = m₂(g + a) = 2(10 + 10).
a = 10 m·s⁻², T = 40 N
Book Example · L-2
Over a smooth pulley a 2 kg block hangs on the left; on the right a 4 kg block sits above a 6 kg block. Find the acceleration a and tensions T₁ (above 2 kg) and T₂ (between 4 kg and 6 kg). (g = 10 m·s⁻²)
a = (m₁g + m₂g − m₃g)/(m₁ + m₂ + m₃) = (60 + 40 − 20)/12 = 80/12 = 20/3 m·s⁻².
6 kg block: 60 − T₂ = 6a ⇒ T₂ = 60 − 6(20/3) = 20 N.
2 kg block: T₁ − 20 = 2a ⇒ T₁ = 20 + 2(20/3) = 100/3 N.
a = 20/3 m·s⁻², T₂ = 20 N, T₁ = 100/3 ≈ 33.3 N

Equilibrium of Bodies · Sine Law & Lami's Theorem

Equilibrium under forces

  • A body/system is in equilibrium when net force = 0 AND net torque = 0: it neither translates nor rotates.
  • Resolve: ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, and the moment about any axis Στ = 0.
torque (moment)τ = F · d (d = perpendicular distance of the force's line of action from the axis)

Sine law & Lami's theorem

triangle of forces (law of sines)a/sinA = b/sinB = c/sinC
Lami's theoremFa/sinα = Fb/sinβ = Fc/sinγ (α, β, γ = angle OPPOSITE / between the other two forces)
  • Applies to exactly three coplanar, concurrent, non-collinear forces keeping a particle in equilibrium.
  • Each Greek angle is measured between the other two forces; α + β + γ = 360°.
Why Lami's works
Draw the three equilibrium forces tail-to-head — since their vector sum is zero they close into a triangle of forces. The law of sines a/sinA = b/sinB = c/sinC applies. Each interior triangle angle is the supplement of the physical angle between the arrows (A = 180° − α), and because sin(180° − θ) = sinθ, the law of sines becomes Fa/sinα = Fb/sinβ = Fc/sinγ — Lami's theorem.
⚠ TrapLami's needs exactly three concurrent forces. For four or more forces (or non-concurrent ones) it fails — fall back to ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0. "Mendeleev" and "Pythagoras" are decoy names — this is Lami's.
Worked examples from the module
Book Example · L-3
A 2 kg block rests on a smooth 37° incline. What horizontal force F keeps it in equilibrium? (g = 10 m·s⁻²)
Resolving the block's forces (N, mg, horizontal F): tan37° = F/(mg).
F = mg·tan37° = (2)(10)(3/4).
F = 15 N
Book Example · L-3
Three forces act on a particle in equilibrium: 10 N up, 10 N down-left, and F down-right, with 120° between each adjacent pair. Find F.
Lami's: F₁/sinθ₁ = F₃/sinθ₃ ⇒ 10/sin120° = F/sin120°.
All three angles are 120°, so the three magnitudes are equal.
F = 10 N
Book Example · L-3
A 2 kg block hangs held by string 1 (making 37° above the horizontal) and a horizontal string 2. Find the tension T₁ in string 1. (g = 10 m·s⁻²)
Weight = 20 N straight down (θ₁ = 90°). By Lami's: T₁/sin90° = 20/sin(90° + 53°) = 20/cos53°.
T₁ = 20/cos53° = 20/(3/5).
T₁ = 100/3 ≈ 33.3 N
Book Example · L-3
Same 2 kg setup — find the tension T₂ in the horizontal string 2.
Lami's: T₂/sin(90° + 37°) = 20/sin(90° + 53°) ⇒ T₂/cos37° = 20/cos53°.
T₂ = 20·cos37°/cos53° = 20·(4/5)/(3/5).
T₂ = 80/3 ≈ 26.7 N
Book Example · L-3
A 30 kg iron block hangs from point C by two ropes: CA makes 45° and CB makes 30° with the horizontal. Find the tensions TAC and TBC. (g = 9.8 m·s⁻²)
Weight W = 30 × 9.8 = 294 N. Angles between forces: opposite TAC = 120°, opposite TBC = 135°, opposite W = 105°.
Lami's: TAC/sin120° = TBC/sin135° = 294/sin105°.
TAC = 294 × 0.866/0.966; TBC = 294 × 0.707/0.966.
TAC ≈ 263.6 N, TBC ≈ 215.2 N
